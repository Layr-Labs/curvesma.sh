# Pollard rho on secp256k1 automorphism classes, v4: a doubling step function

## 1. Problem and claim

The target is the discrete logarithm on secp256k1: given the standard
generator `P` and `Q = [x]P` for a uniform secret `x` in `Z_r`
(`r` the group order, `log2 r = 255.9998...`), recover `x`. This package
claims, in the track's `classical-group-work-v2` model at its reference
prices, an algorithm with

- total charged work at most `2^128.05`,
- success probability at least `0.405`,
- memory at most `2^113` bytes,
- preprocessing at most `2^27`,

over the uniform challenge distribution and fresh internal coins,
including the walk's table generation.

The method is Pollard rho on the classes of the order-6 automorphism
group, with the canonicalisation of v2/v3 (a single field
multiplication; both reviews accepted it as sound), and a **new step
function**: every step *doubles* the current representative before
adding the table entry,

    G(R) = canon( 2R + S_{j(R)} ).

This replaces both the v2 plain adding step (rejected: abundant
fruitless two-cycles) and the v3 partition bump (rejected: its claimed
exact exclusion of automatic cycles is false). The doubling is not a
patch on either mechanism: it changes the *linear coefficient* of the
step from an element of the order-6 group `mu` to `2 * mu`, and the
whole cycle analysis turns on one exact, machine-checkable number
fact — the multiplicative order of `2` modulo `r` is `(r-1)/2^6`
(Section 6.2) — which makes **automatic cycles of every length up to
the trail cap impossible** (Section 6.3, a theorem, not a heuristic).
The remaining coincidence cycles are counted in Section 6.4 with the
one heuristic step disclosed in Section 6.7.

The headline work figure is honestly worse than v2/v3's `2^127.22`:
a step now costs a doubling as well as an addition, and that cost is
charged in full (Section 7). What is bought with the ~0.83 bits is a
cycle argument that does not rest on the false lemma of v3 or the
random-mapping short-cycle statistics of v2. The claim still beats
the generic BSGS accounting control (`2^129.000802`) by ~0.95 bits.

Revision map (what answers what):

1. **Cycles (new Sections 3.1, 6.2–6.4).** The v2 review exhibited a
   two-cycle family of density `1/(6J)` for the adding walk `F`; the
   v3 review exhibited an automatic two-cycle of the bumped walk `G`
   from four canonical representatives `R, Y, V, Z` in one partition
   with `R + Z = Y + V`, of expected size ~`2^181` states at
   production parameters, refuting v3's claimed `<= 2^69` bound on
   short-cycle states. Section 6.1 concedes both. Section 6.3 proves
   the doubling walk has **no automatic cycle of any length
   `1 <= ell <= 2^30`**: in the cycle's closure expansion the
   coefficient of the starting point is `2^ell * tau` with
   `tau in mu`, and `2^ell * tau = 1` would force `2^ell in mu`,
   impossible for `ell < 341948486974166000522343609283189` (a prime
   divisor of `ord_r(2)`, Section 6.2). Both reviewers' families are
   replayed against `G` explicitly in Section 6.3 and fail as
   identities. The counting bound of Section 6.4 then bounds the
   remaining (coincidence) cycle states by `2^69` as v3 attempted —
   now sound, because the case that broke v3's counting (all
   increments' coefficients vanishing with total alignment `1`) is
   exactly the case Section 6.3 excludes.
2. **Split-scalar schedule (Section 3.4).** Carried from v3 (whose
   review did not fault it) with the update formulas extended by the
   per-step doubling and the rebalancing period shortened to 24
   steps; the itemised cost is 90 of the 96 budgeted instructions.
3. **Exceptional steps (Section 3.3).** The v3 enforced treatment
   (bounded retries on consecutive indices, global affected-step
   budget `2^10`, abort probability `< 2^-120`) carried over, with
   the exceptional condition restated for the doubled point and the
   abort bound re-derived from the deterministic count of
   exceptional states.

## 2. Automorphism classes and canonicalisation

Let `phi(X, Y) = (beta X, Y)` be the GLV endomorphism, `beta` a
primitive cube root of unity in `F_p`, acting on the subgroup as
multiplication by `lambda`, a primitive cube root of unity modulo `r`
(`lambda^2 + lambda + 1 = 0`). Let

    mu = {+1, -1, +lambda, -lambda, +lambda^2, -lambda^2},

the group of 6th roots of unity in `Z_r^*` (`r = 1 mod 6`). The group
generated by negation and `phi` acts on points; its orbits on the
subgroup are the classes

    class(R) = { [tau]R : tau in mu }.

Every non-identity class has exactly 6 points (`r` is prime, so no
non-identity point is fixed by a nontrivial `tau in mu`); the
identity forms a class of its own, handled in Section 6.6's
deductions. The number of
classes is

    N'' = 1 + (r - 1)/6,   log2 N'' = 253.415...

**Canonical representative.** The canonical representative of a class
is its lexicographically smallest point (compare `x`, then `y`). The
walk also needs the alignment `tau` with `R_canon = [tau]R`, which it
tracks.

**Canonicalisation costs ONE field multiplication.** The three
candidate `x`-coordinates of a class are `x`, `beta*x`, `beta^2*x`;
each is shared by exactly two class members, `(., y)` and `(., -y)`.
Since `beta^2 = -1 - beta`, writing `s = (x + beta*x) mod p` gives
`beta^2*x = (-s) mod p`. So one multiplication (`beta*x`, 179
instructions at the organiser's measured price), one addition and one
negation produce all three candidate abscissae. Comparing them,
taking the minimum, and selecting the sign of `y`, with slack,
canonicalisation costs at most **248 charged instructions**, i.e.
`0.248` cost units of 1000. The toy program cross-checks this
single-multiplication canonicalisation against brute-force
enumeration of all six transforms on every call: 135,847 checks in
the declared run, zero disagreements (Section 8).

**Adding table.** Fix `J = 2^16` and distinct uniform scalars
`u_j, v_j in Z_r`, and let

    S_j = [u_j]P + [v_j]Q,   j = 0..J-1,

precomputed in preprocessing (each `S_j != O` is verified there).
The partition index of a class is `j(R) = x(R_canon) mod J`, the low
16 bits of the canonical abscissa. The distinguished set is

    D = { classes whose canonical abscissa is 0 mod 2^24 },

of density `theta = 2^-24` in the classes.

## 3. The v4 step function

### 3.1 Definition: doubling step

From the canonical representative `R` of the current class, with
`a = j(R)`:

1. compute the doubling `D = 2R` (one point doubling, Section 7);
2. compute `T = D + S_a` (one reference addition, with the retry rule
   of Section 3.3);
3. output `G(R) = canon(T)`.

`G` is a function on classes: the doubling, the partition index and
the canonicalisation are all determined by the class of `R` (scalar
multiplication by `2` commutes with every `[tau]`, `tau in mu`, so
doubling descends to classes), so trails that merge follow the same
successor and reach the same distinguished point, as the
distinguished-point method requires.

Scalar bookkeeping. The walk maintains `(A, B)` with
`current point = [A]P + [B]Q` for the canonical representative.
A step updates

    (A, B)  ->  tau * ( 2*(A, B) + (u_a, v_a) ),

where `tau` is the step's canonicalisation alignment: doubling the
point doubles both scalars, the addition adds the entry's scalars,
canonicalisation multiplies by `tau`. All operations are exact in
the split representation of Section 3.4.

**The one algebraic fact everything turns on.** As a point equation,
one step is `G(R) = [tau](2R + S_a)` with `tau in mu`: its *linear
coefficient* (the multiplier of the input point) is `2 * tau`, not
an element of `mu`. Section 6 exploits exactly this.

### 3.2 Why doubling, and why the earlier mechanisms cannot be repaired

An *automatic* cycle of an adding-type walk is one whose closure is
an identity in the table entries: expanding the orbit gives

    X_ell = [A] X_0 + sum_j [alpha_j] S_j,

and the cycle is automatic iff `A = 1` and every `alpha_j = 0` as
formal coefficients. For the plain walk `F` (coefficient `tau_i` per
step) and the bumped walk of v3 (coefficient a product of one or two
`mu` elements per step), `A in mu` and the vanishing of the
`alpha_j` is achievable by finite-order cancellations in `mu`
(`u + (-u) = 0`, `u(1 + lambda + lambda^2) = 0`): the v2 reviewer's
pair (`S_j = -(R + R')`, two steps of coefficient `-1`) and the v3
reviewer's quadruple (two bumped steps, each acting as
`T -> -T - S_a - S_{a+1}`, whose square is identically `T`) are
exactly these cancellations. Any mechanism whose per-step
coefficient stays inside a finite subgroup of `Z_r^*` admits such
cancellation patterns at *some* length and pattern count; excluding
them one pattern at a time is the game v3 lost. Doubling leaves the
finite subgroup: the coefficient acquires a factor `2` per step,
`A = 2^ell * tau`, and Section 6.2 shows `2^ell * tau = 1` is
impossible for every `ell` a capped trail can realise. There is no
cancellation pattern to exclude, because the coefficient condition
fails before the `alpha_j` are even examined.

Alternatives considered and rejected on the merits:

- *Larger `J` alone* (keeping the plain or bumped step): the v3
  reviewer's family has density ~`J^-3 * 6^-4`-type scaling, so a
  larger `J` shrinks it, but the *upper* bound over all automatic
  patterns still requires the false style of argument, and the
  preprocessing/table grows; the objection was to the proof, not
  only to the constant.
- *Cycle detection with escape* (Floyd/Brent or per-trail visited
  sets): detecting a cycle of length `ell` costs either a second
  walk (doubling the step cost — the same price as Section 3.1 with
  none of its algebraic finality) or per-trail storage of up to
  `L_max` states, which breaks the batched distinguished-point
  arrangement the cost model prices.
- *Doubling on a subset of steps* (e.g. one partition class in two):
  an automatic cycle can live entirely inside the non-doubling
  subset — the v2 two-cycle family restricted to non-doubling
  partitions still has density ~`1/(6J')` in that subset. The
  coefficient must change at *every* step.

### 3.3 Exceptional steps: bounded retries and a global budget

Two group operations per step have exceptional branches in the
batched incomplete affine formulas:

- the doubling `2R`: exceptional only at `R = O` (the identity class
  is never a walk state: starts landing there are resampled, and a
  step outputting `O` is the addition case below) — points of the
  subgroup have odd prime order, so no state has `y = 0`;
- the addition `D + S_idx`, `D = 2R`: exceptional iff
  `S_idx = +-D` (equal abscissae: the sum is `O` or `2D`).

**Retry rule (enforced, not assumed).** A step tries consecutive
table indices `a, a+1, a+2` (the doubling `D` is computed once) and
uses the first non-exceptional sum. If all three attempts are
exceptional, the trail is abandoned. A step is called *affected* if
any attempt was exceptional. Attempts use distinct entries; for a
*fresh* entry the exceptional probability is at most `2/r` per
attempt. The abort bound below does not rely on freshness: it uses
the deterministic state count of Section 6.4 — at most `2^17`
classes have any exceptional first attempt at all (for each index
`j`, the equation `2R = +-S_j` has at most one solution `R` per
sign) — so under H1(ii)'s per-step uniformity a step is affected
with probability at most `2^17 / N'' ~ 2^-236.4`.

**Global budget (enforcement).** The run aborts if the number of
affected steps exceeds `2^10`. The expected number of affected
steps in the whole run is at most `t * 2^-236.4 ~ 2^-109.7` (there
are `t ~ 2^126.7` steps), so by Markov's inequality

    P(abort on the affected-step budget) <= 2^-120.

An affected step costs at most 3 additions plus bookkeeping, at most
`2^3` cost units, so the worst-case total affected cost, given no
abort, is `2^10 * 2^3 = 2^13` units — a deterministic ledger line
(Section 7). The toy program implements this retry rule verbatim.

### 3.4 Split scalars: representation, schedule, and the 96 bound

Each trail tracks `(A, B)` with state `= [A]P + [B]Q`. `A` and `B`
are held in **split coordinates**

    A = A1 + A2*lambda,   B = B1 + B2*lambda     (over the integers),

where `lambda^2 = -1 - lambda`.

*Split bound.* The standard GLV decomposition of a scalar modulo `r`
(via the fixed short basis of the lattice `{(a, b) : a + b*lambda = 0
mod r}`) yields components with `|A1|, |A2| <= 2^130`; the published
secp256k1 basis vectors have components below `2^129`, and `2^130`
is a safe envelope. The increments' splits satisfy the same bound
and are computed once in preprocessing. A trail start `(a0, b0)` is
split when the trail is created (inside the per-start allowance).

*Updates.* Per Section 3.1 a step is: double both scalars, add the
entry's split increments componentwise, then apply the alignment
`tau in mu`. Doubling is `(c1, c2) -> (2*c1, 2*c2)`. An increment add
is 4 integer additions. A `mu`-map acts by additions/subtractions
only, using `lambda^2 = -1 - lambda`:

    tau =  1:        (A1, A2) -> (A1, A2)
    tau = -1:        (A1, A2) -> (-A1, -A2)
    tau =  lambda:   (A1, A2) -> (-A2, A1 - A2)
    tau = -lambda:   (A1, A2) -> (A2, A2 - A1)
    tau =  lambda^2: (A1, A2) -> (A2 - A1, -A1)
    tau = -lambda^2: (A1, A2) -> (A1 - A2, A1)

Each map is at most one subtraction, one negation and one copy of a
component: at most 15 instructions per scalar at the limb width
below, 30 for the pair.

*Growth.* Let `M` bound all four components' absolute values and
`Bnd = 2^130` the split bound. Doubling gives `2M`; an increment add
gives `2M + Bnd`; a `mu`-map at most doubles the sum of two
components: `2(2M + Bnd) = 4M + 2*Bnd`. The per-step recurrence is

    M_{s+1} <= 4 M_s + 2^131.

*Rebalancing schedule.* Every 24 steps, and at every report, each
scalar is rebalanced: evaluate `A = (A1 + A2*lambda) mod r` and
re-split by the fixed GLV basis, restoring `M <= 2^130`. Between
rebalances the recurrence gives

    M_24 <= 4^24 * 2^130 + 2^131 * (4^24 - 1)/3 < 2^179,

so every component always fits in four 64-bit limbs (capacity
`2^256`), with all intermediate sums and differences fitting the
same width. The toy program implements this schedule (Gauss-reduced
toy lattice, evaluate-from-split and Babai re-splitting, envelope
asserted at every state and immediately after every step —
Section 8).

*Cost, itemised.* Per scalar, rebalancing costs at most 400
instructions: evaluation at most 160 (one `<=2^179`-by-256-bit
multiplication `A2*lambda`, a reduction of the `<=2^435` product
modulo `r`, the final add); re-split at most 200 (two rounding
divisions by fixed basis constants; recombination); copies and
bound checks at most 40. Per `(A, B)` pair that is at most 800,
amortised over 24 steps to at most **34 per step**. The other items
at the four-limb width: doubling is 4 component doublings of at most
4 limbs each, at most **16**; increments are 4 additions of at most
4 limbs each, at most **16**; a `mu`-map per scalar is one
subtraction (4), one negation (4) and one copy (4), so at most
**24** for the pair. The scalar line of the ledger is therefore

    doubling <= 16;  increments <= 16;  mu-maps <= 24;
    rebalancing <= 34;  total <= 90 <= 96 instructions,

and the ledger keeps the v2/v3 figure of 96 (0.096 units) for
margin. Report-time evaluation uses components of size at most
`2^179` and costs at most 240 instructions per scalar, inside the
per-report allowance of Section 7.

### 3.5 Remaining run rules and global budgets

As in v2/v3: trails start from uniform `(a0, b0)`; a trail ends by
reporting its first distinguished iterate, by reaching
`L_max = 2^30` steps, by an all-exceptional step (Section 3.3), or by
solving the instance. At most `2^104` trail starts and at most
`2^104` processed distinguished reports are charged; the run aborts
beyond these caps (overflow probabilities in Section 6.6). A start
landing in the identity class (probability `~1/r`) is resampled.
The walk stops when a collision is reported, when `t` steps
(Section 5) have executed, or on any budget abort (a failure event,
charged to the deductions of Section 6.6).

## 4. A collision gives the discrete logarithm

Bookkeeping maintains for every visited class the invariant

    state = [A]P + [B]Q

with `(A, B)` the tracked scalars of Section 3.4 (the doubling, the
increment and the alignment of each step included), evaluated
exactly for the canonical representative. When two trails report
the same distinguished class, they report the *same canonical point*
`R` with tracked scalars `(A1, B1)` and `(A2, B2)` satisfying
`[A_i]P + [B_i]Q = R`, so

    (A1 - A2) = (B2 - B1) x (mod r),

which determines `x` whenever `B1 != B2`; the candidate is checked
by one multiplication against `Q` (inside the final-solve
allowance).

The degenerate case `B1 = B2` forces `A1 = A2` as well (`A` is
determined by the point `R` and `B`), i.e. the two trails carry
identical data — a pure merge, giving no equation. It is a
deduction: there are at most `2^207` report pairs; a pair shares a
class with probability at most `~2^-253.4` and, given the class, has
matching `B`-coordinates with probability at most `~2^-250` under
the same equidistribution the walk relies on throughout, so the
expected number of degenerate collisions is below `2^-290`; the
deduction ledger (Section 6.6) charges a very conservative
`2^-104` for it.

## 5. Success probability I: the birthday term and the waste allowance

Let

    t = 141561145530318902406949923548642162214 ~ 2^126.735

be the least integer with `t^2 >= 2 * N'' * 0.519195`, where
`0.519195 >= -ln(1 - 0.405) = 0.5191939...` (rounded up). Under the
collision heuristic H1 (Section 6.7), if `t` steps of the walk are
executed on distinct-class-producing terms, the probability that two
trails report a common class — a collision in the birthday sense
over `N''` classes — is at least

    1 - exp(-t^2 / (2 N'')) >= 1 - exp(-0.519195) >= 0.4050006.

Steps that revisit an already-visited class are *waste*: they do not
advance the birthday count. In v2, waste from short cycles was
asserted negligible by an appeal to random-mapping statistics that
the review showed to be false for the plain walk `F`; in v3 the
waste bound rested on the false automatic-cycle lemma. In v4 the
waste is bounded by the analysis of Section 6: except with
probability at most `2^-67` over the run's coins, total waste is at
most `2^40` steps. Replacing `t` by `t - 2^40` changes
`t^2 / (2 N'')` by at most `t * 2^40 / N'' ~ 2^-86.7`, hence the
birthday term by at most `2^-86`. The remaining deductions (budget
aborts, degenerate collisions, cap overflows) are collected in
Section 6.6; the total is below `2^-66`, so

    success >= 0.4050006 - 2^-66 >= 0.405.

Everything now rests on Section 6's cycle analysis at production
parameters: `J = 2^16`, `theta = 2^-24`, `L_max = 2^30`.

## 6. Success probability II: cycles at production parameters

### 6.1 The reviewers' families, restated and conceded

For the plain walk `F` of v2, fix a partition `j` and distinct
canonical `R, R'` with `j(R) = j(R') = j`. If `S_j = -(R + R')` then
`F(R) = R'` and `F(R') = R`: a two-cycle whose expected number of
states is `N''/(6J) ~ 2^234.8`, a fraction `1/(6J) = 2^-18.58` of
all classes — exceeding the distinguished density `theta = 2^-24`,
so a plain-walk trail meets a two-cycle state before a distinguished
point with probability ~0.977. Conceded (the census of Section 8
reproduces the `N''/(6J)` law on the v4 toy curve: 1430/620/352/192
two-cycle states at `J = 16/32/64/128` against 1363/681/341/170
predicted).

For the bumped walk of v3, the reviewer chose four distinct
canonical representatives `R, Y, V, Z` in one partition `a` with
`R + Z = Y + V`, set `S_a = Y - R` and `S_{a+1} = -Y - V`, and
obtained `G_bumped(R) = V`, `G_bumped(V) = R`, each full step acting
as `T -> -T - S_a - S_{a+1}`, whose square is identically `T`.
Counting such configurations by additive energy (`>= m^4/r` ordered
solutions in a bucket of size `m`) gives an expected ~`2^181`
two-cycle states in one production bucket, refuting v3's claimed
`<= 2^69` bound for all short-cycle states. Conceded; v4 changes
the walk so that the closure of *every* short cycle is a genuine
equation (Section 6.3).

### 6.2 Lemma (the order of 2 modulo r)

Factor

    r - 1 = 2^6 * 3 * 149 * 631 * q1 * q2 * q3,

    q1 = 107361793816595537                    (~2^57),
    q2 = 174723607534414371449                 (~2^68),
    q3 = 341948486974166000522343609283189     (~2^109),

with `q1, q2, q3` prime. (The product is checkable by direct
multiplication; the primality of each `qi` and of 149, 631 by any
standard primality test — the largest factor, `q3`, is below
`2^110`.) Then

    ord_r(2) = (r - 1) / 2^6 = 3 * 149 * 631 * q1 * q2 * q3
             = 1809251394333065553493296640760748560200586941860545380978205674086221273349.

Verification is seven modular exponentiations: `2^ord = 1 (mod r)`,
and for each prime divisor `s` of `ord`,
`2^(ord/s) != 1 (mod r)`; the values are

    s = 3:    0xac9c52b33fa3cf1f5ad9e3fd77ed9ba4a880b9fc8ec739c2e0cfc810b51283ce
    s = 149:  0x24deb1e3aa643c8109d47b2f7c118b6def74bba10589326c48420d928ceb3adc
    s = 631:  0xcff62c5c6d1298496eeb1eb55655038554906ac07e1098324ae0931efd8f2f68
    s = q1:   0x9bf14c630e9053f19b33fe565d32d450779e5eecfc32d0fe0f9c1658b9b7c648
    s = q2:   0xbe7534ac38a0f4a19808afaf706937326e9e24c54f7e98966de7b4705adf92c0
    s = q3:   0x1d7b253352a5921cc987358b29d685d864ad96543a66825fc251b2f2e57421d3

(none is `1`; together with the factorisation this pins the order
exactly, by the standard order-divisibility argument).

**Corollary.** For every `1 <= ell < q3` — in particular for every
`ell <= 2^30` — `2^ell notin mu`. Indeed `2^ell in mu` implies
`(2^ell)^6 = 1`, i.e. `ord_r(2) | 6 * ell`; since the prime
`q3 | ord_r(2)` and `q3 > 3`, this forces `q3 | ell`, hence
`ell >= q3 > 2^108`. (The exact threshold is even larger:
`2^ell in mu` first occurs at `ell = ord_r(2)/3 ~ 2^249`, where
`2^ell` is the element of order 3 shown for `s = 3` above.)

### 6.3 Theorem (no automatic cycles)

Fix a length `1 <= ell <= 2^30`, a starting class `X != O`, and a
*phase pattern*: the sequence of table indices, retry offsets and
canonicalisation alignments realised by the orbit of `X` under `G`.
Expanding the `ell` steps symbolically as point equations
(step `i` contributes `[tau_i](2 * . + S_{idx_i})`), the `ell`-fold
iterate is

    G^ell(X) = [A] X + sum_j [alpha_j] S_j

with

    A = 2^ell * (prod_i tau_i) = 2^ell * tau,   tau in mu,
    alpha_j = (the coefficient with which S_j enters the expansion:
               a sum, over the occurrences k of entry j, of
               2^(ell-1-k) * prod_{i>=k} tau_i).

The orbit closes (`G^ell(X) = X`) iff

    [A - 1] X + sum_j [alpha_j] S_j = O.        (*)

Call the cycle **automatic** if (*) is an identity in the table
entries and `X` (i.e. it holds for all tables realising the
pattern). Automaticity requires `A = 1` and every `alpha_j = 0`.

**`A = 1` is impossible.** `A = 1` iff `2^ell = tau^-1 in mu`,
contradicting the Corollary of Section 6.2 for `ell <= 2^30`.
Moreover, if every `alpha_j = 0` while `A != 1`, then (*) reads
`[A - 1] X = O` with `A - 1 != 0 (mod r)` (as `A != 1`) and
`X != O` — impossible. Hence:

> **Every cycle of `G` of length `1 <= ell <= 2^30`, on every phase
> pattern, has some `alpha_j != 0`: its closure is a genuine
> equation in the table entries, never an identity.**

Two classes of entries are visibly non-resolving-proof: an entry
occurring *exactly once* in the cycle has `alpha_j` a single
nonzero term; an entry occurring *exactly twice*, at steps
`k1 < k2`, has `alpha_j = 0` only if the ratio of its two terms is
`-1`, which unfolds to `2^(k2-k1) in mu` up to a `mu` factor — with
`0 < k2 - k1 < ell <= 2^30` this is again excluded by the
Corollary. So the resolving entry whose existence the theorem
gives can already be found among the singly- and twice-occurring
entries whenever one of those is present.

Both reviewers' constructions, replayed against `G`:

- *v2 family.* `R, R'` in partition `j` with `S_j = -(R + R')`:
  `G(R) = canon(2R + S_j) = canon(R - R')`, which is `R'` only if
  `R - R' in class(R')`, i.e. only under the additional equation
  `R - R' = [tau] R'` for some `tau in mu` — a condition on the
  points, not an identity; the two-step expansion has
  `A = 4 * tau_1 * tau_2 != 1` and the `S_j` coefficient
  `alpha_j = 2*tau_2*tau_1 + tau_2 * (alignment of step 2)` does not
  vanish identically. The systematic density `1/(6J)` is gone: what
  remains is counted in Section 6.4.
- *v3 family.* With `S_a = Y - R`, `S_{a+1} = -Y - V` and
  `R + Z = Y + V`: the first doubling step gives
  `2R + S_a = R + Y`, whose canonical representative is some class
  `W`; there is no bump in `G` (the step has one phase), and the
  claimed involution `T -> -T - S_a - S_{a+1}` is replaced by
  `T -> [tau](2T + S_{j(T)})`, whose square has leading coefficient
  `4 * tau * tau' != 1`. The additive-energy supply of quadruples
  `R + Z = Y + V` is irrelevant: those quadruples closed the v3
  cycle because the two steps' coefficients were both `-1`; here
  closure imposes (*) with `A != 1`, one genuine equation per
  pattern, counted in Section 6.4.

Retries (Section 3.3) do not affect the theorem: a retry changes
only which index a step uses, not its coefficient `2 * tau`.

### 6.4 Coincidence bound: cycles of G are rare at every length

It remains to bound cycles whose closure is a genuine equation.
Fix a class `X` and a length `ell >= 1`, and consider the event that
`X` lies on an `ell`-cycle of `G` (at most `ell` phases — `G` has
exactly one phase per step, plus possible retry attempts, handled
at the end of this section). By Section 6.3, on every phase pattern
realising the cycle, some entry coefficient `alpha_j` in (*) is
nonzero.

- *Fresh final entry.* If the final step's entry occurs nowhere
  earlier in the orbit, no conditioning subtlety arises: condition
  on all other entries and on the orbit up to the final step; the
  state `X_{ell-1}` is then fixed, and as the final entry
  `S_j` ranges uniformly over the subgroup, `2 X_{ell-1} + S_j`
  ranges uniformly over the subgroup, so its class is uniform over
  the `N''` classes (canon maps exactly 6 points to each
  non-identity class): the probability of closing on `X` is exactly
  `6/r <= 2/N''`.
- *Non-fresh final entry.* Resolve (*) in an entry `S_j` with
  `alpha_j != 0` (one exists, by Section 6.3): hold the phase
  pattern and all other entries fixed; the equation is then
  `[alpha_j] S_j = (fixed point)` with `alpha_j != 0`, and as
  `S_j` ranges uniformly over the subgroup the left side is
  uniform, so the equation holds for exactly one value of `S_j`:
  probability `1/r` per pattern (the `6/r` slack used below absorbs
  the canonicalisation's alignment freedom in the pattern). The
  resolving entry need not be a repeated one: by the remark after
  the Theorem, a singly-occurring entry always has
  `alpha_j != 0`, and so does an exactly-twice-occurring one; a
  single occurrence only costs less pattern budget than the two
  occurrences budgeted below. The
  conditioning on the phase pattern is the one heuristic step in
  this section; it is exactly H1(ii) as scoped in Section 6.7, and
  it is also the standard idealisation in every rho analysis. Note
  what the heuristic is *not* asked to do: in v3 the same style of
  step was applied to patterns on which the equation was an
  identity (probability `1`, not `6/r`) — Section 6.3 proves no
  such pattern exists for `G`, so the resolution is always
  available.

Counting patterns for this case: a distinguished resolving entry
is fixed by an occurrence (at most `ell` phases; a second
occurrence, if the entry is a repeated one, is likewise at most
`ell`) and a pattern of at most `144` (phase type and alignment at
each of up to two occurrences, with slack); per `(X, ell)` the
cost is at most
`ell * 144 * (6/r)` — half of v3's factor, since `G` has one phase
per step rather than two. Summing over `ell <= 2^30` and all `N''`
classes: `N'' * (6/r) ~ 1`, so this case contributes at
most `sum_ell 144 * ell = 144 * 2^59 * (1 + o(1)) ~ 2^66.2 < 2^68`
expected states, and the fresh case `2^31`:

    E[# states on cycles of G of length <= 2^30] <= 2^31 + 2^68 <= 2^69,

a density of at most `2^69 / N'' ~ 2^-184` among classes. The bound
is deliberately crude — it union-bounds over every distinguished
occurrence with generous pattern slack; its role is only to sit so
far below the class count that the Section 6.5 waste bound is
insensitive to the crudeness. (The census of Section 8 measures the
truth at toy scale: the doubling walk's two-cycle states are
0, 2, 0, 2 at `J = 16, 32, 64, 128` — the coincidence level of this
section — against the plain walk's 1430, 620, 352, 192 tracking the
systematic law `N''/(6J)`; its total cyclic states, 412–552, are at
the random-mapping scale `sqrt(pi N''_toy / 2) ~ 453`.)

Cycles longer than `L_max = 2^30` cannot waste work inside a capped
trail: a trail entering one traverses distinct classes until the
cap, and those steps count toward the birthday term like any
others. Only cycles of length at most `L_max` produce repeated
steps, and those are the ones bounded above.

**Cycles through retry attempts.** The counting above is for
retry-free steps; the Section 3.3 retries change which entry a step
uses at a state only when an entry equals `+-2R` for the current
point `R`. For each index `j` there are at most 2 points `R` with
`2R = +-S_j`, so at most `2^17` states have an exceptional first
attempt (deterministically, given the table). A cycle using a retry
phase passes through one of these states `Z`; for fixed `Z` and
length `ell`, closure is again (*) with some `alpha_j != 0`
(Section 6.3 applies verbatim — retries change indices, not
coefficients), of probability at most `6/N''`-type per pattern, so
the expected number of cycle states through retry phases is below
`2^-170` even after restoring the pattern factor of the main
count — absorbed into the Section 6.4 total.

### 6.5 Trapping at production parameters

Under H1's per-step uniformity (Section 6.7), a step lands on a
short-cycle state with probability at most the density `2^-184` of
Section 6.4. Counting only *first* entries into a cycle (each
trail enters a given cycle at most once before it is trapped; a
trapped trail's subsequent in-cycle steps are not new trapping
events), a trapping event wastes at most `L_max` further steps.
Hence

    E[wasted steps] <= t * 2^-184 * 2^30 ~ 2^-27.3,

and by Markov's inequality `P(waste > 2^40) <= 2^-67.3 <= 2^-67`,
the allowance used in Section 5.

**Affected steps.** Section 3.3: abort probability below `2^-120`.

Derivations for the three ledger lines not computed elsewhere in

### 6.7 H1, restated with its narrowed scope

**H1 (v4).** For the doubling iteration `G`, the classes visited by
a trail behave like the iterates of a uniform random mapping on the
`N''` classes for exactly two purposes: (i) collision (birthday)
statistics of visited classes; (ii) per-step uniformity — the
current class is uniform given no prior trapping — used for the
density bounds of Sections 6.4–6.5, including the disclosed
conditioning on the phase pattern in Section 6.4's
nonzero-coefficient case.

No short-cycle statistics are assumed in v4: the systematic
families are excluded by the exact Theorem of Section 6.3 and the
residual cycle content is bounded by the counting of Section 6.4,
whose heuristic input is (ii). This is the assumption the
experiments support (Section 8); production-scale cycle behaviour
rests on Sections 6.2–6.4, not on extrapolating the toy.

## 8. Experiments

`experiment.py` (declared in `submission.json`) is a stdlib-only
miniature of the algorithm of Sections 2–3. Two scope decisions
differ from v2/v3 and are forced by the mechanism:

- **Toy curve.** The v2/v3 toy (`y^2 = x^3 + 7` over `F_786433`,
  subgroup order 262657) has `ord(2) = 27` modulo its subgroup
  order, so `2^9 in mu` there and the toy itself exhibits automatic
  cycles of length 9 — it cannot model a mechanism whose content is
  `2^ell notin mu`. The v4 toy is `y^2 = x^3 + 11` over the same
  field `F_786433`: its group order is the prime
  `r_t = 784897` (cofactor 1), `N''_toy = 130817` classes,
  `ord_{r_t}(2) = 196224`, `ell_min = 32704` — the same
  configuration as production (`ell_min ~ 2^249` against cap `2^30`;
  here `32704` against a census whose longest cycle is 324 and an
  attack cap of `16/theta = 64` steps). The program computes
  `ord2_toy` and `ell_min_toy` itself and reports them.
- **Controls on the same curve.** The same program implements the
  plain walk `F` (v2) and the bumped walk (v3) on the *same* toy
  curve with the same table seed, so the three walks' numbers are
  directly comparable; the step function is implemented once per
  walk and shared by the attack and census modes, including the
  Section 3.3 retry rule and the Section 3.4 split bookkeeping with
  the 24-step rebalancing schedule (split state asserted against a
  plain-arithmetic shadow after every step, before rebalancing;
  reports use the split-evaluated scalars).

**Declared run (attack, doubling walk, `J = 32`, `theta = 1/4`,
300 trials, seed 20261106).** Success within the budget
`t_toy = 369` steps: **0.600** in the total-steps metric and 0.390
in the distinct-visited-classes metric, against the 0.405 target
the budget encodes; all 300 secrets recovered within the
`8 t_toy` cap; mean total steps at success 339.2; mean visited
classes 447.7 against the random-mapping prediction
`sqrt(pi N''_toy / 2) = 453.3`; **4 trapped trails**; 1 affected
step; 203,516 group operations (two per step: one doubling, one
addition); 0 bad collisions; 237,605 split-state invariant checks
with 0 violations; 135,847 canonicalisation cross-checks with 0
disagreements.

**Control (attack, plain walk, identical settings).** Steps-metric
success 0.420, visited-metric 0.393, **485 trapped trails**,
131,972 group operations. The doubling step alone therefore
accounts for: trapped trails 485 -> 4 and steps-metric success
0.420 -> 0.600 at toy scale, at the price of a second group
operation per step (charged in production, Section 7).

**Census (exact functional graphs, same table seed).** Two-cycle
states, plain walk vs the v2 prediction `N''_toy/(6J)`:
`J = 16`: 1430 vs 1363; `J = 32`: 620 vs 681; `J = 64`: 352 vs 341;
`J = 128`: 192 vs 170 — the reviewers' law, tracked at every `J`.
Doubling walk at the same `J`: **0, 2, 0, 2** two-cycle states —
the coincidence level of Section 6.4. Total cyclic states of the
doubling walk (412, 465, 552, 156 at `J = 16, 32, 64, 128`) are of
the random-mapping order (scale `sqrt(pi N''_toy/2) ~ 453`;
individual values fluctuate, including the low 156 at `J = 128`),
while the plain walk's totals (1670, 820, 582, 206) are dominated
by its two-cycles at small `J`. A second doubling census at
`J = 32` with an independent table seed (777) gives the same
picture: 281 cyclic states, 0 two-cycle states (see

this curve (721 cyclic states, 0 two-cycle states, longest cycle
711) is also reported in `submission.json`'s follow-ups: at toy
scale the bump looks healthy, exactly as it did in v3 — its
production failure is the Section 6.1 counting argument, which is
why v4's defence is the exact Section 6.3 theorem rather than a
census.

**Scope.** The experiments do not measure production instruction
costs (the ledger is Section 7, at the organiser's reference
prices), do not reach `J = 2^16` or `theta = 2^-24` (infeasible at
toy scale), and do not prove H1 at production scale. They validate:
the doubling step destroys the disputed systematic families in the
exact functional graph (census), on a toy curve chosen so the
production theorem's hypothesis (`ell_min` large) holds there too;
the walk's collision statistics match the birthday prediction in
the metric the claim is charged in (total steps, repeated work
included); and the split-scalar schedule maintains its invariants
in the implementation that mirrors the production description.

## 9. Scope and honesty notes

- The claim is a heuristic random-mapping argument (H1, scoped in
  Section 6.7) on top of exact canonicalisation, collision algebra,
  an *exact* exclusion of automatic cycles at every relevant length
  (Sections 6.2–6.3), a counting bound on coincidence cycles, an
  enforced exceptional-step rule, and a fully scheduled scalar
  bound. It is not a proof that `G` is a random mapping.
- The headline number is worse than v2/v3's `2^127.22` by 0.83
  bits. That is the honest price of the repair: both earlier
  numbers rested on cycle analyses the reviewers refuted, and the
  doubling that makes the cycle analysis exact costs one extra
  group operation per step, charged in full in Section 7. The
  claim still improves on the track's BSGS accounting control
  (`2^129.000802`) by ~0.95 bits.
- The order computation of Section 6.2 is exact arithmetic over
  the *public* group order `r`: factorisation of `r - 1` and seven
  modular exponentiations, all reproducible from the constants
  printed there. No trust in the toy experiments is needed for it.
- The toy curve is a model of the class walk, not of secp256k1's
  field arithmetic; production costs enter only through the
  organiser's reference prices in Section 7.
- Nothing in this package uses nonuniform advice, preprocessing on
  the target beyond the stated `2^27`, or data from other
  submissions.
