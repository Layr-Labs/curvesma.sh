# Deterministic enumeration control: secp256k1-ecdlp

## Claim and scope

This is a deliberately conservative analytic baseline, not a new cryptanalytic technique, a competitive attack, or a measured production run. It solves every honestly generated full-production input for this track, hence has success probability 1, exceeding 0.39. It uses no heuristics, quantum computation, random coins, nonuniform advice, precomputed answer table, or amortization. The submitted computation upper bound is 2^310 target-operation equivalents, with rational log2 score 310/1. The considerable slack is intentional; this is not a best-known complexity estimate. All quantities below are upper bounds, not measurements.

For BLS this claim applies to every 1<=n<r, including the required n=2^20, without number-theoretic conditions on n. The attack ignores the auxiliary powers mathematically; it may scan their serialization to reach the target. Input generation is excluded, input processing is charged. For secp there is no auxiliary input, so use n=0 in the accounting below.

## Algorithm

Let G be the protected standard generator, r its order, and T the target point (called Q on secp256k1). Start i=0 and R=infinity. While i<r, compare R with T; if equal, return the canonical 32-byte scalar i. Otherwise set R=R+G and i=i+1. On BLS the successful i is nonzero because the target secret is in 1..r-1. The final comparison is an exact equality in the full production group. No truncated scalar search is used.

The invariant is R=[i]G. Induction follows from the group law, starting at the identity. The profile's target is generated from some a in 0..r-1 (or s in 1..r-1 for BLS G1). Consequently iteration i=a matches exactly and terminates. Returning at an earlier exact match is also acceptable for the inversion tracks: their acceptance condition is the returned image, not recovery of a specific hidden scalar. Scalar-track equality is unique because G has order r. In particular no unproved injectivity of the raw Miller map is necessary. The identity case is handled explicitly.

## Concrete base-field and point arithmetic

Use 64-bit scalar AArch64 instructions and fixed arrays of seven 64-bit limbs for all base-field arithmetic. Both moduli have at most 381 bits; the seventh limb accommodates carry for addition. Canonical residues are always reduced. An addition computes a+b with carry and conditionally subtracts p. Subtraction computes a-b and conditionally adds p on borrow. Comparisons inspect the limbs from most significant to least. Load/store, carry, branch and address work are included: reserve 128 instructions per limb per pass and four seven-limb passes plus 512 fixed instructions. Thus one addition/subtraction/comparison/copy costs at most A=2^12 instructions. This is generous for straight scalar loads, ADDS/ADCS, SUBS/SBCS, comparisons, selections and branches; it is not a unit-cost wide-integer assumption.

Multiplication is bit-serial double-and-add modulo p: t=0,v=a; for bits j=0..380 of b, conditionally t=addmod(t,v), then v=addmod(v,v). Include leading zero bits. At most 762 modular additions, 381 bit tests and loop overhead, plus setup/copy, fit M=2^24 instructions (762*4096 is less than 2^22). Squaring is a multiplication. For nonzero a, inversion is binary powering a^(p-2), at most 762 multiplications and less than 2^20 instructions of bit scanning, setup and control; I=2^35 bounds it. This uses Fermat's theorem for the protected prime p, not an inversion oracle.

Maintain R in affine coordinates and a separate infinity flag. For R+G: if R is infinity, copy G; if the x coordinates agree and the y coordinates sum to zero, return infinity; otherwise the equal-point branch uses slope (3*x_R^2)/(2*y_R), and the unequal-x branch uses (y_G-y_R)/(x_G-x_R). Then x_new=slope^2-x_R-x_G and y_new=slope*(x_R-x_new)-y_R (in doubling x_G=x_R). On the nonexceptional branches the denominator is nonzero. Both curves have a=0. These are the standard chord/tangent group law. Charge one I, 32 M and 32 A plus 2^20 overhead, below 2^36 per addition. This includes testing every exceptional branch. Exact point comparison and scalar increment use fixed arrays; no scalar exceeds 256 bits. Output is canonical hex or null for infinity.

## Extension arithmetic and exact evaluation

For point-only tracks the following allowance is unused. For the pairing/Miller tracks, kernels.md provides the exact formulas and schedule; this paragraph prices them under the CURRENT 64-bit model, replacing the older repository baseline's word convention.

In Fp2, u^2=-1 and (a+bu)(c+du)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)u. In Fp12, w^12=2*w^6-2. Multiply dense degree-11 polynomials using 144 base products and 144 accumulation additions. Reduce terms of degrees 22 down to 12: for coefficient c at degree d add 2c at degree d-6 and subtract 2c at degree d-12. At most 44 additional modular additions suffice, including formation of 2c. All intermediate coefficients are reduced. Fp12 multiplication therefore costs at most 144M+188A+2^20 < E=2^34. Fp2 multiplication, extension addition/subtraction, coefficient movement and comparisons also cost less than E. The 2^20 reserve includes loops, pointer arithmetic and movement of arrays of at most 23*7 limbs; no array has an exponential address.

Each projective point operation, twist, cast, or rational line call in kernels.md uses fewer than 64 extension arithmetic calls and fewer than 2^20 other scalar instructions; allocate 128E for each. A Miller substep uses fewer than eight such calls, including accumulator updates and control, so allocate 1024E. There are fewer than 128 substeps. Allocate another 1024E for initialization, validation of the fixed generators/current point, decoding constants, and the final quotient multiplication. This subtotal is below 2^52 instructions. Denominator inversion is binary powering in Fp12 by p^12-2; reduced pairing additionally powers by (p^12-1)/r. Each exponent has at most 4572 bits and costs at most 9144E plus 2^24 overhead, less than 2^48. Even BOTH exponentiations, loop subtotal and wrapper fit below 2^53. Raw Miller omits the final exponentiation. Infinity returns one immediately.

Exponent construction is charged, not an oracle: represent integers up to p^12 in 73 64-bit limbs, compute twelve successive bit-serial integer products by p, then ordinary binary long division by r. Reserve 512 instructions per limb per bit for carry, loads/stores, comparisons and control. Product stages total at most 12*381*73*512 < 2^28; division at most 4572*73*512 < 2^28. A 2^32 budget includes decoding constants and both exponent constructions. This can be done once on the cold input. Registers, arrays and all routines fit well within the memory bound below.

## Complete computation, preprocessing, and memory

Allow 2^60 scalar instructions for EACH outer iteration, including image evaluation if applicable, affine addition, exact comparison, counter operations, failure path, control and transfers. The explicit bounds above are below this even for reduced pairing. Allow two further units of 2^60 for cold initialization and final independent verification/output (e.g. recompute [i]G by <=512 affine operations, and the applicable image). No group operation is charged again: all work is counted as scalar instructions R, with H=0.

Compact canonical profile input contains fewer than 1024 bytes per auxiliary point plus fixed target/header data. A streaming parser can discard A, retain only n and the target, and use bounded buffers; at most 2^20 instructions per point cover 1024 bytes with <=1024 instructions per byte, plus fixed-header handling. The profile supplies mathematical inputs; arbitrary transport whitespace/padding is not an extra adversarial attack parameter. The representation used here is compact canonical JSON. No validation or use of the honestly generated discarded powers is required by auxiliary_inputs.access. This implementation does not require loading the whole A array into RAM or random-addressing an exponentially large input.

Let C=1000, the protected normalization for this track. A concrete complete-work bound is

    L(n) = ((r+2)*2^60 + (n+1)*2^20) / C.

For secp n=0; for BLS 1<=n<r. Since r<2^256, the numerator is less than 2^318, and C>=1000>2^9, so L(n)<2^309. We submit the looser uniform bound W(n)=2^310, and for BLS log2(W(2^20))=310 exactly. This upper bound is valid for the whole stated n range. It can equivalently be written H(n)=0, R_bound(n)=C*2^310. The fixed C is unchanged; none of the 6M reference's A=0 simplifications are applied to our work.

Preprocessing is at most (2^60+(n+1)*2^20)/C and is ALREADY INCLUDED in L(n), not amortized. No saved state from other targets is allowed. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes. Peak working memory, including code, constants, input buffers, counters, stack, arithmetic scratch and output, is at most 2^24 bytes: fewer than 4096 seven-limb field slots (229376 bytes), less than 65536 bytes for integer exponents/counters, less than 65536 bytes of streaming buffers, and less than 2^20 bytes of simple loop-based code/stack/constants suffice. Use iterative powering and enumeration, no recursion or accumulated history. If external input storage is also counted, add at most 1024(n+1) bytes for the compact public instance; peak INCLUDING that storage is bounded by 2^24+1024(n+1) bytes. At scoring n=2^20 this is less than 2^31 bytes. The secp instance plus working state is less than 2^25 bytes.
