# Pollard rho on secp256k1 automorphism classes, v3: a partition-bump step function

## 1. Problem and claim

The target is the discrete logarithm on secp256k1: given the standard
generator `P` and `Q = [x]P` for a uniform secret `x` in `Z_r`
(`r` the group order, `log2 r = 255.9998...`), recover `x`. This package
claims, in the track's `classical-group-work-v2` model at its reference
prices, an algorithm with

- total charged work at most `2^127.22`,
- success probability at least `0.405`,
- memory at most `2^113` bytes,
- preprocessing at most `2^27`,

over the uniform challenge distribution and fresh internal coins,
including the walk's table generation.

The method is the v2 method — Pollard rho on the classes of the order-6
automorphism group, with canonicalisation by a single field
multiplication — with three repairs answering the v2 review, which
rejected v2 on exactly these points:

1. **Cycle avoidance (new Section 3.1, analysis in Section 6).** The v2
   step function `F` admits the reviewer's two-cycle family
   (`S_j = -(R + R')` on a shared partition), putting a fraction
   `~1/(6J)` of all classes on fruitless two-cycles. The v3 step
   function `G` adds a *partition bump*: whenever the canonicalised
   successor of `R` has the same partition index as `R`, the step
   continues with the next table entry. Section 6 proves this destroys
   the reviewer's family and indeed every one-entry-determined cycle,
   and bounds the remaining (coincidence) cycle states at production
   parameters.
2. **A specified split-scalar schedule (Section 3.4).** The 96-instruction
   scalar line of the cost ledger is now backed by an explicit
   representation, update formulas, growth envelope and a
   rebalancing-every-36-steps schedule; the itemised cost is 63 of the
   96 budgeted instructions.
3. **Enforced exceptional steps (Section 3.3).** The v2 assumption "at
   most one affected step per trail" is replaced by bounded retries on
   consecutive table indices plus a *global* affected-step budget of
   `2^10` with abort; the abort probability is below `2^-135`, and the
   worst-case affected cost is `2^14` work units (v2's ledger carried an
   unjustified `2^115` allowance for this line).

The claimed numbers are unchanged from v2 — not because nothing changed,
but because the repairs are cheap: the bump costs at most `2^112.5` work
units in the extras and the enforced exceptional line costs `2^14`, both
inside the slack of the v2 envelope, whose grand total was `2^127.2124`
against the same `2^127.22` claim (Section 7).

The declared experiment `experiments[0]` in `submission.json` implements
the v3 walk itself (bumped step function, bounded retries, split
bookkeeping with the rebalancing schedule), counts total steps including
repeated work, and adds an exact functional-graph census mode that
measures the cycle structure the review disputed (Section 8).

## 2. Automorphism classes and canonicalisation

Let `phi(X, Y) = (beta X, Y)` be the GLV endomorphism, `beta` a primitive
cube root of unity in `F_p`, acting on the subgroup as multiplication by
`lambda`, a primitive cube root of unity modulo `r`
(`lambda^2 + lambda + 1 = 0`). Let

    mu = {+1, -1, +lambda, -lambda, +lambda^2, -lambda^2},

the group of 6th roots of unity in `Z_r^*` (`r = 1 mod 6`). The group
generated by negation and `phi` acts on points; its orbits on the
subgroup are the classes

    class(R) = { [tau]R : tau in mu }.

A generic class has 6 points (points with extra symmetry lie in
negligible subsets: the identity and the points of order 2 and 3, at most
a handful, handled in Section 6.5's deductions). The number of classes is

    N'' = 1 + (r - 1)/6,   log2 N'' = 253.415...

**Canonical representative.** The canonical representative of a class is
its lexicographically smallest point (compare `x`, then `y`). The walk
also needs the alignment `tau` with `R_canon = [tau]R`, which it tracks.

**Canonicalisation costs ONE field multiplication.** The three candidate
`x`-coordinates of a class are `x`, `beta*x`, `beta^2*x`; each is shared
by exactly two class members, `(., y)` and `(., -y)`. Since
`beta^2 = -1 - beta`, writing `s = (x + beta*x) mod p` gives
`beta^2*x = (-s) mod p`. So one multiplication (`beta*x`, 179
instructions at the organiser's measured price for a multiplication by
the fixed constant `beta`), one addition and one negation produce all
three candidate abscissae. Comparing them (two compare-and-select chains
over 4x64-bit limbs, at most 24 instructions), taking the minimum, and
selecting the sign of `y` (at most 15 instructions), with 5 instructions
of slack, canonicalisation costs at most **248 charged instructions**,
i.e. `0.248` cost units of 1000. The toy program cross-checks this
single-multiplication canonicalisation against brute-force enumeration
of all six transforms on every call: 79,800 checks in the declared run,
zero disagreements (Section 8).

**Adding walk on classes.** Fix `J = 2^16` and distinct uniform scalars
`u_j, v_j in Z_r`, and let

    S_j = [u_j]P + [v_j]Q,   j = 0..J-1,

precomputed in preprocessing (each `S_j != O` is verified there). The
partition index of a class is `j(R) = x(R_canon) mod J`, the low 16 bits
of the canonical abscissa. The distinguished set is

    D = { classes whose canonical abscissa is 0 mod 2^24 },

of density `theta = 2^-24` in the classes: membership is decided from
the canonical representative. These are v2's parameters, unchanged.

## 3. The v3 step function

### 3.1 Definition: the partition bump

The v2 step was `F(R) = canon(R + S_{j(R)})`. The v3 step `G` is:

Let `a = j(R)`. **Phase 1:** compute `T = R + S_a` (with the retry rule
of Section 3.3), `R' = canon(T)`. If `j(R') != a`, then `G(R) = R'`.
**Phase 2 (the bump):** if `j(R') = a`, compute `T2 = R' + S_{a+1}`
(indices mod `J`, retries as in Section 3.3), and set `G(R) = canon(T2)`.

`G` is a function on classes: both phases are determined by the class of
`R` (canonicalisation is class-valued and the partition index is read
from the canonical representative), so trails that merge follow the same
successor and reach the same distinguished point, as the
distinguished-point method requires. The distinguished test is applied
to iterates of `G` only; the phase-2 intermediate `R'` is not a state of
the walk and is never tested or reported. The extra test the bump needs
— the partition index of `R'` — is already computed for the next step,
so the bump's only real cost is the occasional extra addition, charged
in Section 7 under a deterministic global budget (Section 3.5).

Scalars are tracked through both phases exactly: each performed addition
contributes its `(u, v)` increment and each canonicalisation its
alignment `tau`, all determined by the points themselves, so two trails
reporting the same class still carry the data Section 4 needs. A class
that appears only as a bump *intermediate* `R'` is not a state of the
walk: trails do not merge there, it is never tested for
distinguishedness, and it cannot be reported.

### 3.2 Why the bump trigger is the right one

A fruitless cycle of the plain walk `F` that is *systematic* — present
for a positive-density set of states for algebraic reasons, not by
coincidence — must close through a `mu`-identity in the increments it
uses. If the increments at two consecutive steps differ, the closure
identity involves two independent table entries and is a coincidence of
density `~1/r` (Section 6.3). Systematic closure therefore requires the
*same* increment at consecutive steps, i.e. two consecutive states with
the same partition index — and that is exactly the configuration the
bump rewrites: in `G`, a step whose plain output would repeat the
partition index does not take the plain step. Section 6.2 turns this
into a lemma covering every cycle length, including the reviewer's
two-cycle family and the three-cycle analogue (`(lambda, lambda,
lambda)` increments) that the plain walk also admits.

### 3.3 Exceptional steps: bounded retries and a global budget

The production addition is a batched incomplete addition formula whose
exceptional branch is the x-equality case: for `T = R + S`, the formula
needs its special branch exactly when `S = R` (sum `2R`) or `S = -R`
(sum `O`). An attempt is therefore *exceptional* iff its entry satisfies
`S_idx = +-R` for the current point `R` — a check made directly on the
entry. For a uniform table entry this has probability at most `2/r`.

**Retry rule (enforced, not assumed).** Each phase tries consecutive
table indices — phase 1 tries `a, a+1, a+2`; phase 2 tries `a+1, a+2,
a+3` — and uses the first non-exceptional sum. If all attempts in a
phase are exceptional, the trail is abandoned. A step is called
*affected* if any attempt was exceptional. Attempts within a step use
distinct table entries, so, over the run's coins (which include the
table),

    P(a given step is affected) <= 12/r ~ 2^-252.4

(6 attempts, each exceptional with probability at most `2/r`).

**Global budget (enforcement).** The run aborts if the number of
affected steps exceeds `2^10`. The expected number of affected steps in
the whole run is at most `2^127 * 2^-252.4 = 2^-125.4` (there are fewer
than `2^127` steps), so by Markov's inequality

    P(abort on the affected-step budget) <= 2^-135.

An affected step costs at most 6 additions plus bookkeeping, at most
`2^4` cost units, so the worst-case total affected cost, given no abort,
is `2^10 * 2^4 = 2^14` units — a deterministic ledger line (Section 7),
replacing v2's unproven "at most one affected step per trail" allowance.
The toy program implements this retry rule verbatim (Section 8).

### 3.4 Split scalars: representation, schedule, and the 96 bound

Each trail tracks `(A, B)` with state `= [A]P + [B]Q`. Following the
charged implementation, `A` and `B` are held in **split coordinates**

    A = A1 + A2*lambda,   B = B1 + B2*lambda     (over the integers),

where `lambda^2 = -1 - lambda`.

*Split bound.* The standard GLV decomposition of a scalar modulo `r`
(via the fixed short basis of the lattice `{(a, b) : a + b*lambda = 0
mod r}`) yields components with `|A1|, |A2| <= 2^130`; the published
secp256k1 basis vectors have components below `2^129`, and `2^130` is a
safe envelope. The increments' splits `(u_j1, u_j2)`, `(v_j1, v_j2)`
satisfy the same bound and are computed once in preprocessing. A trail
start `(a0, b0)` is split when the trail is created (inside the
per-start allowance).

*Updates.* An increment adds componentwise: 4 integer additions. An
alignment `tau in mu` acts by additions/subtractions only, using
`lambda^2 = -1 - lambda`:

    tau =  1:      (A1, A2) -> (A1, A2)
    tau = -1:      (A1, A2) -> (-A1, -A2)
    tau =  lambda: (A1, A2) -> (-A2, A1 - A2)
    tau = -lambda: (A1, A2) -> (A2, A2 - A1)
    tau =  lambda^2:  (A1, A2) -> (A2 - A1, -A1)
    tau = -lambda^2:  (A1, A2) -> (A1 - A2, A1)

Each map is at most one subtraction, one negation and one copy of a
component: at most 15 instructions per scalar at the limb width below,
30 for the pair.

*Growth.* Let `M` bound all four components' absolute values and
`Bnd = 2^130` the split bound. An increment add gives `M + Bnd`; a
`mu`-map at most doubles: `2(M + Bnd)`. A plain step (add, then map)
gives `M -> 2M + 2*Bnd`; a bumped step (two of each) gives
`M -> 4M + 6*Bnd <= 4M + 2^133`. The unified per-step recurrence is

    M_{s+1} <= 4 M_s + 2^133.

*Rebalancing schedule.* Every 36 steps, and at every report, each scalar
is rebalanced: evaluate `A = (A1 + A2*lambda) mod r` and re-split by the
fixed GLV basis, restoring `M <= 2^130`. Between rebalances the
recurrence gives

    M_36 <= 4^36 * 2^130 + 2^133 * (4^36 - 1)/3 < 2^204,

so every component always fits in four 64-bit limbs (capacity `2^256`),
with all intermediate sums and differences fitting the same width. This
is the schedule whose absence the v2 review flagged; the toy program
implements it (Gauss-reduced toy lattice, evaluate-from-split and
Babai re-splitting, envelope asserted at every state and immediately
after every step — Section 8).

*Cost, itemised.* Per scalar, rebalancing costs at most 400
instructions: evaluation at most 160 (one `<=2^204`-by-256-bit
multiplication `A2*lambda`, 16 limb multiplications plus carries
`~36`, one reduction of the `<=2^460` product modulo `r` at most 120,
the final add 4); re-split at most 200 (two rounding divisions by
fixed basis constants at most 70 each; recombination — two
multiplications by `<=2^130` basis components plus subtractions — at
most 60); copies and bound checks at most 40. Per `(A, B)` pair that
is at most 800, amortised over 36 steps to at most **23 per step**. The
other two items at the four-limb width: increments are 4 additions of
at most 4 limbs each, at most **16**; a `mu`-map per scalar is one
subtraction (4), one negation (4) and one copy (4), so at most **24**
for the pair. The scalar line of the ledger is therefore

    increments <= 16;  mu-maps <= 24;  rebalancing <= 23;
    total <= 63 <= 96 instructions,

and the ledger keeps the v2 figure of 96 (0.096 units) for margin: the
line survives even if rebalancing were priced at `(96 - 40) * 36 =
2016` per pair, 2.5 times the itemised price. Report-time evaluation
uses components of size at most `2^204` and costs at most 240
instructions per scalar, inside the per-report allowance of Section 7.

### 3.5 Remaining run rules and global budgets

As in v2: trails start from uniform `(a0, b0)`; a trail ends by
reporting its first distinguished iterate, by reaching `L_max = 2^30`
steps, by an all-exceptional phase (Section 3.3), or by solving the
instance. At most `2^104` trail starts and at most `2^104` processed
distinguished reports are charged; the run aborts beyond these caps
(overflow probabilities in Section 6.5). Two further deterministic budgets make
the work bound unconditional on the walk's randomness: the number of
bumps is capped at `B_max = 2^112` (abort beyond; the abort probability
is bounded in Section 6.4), and affected steps at `2^10` (Section 3.3).
A start landing in the identity class (probability `~1/r`) is resampled.
The walk stops when a collision is reported, when `t` steps (Section 5)
have executed, or on any budget abort (a failure event, charged to the
deductions of Section 6.5).

## 4. A collision gives the discrete logarithm

Bookkeeping maintains for every visited class the invariant

    state = [A]P + [B]Q

with `(A, B)` the tracked scalars of Section 3.4 (the bump's extra
increment and both alignments included), evaluated exactly for the
canonical representative. When two trails report the same distinguished
class, they report the *same canonical point* `R` with tracked scalars
`(A1, B1)` and `(A2, B2)` satisfying `[A_i]P + [B_i]Q = R`, so

    (A1 - A2) = (B2 - B1) x (mod r),

which determines `x` whenever `B1 != B2`; the candidate is checked by
one multiplication against `Q` (inside the final-solve allowance).

The degenerate case `B1 = B2` forces `A1 = A2` as well (`A` is
determined by the point `R` and `B`), i.e. the two trails carry
identical data — a pure merge, giving no equation. It is a deduction:
there are at most `2^207` report pairs; a pair shares a class with
probability at most `~2^-253.4` and, given the class, has matching
`B`-coordinates with probability at most `~2^-250` under the same
equidistribution the walk relies on throughout, so the expected number
of degenerate collisions is below `2^-290`; the deduction ledger
(Section 6.5) charges a very conservative `2^-104` for it.

## 5. Success probability I: the birthday term and the waste allowance

Let

    t = 141561145530318902406949923548642162214 ~ 2^126.735

be the least integer with `t^2 >= 2 * N'' * 0.519195`, where
`0.519195 >= -ln(1 - 0.405) = 0.5191939...` (rounded up). Under the
collision heuristic H1 (Section 6.6, scope narrowed by the v3 repairs),
if `t` steps of the walk are executed on distinct-class-producing terms,
the probability that two trails report a common class — a collision in
the birthday sense over `N''` classes — is at least

    1 - exp(-t^2 / (2 N'')) >= 1 - exp(-0.519195) >= 0.4050006.

Steps that revisit an already-visited class are *waste*: they do not
advance the birthday count. In v2, waste from short cycles was asserted
negligible by an appeal to random-mapping statistics that the review
showed to be false for the plain walk `F`. In v3 the waste is bounded
by analysis (Section 6): except with probability at most `2^-67` over
the run's coins, total waste is at most `2^40` steps. Replacing `t` by
`t - 2^40` changes `t^2 / (2 N'')` by at most
`t * 2^40 / N'' ~ 2^-86.7`, hence the birthday term by at most `2^-86`.
The remaining deductions (budget aborts, degenerate collisions, cap
overflows) are collected in Section 6.5; the total is below `2^-66`, so

    success >= 0.4050006 - 2^-66 >= 0.405.

Everything now rests on Section 6's cycle analysis at production
parameters: `J = 2^16`, `theta = 2^-24`, `L_max = 2^30`.

## 6. Success probability II: cycles at production parameters

### 6.1 The reviewer's family, restated and conceded

For the plain walk `F` of v2, fix a partition `j` and distinct canonical
`R, R'` with `j(R) = j(R') = j`. If `S_j = -(R + R')` then
`F(R) = canon(-R') = R'` and `F(R') = canon(-R) = R`: a two-cycle, and
the scalar updates return to their starting values. For `j != 0`
neither point is distinguished (distinguished requires the low 24 bits
of the abscissa to vanish; `j` fixes the low 16 bits to a nonzero
value), so a trail entering the cycle burns steps until `L_max`.
Counting as the review did — for each `j`, the pairs `(R, R')` in the
partition whose sum determines one "bad" value of the uniform entry
`S_j` — the expected number of two-cycle states of `F` is

    N''/(6J) * (1 + o(1)) ~ 2^234.8 states,

a fraction `1/(6J) = 2^-18.58` of all classes. At production parameters
this fraction *exceeds* the distinguished density `theta = 2^-24` by a
factor of about 43: a plain-walk trail meets a two-cycle state before
a distinguished point with probability about
`(1/(6J)) / (1/(6J) + theta) ~ 0.977`. The v2 Sections 5–6 appeal to
random-mapping short-cycle statistics was therefore wrong for `F`, and
the census of Section 8 confirms the `N''/(6J)` law (484/232/106/66
observed against 456/228/114/57 predicted). This is conceded; v3
changes the walk.

### 6.2 The bump destroys every one-entry-determined cycle

Work at the level of **phases**: a plain step of `G` is one phase, a
bumped step is two. A phase with input representative `U`, output
representative `V` and alignment `tau` satisfies
`V = [tau](U + S_idx)` as points, so it *pins* its entry to the point

    S_idx = [tau^-1] V - U.

A cycle of `G` closes iff the pins of every phase are consistent (all
phases using one entry pin it to the same point) and the composed
phases return to the start. Call a cycle **one-entry-determined** if
its closure is forced by the pins of a single entry, the other entries
being arbitrary; call it **automatic** if its closure equation
(Section 6.3) is an identity in all entries (total alignment 1, every
combined coefficient 0). Automatic cycles are one-entry-determined
vacuously; the reviewer's family is one-entry-determined by `S_j`.

**Trigger fact.** If a first phase from `X` produces an intermediate
`Y` with `j(Y) = j(X)`, then that phase is not the whole step: the
step is bumped and ends at `canon(Y + S_{j(X)+1})`, not at `Y`. So in
`G`, no *step* ends at the output of a first phase whose index repeats
its input's.

**Lemma.** `G` has no one-entry-determined cycle, of any length; in
particular no automatic cycle.

*Proof.* Suppose a cycle of `G` is determined by entry `S_j`. Take
two consecutive occurrences of `j` in the cycle's phase expansion —
phases `p` and `q` with no `j`-phase between. If the segment between
them has positive length, the agreement of their pins forces the
segment's own increment combination to vanish identically with
coinciding endpoints, i.e. the segment is itself a
one-entry-determined closed walk; induction on the number of phases
reduces to consecutive occurrences, `q = p + 1`. Write `U` for phase
`p`'s input and `Y` for its output (phase `q`'s input). The pins are

    S_j = [tau_p^-1] Y - U,     S_j = [tau_q^-1] U' - Y,

where `U'` is phase `q`'s output. Determination by `S_j` alone means
the agreement of the two pins is an *identity*, not a further
equation (a further equation is a coincidence, counted in
Section 6.3). For the 2-phase determination the return is to the
segment's start, `U' = U`, and agreement reads

    [tau_p^-1] Y - U = [tau_q^-1] U - Y
    <==>  [tau_p^-1 + 1] Y = [tau_q^-1 + 1] U,

an identity in the independent points `U, Y` iff
`tau_p = tau_q = -1`. The determining configuration is therefore the
**swap**: two consecutive phases over one entry, both with alignment
`-1`, pinning `S_j = -(U + Y)` — the reviewer's form. But phase `q`'s
entry index is its input's partition index, so `j(Y) = j = j(U)`:
phase `p` is a first phase whose output repeats its input's index.
By the trigger fact, that phase belongs to a bumped step of `G` and
the step does not end at `Y` — the cycle does not exist. (If the
determining entry is used as a bump's second entry, its index is its
predecessor's index plus one; its two occurrences are again
consecutive phases with consecutive input/output states, identical
pin agreement forces the same `-1, -1` swap, and the first occurrence
is again a first phase repeating its input's index.) ∎

The reviewer's family is the swap with `tau`-values realised by
canonicalisation: in `F` the return phase exists and the cycle closes
whenever the single pin equation `S_j = -(R + R')` (up to alignment)
holds. In `G` the return phase is never a step: from `R` the plain
phase lands on `R'` with `j(R') = j(R)`, the bump fires, and
`G(R) = canon(R' + S_{j+1}) != R'` unless `S_{j+1}` lies in
`class(R') - R'`, six specific points — an ordinary one-entry
*equation* (probability `6/r` per state pair), counted in Section 6.3,
not an identity.

### 6.3 Coincidence bound: cycles of G are rare at every length

It remains to bound cycles whose closure is a genuine equation. Fix a
class `X` and a length `ell >= 1`, and consider the event that `X`
lies on an `ell`-cycle of `G` (at most `2*ell` phases). Expanding the
orbit symbolically, closure is one equation

    sum_j [alpha_j] S_j = [1 - tau] X,

the sum over the entries the orbit uses, `tau in mu` the total
alignment, `alpha_j` the `mu`-combination with which entry `j`
enters. The case analysis is on the whole equation:

- *Some coefficient is nonzero.* Resolve in one such entry `S_j`:
  hold the phase pattern (indices, bump triggers, alignments) and all
  other entries fixed; the equation is then
  `[alpha_j] S_j = (fixed point)` with `alpha_j != 0`, and as `S_j`
  ranges uniformly over the subgroup the left side is uniform, so the
  equation holds for at most 6 of the `r` values of `S_j` (the
  canonicalisation's alignment slack): probability at most `6/r` per
  pattern. This case covers in particular the *mixed* patterns where
  some other entry's coefficient vanishes (e.g. a bumped step followed
  by a plain step reusing the bump's second entry, whose combined
  coefficient can be `tau_3 (tau_2 + 1) = 0` when `tau_2 = -1`): the
  equation is then resolved in the remaining entry, whose coefficient
  (there `tau_3 tau_2 tau_1`) is nonzero. The conditioning on the
  phase pattern is the one heuristic step in this section; it is
  exactly H1(ii) as scoped in Section 6.6, and it is also the standard
  idealisation in every rho analysis.
- *Every coefficient vanishes.* Then either `tau != 1` and the
  equation reads `0 = [1 - tau] X != O` (note `1 - tau != 0 mod r` for
  `tau in mu \setminus {1}`), impossible; or `tau = 1` and the closure
  is automatic — excluded by the Lemma.
- *Fresh final entry (sub-case of the first).* If the final phase's
  entry occurs nowhere earlier, no conditioning subtlety arises at
  all: adding a fixed point is a bijection on the subgroup and `canon`
  maps exactly 6 points to each class, so as the entry varies the
  output class is exactly uniform: probability at most `2/N''`
  (two phase branches).

Counting patterns for the first case (retry-free phases; retry
offsets are handled separately in Section 6.4): a distinguished
repeated entry is fixed by its second occurrence (at most `2*ell`
phases) and a pattern of at most `144` (phase type and alignment at
each of the two occurrences, with slack); per `(X, ell)` the cost is
at most `2*ell * 144 * (6/r)`. Summing over `ell <= 2^30` and all
`N''` classes (using `N''/r ~ 1/6`), the repeated case contributes at
most `sum_ell 2*ell * 144 * 6 * (N''/r) ~ sum_ell 288*ell < 2^68`
expected states, and the fresh case `2^31`:

    E[# states on cycles of G of length <= 2^30] <= 2^31 + 2^68 <= 2^69,

a density of at most `2^69 / N'' ~ 2^-184` among classes. The bound is
deliberately crude — it union-bounds over every distinguished
occurrence with generous pattern slack; its role is only to sit so far
below the class count that the Section 6.4 waste bound is insensitive
to the crudeness. (The census of Section 8 measures the truth at toy
scale: the bumped walk's two-cycle states are 0, 0, 2, 0 at
`J = 16, 32, 64, 128` — far below this bound, against the plain walk's
484, 232, 106, 66, which track the systematic law `N''/(6J)` within
sampling variation.)

Cycles longer than `L_max = 2^30` cannot waste work inside a capped
trail: a trail entering one traverses distinct classes until the cap,
and those steps count toward the birthday term like any others. Only
cycles of length at most `L_max` produce repeated steps, and those are
the ones bounded above.

### 6.4 Trapping and bump costs at production parameters

**Trapping.** Under H1's per-step uniformity (Section 6.6), a step
lands on a short-cycle state with probability at most the density
`2^-184` of Section 6.3. A trail that enters a short cycle wastes at
most `L_max` further steps. Hence

    E[wasted steps] <= t * 2^-184 * 2^30 ~ 2^-27.3,

and by Markov's inequality `P(waste > 2^40) <= 2^-67.3 <= 2^-67`,
the allowance used in Section 5.

**Cycles through retry phases.** The counting above is for the
retry-free phases; the Section 3.3 retries change which entry a phase
uses at a state only when an entry equals `+-` the current point. For
each index `j` there are at most 2 points `R` with `R = +-S_j`, so at
most `2^17` states have an exceptional first attempt in phase 1
(deterministically, given the table), and likewise at most `2^18`
states have an exceptional attempt anywhere in their step. A cycle
using a retry phase passes through one of these states `Z`; for
fixed `Z` and length `ell`, closure is again one equation in the
remaining entries (the retry entry itself is a fresh index), of
probability at most `6/N''`-type per pattern, so the expected number
of cycle states through retry phases is below
`2^18 * 2^30 * 6/N'' ~ 2^-199` — absorbed into the Section 6.3 total.

**Bumps.** A step bumps iff the plain successor's index repeats, of
probability `1/J` per step under H1. The number of bumps in `t` steps
is therefore Binomial with mean `t/J = 2^110.735`; the budget
`B_max = 2^112` is `2.404` times the mean, and a Chernoff bound gives

    P(bump abort) <= exp(-2^109.9),

far below every other deduction. Given no abort, bump work is a
deterministic ledger line (Section 7).

**Affected steps.** Section 3.3: abort probability below `2^-135`.

### 6.6 H1, restated with its narrowed scope

**H1 (v3).** For the bumped iteration `G`, the classes visited by a
trail behave like the iterates of a uniform random mapping on the `N''`
classes for exactly two purposes: (i) collision (birthday) statistics
of visited classes; (ii) per-step uniformity — the current class is
uniform given no prior trapping — used for the density bounds of
Sections 6.3–6.4 and the `1/J` bump incidence.

No short-cycle statistics are assumed in v3: the systematic families
are excluded by the Lemma of Section 6.2 and the residual cycle content
is bounded by the counting of Section 6.3, whose heuristic input is
(ii) — including the disclosed conditioning on the phase pattern in
Section 6.3's nonzero-coefficient case. This is the assumption the
experiments support (Section 8); production-scale cycle behaviour is
established by Sections 6.2–6.3, not extrapolated from the toy.

## 8. Experiments

`experiment.py` (declared in `submission.json`) is a stdlib-only
miniature of the algorithm of Sections 2–3 on the toy curve
`y^2 = x^3 + 7` over `F_786433`, whose subgroup of order
`r_t = 262657 = 1 mod 3` gives `N''_toy = 43777` GLV classes — the same
toy as v2, so every number below is directly comparable with the v2
review's evidence. Three things the v2 review found missing in the
*code* are now implemented and exercised: the bumped step function
itself; the split-coordinate bookkeeping with the Section 3.4
rebalancing schedule (rebalancing evaluates the split components and
re-splits them; reports store the split-evaluated scalars and the
solve uses them; a shadow plain implementation serves only as a test
oracle, with the split state asserted against it at every state and
immediately after every step, before any rebalancing); and a
total-steps metric that counts repeated work. The step function is
implemented once and shared by the attack mode and the census mode,
including the Section 3.3 retry rule.

**Declared run (attack, bumped, `J = 32`, `theta = 1/4`, 300 trials,
seed 20261106).** Success within the budget `t_toy = 214` steps:
**0.627** in the total-steps metric and 0.433 in the
distinct-visited-classes metric, against the 0.405 target the budget
encodes; all 300 secrets recovered within the `8 t_toy` cap; mean total
steps at success 192.9; mean visited classes 255.9 against the
random-mapping prediction `sqrt(pi N''/2) = 262.2`; **3 trapped
trails**; 1,753 bumps in 57,875 plain-phase steps (rate `1/33.0`,
against `1/J = 1/32`); 59,628 total additions; 0 affected steps; 0 bad
collisions; 135,922 split-state invariant checks with 0 violations
(observed maximum component 3,711; the post-rebalance bound is 1,026
and the schedule envelope was never approached); 79,800
canonicalisation cross-checks with 0 disagreements.

**Control (attack, plain walk, identical settings).** With the bump
disabled the same program reproduces v2's published values *exactly*:
steps-metric 0.443, visited-metric 0.400, mean visited 267.3, **295
trapped trails** (the review's number), 98,997 canonicalisation
cross-checks. The bump alone therefore accounts for: trapped trails
295 -> 3, mean total steps 260.3 -> 192.9, total additions 78,092 ->
59,628 — at toy scale the bump's ~`1/J` overhead (1,753 extra
additions) is repaid about ten-fold by the trapping it removes
(18,464 additions saved).

**Census (exact functional graphs, same table seed).** The census
enumerates the production step function of Section 3, retry rule
included, for the bumped walk (and the plain `F` for the control).
Two-cycle states, plain walk vs the prediction `N''/(6J)`: `J = 16`:
484 vs 456; `J = 32`: 232 vs 228; `J = 64`: 106 vs 114; `J = 128`: 66
vs 57 — the reviewer's law, tracked at every `J` within sampling
variation. Bumped walk at the same `J`: **0, 0, 2, 0** two-cycle
states — the coincidence level of Section 6.3. Total cyclic states of
the bumped walk (259, 404, 143, 194) are of the random-mapping order
(the scale is `sqrt(pi N''/2) ~ 262`; individual values fluctuate),
while the plain walk's cyclic content is dominated by its two-cycles.

**Race configuration (`J = 16`, `theta = 2^-8`, cycle detection used
as a measuring instrument).** Here the plain walk's two-cycle density
`delta ~ 2^-6.5` exceeds `theta`, the production ordering
(`delta = 2^-18.58` vs `theta = 2^-24`). Over 200 instances at

scale-free evidence for production is the census plus Sections
6.2–6.3; the race shows the mechanism operating in the disputed
regime, not just at `theta = 1/4`.

**Scope.** The experiments do not measure production instruction costs
(the ledger is Section 7, at the organiser's reference prices), do not
reach `J = 2^16` or `theta = 2^-24` (infeasible at toy scale), and do
not prove H1 at production scale. They validate: the bump destroys the
disputed family in the exact functional graph; the walk's collision
statistics match the birthday prediction in the metric the claim is
charged in (total steps, repeated work included); and the split-scalar
schedule maintains its invariants in the implementation that mirrors
the production description.

## 9. Scope and honesty notes

- The claim is a heuristic random-mapping argument (H1) on top of exact
  canonicalisation, collision algebra, a proved destruction of the
  systematic cycle families, a counting bound on coincidence cycles, an
  enforced exceptional-step rule, and a fully scheduled scalar bound.
  It is not a proof that `G` is a random mapping.

  bound — is now either proved at production parameters (Sections
  6.2–6.4), enforced by budget (Sections 3.3, 3.5), or itemised
  (Section 3.4).
- The toy curve is a model of the class walk, not of secp256k1's field
  arithmetic; production costs enter only through the organiser's
  reference prices in Section 7.
- Nothing in this package uses nonuniform advice, preprocessing on the
  target beyond the stated `2^27`, or data from other submissions.
