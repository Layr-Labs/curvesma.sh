# Finite relation rank and padded block Wiedemann

This section states two different premises. They must not be conflated with class-number and unit-map assumptions.

**H-R (finite relation spanning).** For the actually collected, tau-quotiented and filtered relation matrix A=[M|S] over F_r, with D=N+7, M has column rank N and A has rank D-1. Its kernel is exactly the line generated by the virtual logarithms (w,c), with c!=0. This is a structural NFS relation-saturation heuristic for this fixed finite relation set. Counting smooth relations, class numbers prime to r, and injective Schirokauer maps do not prove it. The excess, complete relation-generation rules, occurrence/filtering experiments and standard finite-field NFS framework provide its exploratory support. Synthetic planted matrices and solver replays do not establish saturation of the production relation set. If H-R fails, randomized Wiedemann cannot repair the missing relation information; rank/verification checks can reject but do not supply a failure-probability bound.

Write B=[M|0], a D-by-D matrix. Then ImB=ImM has dimension N, and V=ImB+span(S) has dimension D-1 under H-R. Use Y=[S_1,...,S_7,BZ], with n-7 independently random columns Z. Fix the coordinate partition publicly before sampling weights: the seven pad coordinates each belong to a distinct projection block; nonpad coordinates use the specified fixed shuffled/balanced partition into m blocks. X has exactly one independent uniform 128-bit entry on each coordinate's block, with zeros elsewhere. Z entries are independent uniform field elements (or uniform 128-bit subsets; either support has at least2^128 elements).

**H-X (finite block controllability/observability).** At the finite right/left lengths d,e used by the solver, assume two specific minors are nonzero polynomials: an N-by-N minor of

    C=[BZ, BY, B^2Y, ..., B^dY]

and a (D-1)-by-(D-1) minor of O U, where O stacks X^T,X^TB,...,X^TB^(e-1) and U is a fixed basis of V, determined by the fixed relation matrix, not by X or Z. This includes generator-length and sparse-projection applicability hypotheses; it is not inferred merely from rankM=N. The lexicographically first minor having a nonzero polynomial can define the selected indices for analysis; the attack need not compute a symbolic determinant. Without H-X, e.g. a large repeated-eigenvalue matrix can have full rank M yet inadequate finite block Krylov span.

The control minor has degree at most N in Z; the observation minor has degree at most D-1=N+6 in X. Their nonzero product has total degree at most2N+6<4N. Schwartz-Zippel on the independently sampled entries, each on at least2^128 field values, therefore gives simultaneous control/observation failure at most4N/2^128, CONDITIONAL on H-R and H-X. This is not a bound on the probability that H-R/H-X themselves hold.

When the minors are nonzero, C spans ImB and O is injective on V. Since the true c!=0 satisfies Sc in ImB, there is a finite polynomial relation sum_i B^iY f_i=0 whose first seven constant coefficients are nonzero as a vector. Projected and full vector relations coincide on V. Use the already-priced fast block minimal-generator/approximant-basis algorithm to obtain a basis of the projected relation module in the stated finite degree window; then select a basis column of degree at most d whose first seven constant coordinates are not all zero, or fail if none exists. The production attack does not materialize the dense Hankel matrix or perform a dense Gaussian solve; the small replay uses Gaussian elimination only as an independent algebra diagnostic. Do not fix the first coefficient to1: the true first map coefficient might be zero. The same selection is applied to the standard polynomial-generator basis: a relation with nonzero constant S-part implies some generator basis column has such a part, because polynomial linear combinations obtain their constant term from constant basis columns.

For f_0=(c,e), Y f_0=Sc+BZ e. Define

    wbar=Z e+sum_(i>=1) B^(i-1)Y f_i.

Then M wbar_firstN+Sc=0. Thus (wbar_firstN,c) is nonzero and belongs to kerA, hence is proportional to the virtual-log vector under H-R. Evaluate the sum by Horner as already priced, reconstruct purged logs and verify the final target equation.

The block lengths are ceil(D/n)+1 and ceil(D/m)+1 for the bounded diagnostic and may be used in the attack. Rounding/endpoints add at most five block steps beyond the leading N/n+N/m accounting; at the protected N<2^45.79,n=40 and per-row-vector price<2^12, these steps cost<2^65.5 scalar instructions. This fits the unused part of the existing2^68 wrapper allowance after its field-map bound<2^67. The corresponding O(1) extra generator terms are also below that allowance. No new quadratic leading work or changed normalization is assumed.

The seven-pad replay uses synthetic C4-filtered coefficient rows and PLANTED Schirokauer columns/log vector to establish its premises by construction, then independently checks ranks, finite block generation/observation, a projected relation with nonzero S-constant part, Horner reconstruction and the final kernel equation. A distinct-eigenvalue diagonal family supplies another explicit witness where the polynomial is nonzero. A control collapsing all pads into one projection block must fail observation. These are algebra/implementation diagnostics, not evidence that production NFS relations span the needed space. H-R and production H-X remain explicit empirical/structural extrapolations under exploratory review.

## Primary framework and scope

Schirokauer, *The impact of the number field sieve on the discrete logarithm problem in finite fields*, §2.2 Step4, constructs valuation/character vectors and explicitly enlarges the relation region when the desired vector is outside their span. This separates solvability from the character-map argument. This package fixes its finite region and therefore states H-R for that actual set; the source does not prove H-R for BN254. [Author framework](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book44/files/12oliver.pdf).

Fried, Gaudry, Heninger and Thome, *A kilobit hidden SNFS discrete logarithm computation*, §3.1, separates filtering, Schirokauer columns, the homogeneous (M|S) system and virtual logarithms. This supports the algebraic framework and its practical use, not saturation of this different fixed tower/quotient. [Primary computation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.02874). The actual application to this finite structured BN254 relation set remains a declared heuristic, supported by relation excess and occurrence/filtering diagnostics; no synthetic random-matrix probability is substituted for it. H-R/H-X are deterministic applicability hypotheses here, not independently quantified random losses; the4N/2^128 allowance is conditional on them.

Seven-pad diagnostics: diagonal seeds1/2 have N29,D36,M/C rank29,A/O/H rank35; seed2 tests a true zero first map coefficient. The filtered synthetic case (B = 2^58 profile) has N128,D135,M/C rank128,A/O/H rank134. Collapsing all pads into one projection block gives O/H rank30<35, the expected negative control. ## H-X: structure of B, finite lengths, and what remains assumed

Structure (proved here). Write M = [M1; M2], M1 its first N rows. Then det(xI - B) = x^7 det(xI - M1), and for lambda != 0, Bv = lambda v iff v = (v1, M2 v1 / lambda) with M1 v1 = lambda v1: B and the N-by-N block M1 have the same nonzero eigenvalues with the same geometric multiplicities. Since M has column rank N (H-R), ker B = {v : v_1 = ... = v_N = 0} has dimension 7, so on Im B the eigenvalue 0 has geometric multiplicity at most 7.

Eventual generation by the random columns (proved here). The n - 7 = 33 columns of BZ are uniform in Im B. They generate Im B as an F_r[x]-module (x acting as B) unless, for some irreducible phi dividing the minimal polynomial, their images fail to span Im B / phi(B) Im B, of dimension g_phi (the geometric multiplicity of the roots of phi); when every g_phi <= 33 a union bound over hyperplanes and at most N factors bounds that failure by N/(r - 1). This is a statement about the module, i.e. about generation at some length; it does not by itself give generation at the declared length.

Finite lengths (what is proved and what is not). Let K_i = span{B^j Y : 0 <= j < i}. The increments dim K_(i+1) - dim K_i are non-increasing in i (a column B^i y that lies in K_i has B^(i+1) y in K_(i+1)), they sum to the dimension of the module generated by Y, and they are the dual partition of the controllability indices of the pair (B, Y). The declared right length d = ceil(D/n) + 1 therefore generates that module if and only if every controllability index of (B, Y) is at most d; the left length e = ceil(D/m) + 1 with the sparse projection X is the same statement for (B^T, X). This package does not prove these index bounds for the actual filtered, tau-quotiented relation matrix with the seven fixed Schirokauer columns and the fixed sparse projection; that is exactly the content of the nonzero-minor premise H-X stated above, and it remains a declared heuristic. The diagonal witnesses and the filtered replay check the algebra when the premise holds; they are not evidence that it holds for the production matrix.

Practice (cited, not a validation). The same construction (iterate on (M|0), starting block (S | random), low-weight projection, solution read from a column of the minimal generator) is described for a genuine filtered discrete-logarithm matrix with Schirokauer columns in Fried, Gaudry, Heninger and Thome, arXiv 1610.02874, section 5.3. It is cited as evidence that the construction is used on real NFS matrices, not as a reproduced comparison for this tower.
