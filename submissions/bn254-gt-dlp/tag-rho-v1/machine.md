# Machine model and instruction ledger

All work is charged in scalar AArch64 instruction equivalents R and divided by C = 20,000
(`tracks/docs/COST_MODEL.md`, BN254 section). No BN254 operation is charged as one unit;
every field operation is priced through its implementation, down to executed kernel
listings. Sections 1, 2 and 2a (the base-field leaves and the fused Fp2 leaves) and the
counted Fp12 routines of section 3 are unchanged from the accepted package 7e58d165 (which
in turn extends 2ece9f04). New here: the fused tag kernel `c1dot` (section 2b), the
tag-traced step and chunk accounting (section 4) and the multiset product table (section 5).

## 1. Representation

Fp elements are four 64-bit limbs in Montgomery form x~ = x * 2^256 mod p, fully reduced
(0 <= x~ < p). The same kernels serve Z/r (exponent arithmetic) with the constants of r.
p and r are both below 2^254, so a + b < 2^255 never carries out of 256 bits, and the
Montgomery product of reduced inputs needs one final conditional subtraction.

Tower (the one the profile maps to, checked in `experiment.py` part A):
Fp2 = Fp[u]/(u^2+1), Fp12 = Fp2[w]/(w^6 - xi), xi = 9 + u. Grouped for the product as
Fp6 = Fp2[v]/(v^3 - xi), v = w^2, Fp12 = Fp6[w]/(w^2 - v). The profile basis
(w^12 = 18 w^6 - 82, u = w^6 - 9) maps to tower coefficients by
c_m(tower) = (c_m + 9 c_{m+6}) + c_{m+6} u; this conversion is done once for z at setup.

## 2. Leaf kernels

The three leaves are straight-line listings that read operands through x1, x2, write the
result through x0, use only caller-saved registers x0..x17 (no spills, no frame) and end
in `ret`. Their constants (m0..m3 and n0 = -m^-1 mod 2^64) sit at label K of a block per
modulus. `experiment.py` part K executes every listing on an instruction-level simulator of
the AArch64 subset used (ldr/ldp/stp/adr, mul/umulh, adds/adcs/adc, subs/sbcs, csel,
csetm, and), on 12 x 12 edge pairs plus random and all-limbs-large inputs for both p and
r, and checks the result against Python integers. Declared run: 519 cases per modulus,
0 failures. The executed instruction count is the listing length (straight line, `ret`
included).

| Leaf | Listing length | Call site (BL + up to 3 pointer set-ups) | Charged per call |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fp_mul (Montgomery product, product scanning then 4 word REDC rounds, 1 conditional subtraction) | 158 | 4 | **162** |
| fp_add (add, trial subtract, 4 csel) | 22 | 4 | **26** |
| fp_sub (subtract, borrow mask, masked add of m) | 23 | 4 | **27** |

## 2a. Fused Fp2 leaves (new)

Three further straight-line listings work on Fp2 elements (two consecutive Fp residues, 64
bytes). They read through x1 (and x2), write through x0, and besides x0..x17 use the
callee-saved registers x19..x28, which they save in a stack frame on entry and restore
before `ret`; the save, restore and `sp` adjustment instructions are part of the listing
and are counted. Part K executes each listing on the simulator (now also `sub`/`add` with
an immediate for `sp`) for the modulus p, with a separate output and with the output
aliased to operand a, on edge, random and all-limbs-large inputs, compares with Python
integers and checks after every run that `sp` and x19..x28 hold their entry values and
that only the operands' output area and the frame were written. Declared run: 342 runs per
listing set, 0 failures. Every output is fully reduced, so all leaves compose freely.

Two code blocks are shared (generated by `mulu_core` and `red_core` in `experiment.py`):

* `mulu` (63 instructions, registers only): the unreduced 8-limb product of two 4-limb
  integers by operand scanning; it is the first 69 instructions of `fp_mul` without its 6
  loads. Exact for all 4-limb inputs.
* `red` (82 instructions, registers only): Montgomery reduction of an 8-limb integer
  T < p*2^256: four word rounds (74 instructions, the same rounds as in `fp_mul`) give
  (T + q p)/2^256 < 2p, and T + q p < 2p*2^256 < 2^511 never carries out of 8 limbs; one
  conditional subtraction (8) gives the canonical residue T*2^-256 mod p.

The range facts used: p < 2^254, hence 2p < 2^255, 4p < 2^256 and 4p^2 < p*2^256.

| Leaf | What it computes (stored Montgomery residues) | Range argument | Listing length | Charged per call (+4) |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| f2mul | (a0 + a1 u)(b0 + b1 u): T0 = a0 b0, T1 = a1 b1, T2 = (a0+a1)(b0+b1) by three `mulu`; c1 = red(T2 - T0 - T1); c0 = red(T0 - T1, plus p*2^256 if the subtraction borrowed) | sums a0+a1, b0+b1 < 2p fit 4 limbs; T2 < 4p^2 < 2^512; T2 - T0 - T1 = a0 b1 + a1 b0 exactly, in [0, 2p^2); T0 - T1 in (-p^2, p^2), so after the masked addition of p to the high half it lies in [0, p*2^256) and is unchanged mod p | 443 | **447** |
| f2sqr | (a0 + a1 u)^2: c1 = red(2 a0 a1); c0 = red((a0+a1)(a0-a1+p)) | 2 a0 a1 < 2p^2; a0+a1 < 2p; a0-a1+p lies in (0, 2p), so its 256-bit wrap-around computation is exact; product < 4p^2 | 336 | **340** |
| f2mulxi | (9 + u)(a0 + a1 u) = (9 a0 - a1) + (9 a1 + a0) u | 9t by three doublings and one addition, each followed by a conditional subtraction of p in registers; one masked-borrow subtraction; no multiplication | 151 | **155** |

Instruction make-up of f2mul: frame and register save/restore 13; 12 operand loads (ldp)
and 8 stores of T0, T1 (stp), 8 reloads; 3 x 63 `mulu`; 8 for the two sums; 16 + 8 limb
subtractions, 1 mask, 4 `and`, 4 additions; 4 constant loads; 2 x 82 `red`; 4 result
stores. In the base package the same Fp2 product was a routine of 3 fp_mul, 2 fp_add and
3 fp_sub leaf calls charged 3*162 + 2*26 + 3*27 + 32 = 651; the Fp2 squaring was
2*162 + 2*26 + 27 + 32 = 435; the multiplication by xi was 9*26 + 27 = 261.

Non-leaf routine calls (Fp4 squaring, Fp6 multiplication, the two Fp12 routines) are charged
**32** instructions each: call site, a frame with four callee-saved registers, pointer
bookkeeping for temporaries on the stack, the epilogue and slack. Fp2 additions and
subtractions are inline pairs of leaf calls and are charged as those leaf calls.


## 2b. Fused tag kernel c1dot (new)

`c1dot` computes, for a chunk base x = (x_0, ..., x_5) (the six Fp2 coefficients of the
current base element, tower of section 1) and a stored **tag block** Qt = (Qt_0, ..., Qt_5)
of a table entry P (section 5), the Fp2 value

    c = sum_{i<6} x_i * Qt_i,   Qt = (P_1, P_0, xi P_5, xi P_4, xi P_3, xi P_2),

which is exactly the w^1 coefficient c_1 of the product x * P in Fp12 = Fp2[w]/(w^6 - xi)
(c_1 = x_0 P_1 + x_1 P_0 + xi (x_2 P_5 + x_3 P_4 + x_4 P_3 + x_5 P_2); `experiment.py`
part A `tag_c1_ok`, part C every step). It reads through x1 (base block) and x2 (tag block);
each block holds, for i = 0..5 at offset 96 i, the two canonical Fp residues of the element
and their **exact integer sum** e_0 + e_1 (< 2p < 2^255, not reduced mod p; stride 96 bytes).
The Karatsuba identity below needs the exact sum: a sum reduced mod p would make S2 - S0 - S1
negative and the result wrong. It writes a = Re c at [x0] and b = Im c at
[x0 + 32], both canonical Montgomery residues.

Algorithm (one straight-line listing, 1,662 instructions, `c1dot.listing.txt`):

* Lazy Karatsuba over the six terms. S0 = sum x_i0 Qt_i0, S1 = sum x_i1 Qt_i1,
  S2 = sum (x_i0 + x_i1)(Qt_i0 + Qt_i1), each accumulated as an unreduced 9-limb integer:
  18 unreduced 4x4-limb products (the `mulu` rows of section 2a with the multiplier limb
  loaded per row, 63 + 4 loads each; 2 `ldp` for the multiplicand), 15 nine-limb
  accumulations (9 instructions each).
* Ranges: canonical inputs are < p and stored sums < 2p, so S0, S1 < 6p^2 and
  S2 < 24p^2 < 2^512 (p < 2^253.7). Im c = S2 - S0 - S1 is exactly
  sum (x_i0 Qt_i1 + x_i1 Qt_i0), in [0, 12p^2); Re c is represented by
  S0 - S1 + 6p^2 (6p^2 is a stored multiple of p), in (0, 12p^2).
* Each of the two values t < 12p^2 is reduced by the four Montgomery word rounds of
  section 2a (74 instructions): U = (t + q p)/2^256 < 12p^2/2^256 + p < 3.3p, then a
  conditional subtraction of 2p and one of p (8 instructions each) give the canonical
  residue t 2^-256 mod p.
* Registers: x3..x11 accumulator, x12..x17, x19, x20 product, x21..x24 temporaries,
  x25..x28 multiplicand, x0 the multiplier limb (x0 is parked in the frame and reloaded
  for the two result stores). Frame: 256 bytes (callee-saved x19..x28, x0, S0, S1);
  the save, restore and `sp` adjustment are in the listing and counted.

Instruction make-up: 18 x 69 (products with loads) + 15 x 9 (accumulations) + 3 (zero top
limbs) + 10 (park S0, S1) + 28 (Im) + 2 x 96 (constant loads, rounds, two conditional
subtractions) + 32 (Re with the 6p^2 offset) + 6 (result stores and x0 reloads) + 14
(frame, `ret`) = 1,662, the executed count. Part K executes the listing on the instruction
simulator against Python integers on 231 inputs per declared run (all-zero, all-(p-1),
mixed edge limb patterns and random), checks that `sp` and x19..x28 are restored and that
only the output and the frame are written: 0 failures. Charged per call: **1,666** (+4 call
site).

The constant block K of `c1dot` holds p, n0 = -p^-1 mod 2^64, 2p and 6p^2 (17 words).

**Unreduced sums: `add4sums` (61 instructions).** The sums of a base block are written by a
second straight-line listing: for i < 6, four `ldp` (e_0, e_1), `adds`/`adcs`/`adcs`/`adc`,
two `stp` at offset 96 i + 64 (10 instructions each), and `ret`. Both summands are canonical
(< p), so the 4-limb sum is exact. Part K executes it on the c1dot test inputs (0 failures)
and part C builds every base block that it passes to the c1dot listing with this listing.
Charged per call: **65**. The tag blocks of the table are written the same way when the table
is built. (The dense product's table pre-sums of section 3 are different: they are canonical
`fp_add` results, as the fused Fp2 product requires reduced inputs.)

## 4. Per-step and per-chunk work (tag tracing)

A walk holds a **chunk base** x (a materialised GT element, in the 96-byte-stride base block
with its six stored sums) and the sorted multiset of table items chosen since (at most l - 1
= 6 two-byte item indices). Step j of a chunk (j = 0..6) needs c_1 of y_j = x * P_j, P_j the
product of the j items: for j = 0 this is the stored w^1 coefficient of x itself (no
arithmetic); for j >= 1 it is one `c1dot` call on x and the tag block of the table entry of
the multiset. After the seventh item is chosen, the chunk ends: x <- x * P_7 by one dense
product with the stored Karatsuba sums of the size-7 entry.

Every step (j = 0..6):

| Work | Instructions |
| --- | ---: |
| keys from c_1 and the key minimum (section 3) | 1,050 |
| table index h = (K >> 3) mod 32 (ubfx), DP test on (K >> 13) mod 2^70 across two limbs (lsr, orr with masked limb, cbz) | 6 |
| item e = 12 h + t (madd) | 1 |
| insert e into the sorted multiset of <= 6 halfwords (load count, <= 6 x (ldrh, cmp, b, strh), store count) | 34 |
| multiset rank by the combinatorial number system: rank = sum_i C(e_i + i, i + 1) over the sorted items, one table load and add per item, plus the size offset; entry address = base + rank * stride (madd) | 31 |
| 2-cycle test: four limbs of K against K two steps back (2 ldp each, cmp, 3 ccmp, b.eq) | 10 |
| checkpoint test: four limbs of K against the checkpoint K | 10 |
| history: store K (2 stp) and rotate pointers (K, base handle, multiset) | 8 |
| step counter, checkpoint period test (and #63, cbnz), amortised checkpoint store | 6 |
| stuck-walk counter, two words (adds, adc; threshold 20/theta = 5 x 2^72: one exact compare of the high word with 1,280, cmp, b.hs) | 4 |
| work meter: add the step price to a 128-bit accumulator and compare with the cap | 6 |
| walk loop: next walk-state pointer, index increment, wrap test, branch | 8 |
| **itemised (besides the keys)** | **125** |
| **charged (COMMON)** | **300** |

Steps j = 1..6 additionally call `c1dot`: **1,666**.

Per chunk (once every 7 steps):

| Work | Instructions |
| --- | ---: |
| dense Fp12 product x * P_7 with the stored sums of the size-7 entry, written into the alternate base block at stride 96 | 11,803 |
| six exact sums x_i0 + x_i1 of the new base (`add4sums`, section 2b) | 65 |

| bookkeeping: swap base blocks, reset the multiset count, chunk counter, branch (itemised 24) | 200 |

A chunk of 7 steps costs 7 x (1,050 + 300) + 6 x 1,666 + 12,120 = **31,566** instructions:
**4,509.43 instructions = 0.225471 units per step**.

Escapes (proof.md section 3.4): materialise the selected element (one dense product with
the stored sums of its entry, 11,803), square it (5,386), double the exponents (2 fp_add,

instructions = 3.90 steps (charged as at most 4 steps in the cycle law).

## 5. Table, reports and setup

**Items.** R = 32 multipliers M_h = g^{a_h} z^{b_h} (fresh uniform a_h, b_h) and their 12
Frobenius images: 384 items Tab[h][t] = M_h^{p^t}, with exponents (a_h p^t, b_h p^t) mod r.

**Multiset product table.** For every multiset S of k items, 1 <= k <= 7, the entry holds
P_S = product of its items (384 bytes), the 12 stored Karatsuba sums of P_S (768 bytes),
the exponent pair (A(S), B(S)) = sum over items (64 bytes), and for k <= 6 the tag block of
P_S (six Fp2 elements with their sums, 576 bytes). Entries are addressed by the multiset
rank (combinatorial number system, section 4); the counts are C(383 + k, k):
384; 73,920; 9,511,040; 920,193,120; 71,406,986,112; 4,629,552,932,928;
257,932,234,834,560 (2^47.90 entries in all), **2^58.16 bytes**. Each entry is built from
its parent (S minus its largest item) by one dense product, 24 additions for the sums,
4 multiplications by xi and 6 exact additions for the tag block (charged as 6 fp_add), and
400 instructions for enumeration, the two exponent additions mod r and the copies: under **2^47.35 units** for the whole table, built once for this target and
charged (it depends on z). Every multiset that a chunk can reach is in the table, so a
lookup never misses.

**Reports.** At a distinguished class the canonical exponents are
(alpha_j, beta_j) = (alpha + A(S_j), beta + B(S_j)) times p^kappa: 2 fp_add and 2 fp_mul
mod r; with fingerprint extraction, hashing into the chained multimap and an 80-byte record
this is under 2,000 instructions, **charged 10,000** (0.5 unit). With theta = 2^-70 there
are at most 2^55.25 reports: under 2^54.25 units.

**Setup.** Conversion of z, the check z^r = 1, the 384 items with exponents, 2^20 walk
starts (each g^alpha z^beta under 400 units, plus its base sums): under 2^31 units. The
table: 2^47.35 units. Restarts (at most 2^48, each under 400 units): 2^56.6 units.
False-candidate verification: under 600 units. All non-walk work is below **2^57** units.

**Memory and addressing.** Table 2^58.16 bytes; distinguished-point store at most 2^55.25
records x 80 bytes at load factor 2/3: 2^62.2 bytes; 2^20 walk states (base block 576 bytes,
history, multiset): under 2^31 bytes. Peak below **2^63 bytes**. Machine model: the walks run
on scalar AArch64 processors that share one flat 64-bit address space holding the read-only
table and the DP store (every address is an ordinary 64-bit pointer; no structure exceeds
2^64 bytes, and no message passing is assumed); work is summed over processors. Memory is
reported, not scored.
