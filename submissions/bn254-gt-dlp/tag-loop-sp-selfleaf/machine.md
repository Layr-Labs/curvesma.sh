# Priced implementation and instruction bounds

**This package (tag-loop-sp-selfleaf) replaces the step loop. See "Step loop v2" at the end of this file.**
The step price is 213, not 217, and the per-step total is 249, not 253. The window-end charge of 9,216, the
product (8,679), all caps, counters, meters and storage figures below are unchanged. Where the text below says
217 or 253, it describes the parent's loop.

All counts are executed scalar AArch64 instructions (64-bit general registers; arithmetic, logic, loads,
stores, address formation, branches, calls and returns count one each). They are counts of supplied or
generated listings, not timings. One processor; W = R/20,000.

## Evidence chain

* Step loop: generated by `lazy.py` part S, encoding-validated and executed trip by trip against the
  definition (217 per certified trip). The base package's `ttstep.c` / `ttstep.disasm.txt` (273 per trip,
  recounted by its `experiment.py`) remain as the reference implementation.
* Window product: generated by `lazy.py` part L as one straight-line program and executed instruction by
  instruction on a simulator of the scalar subset it uses (LDP/STP/LDR, MUL/UMULH, ADDS/ADCS/ADC,
  SUBS/SBCS/SBC, EXTR/LSL, CSEL, UDIV, ADD/SUB-immediate, ADR, RET), compared with big-integer products;
  executed length **8,679** on every input (straight line). Before execution an encoding validator checks
  every instruction: LDP/STP offsets multiples of 8 in [-512, 504], LDR/LDUR offsets encodable, ADD/SUB
  immediates 12-bit (optionally shifted by 12), shift amounts 0..63, x18 unused, SP only as a base or in
  ADD/SUB-immediate; part L reports 0 violations. `{"seed":1,"listings":true}` prints the listing.
* `fr_add` (label updates) and the remaining tower routines (setup, exact evaluation, table build):
  `tower.c` / `tower.disasm.txt` of the base package, recounted by `experiment.py`.

## The step loop: 217 instructions per step (generated, `lazy.py` part S)

Registers: x0 = P (current entry pointer), x1 = y (48 limbs of Y0), x2 = trip budget, x3 = A, x4 = UMULH
temporary, x5/x6 = (Flo, Fhi) pair, x7/x8 = reloaded limbs, x9 = scratch, x10 = child; 19 limbs resident
in x11..x17 and x19..x30 (loaded once per call). One trip:

1. For each limb j: `ldp x5, x6, [x0, #off_j]`; `umulh x4, y_j, x5`; `madd x3, y_j, x6, x3` (`mul` for the
   first); `add x3, x3, x4`. The 29 non-resident limbs are reloaded by 14 `ldp` and 1 `ldr` from `[x1, #8j]`.
   That is 48 LDP + 48 UMULH + 48 MADD/MUL + 48 ADD + 15 loads = 207.
2. Certificate: `add x9, x3, #96`; `and x9, x9, #0x1fffffffffffffff`; `cmp x9, #96`; `b.ls EXACT`.
3. Selector and child: `lsr x9, x3, #61`; `ldr x10, [x0, x9, lsl #3]`; `cbz x10, REBASE`.
4. `mov x0, x10`; `subs x2, x2, #1`; `b.ne LOOP`.

That makes **217**. Every offset is encodable (validated): F pairs at P - 512 .. P - 16 (limbs 0..31) and
P + 64 .. P + 304 (limbs 32..47), child pointers at P + 0 .. P + 56, limb loads at y + 0 .. y + 376. The
loop has one backward branch, no call and no store. Its two forward exits are not taken on a certified
in-window trip, so such a trip executes exactly 217 instructions (part S checks the executed length of
every trip). The prologue (frame, cursor pointer, 6 STP, 10 limb loads) is 18 instructions and each exit
path (store the cursor, status, 6 LDP, frame, RET) is 12, once per call. The A computed is the same sum
mod 2^64 as in the base package (only the order of additions differs), so the certificate lemma of proof.md
section 2 applies unchanged. The base package's `ttstep.c`/`ttstep.disasm.txt` (273 per trip) are kept
as the reference implementation; its experiment still recounts them.

## Table entry

Each entry is 2,624 bytes and is addressed by P = entry + 512: F words of limbs 0..31 at P - 512 .. P - 1,
the 8 child pointers (each of the same form) at P .. P + 63, F words of limbs 32..47 at P + 64 .. P + 319,
then 216 words holding T_e as the 54 canonical
Karatsuba operands the generated product reads (its 12 coordinates and their Fp2-, Fp4- and
Fp12-level sums, in the order of `lazy.py` `table_operands`), and 8 words (alpha, beta). The step loop
reads only F and the pointers. The window product reads only the 54 operands.

## Window end: at most 9,216 instructions

`rebase` = Y0 <- Y0 * T_e by the generated routine (8,679, including its frame: SUB sp by #1, LSL #12 and
by #112, saves and restores of x19..x30 at [sp, 0..80], four scratch base registers by ADD sp, #624 +
1024 i, ADR of the constant pool, and `ret`), plus two label additions mod r with the base package's
`fr_add` (34 each), plus 20 instructions of glue (call site, pointer set-up). That is 8,767.

**The generated product.** It uses the Fp12 = Fp4[t]/(t^3 - s), Fp4 = Fp2[s]/(s^2 - xi) grouping of the
stored coordinates, with cubic Karatsuba over Fp4, Karatsuba over Fp2 and Karatsuba Fp2, 54
256x256-bit products (67 instructions each: 4 LDP, 16 MUL, 16 UMULH, 31 ADDS/ADCS/ADC). Fp2- and Fp4-level results stay unreduced as 9-word
two's-complement values. Each of the 12 output coordinates is one accumulator over the Fp4-level values,
plus one constant multiple of p, and then a single Montgomery reduction with canonicalisation (107
instructions). That makes 12 reductions instead of 54 (lazy reduction, Aranha et al., eprint 2010/526).

The generator asserts worst-case bounds for every stored value: operands < 2^256, products < 2^512,
intermediates in (-2^575, 2^575), reduction input >= 0, t < 2^264 after the word rounds, and
remainder < 2p before the final conditional subtraction. The output is canonical, Y0 * T_e in Montgomery
form, which is the form the step loop reads. Opcode histogram (part L): ADCS 1,808, LDP 1,438, MUL
1,104, UMULH 1,092, SBCS 920, ADC 590, STP 558, ADDS 542, SUBS 200, EXTR 172, SBC 116, CSEL 96, LSL 20,
UDIV 12, ADD/SUB-immediate 9, ADR 1, RET 1.

One window end = re-base 8,767 + loop entry 18 + exit 12 + driver <= 160 = 8,957 <= **9,216**.

## The driver: a three-word global step counter

As in the base package, except that the global step counter (initially 2^133) is a **three-word**
integer and the per-walk length counter (initially 2^90) a two-word integer. Before each call the driver
sets the 64-bit trip budget to min(steps left, walk length left, 2^62). After the call it subtracts the
trips made from both counters (three-word and two-word subtractions with borrow, and compares). It
dispatches REBASE (window end), EXACT (uncertified tag) and BUDGET (a counter reached zero) exactly as
the base package describes. All driver work per call is below 160 instructions (base: 128 with two-word
counters). The extra word costs 4 instructions (one SBCS and one comparison step each). Every call ends
within 256 trips, so this is charged once per window end.

**Counter widths.** Every cap is enforced by a counter wide enough to hold it, and the update cost is
inside the price stated for the item it counts:

| counter | cap | width | update | charged in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| global step counter | 2^133 | 3 words | subtract with borrow + compare, <= 8 per call | driver <= 160 per call |
| per-walk length counter | 2^90 | 2 words | subtract with borrow + compare, <= 6 per call | driver <= 160 per call |
| trip budget | 2^62 | 1 word | `subs x2, x2, #1` | the 217 of a trip |
| walk counter | 2^52 | 1 word | add + compare, 3 per walk | walk start (< 2^26) |
| exact-evaluation meter | 2^90 | 2 words | add with carry + compare, <= 4 | exact evaluation (< 2^15) |

No counter can wrap.

## Exact evaluation (metered, at most 2^90 times)

As in the base package (re-base, two conversions out of Montgomery form, t = Re(a_0) - 9 Im(a_0), three
four-limb comparisons with multiples of p/8 for s, one for the distinguished test t < ceil(p/2^84), the
aborted trip (< 217), one loop exit (12) and re-entry (18), driver, meter). Below 2^15 instructions, so at most 2^105
in total. The exact-evaluation meter (cap 2^90) is a two-word counter: at most 4 instructions per update,
inside the 2^15 per evaluation.

## Storage

Table: 525,783,425,977,953 x 2,624 bytes = 2^60.259 bytes. Store: 2^54 x 96 = 2^60.585 bytes. Multipliers,
constants, binomial table, code, stacks and the routines' 4 KiB frames: under 2^30 bytes. Peak below
**2^62 bytes**. Every address is an ordinary 64-bit pointer below 2^63. Memory is reported, not scored.

## Step loop v2: 213 instructions per certified trip (`ttloop2.py` part S2)

Registers: x0 = P (current entry), x2 = trip budget, x3 = A, x4 = UMULH temporary and then the certificate
and selector scratch, x5/x6 = (Flo, Fhi), x7/x8 = reloaded limb pair. There are **22 resident limbs**, in
x1, x9, x10, x11..x17 and x19..x30. The window base Y0 sits in the caller's frame, at the caller's SP, so
limb j is at [SP, #112 + 8j] inside the kernel (frame 112; offsets #112..#488, encodable LDP immediates).

One trip:
1. 22 resident limbs: `ldp x5, x6, [x0, #off_j]`; `umulh x4, y_j, x5`; `madd x3, y_j, x6, x3` (`mul` for
   j = 0); `add x3, x3, x4`. That is 4 each.
2. 26 non-resident limbs in 13 pairs: `ldp x7, x8, [sp, #112 + 8j]`, then 4 each. That is 13 + 104.
3. Certificate: `add x4, x3, #96`; `and x4, x4, #0x1fffffffffffffff`; `cmp x4, #96`; `b.ls EXACT`.
4. Selector and child: `lsr x4, x3, #61`; `ldr x0, [x0, x4, lsl #3]`.
5. `subs x2, x2, #1`; `b.ne LOOP`.

Total: 88 + 117 + 4 + 2 + 2 = **213** (48 LDP of F + 13 LDP of limbs = 61 LDP, 48 UMULH, 47 MADD, 1 MUL,
48 ADD, 1 LDR, 8 others). There is no store and no call, and the loop has one backward branch.

The prologue is 19 instructions: frame, cursor pointer, 6 STP of x19..x30, 11 LDP of the 22 resident limbs.
Each of the two exit paths (BUDGET, EXACT) is 12 instructions: cursor store of e, A and budget, status,
6 LDP, frame, RET.

**Leaves.** An entry of multiset size 256 stores its own pointer (P) in all eight child slots. This is the
only table change, and the build cost is unchanged.

**Driver.**
- It sets budget = min(steps left, walk left, 257 - d0), with d0 = 0 at a walk start and d0 = 1 otherwise.
- A BUDGET return with no counter limit means the last trip was the leaf's own certified trip. The driver
  then re-bases (Y0 <- Y0 * T_e, labels) with s = A >> 61 from the cursor, and continues at the root's
  child s. This is the parent's REBASE path, entered from the BUDGET status.
- A counter-limited BUDGET return ends the walk or the run, as in the parent.
- The extra driver work is one compare of the budget with 257 - d0 and one branch. The driver per call is
  charged 168 (parent 160).

**Window end.** 8,679 + 88 + 19 + 12 + 168 = 8,966 <= **9,216** (charged, unchanged).

**Per step.** 213 + 9,216/256 = **249**.
- Every run: R <= 213 x 2^133 + 9,216 (2^133/256 + 2^52 + 1) + 2^110, which is W <= 2^126.68.
- Expected: E[W] <= 2^119.6486 <= 2^119.65 (`ttloop2.py` part G2).


## Distinguished-point store (replaces the parent's open-addressing table)

**Key.** The full distinguished element Y0, as left by the exact evaluation's re-base: 12 canonical
Montgomery residues of 4 limbs each, 3,072 bits. Limb (k, i) is at key bits 256 k + 64 i. Key equality
is exactly element equality, so there are no false matches.

**Structure.** A binary Patricia (crit-bit) trie.
- An internal node is 32 bytes: the bit index, two tagged 64-bit child indices and a pad word.
- A leaf is 512 bytes: the key (384), the labels a and b (64), the walk ID (8), flags (8) and pad (48).
- Nodes are allocated sequentially, with 64-bit indices.
- At N = 2^52 (one operation per walk, walk cap 2^52):
  2^52 x 512 + (2^52 - 1) x 32 bytes = 2^61.0875 bytes.

**Operation.** Lookup and insert are one operation.
1. Traverse from the root, at most 3,072 internal nodes, since bit positions strictly decrease. Each node
   costs 16 instructions: limb index, keyed limb load, variable shift, bit, child load, tag test, branch.
2. Compare the 48 limbs (192). On equality, return the stored record, which is kept; this is a true
   collision.
3. Otherwise find the first differing limb and CLZ it (200).
4. Traverse again from the root to the insertion point (at most 3,072 x 16).
5. Link the new internal node (16), write the leaf including the materialised element (96), plus
   call/frame (64).

The worst case is **98,872** instructions, charged **2^17**. At most 2^52 operations give 2^69
instructions. The fixed-work sum stays below 2^106 < 2^110: the other rows are unchanged, and this row
replaces the parent's < 2^75.

**Storage, revised.** Table 525,783,425,977,953 x 2,624 = 2^60.2590 bytes, plus store 2^61.0875 bytes,
plus under 2^30 bytes of other data, gives a peak below **2^61.7320 bytes** (`ttloop2.py` part D,
rounded up). Every address is a 64-bit pointer below 2^63.

## Driver and the counted steps (connection to the mathematics)

Each certified trip of the kernel is one mathematical step Y <- Y * M_s, s = A >> 61. Entry sizes go up
by exactly 1 per certified trip until a leaf (size 256) is reached. A leaf's trip is the 256th step of a
window started at size 0 or 1, and it is the step realised by the re-base Y0 <- Y0 * T_leaf followed by
the root's child s.

The driver uses budget = min(steps left, walk left, 257 - d0):
- A BUDGET return with no counter limit therefore means exactly 257 - d0 certified trips, the last at the
  leaf.
- An EXACT return means the uncertified trip is not counted by the kernel. The driver counts it as one
  step after the exact evaluation.
- A counter-limited return ends the walk or the run.

So the driver's step count equals the number of mathematical steps, and every step costs at most 213 in
the kernel, plus at most 9,216 per window end. `ttloop2.py` part R checks this equivalence step by step
against the parent's null-leaf semantics, executing the kernel at W = 4.
