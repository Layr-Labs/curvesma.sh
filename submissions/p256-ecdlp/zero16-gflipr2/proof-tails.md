# Section16: correction tails and the disclosed heuristic H_T

Sites. Each X-pair has six data-dependent correction tails in the leaf passes, three per incidence; all other leaf instructions are path-independent (table lookups and folds are branch-free). All six are carry-only REDC tails (B.CC over four): forward A_j=M(A_(j-1),e_j); backward inv=M(A_(j-1),B), B<-M(e_j,B), i1=M(d2,inv), and the squares w=S(i1), w=S(i2). The worst case 3*4=12 per incidence is inside 597, so with no tail taken an incidence costs 585: per call 359/2 per pair forward and 1981/2 backward, per-Y, per-call, flush and driver terms unchanged. The per-Y kernels (chain step, Y-entry preparation) have eleven tail sites per Y; they are charged in full in the 1696 per Y and use no heuristic. The per-run worst case keeps all tails.

E-row. Group g's tail work is executed only if T>g-1, an event fixed by H_g (section15). So E[F]<=sum_g PHI[g]*max E[F_g|H_g], F_g the tail excess of group g's batches: given H_g those batches use fresh chain coins (c,s) of group g, and H_T bounds the conditional rates (linearity only; no independence between sites, pairs or batches). A defective batch (given H_g, probability at most d_batch: a union bound over the fresh chain points) is charged12 per incidence. flip_ledger.py: correction_tails=sum_g PHI[g]*N*L*2^20*(4.8+7.2*d_batch), first_prefix_tail=sum_g PHI[g]*N*L*8. The PHI=1 bound and the per-run worst case keep every tail on every batch.

H_T (the one heuristic, as in 008389f5): for a non-defective batch, given the history H_g before its group and over the group's fresh chain coins, each tail of each of the six sites at each pair position is taken with probability at most0.4, except two structurally special positions per batch, both charged again in full (4 each, first_prefix_tail): the first forward multiply A_0=M(R,e_0), whose multiplicand is the Montgomery one R=2^256-p<2^224, and the final backward update B_(-1)=M(e_0,B_0), which represents one, so its raw value is 2^256-p or R0 and its tail is almost always taken. Support: (1) the Montgomery extra-reduction model (Schindler 2000; Walter-Thompson 2001): with m uniform and operands behaving as uniform residues, P(T>=R0) is about E[ab]/(p*R0): about1/4 for two full operands and1/3 for the square of a full operand; the folded table sums e, i2 and the canonical d2 are full residues below 2^256. (2) probe.py runs the exact integer tail model of all six sites (the model checked call by call against the executed listing: executed count minus four times the taken tails equals the measured formula on every call) on random P-256 points: 32 batches of 4 Y's by 16 pairs at seed97 give site rates0.2495 (forward),0.2373 (inv),0.2563 (B),0.2461 (i1),0.3442 and0.3208 (squares), mean excess3.31 per incidence against the charged4.8; the forced final update has rate1.0 and the first prefix multiply0.0, as stated. (3) A per-position census of the same model, run outside the hosted experiment with the shipped code (`python3 -c "import flip_check as fc; print(fc.census(SEED, 3000))"` for SEED 1 and 2, 3000 batches of 64 pair positions each) found every site at every position at most0.3547, except the forced final update (rate1.0); inv and the first prefix multiply at position0, whose multiplicand is the small Montgomery one, are at0.0; binomial standard error about0.009. (4) Under H_g the X's and their tables are fixed and only the Y's are fresh: with the X set held fixed (fc.census(...,fixed_x=True)), 8 X sets of 3000 batches each, run outside the hosted experiment, give every site at most0.3346, every non-exceptional position at most0.364 and mean excess at most3.35 against the charged4.8; probe.py repeats a small fixed-X census (4 X sets of16 batches) and asserts every site rate below0.4. Limitations: H_T is unproved (representatives are deterministic functions of the points); the census is finite. H_T enters only the expected-work bound; success (section6) and the per-run worst case use no tail assumption.

# Section17: run gate

First one coordinator reads a fresh 64-bit RNDR word u (a coin independent of the challenge and of all other coins) and broadcasts g=[u<K], K=7612861736655233606. If g=1 every node runs sections2-16 unchanged at L=112786453; otherwise all nodes halt and the coordinator outputs the scalar0. The gate is charged64 instructions on every run (read, compare, branch, broadcast, default output).

Success. g is independent of the run, so Pr(success)>=(K/2^64)*s_run, with s_run>=0.9450099630874899656845 the section6 bound at this L. K=K0+2048, where K0 is the least integer with (K0/2^64)*s_run>=0.39 for the exact rational s_run (flip_ledger.py asserts K0 and K0-1); K/2^64 times the printed s_run is already above0.39, giving0.3900000000000001049303.

Work. Pointwise, work is64 plus g times the run's work, so E[work]=64+(K/2^64)*E_run, with E_run the section15 total (rows, caps and H_T unchanged): 2^124.8837... units overall, 2^126.1606... for an opened run, whose per-run worst case is section15's. This is section15's expectation convention (over the challenge and all algorithm coins) with one more coin; nothing is amortized over targets, and a closed gate is charged and counted as a failure.

Choice of L. Success rises with L and the gated total falls with it (E_run/s_run decreases), so L is the largest chain length the per-node memory allows. With32-byte home records (as1e5413b0: next word, K3' with K2 bits58..63 in bits29..44, (w2,w3); section12) and58 hash-index bits (2^58 head words at[7*2^61,2^64); route.s BFI width58), L=112786453 is the largest L whose record array ends below the heads (flip_ledger.py asserts L and L+1). # Section12 receiver: hash join and in-record next words

The receiver (route.s receive) joins every accepted record into its home's hash chains at arrival; there is no separate join pass. Each home owns2^58 head words at[7*2^61,2^64): the head of index K2 mod2^58 is at7*2^61+8*index, formed by one BFI of K2's low58 bits into bits3..60 of the resident register x12 (bits61..63 set, bits0..2 clear; no other instruction writes x12), zeroed once by zero16384 before the first group, and one home record array of32-byte records below7*2^61 with a register-resident cursor and end. A record pointer p addresses the record's w2 word: its next word at p-16, K3' at p-8, (w2,w3) at p; heads, next words and list entries hold such pointers (zero ends a chain), and make_requests reads (w2,w3) at p. SWP (FEAT_LSE, like LDADD) is one load-and-store; after the zeroing only the receiver writes its heads and records. Records are appended in arrival order, so every accepted record is stored and inserted exactly once; records dropped at the full array are neither stored nor inserted.

Accepted path (12 including RET): the LD64B of the whole packet, which releases the window, and the DONE-bit branch (2); CMP/B.HS of the cursor against the last admissible position (2); a pre-indexed STP of (w2,w3) advancing the cursor by32 (1); the BFI head address (1); LSR and BFI forming K3', the packet's K3 with bits29..44 (fraction bits below the key) replaced by K2's bits58..63 and zeros (2); an SWP that stores the new pointer into the head and returns the old head (1); an STP of (old head,K3') (1); CBZ on an empty chain and RET (2). Every record of a chain has the same K2 bits0..57 (its head index), so K3' equality is exactly agreement on K2 and the masked K3, i.e. on all176 key bits. Per visit: one LDP of the prior's (next,K3'), CMP, B.EQ and a CBZ/CBNZ on the next pointer (4); the walk alternates two pointer registers, so no move is needed. On a K3' match: an LDP of the prior's (w2,w3), two EORs with the new record's (w2,w3), ORR, TST of bits0..34 (mask0x7ffffffff: the source node (i,j)), CCMP of the list cursor against the list end when the sources differ, and B.HS back into the walk (7); on a match from another node with room the post-indexed STP of (prior,new) replaces the visit's pointer test, then RET; on a same-node match, or with the list full, the walk continues. A full-array drop costs5. The receive row is12 per potential record; walks are charged4 per visit and7 per K3' (full-key) match (section15); a listing adds nothing.

Same-index records form one arrival-order chain, newest first, every member reachable from the head: the new record is prepended (its next word is the old head) before the walk, which starts at the old head. The walk is uncapped and runs to the first prior from another source node agreeing on K2 and the masked K3 while the list has room, else to the chain end; chains are finite (one home's records, at most CAP_H), so every walk terminates on every run and no overflow event exists. Each new record is therefore linked to its most recent176-bit-agreeing predecessor from another source node (while the list has room), and the listed pairs are a subset of the cross-node176-bit-matching pairs at their home: exactly the pairs the candidate, request and relay rows (d_cl, d_rq, d_rl) bound (section6). The candidate list is cumulative with its2^28 cap checked at every attempted append; the final DONE marker of each group stores the list cursor to the receiver's list-end cell before the release STLR, and the main context loads it after acquiring the DONE cell, so the group's new candidates are exactly the entries between the previous and the published end. routing_check.py executes the receiver on the true layout (persistent resident registers, x12 checked after every call, heads at7*2^61, toy K2 below2^6 except top bits so distinct K2 share chains, planted cross-group and same-node same-key pairs, full home arrays and full lists): heads, next words, record words (K3' included), listed pairs, the published list end and record cursor, and the exact per-call count equal the path model, every head-following reaches exactly its index's members newest first, every listed pair joins two source nodes, same-node matches occur and are skipped, and while the list is not full each record is listed with exactly its latest earlier same-key record from another node.
