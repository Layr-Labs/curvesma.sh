## 2. Random construction, chains and pair arithmetic

There are N=2^70 nodes, indexed (i,j) with each coordinate in [0,2^35). At each node draw b and MX=1024 independent a_j uniformly modulo r, and for each of the G*YB=2154496 chains (group g<2104, lane k<1024) a start c_(g,k) uniform in [0,r) and a step s_(g,k) uniform in {1,...,r-1}. Coins at different nodes are independent. Each scalar draw tries at most eight fresh 256-bit values, rejecting out-of-range values and zero where required; a cap failure aborts the run. The rejection probability is below 2^-31, so the union cap loss is at most N(MX+2*G*YB+1)*2^-248. Sampling is charged 256 scalar instructions per requested scalar. Under the protected ideal-coin convention, RNDR supplies the bits; a hardware-failure assumption is not silently added.

Construct R_g=[b]Q, the permanent points X_j=R_g+[a_j]P (j<1024) and, for every chain, C=[c]P and S=[s]P (cold generation, COLD_ACCOUNTING.md). Chain (g,k) has the L=112786453 points Y_(g,k,u)=[c+u*s]P, u<L: Y_0=C and Y_(u+1)=Y_u+S by the chain step ychain (section3). The X-pairs are (X_2J, X_2J+1), J<512. One batch is one step of the 1024 chains of the current group: its 1024 Y-points against all 1024 X's, 2^20 incidences, 2^19 X-pairs. Group g consists of its L batches (rounds); all nodes run group g at the same time (section15). The executed leaves of an incidence are X+Y and X-Y; per node n0=2*1024*G*1024*L leaves, log2(N*n0)=128.787937 (truncated). A leaf X_j+sY_(g,k,u) is [m]P+[n]Q with m=a_j+s(c+u*s_(g,k)) and n=b.

Records. Y index = 1024*rho'+k with stored round rho'=g*L+u for groups g<1218 and rho'=(g-1218)*L+u for g>=1218, both below2^37 (47 bits); the home bit h=[g>=1218] is recovered at the home from the record's array position (section17), and the round is rho=rho'+1218*L*h, X index j (10 bits). The record is (K2,K3,w2,w3) with w2=i|(Ylo<<35) (Ylo = Y index bits[0,29)) and initially w3=d2|(sign<<35)|(j<<36)|(Yhi<<46) (Yhi = Y index bits[29,47)); h2s replaces only w3 bits0..34 with the source column j. The 35+29/35+1+28 word allocation of 008389f5 is unchanged; only the meaning of the 57 index bits changes (X index 29 bits and Y index 28 bits there). sign 0 is X+Y, sign 1 is X-Y.

Pair arithmetic (field equations, Montgomery scaling understood). For Y=(x_Y,y_Y) and the pair's x1=x(X_2J), x2=x(X_2J+1), d1=x_Y-x1, d2=x_Y-x2 and e=d1*d2=x_Y^2-(x1+x2)x_Y+x1*x2. The forward pass forms e for every pair of the batch and the prefix products A_j=prod(e); one inverse of the last prefix serves the batch; the backward pass maintains B=1/A_j, forms inv=B*A_(j-1)=1/e and updates B<-B*e. Then 1/d1=d2*inv and, since 1/d1-1/d2=(x1-x2)/e, 1/d2=1/d1+(x2-x1)*inv. For each incidence with i=1/d, w=i^2: x(X-+Y)=(y_Y^2+y_X^2)w-+2y_Y(y_X w)-x(X)-x(Y), so the labels follow from two truncated products (section3). No a=0 identity is used. Identity X (probability MX/r per node) or an incidence with x(Y)=x(X) or a degenerate chain step makes the batch defective; the passes then complete as bounded garbage (no zero test), and every defect is charged (section6).

## 3. Field arithmetic, tables, fraction labels and executed counts

Field kernels. M(a,b) is the k_mul body of p256_fields.s with operands in registers (register renaming only; the s2n-bignum-derived core of the section "Short Montgomery multiplication"): for a,b<R0 it returns a representative of a*b*R^-1 below R0 in 95 instructions plus B.CC, plus a 4-instruction carry-only tail (subtract p) taken iff the raw REDC value reached R0. S(a) is the k_sqr body likewise: 75 plus B.CC, plus the tail. The generated loops reserve x0=0xffffffff and x2=MC=0xffffffff00000001; the tail is ADDS #1, SBCS x0, SBCS xzr, SBC x2. probe.py checks both streams bit-exactly through every kernel that uses them and the standalone k_mul on edge operands.

Tables. Every per-X and per-X-pair constant c has a 1 TiB table: row t<2^32 at +256t, slot k<8 at +32k holding (c*t*2^(32k)+[k=0]*add0) mod p, canonical. Pair J's four tables are at 2^51+2^42*J+2^40*tau: tau0 (c=-(x1+x2), add0=x1*x2*R), tau1 (c=x2-x1), tau2 and tau3 (c=y*R^-1 for X_2J, X_2J+1), with x, y plain affine coordinates. 512 pairs fill [2^51,2^52) exactly (fixed_layout.py). A lookup of a 256-bit integer v=sum_k v_k 2^(32k) reads slot k of row v_k for k<8: the address register keeps the table base in bits40..63, BFI inserts the 32-bit window into bits8..39 (LSR #32 first for odd k), bits6..7 select the 64-byte line (ORR #64, EOR #192, ORR #64 between windows), and one LD64B reads the line; the window's slot is its lower (even k) or upper (odd k) four words, added with ADDS/ADCS and the carry counted by CSET/ADC (window0 of a lookup without addend uses two LDP). The sum of the slots is congruent to c*v (plus add0) for any representative v. Lookups cost 59 without addend and 63 with a four-word addend, plus one ORR for the table select in the backward pass.

Fold. A lookup with addend returns V=o+c*2^256 with o<2^256 and c<=8 (eight slots below p plus an addend below 2^256). The branch-free fold adds F[c]=c*(2^256-p) from a nine-entry table at node address0 (LSL, two LDP, four ADDS/ADCS), then adds 2^256-p once more masked by the carry (ADC, SUB from xzr, two AND with 0xffffffff00000000 and 0xfffffffe, four ADDS/ADCS): 15 instructions. If step one does not carry the result is o+c*delta<2^256 (delta=2^256-p<2^224); if it carries, o+c*delta-2^256<2^228 and adding delta stays below2^256; in both cases the result is congruent to V. It is exact on every input: no data-dependent branch, no correction tail.

Forward pass flip_fwd (one call per batch). Per Y: load x_Y (Montgomery) and S_Y=S(x_Y) from the Y entry (12 per Y including the loop). Per pair: e=fold(S_Y+lookup(tau0; x_Y)) (63+15), ST64B of the 64-byte batch entry [e, A_(j-1)] from x16..x23, two LDP of A_(j-1) back as the multiplier, A_j=M(A_(j-1),e), and the pointer and table-base steps: exactly 359/2 per pair (four pairs per unrolled loop test) plus 12 per Y and 18 per call, plus 4 per taken tail (one site per pair). The Montgomery one R=F[1] seeds A_(-1); the final prefix is stored after the last entry for the inverse.

Backward pass flip_bwd (pairs in reverse). Per pair: one LD64B of the entry into x8..x15; B from the frame; inv=M(A_(j-1),B) and B<-M(e,B) (B is the preserved operand of both); d2=x_Y-x2 with the masked +p correction (SUBS chain, SBC mask, two AND, four ADDS/ADCS: 11, canonical since both inputs are); i1=M(d2,inv); i2=fold(i1+lookup(tau1; inv)); i2 is spilled to the frame while the first incidence is labelled. Per incidence (i in x24..x27): w=S(i); TP1=TP(w; rows(y_Y^2 R^-1)+rows(y_X^2 R^-1)) with the Y rows read by two LD64B of the Y entry and the X rows added in place by eight LDP from the X area (73); U=TP1-T(x(Y))-T(x(X)) (an LD64B of the Y entry's third line, which also brings IDY, two LDP of T(x(X)) and the X index word idX, six subtractions and the ID ORR: 11); N1=lookup(tau2 or tau3; w) (60, five limbs: the carry c<=7 is the fifth); TP2 starts from PRE[c] (ADD, LDP, LDR) and adds the four full limbs against rows0..3 of 2y_Y read by two LD64B with their address ADDs (55 in all); L+=U-TP2 and L-=U+TP2, each emitted as one record exactly as in 008389f5 (3+9 and 3+8). The backward pass costs exactly 1981/2 per pair plus 11 per Y and 24 per call, plus 4 per taken tail (five sites per pair: inv, B, i1 and the two squares), plus the flush terms. Per incidence the tail-free cost is (359+1981)/4=585, the worst 597.

Montgomery bookkeeping: entries hold e*R and A_j*R; B=(1/A_j)R, inv=(1/e)R, i=(1/d)R, w=i^2 R; TP(w; rows(y^2R^-1)) has c*m=y^2 i^2; N1 is congruent to (y_X R^-1)w=y_X i^2; TP(N1; rows(2y_Y)) has c*m=2y_X y_Y i^2.

Fraction labels. For constants c let row_k(c)=floor(2^256*(c*2^(64k) mod p)/p), and for m=sum m_k 2^(64k) let TP(m;rows)=sum_k floor(m_k*row_k/2^64) mod2^192 (the words at 2^64..2^255 of each limb product). ## 5. Distribution and collision probability

All probability statements refer to the coins of section2. Condition on Q. Facts: (F1) given b, the X_j are i.i.d. uniform and independent of every chain; given all points, b stays uniform (for each b exactly one tuple of a_j gives the X_j). (F2) Each Y_(g,k,u) is uniform on the group (c uniform), independent of the X's and of every other chain. (F3) Pairwise chain uniformity: for u!=v (both below L<r), (Y_u,Y_v) of one chain is uniform on the r(r-1) pairs of distinct points, because (c,s) -> (c+us, c+vs) is a bijection from Z_r x (Z_r\{0}) onto pairs with distinct coordinates; given one point of a chain, any other point of it is uniform on the remaining r-1 points. (F4) Every leaf X_j+-Y is uniform; leaves of different nodes are independent.

Let Gamma be the unordered pairs of leaves from different nodes, |Gamma|=(S^2-N*n0^2)/2, and I_alpha the class-collision indicator of alpha={L1,L1'}; P(I_alpha)=P1=(2(r-1)+1)/r^2 (the singleton identity included) and mu=|Gamma|P1. I_alpha is a function of the coins of its four generators: the X and the chain of each leaf. Let B_alpha be the pairs sharing an X or a chain with alpha; I_alpha is independent of the whole collection outside B_alpha (disjoint coin sets). An X lies in 2*Y_node leaves and a chain in 2*MX*L, so |B_alpha|<=12(Y_node+MX*L)S, conservatively.

Second moments. Write a leaf as [m]P+[n]Q with m=a+sigma(c+u*s), n=b. For a unit w in{1,-1}, E_alpha(w) is the linear relation a+sigma1(c+u s)+bx-w(a'+sigma1'(c'+u' s')+b'x)=0 over Z_r (x=log Q); I_alpha<=sum_w 1[E_alpha(w)]. Let beta in B_alpha, beta!=alpha. Case1: beta has a generator outside alpha's four. Condition on everything except that generator's a or c, which enters beta's relation with a unit coefficient and not alpha's: P(E_alpha(w1) and E_beta(w2))<=(1/r)(1/r). Case2: beta uses exactly alpha's two X's and two chains (positions v, v' and signs may differ; it lies in the same two nodes). Condition on s, s', b, b', x; (a,c,a',c') stays uniform on Z_r^4 and the two relations have coefficient rows (1,sigma1,-w1,-w1 sigma1') and (1,sigma2,-w2,-w2 sigma2'). Unequal rows have rank two: probability 1/r^2. Equal rows force sigma2=sigma1, w2=w1, sigma2'=sigma1'; subtracting leaves sigma1(v-u)s-w1 sigma1'(v'-u')s'=0 with (v,v')!=(u,u'): if one difference is zero the other forces s or s' to zero (impossible), otherwise at most one s' fits each s, so P<=(1/r)(1/(r-1)). Summing over the four unit pairs, E[I_alpha I_beta]<=4/(r-1)^2, conservatively bounded by36/(r-1)^2. ## 6. Defects, capacity losses and success expression

Every computed key of a point Z lies in K(Z)={floor((T(Z)+e)/2^16): e in[-21,12]}, at most two consecutive values (section3); FRACTION_LABEL.md gives the point-only cell bounds. Table lookups and folds are exact on every input, so they add no defect. A batch is defective when one of its incidences has x(Y)=x(X), any chain point of its group up to its round is the identity (the starts C included) or was reached by a step with x(Y_(u-1))=x(S) (Y_(u-1)=+-S), or an X is the identity: a degenerate step garbles the batched inverse of that chain step and hence every lane of every later round of the group. Per batch d_batch=YB*MX*2/(r-1)+L*YB*3/(r-1)+MX/(r-1) (given X, Y=+-X has probability at most 2/(r-1); given s, each chain point's three degenerate events are three values of c, over at most L*YB points of the group; each X is the identity with probability 1/r). Excluding every defective batch of the whole run, and any identity X, costs N*G*L*d_batch+N*MX/r (global term). A defective batch's cost is no larger: every kernel count is data-independent except the correction tails, charged at their worst on defective batches, so its passes complete as bounded garbage; a degenerate chain step garbles the chain state of all 1024 lanes for the rest of the group, and every such later batch is itself counted defective by the definition above.

## 8. Self-contained zero-collision probability lemma

Let I_alpha be Bernoulli variables with means p_alpha and dependency
neighborhoods B_alpha containing alpha. Assume I_alpha is independent of the
entire collection outside B_alpha, not merely pairwise independent. Put
W=sum I_alpha and lambda=sum p_alpha. For k>=1 define
f(k)=integral_0^1 exp(-lambda*t)*t^(k-1) dt, and set f(0)=f(1).
Integration by parts gives
lambda*f(k+1)-k*f(k)=-exp(-lambda) for k>=1, while at k=0 it is
1-exp(-lambda). Thus this is exactly the Stein equation for the zero event.
Also 0<=f(k)<=1 and f(k) decreases for k>=1, so every successive difference
has absolute value at most one, including k=0. This gives a Lipschitz bound
on all nonnegative integers, without a limiting argument.

Set W_alpha=sum_(beta outside B_alpha) I_beta. Independence gives
E[I_alpha*f(W_alpha+1)]=p_alpha*E[f(W_alpha+1)]. Add and subtract these terms
in E[lambda*f(W+1)-W*f(W)]. The first resulting difference is bounded by
p_alpha*E[W-W_alpha]=sum_(beta in B_alpha) p_alpha*p_beta. In the second,
when I_alpha=1, W-(W_alpha+1)=sum_(beta in B_alpha excluding alpha) I_beta;
its bound is the sum of E[I_alpha*I_beta] over those beta. Summing proves
|Pr(W=0)-exp(-lambda)|<=b1+b2 with exactly the quantities used in Section 5.
This reproduces the relevant dependency-graph inequality directly. It
does not assume Poisson-distributed collisions or independent leaves in a node.

**Groups.** The run is partitioned once, before it starts, into G=2104 groups: group g is the L rounds (batches) of the 1024 chains (g,k), each round one chain step of those chains against all 1024 X's, so every group has exactly 2^20*L incidences and 2^21*L leaves per node. The stop flag is a latched word set only by a home whose resolver has verified [x]P=Q, broadcast with the barrier release and tested by every node before the next group (5 instructions, phase-control row); a set flag halts the run with that x as output. No group is skipped for any other reason. Points, scalars, coins and caps are those of sections2 to6; only the execution order and the detection pass change.

**Stopping time.** Let T be the first group whose resolution confirms an x (T=G+1 if none). Group g's arithmetic, emission, hop1, hop2 (with the receiver's append and insert) and per-group fixed work are deterministic given g and are executed iff T>g-1. The success event is {T<=G}, and the section6 expression is evaluated for the full run: the chain detection does not depend on group boundaries (every record walks the chain of all its priors at arrival, every accepted record is inserted exactly once, the candidate list is cumulative, and a group's candidates are resolved before its stop test). Thus Pr(T<=G)>=0.9450099630874899656845 at L=112786453 (flip_ledger.py); section17 gates the run.

**Non-stopping bounds.** Pr(T>g-1) is at most the probability that no useful collision among the leaves of groups1..g-1 of every node is resolved by the end of group g-1. The section6 expression re-evaluated for the first g-1 groups (same N, MX, YB, L; CAP_PG, CAP_H and the candidate, request and relay caps fixed at their full-run values; the dependency, variance and global-defect terms kept at their full-run sizes; calc(L,g-1,caph) in flip_ledger.py) lower-bounds that resolution probability: the Chen-Stein bound and every defect, capacity and usefulness row condition only on coins and records of prefix groups, prefix means are at most full means, the fixed caps keep every Chebyshev gap at least its prefix value, and later groups never unlink a prefix record from its chain nor remove a listed pair. Hence PHI[g]=min(1,1-lo(g-1)), rounded up to a multiple of2^-120, bounds Pr(T>g-1); PHI[1]=1, nonincreasing, PHI[2104]<0.05513; the group E-factor sum_g PHI[g]/G is below0.50731.

**Expected work.** By linearity over the indicators1[T>g-1], E[work]<=W_rest+sum_g PHI[g]*W_g+W_sameB+W_K2+W_def+W_tails, where W_tails is the two PHI-weighted tail rows of section16 and W_g is the group's per-batch and per-record work (section14) plus its main-pair walk bound (below), W_rest holds the node-initialization, hash-head-zeroing and table rows, the per-group fixed work of all2104 groups, the four phase-4 stages per group, generation, sampling and the resolution, source-service, barrier, control and output rows, all at their worst case, and the global rows are charged without PHI. No independence between stopping and the chains is claimed.

## Short Montgomery multiplication

The scalar multiplication core is adapted from Amazon s2n-bignum commit56152cdb51f47ef9f63b179cda0a8b7518379240, arm/p256/bignum_montmul_p256_alt.S, licensed Apache-2.0 OR ISC OR MIT-0 (see the repository LICENSE). The source and its arm/proofs/bignum_montmul_p256_alt.ml are at https://github.com/awslabs/s2n-bignum/tree/56152cdb51f47ef9f63b179cda0a8b7518379240 . The upstream HOL proof does not certify our patched tails or register transformations.

The core forms the full four-by-four schoolbook product with MUL/UMULH and explicit carries, then reduces the low limbs four times with the cancellation digit t (MC=2^64-2^32+1: add t shifted32 to the next limb, carry t>>32 onward, add the low/high halves of t*MC to the remaining two limbs with carry), each step an exact division of the current integer plus t*p by2^64. This gives raw T=(ab+m*p)/R0 with0<=m<R0 and T<R0+p for a,b<R0. B.CC consumes the final high carry: the tail keeps T if T<R0 and returns T-p otherwise, always in[0,R0).

Including RET and standalone tail constants, the standalone k_mul costs110 instructions when correcting and104 otherwise (probe short_mul). In the generated kernels the operands are registers: flip_witness.mul_lines takes the k_mul body without its four loads, its MOV of MC and its stores, renames registers (a bijection on fourteen registers; the multiplicand is consumed, the multiplier preserved) and uses x2 for MC and the reserved x0, x2 in the tail; sqr_lines does the same for k_sqr (its CBZ on a separate carry word becomes B.CC on the same carry). The streams therefore execute exactly the instructions of p256_fields.s up to renaming: 95 plus B.CC (multiply) and 75 plus B.CC (square), plus the 4-instruction tail when taken, checked bit-exactly in every probe call.
