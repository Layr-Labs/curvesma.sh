# P-256: a 72-bit processor grid through source label recovery

This is a complete exploratory analytical claim. The sole heuristic is the inherited, disclosed history-conditional H_T for Montgomery correction tails; success and every-run bounds do not use it. The exact target, normalization and ideal-machine premises are unchanged. No acceptance of a predecessor is a mathematical premise.

The structural change is to remove the sign and the low X-index bit from every hot record. Rare source requests recover those two bits by reconstructing four points. This permits a 2^36 by2^36 grid in the same128 metadata bits, with a longer useful search and a130-bit home/hash cell at the existing58-bit hash index. The kernels' per-incidence bound is unchanged; this is a geometry and stored-versus-recomputed change, not an instruction reduction or an unused success-gate allowance.

## 2. Random construction and record geometry

At each node (i,j), independently draw b and MX independent a_j uniformly modulo r. Independently for each of G*YB chains draw c uniformly modulo r and nonzero s uniformly modulo r. Construct R_g=[b]Q, X_j=R_g+[a_j]P, C=[c]P and S=[s]P. Chain u has Y_u=[c+us]P,0<=u<L. A requested scalar uses at most eight256-bit rejection samples; rejection probability is below2^-31, and all cap failures are charged in section6. Ideal RNDR coins are the protected coin convention. Sampling costs256 instructions/requested scalar. Independent random steps are essential for the pairwise chain-uniformity proof in section5.

One round pairs its1024 current Y values with every X, yielding2^20 incidences. Every incidence emits X+Y and X-Y. Leaf scalar coefficients are m=a_j+sigma(c+us),n=b. Consecutive X values form512 X-pairs; the unchanged paired-inversion arithmetic in section3 computes both labels. There are n0=2*MX*G*YB*L leaves per node and S=N*n0 overall.

Let rho=g*L+u. The global Y index v=1024*rho+k is47 bits, since G*L=88000000000<2^37. No epoch split or address-derived epoch bit is needed. Write v_lo=v mod2^28,v_hi=floor(v/2^28),J=floor(j_X/2). The two metadata words are

    w2 = i | (v_lo<<36)
    w3 = d2 | (J<<36) | (v_hi<<45)     before hop2,
    w3 = j | (J<<36) | (v_hi<<45)      after hop2.

Both are64 bits:36+28 and36+9+19. Neither sigma nor j_X mod2 is stored. For T(Z)=floor(2^192*x(Z)/p), the computed label differs by[-21,12]. The175-bit key is bits[17,192). If label limbs are (low,middle,high), d1=middle mod2^36, d2=low>>28, K2=high, and K3=EXTR(low,middle,#36). Home bits are[28,100). K3 has low28 bits[100,128) and high19 significant bits[17,36), with eight repeated home bits. The receiver replaces ignored K3 bits28..44 by K2 bits58..63 and zeros. Mask0xffffe0000fffffff selects the raw significant bits; after insertion exact K3' comparison plus the shared58-bit hash index is exactly equality of the full175-bit key. ## 5. Distribution and collision probability

All probability statements refer to the coins of section2. Condition on Q. Facts: (F1) given b, the X_j are i.i.d. uniform and independent of every chain; given all points, b stays uniform (for each b exactly one tuple of a_j gives the X_j). (F2) Each Y_(g,k,u) is uniform on the group (c uniform), independent of the X's and of every other chain. (F3) Pairwise chain uniformity: for u!=v (both below L<r), (Y_u,Y_v) of one chain is uniform on the r(r-1) pairs of distinct points, because (c,s) -> (c+us, c+vs) is a bijection from Z_r x (Z_r\{0}) onto pairs with distinct coordinates; given one point of a chain, any other point of it is uniform on the remaining r-1 points. (F4) Every leaf X_j+-Y is uniform; leaves of different nodes are independent.

Let Gamma be the unordered pairs of leaves from different nodes, |Gamma|=(S^2-N*n0^2)/2, and I_alpha the class-collision indicator of alpha={L1,L1'}; P(I_alpha)=P1=(2(r-1)+1)/r^2 (the singleton identity included) and mu=|Gamma|P1. I_alpha is a function of the coins of its four generators: the X and the chain of each leaf. Let B_alpha be the pairs sharing an X or a chain with alpha; I_alpha is independent of the whole collection outside B_alpha (disjoint coin sets). An X lies in 2*Y_node leaves and a chain in 2*MX*L, so |B_alpha|<=12(Y_node+MX*L)S, conservatively.

Second moments. Write a leaf as [m]P+[n]Q with m=a+sigma(c+u*s), n=b. For a unit w in{1,-1}, E_alpha(w) is the linear relation a+sigma1(c+u s)+bx-w(a'+sigma1'(c'+u' s')+b'x)=0 over Z_r (x=log Q); I_alpha<=sum_w 1[E_alpha(w)]. Let beta in B_alpha, beta!=alpha. Case1: beta has a generator outside alpha's four. Condition on everything except that generator's a or c, which enters beta's relation with a unit coefficient and not alpha's: P(E_alpha(w1) and E_beta(w2))<=(1/r)(1/r). Case2: beta uses exactly alpha's two X's and two chains (positions v, v' and signs may differ; it lies in the same two nodes). Condition on s, s', b, b', x; (a,c,a',c') stays uniform on Z_r^4 and the two relations have coefficient rows (1,sigma1,-w1,-w1 sigma1') and (1,sigma2,-w2,-w2 sigma2'). Unequal rows have rank two: probability 1/r^2. Equal rows force sigma2=sigma1, w2=w1, sigma2'=sigma1'; subtracting leaves sigma1(v-u)s-w1 sigma1'(v'-u')s'=0 with (v,v')!=(u,u'): if one difference is zero the other forces s or s' to zero (impossible), otherwise at most one s' fits each s, so P<=(1/r)(1/(r-1)). Summing over the four unit pairs, E[I_alpha I_beta]<=4/(r-1)^2, conservatively bounded by36/(r-1)^2. Random steps are essential: with a shared public step, equal position differences would correlate fully.

The dependency-graph Poisson bound gives Pr(no cross-node class collision)<=exp(-mu)+b1+b2 with b3=0 (independence outside B_alpha, above), b1=sum_alpha sum_(beta in B_alpha) p_alpha p_beta<=|Gamma|*12(Y_node+MX*L)S*P1^2 and b2=sum_alpha sum_(beta in B_alpha, beta!=alpha) E[I_alpha I_beta]<=|Gamma|*12(Y_node+MX*L)S*36/(r-1)^2 (chain-mates and shared X's included); CS=|Gamma|*12(Y_node+MX*L)S*(P1^2+36/(r-1)^2) (section8 proves the inequality). This is the finite dependency-graph theorem, not a random-walk heuristic; no previous verdict or external retrieval is a premise.

Given all points, a nonidentity colliding pair has a definite sign w, and n'-w*n vanishes with probability exactly 1/r since the two nodes' b stay independent uniform; such pairs are discarded. For a good witness, whose class fraction T avoids the33 label-error residues modulo2^17 (section6), both witness records carry the key floor(T/2^17); every other record with that key, of another class or a third member of the witness class, is a charged intruder if it reaches the witness home between the two witness records (section6). The receiver lists a full-key match only between records of two different source nodes (section12), and the two witness records come from different nodes. Under the no-intruder event every record between them in their hash chain has another key, so the later witness record's walk meets the earlier one as its first full-key match and lists the witness pair itself (while the list has room); that pair is useful by the event above. Each witness incidence also has an other-sign leaf (X-sY beside X+sY), so four leaves are fixed by the witness's generator points: the two witness leaves and their two siblings. Unconditionally the four are independent uniform, because (X,Y)->(X+sY,X-sY) is invertible at each node and the two nodes' coins are independent.

## 6. Defects, capacity losses and success expression

Every computed key of a point Z lies in K(Z)={floor((T(Z)+e)/2^17): e in[-21,12]}, at most two consecutive values (section3); FRACTION_LABEL.md gives the point-only cell bounds. Table lookups and folds are exact on every input, so they add no defect. A batch is defective when one of its incidences has x(Y)=x(X), any chain point of its group up to its round is the identity (the starts C included) or was reached by a step with x(Y_(u-1))=x(S) (Y_(u-1)=+-S), or an X is the identity: a degenerate step garbles the batched inverse of that chain step and hence every lane of every later round of the group. Per batch d_batch=YB*MX*2/(r-1)+L*YB*3/(r-1)+MX/(r-1) (given X, Y=+-X has probability at most 2/(r-1); given s, each chain point's three degenerate events are three values of c, over at most L*YB points of the group; each X is the identity with probability 1/r). Excluding every defective batch of the whole run, and any identity X, costs N*G*L*d_batch+N*MX/r (global term). A defective batch's cost is no larger: every kernel count is data-independent except the correction tails, charged at their worst on defective batches, so its passes complete as bounded garbage; a degenerate chain step garbles the chain state of all 1024 lanes for the rest of the group, and every such later batch is itself counted defective by the definition above.

For the36-bit slot pattern (d1, fraction bits[64,100)), KB(36)=(513/512)*2^220/(r-1), and KBH=(513/512)*2^184/(r-1) for the72-bit home pattern (bits[28,100)), by the interval lemma of FRACTION_LABEL.md. The pair-load term h uses the crude home coefficient2+66*2^-28 (KB72); the175-bit key uses KB(175)=(2+66*2^-17)*2^81/(r-1); fixed cells whose lowest fixed bit is at least28 use2+66*2^-28 (KBC). These concern the fixed set of keys a point can receive, not the selected value. Let rho=(r/(r-1))^4.

Condition on the witness's two leaf points, that is on its two X's and its two Y points, but not on the steps of the witness chains, and never on global cleanliness or actual raw-label choices; by (F3) every other point of a witness chain stays uniform on r-1 points. Retain the reservation12 fixed leaves. The witness's hop1 group slot receives the group's records of its node: mean m_p=2*MX*YB*L*KB36+1 and variance bound v_p=m_p+1+60*(YB*L+MX)*KB36+4*(L^2+MX*L)*KB36. Its home record array spans all groups: mean m_h=S*KBH+1, variance v_h=m_h+1+60*(Y_node+MX)*KBH+4*(L^2+MX*L)*KBH. Then d_p=v_p/(CAP_PG-12-m_p)^2 and d_h=v_h/(CAP_H-12-m_h)^2, CAP_H=ceil(m_h)+2^41. The witness's group is predetermined by its chain, so the uniform bound over the equal groups applies and no group multiplier is needed. There is only one pair-slot loss, because no second-hop slot exists.

Variance justification. Two leaves on different chains are independent unless they share a free X, and then X+-Y1, X+-Y2 with independent uniform Y1, Y2 are still independent. Leaves on one free chain at different positions: with different X's at least one X is free, so independent; with one fixed X, (Y_v,Y_w) is uniform on distinct pairs (F3), covariance at most |C|^2/(r^2(r-1)); with one free X', the difference of the two leaves is a uniform nonzero point, covariance of the same size. The two leaves of an incidence with both generators free are an invertible linear image of (X,Y): independent. Correlated pairs that remain: (i) an incidence with exactly one witness generator (the witness X with a free Y: YB*L per group, Y_node over the run; the witness Y with a free X': MX), whose two leaves are functions of the free generator, covariance at most KB: the 60(...) term, with the generous factor of 008389f5; (ii) two leaves X+-Y_v, X+-Y_w of the witness X on the witness chain (s free, so both depend on one coin): at most (2L)^2 ordered pairs, covariance at most KB; (iii) a free X' with Y_(u+t) and -Y_(u-t) of the witness chain, whose difference 2Y_u is fixed: at most 4*MX*L ordered pairs, covariance at most KB. (ii) and (iii) are the 4(L^2+MX*L) term. The aggregate of the tiny terms is below one at the stated sizes. Chebyshev then yields the displayed conditional drop probabilities. The exact script asserts CAP_PG-12>m_p and CAP_H-12>m_h before division; no independent hashing distribution is assumed.

Condition on the witness's two leaf points, as above. The two leaves of a competing listed pair are functions of the coins of two different nodes (each node's X's, chains and b are its own; F1, F2), and conditioning on the witness reveals each node's coins separately, so the two leaves stay conditionally independent. ## 8. Self-contained zero-collision probability lemma

Let I_alpha be Bernoulli variables with means p_alpha and dependency
neighborhoods B_alpha containing alpha. Assume I_alpha is independent of the
entire collection outside B_alpha, not merely pairwise independent. Put
W=sum I_alpha and lambda=sum p_alpha. For k>=1 define
f(k)=integral_0^1 exp(-lambda*t)*t^(k-1) dt, and set f(0)=f(1).
Integration by parts gives
lambda*f(k+1)-k*f(k)=-exp(-lambda) for k>=1, while at k=0 it is
1-exp(-lambda). Thus this is exactly the Stein equation for the zero event.
Also 0<=f(k)<=1 and f(k) decreases for k>=1, so every successive difference
has absolute value at most one, including k=0. This gives a Lipschitz bound
on all nonnegative integers, without a limiting argument.

Set W_alpha=sum_(beta outside B_alpha) I_beta. Independence gives
E[I_alpha*f(W_alpha+1)]=p_alpha*E[f(W_alpha+1)]. Add and subtract these terms
in E[lambda*f(W+1)-W*f(W)]. The first resulting difference is bounded by
p_alpha*E[W-W_alpha]=sum_(beta in B_alpha) p_alpha*p_beta. In the second,
when I_alpha=1, W-(W_alpha+1)=sum_(beta in B_alpha excluding alpha) I_beta;
its bound is the sum of E[I_alpha*I_beta] over those beta. Summing proves
|Pr(W=0)-exp(-lambda)|<=b1+b2 with exactly the quantities used in Section 5.
This reproduces the relevant dependency-graph inequality directly. It
does not assume Poisson-distributed collisions or independent leaves in a node.

## 14. Expected work structure

For group g, use s_g=N*2*MX*YB*L and p_g=g*s_g. Main walks charge4*PAIRM*(s_g*p_g+s_g*(s_g-1)/2-sameB_g), where PAIRM=KBC(130)+1/(r-1); the same-batch row uses2*KBC(130)+1/(r-1) without stopping weights. ## 15. Grouped early abort, stopping time and the expected-work ledger

**Groups.** The run is partitioned once, before it starts, into G=1600 groups: group g is the L rounds (batches) of the 1024 chains (g,k), each round one chain step of those chains against all 1024 X's, so every group has exactly 2^20*L incidences and 2^21*L leaves per node. All nodes run group g at the same time, separated by barriers: hop1 of the group's records into fresh per-group slots (CAP_PG=90112 records, BLOCK_PROTOCOL.md), hop2 into the cumulative home record arrays with the receiver's hash join at arrival, the four resolution stages for the group's new candidates, and the stop test. The stop flag is a latched word set only by a home whose resolver has verified [x]P=Q, broadcast with the barrier release and tested by every node before the next group (5 instructions, phase-control row); a set flag halts the run with that x as output. No group is skipped for any other reason. Points, scalars, coins and caps are those of sections2 to6; only the execution order and the detection pass change.

**Stopping time.** Let T be the first group whose resolution confirms an x (T=G+1 if none). Group g's arithmetic, emission, hop1, hop2 (with the receiver's append and insert) and per-group fixed work are deterministic given g and are executed iff T>g-1. The success event is {T<=G}, and the section6 expression is evaluated for the full run: the chain detection does not depend on group boundaries (every record walks the chain of all its priors at arrival, every accepted record is inserted exactly once, the candidate list is cumulative, and a group's candidates are resolved before its stop test). Thus Pr(T<=G)>=0.9928257668761900698688 at L=55000000 (flip_ledger.py); section17 gates the run.

**Non-stopping bounds.** Pr(T>g-1) is at most the probability that no useful collision among the leaves of groups1..g-1 of every node is resolved by the end of group g-1. The section6 expression re-evaluated for the first g-1 groups (same N, MX, YB, L; CAP_PG, CAP_H and the candidate, request and relay caps fixed at their full-run values; the dependency, variance and global-defect terms kept at their full-run sizes; calc(L,g-1,caph) in flip_ledger.py) lower-bounds that resolution probability: the Chen-Stein bound and every defect, capacity and usefulness row condition only on coins and records of prefix groups, prefix means are at most full means, the fixed caps keep every Chebyshev gap at least its prefix value, and later groups never unlink a prefix record from its chain nor remove a listed pair. Hence PHI[g]=min(1,1-lo(g-1)), rounded up to a multiple of2^-120, bounds Pr(T>g-1); PHI[1]=1, nonincreasing, PHI[1600]<0.008; the group E-factor sum_g PHI[g]/G is below0.35.

**Expected work.** By linearity over the indicators1[T>g-1], E[work]<=W_rest+sum_g PHI[g]*W_g+W_sameB+W_K2+W_def+W_tails+W_fold, where W_fold is section18 and W_tails is section16 (defective tails globally unweighted) and W_g is the group's per-batch and per-record work (section14), its per-group fixed work, four phase-4 stages and barriers, plus its main-pair walk bound (below), W_rest holds the node-initialization, hash-head-zeroing and table rows, generation, sampling and the resolution, source-service, control and output rows, all at their worst case, and the global rows are charged without PHI. No independence between stopping and the chains is claimed.

**Walk means (history-conditional domination with a validity split).** Let H_g=(all coins of groups<g at every node, all X's, every node's b and R_g, Q). The event {T>g-1} is a function of H_g: records, chains, listed pairs, requests, replies, the resolver's sign test, the usefulness test and [x]P=Q are deterministic functions of those points, scalars and Q. Group g's walk cost at a home is sum over its inserts u of the visits V_u, and V_u is at most the number of priors v in u's hash chain (every visit examines one distinct chain member, section12). Visits are counted against the130-bit cell (home72 bits plus the58 hash-index bits of K2: fraction bits[28,100) and[128,186)): every prior in u's chain lies in that cell. A record's actual label is a deterministic function of its batch (the batch's 1024 Y points and the 1024 X's), because the batch inversion couples all its incidences. Split the pairs (u,v): same-batch pairs, and main pairs, whose records come from different batches (in earlier groups or in group g). For a main pair split on v's validity. If v's batch is defective (probability at most d_batch), v is visited by at most CAP_H later records at its home, each visit possibly K2-verified: charged globally in DEF below. Otherwise v's actual label equals its ideal label, a function of the X's and of the genuine chain points of v's batch (one per lane), so bound the event that u's key set meets the cell of v's IDEAL label: condition on H_g and on those genuine points only; they reveal at most one point of u's chain, so by (F3) u's Y point is uniform on at least r-1 points and u's leaf X_u+sY_u is uniform off at most one point. If u's batch is not defective, u's record key lies in the at-most-two-value key set of that leaf's class, so the probability that this set meets the one fixed130-bit cell of v's label is at most CELL130=(2+66*2^-28)*2^126/(r-1) (FRACTION_LABEL.md; the cell's lowest fixed bit is28), and the leaf is the identity with probability at most1/(r-1). So Pr(u's key set meets the cell of v's ideal label, u valid | conditioning)<=PAIRM=CELL130+1/(r-1) uniformly. If u's batch is defective (probability at most d_batch, which counts every chain point of its group up to its round), u's label is garbage and its walk is at most CAP_H visits, each possibly K2-verified; with the defective priors above, DEF=2*(4+7)*d_batch*CAP_H*S charges both globally. Hence E[1[T>g-1]*walks_g^main]<=PHI[g]*4*PAIRM*(s_g*p_g+s_g(s_g-1)/2-sameB_g)+DEF, with s_g the group's leaves over all nodes, p_g the earlier groups' leaves and the pair count deterministic: the conditional bound is established before multiplying by PHI[g]. Same-batch pairs (per group N*L*2^21*(2^21-1)/2) are charged globally without PHI and WITHOUT fixing either actual label: both actual keys lie in the fixed key sets K_u and K_v of their ideal classes, so the records share a chain only if some130-bit cell meets both sets, and Pr<=sum over cells C of Pr(K_u meets C and K_v meets C). For two leaves with different Y, condition on the X's and on all chain coins except those of one leaf's chain: that leaf's Y is uniform (F2, F3), so the sum is at most2*CELL130; for the two leaves of one incidence, (X+Y,X-Y) is an invertible linear image of(X,Y) with X and Y independent uniform (these global rows use unconditional probabilities), so the leaves are independent uniform and the same bound holds; two leaves with the same Y and different X's are handled by the fresh a of either X. With an identity leaf (probability at most1/(r-1)): PAIRM_SAME=2*CELL130+1/(r-1) (SXV in the ledger). ## Short Montgomery multiplication

The scalar multiplication core is adapted from Amazon s2n-bignum commit56152cdb51f47ef9f63b179cda0a8b7518379240, arm/p256/bignum_montmul_p256_alt.S, licensed Apache-2.0 OR ISC OR MIT-0 (see the repository LICENSE). The source and its arm/proofs/bignum_montmul_p256_alt.ml are at https://github.com/awslabs/s2n-bignum/tree/56152cdb51f47ef9f63b179cda0a8b7518379240 . The upstream HOL proof does not certify our patched tails or register transformations.

The core forms the full four-by-four schoolbook product with MUL/UMULH and explicit carries, then reduces the low limbs four times with the cancellation digit t (MC=2^64-2^32+1: add t shifted32 to the next limb, carry t>>32 onward, add the low/high halves of t*MC to the remaining two limbs with carry), each step an exact division of the current integer plus t*p by2^64. This gives raw T=(ab+m*p)/R0 with0<=m<R0 and T<R0+p for a,b<R0. B.CC consumes the final high carry: the tail keeps T if T<R0 and returns T-p otherwise, always in[0,R0).

Including RET and standalone tail constants, the standalone k_mul costs110 instructions when correcting and104 otherwise (probe short_mul). In the generated kernels the operands are registers: flip_witness.mul_lines takes the k_mul body without its four loads, its MOV of MC and its stores, renames registers (a bijection on fourteen registers; the multiplicand is consumed, the multiplier preserved) and uses x2 for MC and the reserved x0, x2 in the tail; sqr_lines does the same for k_sqr (its CBZ on a separate carry word becomes B.CC on the same carry). The streams therefore execute exactly the instructions of p256_fields.s up to renaming: 95 plus B.CC (multiply) and 75 plus B.CC (square), plus the 4-instruction tail when taken, checked bit-exactly in every probe call.

## 20. Source recovery: the omitted sign and X bit

A request contains the two raw metadata words, the home/candidate/endpoint tags, a zero epoch word and the stored K3'. The source decodes the unique Y index and X-pair J. It regenerates Y=[c+us]P by the already charged generator item. It constructs four finite candidates X_(2J)+Y,X_(2J)-Y,X_(2J+1)+Y,X_(2J+1)-Y, computes each canonical exact T, forms its K3' by the same extraction/insertion as the receiver, and keeps the candidate only when exactly one matches. On a degeneracy or ambiguity it returns an incomplete reply, counted as a loss. For the unique match it sends the actual X, Y, a, b and c+us, and its recovered sign in reply chunk7. Scalar reconstruction and final verification use those values, never an inferred sign at the home.

For a good collision witness, T avoids the33 bad residues. Its original emitted key therefore equals the exact key, including every retained K3' bit, so the true candidate is present. K3' comparison fixes a subset of45 true-fraction bits: positions[100,128) (28),[17,28) (11),[186,192) (6). Discarding its other eight bits only weakens the ambiguity bound. The point-only cell lemma gives B45=(2+66*2^-17)*2^211/(r-1).

There are three wrong candidates per endpoint. A candidate from the other X has an independent uniform a and is uniform even after the witness's own point is fixed. For the same X, the wrong sign is its sibling X-sigmaY. Before conditioning on a cross-node collision, (X,Y)->(X+Y,X-Y) is invertible over the odd prime-order group, so the sibling is uniform and independent of its witness leaf and of the other node's leaf. Thus for any fixed cross-node leaf pair and sign relation, joint collision-and-ambiguity probability is at most the collision probability times B45, with the conservative rho and1+1/r factors covering the nonidentity/fiber conventions. Union over three alternatives at each of two endpoints and over Gamma pairs costs at most6*mu*(1+1/r)*rho*B45. This global union bound precedes capacity/cleanliness conditioning; no claim that the sibling is random after both generators have been revealed is needed. The same argument applies to each prefix with its own mu. Ambiguity loss is about2.24e-12 and is retained exactly, not ignored.

Extra work is bounded per capped request, on every outcome. Four complete canonical affine additions at45056 each and four restoring exact192-bit fractions at8256 each total213248; scalar/control/copy overhead below2^18 leaves total475392. This explicit bound is added to every one of N*REQ_CAP source requests. It covers inspecting four candidates, comparisons, recovered sign and all finite special cases. Requests remain64 bytes and replies remain eight64-byte chunks, so the original four-stage allocations/copy envelope suffices. The extra stored-K3' load and request-word store replace slack in the conservative per-request1024 copy row and per-group phase4 allowance; the executed new request count is checked and remains far below those bounds. No duplicate reply is sent for an ambiguity.

The shortened metadata preserves the original175-bit collision/candidate predicate; it changes only recovery after listing. Source and candidate caps are halved so that36-bit home coordinates and28-bit candidate IDs fit a64-bit request tag. Their revised success losses and worst-case work are recomputed, rather than inherited from the previous caps.
