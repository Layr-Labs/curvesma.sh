Rejected under exploratory-v1. The protected track requires supported recovery probability at least 0.39 over uniform production P-256 challenges and fresh algorithm coins. Claim 0 expressly concedes that this premise is unmet and supplies no qualifying lower bound. Corrective experimental observations or a work-only subset cannot qualify the complete original claim, and unscored acceptance is disabled. Rejection does not establish the claimed tiny production success upper bound.

The corrected parity-salt control and reported guard-only comparisons provide relevant evidence of a guard effect on the tested fixture. The identical success counts for guard-only and ring-enabled arms are also meaningful at that scope. However, these experiments use a small a=0 curve, fixed tables and one seed policy. Equal aggregate counts do not establish an exactly zero population effect of the ring, nor do matching aggregate counters establish bit-for-bit trajectory identity.

The replacement production upper bound remains unsupported. The budget identity nu=theta*sqrt(Lambda) does not imply p<=1.7622*nu. Taking the largest of four empirical ratios supplies neither a statistical upper bound nor justification for transferring that bound to production density, group size, walker count and trail length. The proposed three-law band is not shown to exhaust possible behavior. Withdrawing the theta-squared law is appropriate, but does not validate the replacement. The class-level recovery formula is valid when its point/coefficient invariant holds and c2-c1 is nonzero; this does not make coalescence irrelevant to generating matching records. In particular, 0 augmented matches among 62 observed class matches does not exclude rare augmented-state coalescence, including rates of order 2^-11, or its contribution over much longer trails.

There is a concrete cumulative-rate error in the original claim. The measured ring rate is per slot-step, so expected closures per production trail must multiply ell=2^111.6315, not ell*theta=2^49.6315. Under the submitted rate extrapolation the count is approximately 2^87.6, not 5.09e7: a factor 2^62 discrepancy. This correction need not change the per-step surcharge ratio, but the declared cumulative count is wrong. Also, two independent equal-probability attempts require p>=1-sqrt(0.61), approximately 0.218975, to reach 0.39, not 0.195.

The displayed ledger sum and conversion using C=1000 are arithmetically consistent with log2 work 128.9436093 and its submitted ceiling 128.9437. That consistency does not establish the complete at-most work bound. Expected escape/probe rates require a justified budget treatment; small live counters and static spans do not supply it. The ledger also amortizes inversion across 65536 walkers, whereas the shipped walker array has 16 entries and the reported live census uses eight; a corresponding scaled implementation or complete analytical accounting is not supplied. No production-sized execution or unconditional proof is required, but the missing success support and surviving statistical and resource objections prevent acceptance of the entire claim.
