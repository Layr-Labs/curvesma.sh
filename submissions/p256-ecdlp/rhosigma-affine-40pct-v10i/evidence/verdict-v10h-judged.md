Reject the complete claim under exploratory-v1. The protected objective requires success at least 0.39 over uniform production P-256 challenges and fresh algorithm coins. The submission expressly disclaims that guarantee and any production-length transfer. Its sparse fixture recovery rates are 0.2405–0.2535, with reported CP-95 lower endpoints 0.2294–0.2422. The dense fixture endpoint 0.409822 concerns a different recording configuration that the submission excludes under its production storage cap. Neither these measurements nor their honest disclosure supplies the required production success argument; no supported, fully charged amplification is given.

The computation ledger also lacks a working implementation or adequate analytical accounting for the claimed complete algorithm. In p256_recproc.c, batch_inv inverts pre[n-1], which omits d[n-1], then uses back-substitution indexing inconsistent with that prefix definition. Its pre[64] allocation cannot accommodate the declared 65,536-walker batch. The counted step still uses PROD/SUF placeholders rather than the demonstrated inverse schedule and does not maintain the recovery coefficients. recproc drops occupied buckets without collision matching or solving, and its b-copy reads beyond the declared six-word QREC row. Its 40-instruction count therefore does not establish the price of the declared production record processor. Moreover, fe_inv performs a final correction multiplication after the 256 squarings and 128 conditional multiplications: the stated exact count of 384 fe_mul omits that operation and does not account for exponentiation control work. Recomputing 127.772244 from the ledger's inputs does not validate those inputs or the total bound.

The compressed-storage/replay claim is underspecified at production scale. With 2^16 walkers and approximately 2^127.63 total steps, uninterrupted trails have approximately 2^111.63 positions per walker, not representable by the proposed 32-bit step-index. No bounded-trail/restart and identifier scheme establishes exact reconstruction within the stated layout, and the claimed replay cost of at most 2^-40 of total step work is unsupported.

The claimed diagnosis of the 208 walk mismatches is also not established. A concrete oracle inconsistency exists: the shipped C canonicalizer keeps even Montgomery representatives, whereas the Python point-walk oracle keeps odd representatives. Thus those mismatches do not establish the asserted noncanonical-reduction cause. The isolated field tests and conditional single-table cycle identity are useful limited evidence, but they do not resolve these production correctness, success, storage and accounting objections. The candidate-generated certificate is not certification of the complete claim. These are missing premises and concrete implementation discrepancies, not a demand for a full-scale attack or unconditional proof.
