## 5. Distribution and collision probability

All probability statements refer to the coins of section2 and of Phase 0. Condition on Q. Below the X's are constants: statements hold for every Phase-0 outcome off its abort and off B_rel (PHASE0, Lemma R), and X-dependent quantities are averaged over Phase 0 by Lemma M. Facts: (F1) given all points of a node, its b stays uniform (for each b exactly one tuple of c's gives the chain starts), independently across nodes. (F2) Each Y_(g,k,u) is uniform on the group (c uniform), independent of every other chain. (F3) Pairwise chain uniformity: for u!=v (both below L<r), (Y_u,Y_v) of one chain is uniform on the r(r-1) pairs of distinct points, because (c,s) -> (c+us, c+vs) is a bijection from Z_r x (Z_r\{0}) onto pairs with distinct coordinates; given one point of a chain, any other point of it is uniform on the remaining r-1 points. (F4) Every leaf X_j+-Y is uniform; leaves of different nodes are independent.

Let Gamma be the unordered pairs of leaves from different nodes, |Gamma|=(S^2-N*n0^2)/2, and I_alpha the class-collision indicator of alpha={L1,L1'}; P(I_alpha)=P1=(2(r-1)+1)/r^2 (the singleton identity included) and mu=|Gamma|P1. **Gamma'** is Gamma without the pairs whose two X's are both held by both nodes with j>=j' in pool order. Two nodes of one class hold the same1024 X's, of one slope and different classes none in common, of different slopes32 (PHASE0), so a node pair keeps the fraction1-s(s+1)/(2*MX^2) of its n0^2 leaf pairs (s=1024,0,32): |Gamma'| is the ledger's gam_p and mu'=|Gamma'|P1. I_alpha is a function of the coins of its two chains. Let B_alpha be the pairs sharing a chain with alpha; I_alpha is independent of the whole collection outside B_alpha (disjoint coin sets). A chain lies in2*MX*L leaves, so |B_alpha|<=12(Y_node+MX*L)S, conservatively.

Second moments on Gamma'. Write a leaf as [m]P+[n]Q with m=a+sigma(c+u*s), n=sigma*b, and c~=c+b*x (x=log Q), uniform and independent across chains. For a unit w in{1,-1}, E_alpha(w) is the relation a+sigma1(c~+u s)-w(a'+sigma1'(c~'+u' s'))=0 over Z_r; I_alpha<=sum_w 1[E_alpha(w)]. Let beta in B_alpha, beta!=alpha, both in Gamma'. Case1: beta has a chain outside alpha's two. Condition on everything except that chain's c~, which enters beta's relation with a unit coefficient and not alpha's: P(E_alpha(w1) and E_beta(w2))<=(1/r)(1/r). Case2: beta lies on alpha's two chains (X's, positions and signs may differ). Condition on s, s'; (c~,c~') stays uniform on Z_r^2 and the two relations have coefficient rows (sigma1,-w1 sigma1') and (sigma2,-w2 sigma2'). Rows that are not proportional have rank two: probability1/r^2. Proportional rows (factor kappa=+-1) leave kappa*E_beta-E_alpha: D+sigma1(v-u)s-w1 sigma1'(v'-u')s'=0 with the constant D=kappa(a_j2-w2 a_j2')-(a_j1-w1 a_j1'). If (v,v')!=(u,u'), at most one s' fits each s (or one s, if one difference is zero): P<=(1/r)(1/(r-1)). If (v,v')=(u,u'), beta uses alpha's two Y points and the event is D=0. As a formal identity in the pool's scalars, D=0 holds only for beta=alpha, for the swap (j2,j2')=(j1',j1) with w2=w1, kappa=-w1, or for j1=j1', j2=j2', w1=w2=1 (both say Y=+-Y'); the last two need both X's held by both nodes, and Gamma' contains at most one pair of each such family (a swap partner has j>=j' on one side; pairs with a shared j=j' are dropped). Any other D=0 is a formally nonzero relation among at most four selected X's: B_rel. So off B_rel, E[I_alpha I_beta]<=4/(r-1)^2 for alpha!=beta in Gamma', conservatively bounded by36/(r-1)^2. Random steps are essential: with a shared public step, equal position differences would correlate fully.

The dependency-graph Poisson bound gives Pr(no class collision in Gamma')<=exp(-mu')+b1+b2+P_REL with b3=0 (independence outside B_alpha, above), b1<=|Gamma|*12(Y_node+MX*L)S*P1^2 and b2<=|Gamma|*12(Y_node+MX*L)S*36/(r-1)^2; CS=|Gamma|*12(Y_node+MX*L)S*(P1^2+36/(r-1)^2) (section8 proves the inequality). This is the finite dependency-graph theorem, not a random-walk heuristic.

Given all points, a nonidentity colliding pair has a definite sign w, and n'-w*n=sigma'b'-w*sigma*b vanishes with probability exactly1/r since the two nodes' b stay independent uniform (F1); such pairs are discarded. For a good witness, whose class fraction T avoids the33 label-error residues modulo2^17 (section6), both witness records carry the key floor(T/2^17); every other record with that key, of another class or a third member of the witness class, is a charged intruder if it reaches the witness home between the two witness records (section6). The receiver lists a full-key match only between records of two different source nodes (section12), and the two witness records come from different nodes. Under the no-intruder event every record between them in their hash chain has another key, so the later witness record's walk meets the earlier one as its first full-key match and lists the witness pair itself (while the list has room); that pair is useful by the event above. **Fixed leaves.** Given the witness's two Y points, every leaf through them is fixed: FIXED=4*MX leaves (2*MX per point). Each is+-Z+D for the witness value Z, with D a fixed combination of at most four X's, so sums over them are Lemma M averages (factor F2 at the witness's own node, F4 across the two nodes).

## 6. Defects, capacity losses and success expression

Every computed key of a point Z lies in K(Z)={floor((T(Z)+e)/2^17): e in[-21,12]}, at most two consecutive values (section3); FRACTION_LABEL.md gives the point-only cell bounds. Table lookups, the DT step and folds are exact on every input, so they add no defect. A batch is defective when one of its incidences has x(Y)=x(X), or any chain point of its group up to its round is the identity (the starts C included) or was reached by a step with x(Y_(u-1))=x(S) (Y_(u-1)=+-S): a degenerate step garbles the batched inverse of that chain step and hence every lane of every later round of the group. Per batch d_batch=YB*MX*2/(r-1)+L*YB*3/(r-1)+MX/(r-1) (given X, Y=+-X has probability at most 2/(r-1); given s, each chain point's three degenerate events are three values of c, over at most L*YB points of the group; the last term is kept as slack, a mined X=[a]P with a in[1,r) is never the identity). Excluding every defective batch of the whole run costs N*G*L*d_batch (global term). A defective batch's cost is no larger: every kernel count is data-independent except the correction tails, charged at their worst on defective batches, so its passes complete as bounded garbage; a degenerate chain step garbles the chain state of all 1024 lanes for the rest of the group, and every such later batch is itself counted defective by the definition above.

For the36-bit slot pattern (d1, fraction bits[64,100)), KB(36)=(513/512)*2^220/(r-1), and KBH=(513/512)*2^184/(r-1) for the72-bit home pattern (bits[28,100)), by the interval lemma of FRACTION_LABEL.md. The pair-load term h uses the crude home coefficient2+66*2^-28 (KB72); the175-bit key uses KB(175)=(2+66*2^-17)*2^81/(r-1); fixed cells whose lowest fixed bit is at least28 use2+66*2^-28 (KBC). These concern the fixed set of keys a point can receive, not the selected value. Let rho=(r/(r-1))^4.

**Conditioning and the three kinds of leaves.** Condition on the witness's two Y points (the X's are constants), not on the steps of the witness chains, and never on global cleanliness or actual raw-label choices. (i) The FIXED=4*MX fixed leaves (section5). (ii) The witness-chain leaves: the other WC=4*MX*L leaves of the two witness chains. Each is uniform on r-1 points (F3), but all leaves of one witness chain are functions of its one free step, so no product bound holds among them: they are bounded by first moments (Markov) only. (iii) Free leaves, on all other chains. Free leaves on different chains are independent; on one chain at different positions, (Y_v,Y_w) is uniform on distinct pairs (F3), covariance at most |C|^2/(r^2(r-1)); two leaves through one free Y point (X_j+-Y and X_j'+-Y, or the two of an incidence) are B=+-A+D with D a fixed nonzero combination of their X's, and Lemma M bounds the Phase-0 average of such a pair event, summed over a node's X's, by F2 times its value for independent uniform points.

**Slots.** The witness's hop1 group slot receives the group's records of its node. Free records: mean m_p=2*MX*YB*L*KB36+1 and variance v_p=m_p+1+YB*L*(2*MX)^2*F2*KB36^2 (the last term: ordered pairs through one Y point; the+1 covers the F3 covariances, whose aggregate is below one at the stated sizes). Witness-chain records there: mean at most2*MX*L*rho*KB36. The slot reserves FIXED entries for the fixed leaves and2^20 for the witness chain: d_p=v_p/(CAP_PG-FIXED-2^20-m_p)^2+2*MX*L*rho*KB36/2^20 (Chebyshev, then Markov). The home record array spans all groups: m_h=S*KBH+1, v_h=m_h+1+N*Y_node*(2*MX)^2*F2*KBH^2 and d_h=v_h/(CAP_H-FIXED-2^40-m_h)^2+WC*rho*KBH/2^40, CAP_H=ceil(m_h)+2^41. The witness's group is predetermined by its chain, so the uniform bound over the equal groups applies and no group multiplier is needed. There is only one pair-slot loss, because no second-hop slot exists. The exact script asserts both gaps positive before division; no independent hashing distribution is assumed.

**Pair loads.** Let M=2*KB(175), since a fixed opposite point offers at most two keys. Candidates, requests and relays exist only for listed pairs, and a listed pair always joins records of two different source nodes (section12). Pairs with a fixed or witness-chain leaf: a free or witness-chain leaf is uniform on at least r-1 points given the other node's coins and the witness, so such a pair agrees on a key with probability at most rho*M; two fixed leaves of the two witness nodes differ by a form in at most four X's (Lemma M4: F4). Their expected number is at most e_o=rho*M*((FIXED+WC)(S+FIXED+WC)+FIXED^2*F4)=0.026 (ledger e_o), charged by Markov at an allowance TI=2^20 inside each cap (2^25 at a relay); the witness pair and its swap partner (the only pairs of fixed leaves whose form vanishes formally) use the reserved entries4,8 and108. Pairs of two free leaves have the means
 h=rho*S^2*KB(72)*M (candidates at the witness home),
 g=rho*n0*S*M (pairs at a witness source node; two requests each),
 v=rho*(S/2^36)*S*KB(36)*M (pairs at a relay;18 records each):
the two leaves are functions of the coins of two different nodes, each uniform, so the home pattern and the key event multiply. Same-node pairs are never listed and consume no capacity.

The intruder rate is rho*(S+FIXED*F2)*KB(175): a hash walk lists only at a prior record from another source node agreeing with the new record on all 175 key bits (home, K2 and the masked K3), so only a record other than the two witness records whose computed key equals the witness key, arriving at the home between them, can intervene (section15); every other record is merely visited. A free or witness-chain record's leaf is uniform on at least r-1 points given the witness, whatever its class, so its key set contains the witness key with probability at most rho*KB(175); a fixed leaf is+-Z+D, and Lemma M bounds the sum over the fixed leaves by FIXED*F2 times that value. If no intruder arrives and the capacity events do not occur, the witness pair is listed and resolved. False partial-key matches can exhaust caps; their work and losses are exactly what the pair-load bounds cover.

**Good collisions (D).** Let J_alpha=I_alpha*1[the class fraction T of alpha's class avoids the33 label-error residues modulo2^17], alpha in Gamma'. J_alpha is a function of alpha's two chains, so it is independent of {J_beta: beta outside B_alpha}; E[J_alpha]<=P1 and E[J_alpha*J_beta]<=E[I_alpha*I_beta], so section8 gives Pr(W_good=0)<=exp(-lambda_g)+b1+b2+P_REL for W_good=sum over Gamma' of J_alpha, where lambda_g=sum E[J_alpha]. By the count after the expression, E[J_alpha]>=P1-4*33*2^239/(r-1)^2, so lambda_g>=mu_g=|Gamma'|P1-|Gamma|*4*33*2^239/(r-1)^2 and exp(-lambda_g)<=exp(-mu_g). The run fails only if no good collision exists or some good collision is lost; a bad collision is not counted, so no label-loss row is subtracted. ## 8. Zero-collision probability lemma

Lemma. Let I_alpha be Bernoulli variables with means p_alpha and neighborhoods B_alpha containing alpha, each I_alpha independent of the entire collection outside B_alpha (not merely pairwise). For W=sum I_alpha and lambda=sum p_alpha, |Pr(W=0)-exp(-lambda)|<=b1+b2 with b1=sum_alpha sum_(beta in B_alpha) p_alpha*p_beta and b2=sum_alpha sum_(beta in B_alpha, beta!=alpha) E[I_alpha*I_beta]. It is the zero-event case of the Chen-Stein bound, cf. Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon, Two moments suffice for Poisson approximations (Ann. Probab.17 (1989)), Theorem1, with b3=0; the self-contained proof below does not depend on the citation.

Self-contained proof. For k>=1 let f(k)=integral_0^1 exp(-lambda*t)*t^(k-1) dt and f(0)=f(1). Integration by parts gives lambda*f(k+1)-k*f(k)=-exp(-lambda) for k>=1 and1-exp(-lambda) at k=0, the Stein equation of the zero event; 0<=f<=1 and f decreases for k>=1, so every successive difference is at most1 in absolute value. With W_alpha=sum_(beta outside B_alpha) I_beta, independence gives E[I_alpha*f(W_alpha+1)]=p_alpha*E[f(W_alpha+1)]. Adding and subtracting these terms in E[lambda*f(W+1)-W*f(W)] leaves one difference bounded by p_alpha*E[W-W_alpha]=sum_(beta in B_alpha) p_alpha*p_beta and, on I_alpha=1, one bounded by sum_(beta in B_alpha, beta!=alpha) E[I_alpha*I_beta]. Summing proves the lemma. It applies verbatim to the good-collision indicators J_alpha of section6 (D) and assumes neither Poisson-distributed collisions nor independent leaves in a node.

## 15. Grouped early abort, stopping time and the expected-work ledger

**Groups.** The run is partitioned once, before it starts, into G=1600 groups: group g is the L rounds (batches) of the 1024 chains (g,k), each round one chain step of those chains against all 1024 X's, so every group has exactly 2^20*L incidences and 2^21*L leaves per node. All nodes run group g at the same time, separated by barriers: hop1 of the group's records into fresh per-group slots (CAP_PG=4000000 records, BLOCK_PROTOCOL.md), hop2 into the cumulative home record arrays with the receiver's hash join at arrival, the four resolution stages for the group's new candidates, and the stop test. The stop flag is a latched word set only by a home whose resolver has verified [x]P=Q, broadcast with the barrier release and tested by every node before the next group (5 instructions, phase-control row); a set flag halts the run with that x as output. No group is skipped for any other reason. Points, scalars, coins and caps are those of sections2 to6; only the execution order and the detection pass change.

**Stopping time.** Let T be the first group whose resolution confirms an x (T=G+1 if none). Group g's arithmetic, emission, hop1, hop2 (with the receiver's append and insert) and per-group fixed work are deterministic given g and are executed iff T>g-1. The success event is {T<=G}, and the section6 expression is evaluated for the full run: the chain detection does not depend on group boundaries (every record walks the chain of all its priors at arrival, every accepted record is inserted exactly once, the candidate list is cumulative, and a group's candidates are resolved before its stop test). Thus Pr(T<=G)>=0.9995566604050375361859 at L=66800000 (flip_ledger.py); section17 gates the run.

**Non-stopping bounds.** Pr(T>g-1) is at most the probability that no useful collision among the leaves of groups1..g-1 of every node is resolved by the end of group g-1. The section6 expression re-evaluated for the first g-1 groups (same N, MX, YB, L; CAP_PG, CAP_H and the candidate, request and relay caps fixed at their full-run values; the dependency, variance and global-defect terms kept at their full-run sizes; calc(L,g-1,caph) in flip_ledger.py) lower-bounds that resolution probability: the Chen-Stein bound and every defect, capacity and usefulness row condition only on coins and records of prefix groups, prefix means are at most full means, the fixed caps keep every Chebyshev gap at least its prefix value, prefix second-moment rows use the prefix's own S, h, g, v and T (section6 (M)), and later groups never unlink a prefix record from its chain nor remove a listed pair. ## Short Montgomery multiplication

The scalar multiplication core is adapted from Amazon s2n-bignum commit56152cdb51f47ef9f63b179cda0a8b7518379240, arm/p256/bignum_montmul_p256_alt.S, licensed Apache-2.0 OR ISC OR MIT-0 (see the repository LICENSE). The upstream HOL proof does not certify our patched tails or register transformations.

The core forms the full four-by-four schoolbook product with MUL/UMULH and explicit carries, then reduces the low limbs four times with the cancellation digit t (MC=2^64-2^32+1: add t shifted32 to the next limb, carry t>>32 onward, add the low/high halves of t*MC to the remaining two limbs with carry), each step an exact division of the current integer plus t*p by2^64. This gives raw T=(ab+m*p)/R0 with0<=m<R0 and T<R0+p for a,b<R0. B.CC consumes the final high carry: the tail keeps T if T<R0 and returns T-p otherwise, always in[0,R0).

Including RET and standalone tail constants, the standalone k_mul costs110 instructions when correcting and104 otherwise (probe short_mul). In the generated kernels the operands are registers: flip_witness.mul_lines takes the k_mul body without its four loads, its MOV of MC and its stores, renames registers (a bijection on fourteen registers; the multiplicand is consumed, the multiplier preserved) and uses x2 for MC and the reserved x0, x2 in the tail; sqr_lines does the same for k_sqr (its CBZ on a separate carry word becomes B.CC on the same carry). The streams therefore execute exactly the instructions of p256_fields.s up to renaming: 95 plus B.CC (multiply) and 75 plus B.CC (square), plus the 4-instruction tail when taken, checked bit-exactly in every probe call.

## 20. Source recovery: the omitted sign and X bit

A request contains the two raw metadata words, the home/candidate/endpoint tags, a zero epoch word and the stored K3'. The source decodes the unique Y index and X-pair J. It regenerates Y=R_g+[c+us]P by the already charged generator item. It constructs four finite candidates X_(2J)+Y,X_(2J)-Y,X_(2J+1)+Y,X_(2J+1)-Y, computes each canonical exact T, forms its K3' by the same extraction/insertion as the receiver, and keeps the candidate only when exactly one matches. On a degeneracy or ambiguity it returns an incomplete reply, counted as a loss. For the unique match it sends the actual X, Y, a, b and c+us, and its recovered sign in reply chunk7. Scalar reconstruction and final verification use those values, never an inferred sign at the home.

For a good collision witness, T avoids the33 bad residues. Its original emitted key therefore equals the exact key, including every retained K3' bit, so the true candidate is present. K3' comparison fixes a subset of45 true-fraction bits: positions[100,128) (28),[17,28) (11),[186,192) (6). Discarding its other eight bits only weakens the ambiguity bound. The point-only cell lemma gives B45=(2+66*2^-17)*2^211/(r-1).

There are three wrong candidates per endpoint, all through the witness's Y point: X'+-Y for the pair's other X and the sibling X-sigmaY. Each is+-Z+D for the witness leaf Z, with D=X'-+X or2X fixed, so by Lemma M (M1, M2) the Phase-0 average of the joint collision-and-ambiguity probability, summed over a node's X's, is at most F2 times its value for an independent uniform candidate, which is the collision probability times B45 (rho and1+1/r cover the nonidentity/fiber conventions). Union over three alternatives at each of two endpoints and over Gamma costs at most6*mu*(1+1/r)*rho*F2*B45. This global union bound precedes capacity/cleanliness conditioning. The same argument applies to each prefix with its own mu. Ambiguity loss is about1.36e-7 (ledger id_recovery_loss) and is retained exactly, not ignored.

Extra work is bounded per capped request, on every outcome. Four complete canonical affine additions at45056 each and four restoring exact192-bit fractions at8256 each total213248; scalar/control/copy overhead below2^18 leaves total475392<2^22. This generous2^22 is added to every request a source can serve; over the run there are at most2*N*CAND_CAP of them (section7). It covers inspecting four candidates, comparisons, recovered sign and all finite special cases. Requests remain64 bytes and replies remain eight64-byte chunks, so the original four-stage allocations/copy envelope suffices. The extra stored-K3' load and request-word store replace slack in the conservative per-request1024 copy row and per-group phase4 allowance; the executed new request count is checked and remains far below those bounds. No duplicate reply is sent for an ambiguity.
