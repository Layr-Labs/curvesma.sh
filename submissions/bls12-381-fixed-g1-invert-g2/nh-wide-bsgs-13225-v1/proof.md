# Explicit NH-style almost-universal hashing for wide-routed GT BSGS

This is a classical analytic accounting control for the exact protected profile, not a practical attack or a claim of novelty. The attack uses independent charged random bits for an explicitly sampled almost-universal hash. It uses no auxiliary powers, searched advice, random oracle or heuristic. It returns a G2 point for the supplied G1 point. The claimed bound is W <= 2^132.25 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents, with C=20,000 scalar AArch64 instruction equivalents. Success is at least 0.4 over uniform production challenges and independent fresh hash coins. Peak memory is at most 2^139 bytes; preprocessing is at most 2^132.25 reference equivalents, included in W. Nonuniform advice is absent (the max(1,bytes) reporting convention gives log2=0).

## Algebra and complete algorithm

Use the protected prime order r and field modulus p, and the public generator Q0. The reduced pairing is bilinear and nondegenerate on these prime-order groups. For every allowed P != infinity, g=e(P,Q0) has order r: writing P=bP0 with nonzero b, nondegeneracy gives e(P0,Q0) order r and exponentiation by b preserves order. No computation of b is used. Thus each allowed z has a unique a in [0,r) with z=g^a. This is precisely the input domain, including identity.

## Signed baby table and exact coverage

Set m=2^126, D=2m-1 and k=2^127. Write a GT value as v=e(w^2)+w o(w^2), where e and o have six coefficients in Fp. Let K(v) be the complete tuple of its six even coefficients (in the internal canonical Montgomery encoding below), occupying 36 64-bit words. Keep a sign t in {+1,-1} by lexicographically comparing the odd-coefficient tuple with its negative: t=+1 when the tuple is smaller or equal, otherwise -1. This selects the same canonical orientation for v and its inverse. The zero odd-part tie is identity in GT, and uses t=+1.

The shortened key is exact for the required inverse class, not a fingerprint. As proved below, p^6 Frobenius fixes even coefficients and negates odd ones and equals inversion on GT. Thus v has norm1 to Fp6: e^2-w^2 o^2=1. Two GT elements with the same e have odd parts o1,o2 satisfying o1^2=o2^2. In a field of odd characteristic this forces o1=o2 or o1=-o2, so the two elements are equal or inverse. Conversely inverse elements have identical even coefficients. Therefore K(v)=K(u) exactly when u=v or u=v^-1. The entire six-coefficient key is compared, without a collision assumption. If odd part is zero, norm1 gives v=+1 or -1, and the odd prime-order GT excludes -1.

In this protected polynomial model inversion on GT is inexpensive and exact. The defining polynomial is even and irreducible of degree 12. Thus w^2 has degree 6 over Fp: w^2 satisfies Y^6-2Y^3+2, so its degree is at most 6, while adjoining its square root w has degree at most twice that degree and actually has degree 12; equality forces degree 6. The p^6 Frobenius fixes w^2 and sends w to -w, since w is not in Fp6. It therefore negates exactly the odd coefficients. Since GT has order r and embedding degree 12, p^6=-1 modulo r (order 12 gives a nontrivial element of order 2 modulo the prime r). Hence v^(p^6)=inv(v) on GT. Computing the negative odd tuple costs at most six modular negations; selecting the orientation and extracting K uses at most 36 word comparisons plus local copies. Charge 2^14 scalar instructions per canonicalization, including all local loads, stores and control.

Generate the m records (K(g^j),j,s_j) for 0<=j<m, where s_j is the canonicalization sign. Generate g^j sequentially, starting from 1, and insert the records into the universal-hash table specified below. These keys are distinct by the norm argument: equality implies j=+j' or j=-j' modulo r, and 0<=j,j'<m, 2m-2<r forces j=j'. The key, sign, exponent and chain link fit in a 512-byte record. Full-key equality is used throughout; no probabilistic fingerprint equality is used.

Compute h=g^(-D) by binary powering modulo r. Set v=z. For i=0,...,k-1, compute K(v) and orientation t, and look up that key using the hash bucket and a full-key chain scan specified below. A hit at baby record (j,s_j) gives epsilon=t*s_j and a=iD+epsilon*j modulo r. Reconstruct the canonical residue a by one conditional addition of r if the signed value is negative; positive values are less than r. Return Q=[a]Q0 only after checking the exact e(P,Q)=z. If no hit is found, replace v by v*h and continue. At loop end report failure. All loops, failed challenges and routing-budget aborts are charged. The charged routing/hash work is metered as specified below, with no free restart or amplification.

A hit is equivalent to a=iD+j or a=iD-j modulo r for some 0<=j<m. Each giant step therefore covers the consecutive integer interval [iD-(m-1),iD+(m-1)] of exactly D=2m-1 elements. Adjacent intervals are disjoint and touch consecutively, so all k steps cover exactly kD=2^254-2^127 consecutive integers starting at -(m-1). Their residues modulo r remain distinct because kD<2^254<r. Thus the exact algebraic coverage probability for uniform z before hash-budget aborts is (2^254-2^127)/r for every fixed valid P. It is greater than 1/2: r<(15/16)*2^255 from its leading hexadecimal digits (0x73... < 0x78...), while kD>(15/16)*2^254 because 2^127<2^250. This is an exact counting argument, not an independence heuristic. The final success lower bound accounts additionally for routing/hash budget aborts below. The input P remains the supplied point. Identity z gives j=i=0 and Q=infinity. Every output is correct; uncovered inputs consume the bounded unsuccessful search and report failure.

## Explicit conservative scalar arithmetic

Use explicit Montgomery arithmetic instead of bitserial multiplication. Set B0=2^32, n=12 and R=B0^12=2^384. Encode each coefficient c as the canonical integer cR mod p in [0,p), stored in twelve 32-bit limbs packed in six 64-bit words. The encoding is injective. The projected table keys are complete even-coefficient internal encodings; external input/output stays in the exact protected encoding. Addition and negation still cost <=256 scalar instructions with six 64-bit words. Negating odd internal coefficients gives the same Frobenius map, because the Montgomery encoding is Fp-linear. Lexicographic order only chooses an inverse representative and need not be the external ordering.

Here is a full multiplication algorithm. Compute the ordinary product of a,b<p in a zero-initialized 26-limb array T. For row i=0..11 initialize carry=0; for j=0..11 set t=a_i*b_j+T_(i+j)+carry, store t mod B0 and carry=floor(t/B0); store row-end carry in T_(i+12). All factors, array limbs and carry are <=B0-1, so t<=B0^2-1 fits 64 bits. A 64-bit multiplication of 32-bit operands, adds, mask/shift, local limb loads/stores and loop/address control suffice. Charge 32 instructions per iteration, or 144*32 for the product loops.

Let n0prime=-p_0^(-1) mod B0. For i=0..11 set q=(T_i*n0prime) mod B0. With carry=0, for j=0..11 compute t=q*p_j+T_(i+j)+carry, storing low 32 bits and carrying high 32 bits. Each t again fits 64 bits. Add the final carry at i+12 and propagate it until zero through the 26-limb array. Bound propagation by 26 updates per row, 312 in total, each costing at most 16 instructions. Charge 32 per inner multiply-add (144 iterations) and 16 per q setup (12). The low limb cancels since q*p_0+T_i=0 modulo B0, and previous low limbs remain zero. After all rounds, discard the twelve low zero limbs. This gives u=(ab+Mp)/R for 0<=M<R, with u<2p since ab<p^2 and p<R. One conditional subtraction yields the Montgomery product in [0,p). All intermediate values fit 26 limbs: ab+Mp<R^2+Rp<2R^2, far below 2^832. Final u fits twelve limbs since 2p<2^382.

Charge 512 instructions for final subtraction/selection and 4096 for all row setup/end stores, zeroing, packing/unpacking, calls, local addressing and control not included above. Total <=2*144*32+312*16+12*16+512+4096=19008<2^15 instructions per Fp multiplication. These are scalar 64-bit operations on 32-bit limbs, not an arbitrary-width product or hidden 128-bit primitive. All sources and destinations are bounded local workspace.

Fp12 multiplication uses schoolbook convolution (144 Fp products and 144 additions), followed by reduction of degrees 22 down to 12 with w^12=2w^6-2, at most 66 additional additions/subtractions. Its cost is <144*2^15+(144+66)*256+2^20<2^23 instructions, including an extra 2^20 for polynomial loops, copies and local addressing. This is an actual implementation bound divided by the unchanged C, not a new reference price.

Convert a decoded coefficient c into internal form by Montgomery multiplication with R^2 mod p; convert back by Montgomery multiplication with ordinary integer 1. Those operations accept any canonical operands <p. The fixed constants can be generated by modular powering/Euclid within the existing 2^64-instruction wrapper budget. Conversion is only for fixed inputs, output and constants, not per table operation. The pinned pairing formulas are evaluated over the isomorphic internal field and preserve their exact mathematical outputs. All equality/hash claims concern complete exact inverse-class keys over injective internal coefficient encodings. There is no free conversion oracle.

## Explicit almost-universal hashing and shorter routed records

Use B=2^128 buckets and m=2^126 fixed record slots. A bucket entry holds an empty marker or a 128-bit record index in 32 bytes. A record has 36 key words, a two-word exponent, one sign word and a two-word next index: 41 meaningful 64-bit words, padded to 512 bytes. Padding is initialized locally but is never transmitted or compared. Every comparison uses the full exact K key. Each baby j has slot j. Head insertion reads the old bucket head, writes the record with next=old head and writes the new head: three memory accesses, with no duplicate search because baby keys are distinct.

Use an explicit NH-style almost-universal family on the fixed2304-bit exact K key, then independent binary compression. Inspiration/attribution only: the keyed32-bit product-sum approach of NH in RFC4418 section5.2.2 (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4418#section-5.2.2). No UMAC authentication theorem, AES/PRF, nonce or random-oracle premise is used. The exact family and a conservative collision proof are supplied here.

Split K into72 ordered32-bit words x0,...,x71. An arm has72 independent uniform32-bit keys k0,...,k71 and computes N_k(x)=sum_i (((x_(2i)+k_(2i)) mod2^32)*((x_(2i+1)+k_(2i+1)) mod2^32)) mod2^64, i=0..35. For any fixed distinct x,y, choose a differing word in some pair. Condition all keys except the partner word's key t; the keyed value on the differing coordinate is A versus A', with nonzero integer delta=A-A' and |delta|<2^32. As t ranges over [0,2^32), the two partner sums have at most two wrap breakpoints, dividing its domain into at most three intervals. On each interval the difference of the complete arm sums is delta*t+constant modulo2^64. Two distinct t in an interval cannot both be roots, since their nonzero product delta*(t1-t2) has absolute value below2^64. At most three t cause collision, so Pr[N_k(x)=N_k(y)]<=3/2^32. If the differing coordinate is second in its pair, exchange roles. All other pairs contribute a conditioned constant; this proof needs no message-distribution assumption.
