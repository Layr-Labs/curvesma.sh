## Exactly the independence needed by the finite collision proof

For any four distinct field inputs, the coefficient-to-output matrix of a cubic is Vandermonde with nonzero determinant. Thus any <=4 distinct values V(j) are independent uniform Fr, and likewise for Jv, independently between the two families. It is NOT claimed that all2W values are jointly independent. Any term in the first/second collision moments involves at most four distinct j and four distinct k, so this limited independence supplies exactly the joint law those terms use.

Similarly t<L<q are distinct field inputs. Any <=4 F(t) values are independent uniform Fq, as are G(t), with the two output families independent. Truncation is accounted for, not called uniform: residues modulo W have 2^35 preimages except index W-1, which has 2^35-1. Define

 a_min=(q-W+1)/q, a_max=(q+1)/q.

Relative to a uniform92-bit index, each probability ratio lies between a_min and a_max. For d<=4 distinct sample times the JOINT law of their two indices is between a_min^(2d) and a_max^(2d) times the ideal independent uniform index law. This is a pointwise finite-density statement, not a union over L approximate samples. It avoids an invalid L-times-total-variation argument.

For fixed input z=h^a with a!=0, let A_j=1+a*v_j and B_k=a*w_k. Each relevant <=4-value marginal is independent uniform within its labeled Fr family, with the families independent. A sampled raw exponent is x_t=A_j+B_k. Distinct parameter pairs (j,k) yield independent uniform raw pairs, even when a single table index is shared. All moment statements below follow from these marginals; no walk, Poisson or mixing premise occurs.

Let C count useful time pairs with distinct parameter pairs, x_s!=0 and x_t=alpha*x_s for alpha among the eleven nonidentity p-powers. With B_i=binom(L,i), define

 mu=B_2*(1-W^-2)*11*(r-1)/r^2,
 U2=3*11^2*[ B_3*(r^-2+(r*W^2)^-1)
              +B_4*(r^-2+4/(r*W^4)) ].

For ideal independent uniform selectors the material source's finite argument gives E C=mu and E binom(C,2)<=U2. The full argument is recalled here: expand each equality as a nonzero linear row in its labeled A/B variables. Coefficients are1,-alpha or1-alpha and are nonzero because alpha!=1. Independent rows have probability1/r^2; dependent rows have probability at most1/r. Dependence requires the same labeled support in BOTH table families. Two edges sharing one time then require the two other parameter pairs to coincide, probability1/W^2. Two disjoint edges have at most four ordered support matches, probability<=4/W^4. There are three two-edge shapes on three times and three matchings on four times, and 11^2 phase choices. Counting these necessary conditions can only overestimate the dependent-row contribution. The linear laws need <=4 values in each table family and therefore remain exact for the cubic tables.

Now apply the selector density bounds to each nonnegative term: E C>=a_min^4*mu and E binom(C,2)<=a_max^8*U2. Pointwise 1[C>0]>=C-binom(C,2), so useful-collision probability is at least a_min^4*mu-a_max^8*U2. This is an exact finite moment bound for the actual finite seed family.

## Exact twelve-orbit key on the native full field

All field arithmetic here uses the organizer-pinned tower Fp2=Fp[u]/(u^2+1), Fp6=Fp2[v]/(v^3-1-u), Fp12=Fp6[w]/(w^2-v). The native Montgomery scale is fixed throughout. Express raw X=a0+a1*w^2+a2*w^4+b0*w+b1*w^3+b2*w^5 with Fp2 coefficients. The order of p modulo r is12. Summing X,X^(p^4),X^(p^8) gives c=3(a0+b1*w^3); their product is1 and their second elementary symmetric sum is c^(p^2). They are the roots of Y^3-cY^2+c^(p^2)Y-1. Thus the raw trace determines exactly the three p^4-orbit elements. This is the cubic XTR mechanism, whose material use is credited through e750ade2; the primary construction is Lenstra-Verheul, CRYPTO2000: https://www.cs.ru.nl/E.Verheul/papers/crypto2000/crypto2000.pdf .

Copy a0 (raw bytes0..95) and b1 (raw bytes384..479) to represent c/3 without a field division. The p^2-conjugate trace negates b1 and fixes a0. Canonicalize the pair b1,-b1 by unsigned high-limb-first comparison of its two native Fp coefficients. Choose the first nonzero coefficient x and negate BOTH components iff x>=ceil(p/2); zero components map to0. A nonidentity GT point cannot have b1=0: its cubic would factor over Fp2 as (Y-1)*(Y^2+(1-c)Y+1), placing a nonidentity root in Fp4, contrary to embedding degree12. Therefore the six-key F(X)=(a0,canon_pm(b1)) has exactly the six even Frobenius fibers. Identity is detected by b1=0 and aborts, as charged above.

To add the odd class, compute the actual constant C=(1+u)^((p-1)/2) cold in Fp2. It is the multiplier for p-Frobenius on w^3 and obeys C^2=-u. Since p mod8=3,2 is a nonsquare. Writing C=(a,b) forces a=b=s and2s^2=-1; the a=-b alternative would make2 a square. Use the sign obtained by actual exponentiation, not a free square-root selection. For canonical B=Br+Bi*u, C*conj(B)=(s*(Br+Bi),s*(Br-Bi)). Compute these with two complete Fp add/sub operations and TWO full native Montgomery products. Conjugate a0 by negating its imaginary coefficient and normalize the new B under sign. The result is F(X^p), irrespective of the earlier choice between B and -B.

Set S(X) to the full192-byte minimum of F(X) and F(X^p). The classes are distinct for nonidentity X, since otherwise the odd p-power would lie in its even orbit, contradicting order12. Applying p swaps the classes; applying p^2 preserves each. Equal S thus identifies exactly the full twelve-element Frobenius orbit, with no extra fibers, subgroup-size reduction, truncated equality or raw-point reconstruction. All eleven phase candidates are verified as specified above.

The accompanying project_six381.S supplies the entire projection and sign helper, not merely an interface claim. Its helper has97 static instructions over both branch bodies and its wrapper48; with the outer caller BL this is at most146. Retain192 including all interfaces. The helper uses only x0..x17, preserves x18/LR, handles zeros, and supports exact in-place or disjoint B buffers. The wrapper preserves x19/LR and initializes every key byte before identity return.
