# Deterministic FAPI-1 via explicitly charged polynomial BSGS

This is a classical analytic upper bound, not a practical pairing break, a timing measurement, or a claim of novelty. The declared total work is at most 2^137 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents, with success probability greater than one half. The sole score is the rational integer 137. Peak storage is below 2^145 bytes. Preprocessing is below 2^137 reference equivalents and is included in total work. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes. The enormous table is part of the mathematical attack and is not required on the reviewing machine: the rules expressly allow analytical instruction bounds without production-size execution.

## Supplied-point reduction and complete algorithm

Use exactly the protected BLS12-381 profile, prime subgroup order r, standard Q0 and supplied nonidentity P. Decode all inputs canonically and perform the prescribed membership checks. Compute h=e(P,Q0) with the pinned pairing convention, i.e. pairing(Q0,P). Nondegeneracy and prime order imply h has order r for every allowed P. Never substitute a solver-chosen P. Write z=h^a for the unique a in [0,r). The objective is the G2 point [a]Q0, not the exponent alone.

Set m=2^127. Make m records (h^j,j), j=0,...,m-1, including the identity, constructed by successive multiplication by h. Keep all twelve canonical polynomial coefficients (in the Montgomery encoding described below). Sort by their full representation using bottom-up mergesort and a second array. Compute d=h^(-m). Set v=z. For i=0,...,m-1, binary-search v in the sorted baby array. If it matches j, set a=i*m+j, verify h^a=z, reconstruct Q=[a]Q0, verify e(P,Q)=z and return the canonical G2 point. Otherwise update v by d before the next search. If no match exists after m searches, return infinity; this is a failed inversion for these targets. The identity target returns infinity immediately and succeeds. Input subgroup checks and final output handling remain charged.

The protected order satisfies 2^254<r<2^255. Thus every tested exponent i*m+j lies in [0,2^254), with no wraparound modulo r and no duplicate candidates. The algorithm succeeds exactly when the unique actual exponent a is in this prefix. In that case a=i*m+j by Euclidean division with both i,j<m, so the correct search position occurs. Conversely, if a>=2^254, no comparison can succeed. Returning infinity on failure cannot accidentally satisfy such a nonidentity target. For every allowed supplied P the exponent of independently uniform z relative to h is uniform on [0,r). Hence success probability is exactly 2^254/r>1/2>0.39. This is a rigorous distributional probability, not a heuristic or reviewer-confidence score. The value is approximately 0.552065, but only the exact inequality is needed. No random algorithm coins or amplification are used.

The attack intentionally searches a fixed public exponent interval. Its distributional guarantee applies to the independently uniform target specified in the protected profile. It makes no guarantee for an adversarially selected target outside the prefix. Neither P nor z is modified to improve the distribution. All failures consume their full search budget, which is included in the worst-case total below. No cost is divided by the success probability or amortized over targets.

## Six-limb arithmetic schedule

Represent Fp residues by six radix-b=2^64 limbs, with R=b^6=2^384. Montgomery representation is xR mod p, always canonical in [0,p). Compute n0=-p[0]^-1 mod b and R mod p, R^2 mod p by charged elementary arithmetic during setup. Convert into Montgomery representation with a product by R^2 mod p, and out with a product by ordinary one. Equality remains exact and canonical. No nonuniform table is searched or embedded.

For modular addition, retain the seventh carry limb after the six-limb sum, subtract p including that carry, and select the canonical result. For subtraction, add p back conditionally after the borrow pass. At most eight passes over at most eight positions, at sixteen scalar instructions per position, plus fewer than 1024 initialization/control instructions fit below 2^11 instructions per addition, subtraction, comparison or field copy. The actual three-pass arithmetic has substantial slack inside this bound. It includes arithmetic, flags, logical selects, loads, stores and address increments. All temporary carries remain explicit until canonical reduction.

To multiply, form the full twelve-limb product in a fourteen-limb temporary initialized to zero. Use six schoolbook rows, each with six multiply-accumulates and a bounded carry propagation into the remaining higher limbs. A cell forms a_i*b_j+T_k+carry. This can be as large as b^2-1 and therefore requires both halves of a 128-bit intermediate. On scalar AArch64 use MUL and UMULH for the two halves, then ADDS/ADC to add T_k and carry, propagating each low-half carry into the high half. A small fixed instruction sequence realizes this exact two-word value. Both multiplication instructions, the carry additions, all loads/stores, masks, branches and address work are included in a 64-instruction cell allowance. No unit-cost 128-bit multiply is assumed. The high sum does not overflow because the whole mathematical value is at most b^2-1.

For Montgomery reduction, for i=0,...,5 compute q=T_i*n0 mod b using the low-half multiply and add q*p*b^i with six multiply-accumulates and carry propagation. The low limb becomes zero. After six iterations discard the six zero limbs, retaining all upper temporary limbs until the final subtraction. Because original T<p^2 and the added multiple is below Rp, U=T/R after the cancellations is below p^2/R+p<2p. Subtract p once conditionally for the canonical Montgomery product. This proves correctness and the single-subtraction bound. Do not truncate an outstanding high carry.

There are 72 multiply-accumulate cells across product and reduction. At 64 instructions each they cost 4608 instructions. Carry propagation has at most twelve rows times fourteen positions; at 32 instructions per position it costs 5376 instructions. Allocate another 4096 instructions to temporary initialization, row setup, six q calculations, final subtraction/selection, call/return and other fixed overhead. Total is 14080<16384=2^14 scalar instructions per base-field product. This intentionally allows scanning every remaining carry limb after every row. No data-dependent carry can exceed fourteen positions. Squaring uses the same product. Nonzero inversion by exponent p-2 needs fewer than 768 such products plus loop work and is far below the deliberately loose setup allowances retained below.

## Dense polynomial extension arithmetic

Keep the protected basis Fp[w]/(w^12-2*w^6+2), with twelve canonical Montgomery coefficients. A dense product uses 144 base-field products, at most 144 accumulator additions, and descending reductions for degrees 22 down to 12 using w^k=2*w^(k-6)-2*w^(k-12). Doubling a coefficient is modular addition. Fewer than 1024 base additions, copies, accumulator operations and coefficient initializations cover all lower-order arithmetic and transfers. Base products cost 2^14 each; lower-order field operations cost 2^11 each. Their subtotal is 144*2^14+1024*2^11=4456448<2^23 instructions. Bound all extension-level loop control, coefficient offsets and scalar-counter bookkeeping together by another 2^20 instructions: fewer than 2^16 such elementary bookkeeping actions, each taking at most sixteen scalar instructions, suffice for the fixed convolution and reduction loops. The complete BSGS product/step therefore costs at most 4456448+1048576=5505024<2^23 instructions. Charge 2^23 per step, leaving more than two million additional instructions of margin. Table record transfers are charged separately.

No inversion occurs in the repeated multiplication schedule. Nonzero Fp12 inversion can use exponent p^12-2 with fewer than 9216 products; it is needed only a fixed number of times and fits well below the setup's conservative 2^58 operation allowance. Exact equality uses canonical coefficient words. No sparse-product calibration, Frobenius shortcut, probabilistic hash or number-theoretic heuristic is assumed. Montgomery conversion changes coefficient encoding only, not the pairing or extension-field polynomial.

## Records, sorting, lookup and address work

Each field coefficient has six 64-bit words (48 bytes); a twelve-coefficient GT element occupies 576 bytes. A 256-bit index takes another 32 bytes. Tags, alignment and logical metadata fit within a 1024-byte record. Comparisons use a consistent unsigned lexicographic ordering of all 72 coefficient words, independently of the exponent field, with exact equality. Each complete record comparison or copy, including all index/address work, control and transfers, is charged 2^12 instructions. Scan the entire 1024-byte record as at most 128 words. Sixteen scalar instructions per word suffice for loads/stores or compares, three-word logical address updates, loop control and branches, totaling at most 2048 instructions. Allow another 2048 for fixed setup, selection, scalar-index comparisons and return; actual comparison scans only 72 key words. Use the strict upper bound 4096 per record operation. These actions require no multiply or hidden large-integer operation: an address increment uses a low-word addition and at most two carry updates. Thus the sixteen-instruction loop budget has margin over a load/store copy or a pair of loads plus comparison and branch. This cost includes multiword arithmetic on logical addresses.

The table is theoretical large storage, as in earlier reviewed BSGS controls. Logical addresses use three 64-bit words; never truncate them to a single machine pointer. The model charges logical memory accesses and transfers, not physical interconnect construction or latency. No physically available memory of this size is claimed. The machine running review only checks an analytical claim; it need not allocate the attack's table.

At m=2^127, bottom-up mergesort has 127 passes, each with at most m comparisons and 2m copies, allowing transfers between arrays. Binary search takes at most 128 comparisons for each of m giants. Allow another m record operations for initialization and residual bookkeeping. The total is at most 510*m<2^136 record operations. Charged at 2^12 instructions each, this is strictly below 2^148 instructions. Extra fixed operations, including integer constants, are absorbed by the large setup allowance; per-step arithmetic is separately charged. No sorting, lookup or record initialization is omitted, and no expected constant-time hashing is used.

## Setup, output and exact pairing

Allocate a separate 2^90-instruction allowance for all fixed setup and finish work: canonical parsing, subgroup checks, sqrt(r), public-constant loading, the two pinned pairings, inverses, exponentiations for the stride and verification, G2 scalar multiplication, encoding and final comparison. This allowance is not amortized across targets. The following elementary bounds explain its adequacy rather than treating a pairing as free.

A subgroup check or scalar multiplication needs fewer than 512 complete point operations. Affine G1 formulas use base arithmetic and inversion. For Fp2, (a+bu)^-1=(a-bu)/(a^2+b^2), u^2=-1; multiplication uses four base products and a bounded number of sums. Complete G2 addition/doubling uses fewer than 1024 base multiplications/additions and inversions, so even charging the inversion bound 2^41 to every one gives below 2^52 instructions per point step. Infinity, equal points, inverse pairs and y=0 are checked before division. Fewer than 2^12 such steps cover membership checks, reconstruction and other point work, well below 2^90.

Compute the exact protected optimal-ate map with the pinned 64-bit loop count and final exponent (p^12-1)/r. A Miller iteration contains bounded line-function arithmetic and point updates, each expressible with fewer than 2^10 extension operations even using affine coordinates. For inverses, exponentiate to p^12-2 as above; thus one extension arithmetic operation, generously including an inverse, costs below 2^58 instructions. The loop, exceptional cases and exponentiation to the less-than-4608-bit final exponent fit below 2^20 such operations per pairing. This gives below 2^78 instructions per pairing. Two pairings, input checks, output and all remaining fixed exponents fit far below 2^90. This uses the supplied P in both pairings. No alternative pairing or unnormalized Miller value is substituted.

## Total and supplementary quantities

At most m baby and m giant products occur: at most 2^128 complete steps. At 2^23 instructions each they cost at most 2^151 instructions. Record sorting, lookup and transfers cost less than 2^148 instructions. The separate 2^90 setup/finish allowance covers representation conversion, constants, input and subgroup checks, both pairings, inverses, output and independent verification. Total work is strictly below 2^151+2^148+2^90 scalar instructions. Dividing by 2^137 gives fewer than 16384+2048+2^-47=18432+2^-47 instructions per unit of 2^137. Since this is less than fixed C=20000, the entire normalized work is below 2^137 reference equivalents. Declare upper-bound score 137 unchanged. This is an analytical bound, not an exact measured runtime.

Preprocessing is included as a subset of total work and below 2^137 equivalents. Failed searches are charged in full without amortization or retries. Two arrays of 2^127 records of 1024 bytes occupy at most 2^138 bytes; peak below 2^145 bytes includes code, inputs, constants, outputs, metadata and arithmetic scratch. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes. No random coins, auxiliary powers or parallel elapsed-time discount are used. Success remains exactly 2^254/r>1/2 by the preceding prefix proof. Memory remains supplementary only.

Its broad score of 180 is a comparator, not a score inherited as a qualification. Standard group laws and the protected polynomial representation suffice for correctness.

This package intentionally declares no executable experiment. The proof is finite arithmetic accounting and exact group algebra; toy cyclic-group success would not establish the production bound.
