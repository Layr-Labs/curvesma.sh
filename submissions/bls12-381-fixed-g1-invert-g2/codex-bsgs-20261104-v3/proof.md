# Deterministic FAPI-1 via explicitly charged polynomial BSGS

This is a classical analytic upper bound, not a practical pairing break, a timing measurement, or a claim of novelty. The declared total work is at most 2^140 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents, with success probability greater than one half. The sole score is the rational integer 140. Peak storage is below 2^145 bytes. Preprocessing is below 2^140 reference equivalents and is included in total work. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes. The enormous table is part of the mathematical attack and is not required on the reviewing machine: the rules expressly allow analytical instruction bounds without production-size execution.

## Supplied-point reduction and complete algorithm

Use exactly the protected BLS12-381 profile, prime subgroup order r, standard Q0 and supplied nonidentity P. Decode all inputs canonically and perform the prescribed membership checks. Compute h=e(P,Q0) with the pinned pairing convention, i.e. pairing(Q0,P). Nondegeneracy and prime order imply h has order r for every allowed P. Never substitute a solver-chosen P. Write z=h^a for the unique a in [0,r). The objective is the G2 point [a]Q0, not the exponent alone.

Set m=2^127. Make m records (h^j,j), j=0,...,m-1, including the identity, constructed by successive multiplication by h. Keep all twelve canonical polynomial coefficients (in the Montgomery encoding described below). Sort by their full representation using bottom-up mergesort and a second array. Compute d=h^(-m). Set v=z. For i=0,...,m-1, binary-search v in the sorted baby array. If it matches j, set a=i*m+j, verify h^a=z, reconstruct Q=[a]Q0, verify e(P,Q)=z and return the canonical G2 point. Otherwise update v by d before the next search. If no match exists after m searches, return infinity; this is a failed inversion for these targets. The identity target returns infinity immediately and succeeds. Input subgroup checks and final output handling remain charged.

The protected order satisfies 2^254<r<2^255. Thus every tested exponent i*m+j lies in [0,2^254), with no wraparound modulo r and no duplicate candidates. The algorithm succeeds exactly when the unique actual exponent a is in this prefix. In that case a=i*m+j by Euclidean division with both i,j<m, so the correct search position occurs. Conversely, if a>=2^254, no comparison can succeed. Returning infinity on failure cannot accidentally satisfy such a nonidentity target. For every allowed supplied P the exponent of independently uniform z relative to h is uniform on [0,r). Hence success probability is exactly 2^254/r>1/2>0.39. This is a rigorous distributional probability, not a heuristic or reviewer-confidence score. The value is approximately 0.552065, but only the exact inequality is needed. No random algorithm coins or amplification are used.

The attack intentionally searches a fixed public exponent interval. Its distributional guarantee applies to the independently uniform target specified in the protected profile. It makes no guarantee for an adversarially selected target outside the prefix. Neither P nor z is modified to improve the distribution. All failures consume their full search budget, which is included in the worst-case total below. No cost is divided by the success probability or amortized over targets.

## Limb arithmetic schedule

This revision uses twelve radix-b=2^32 limbs for Fp residues, each stored in one 64-bit register or word, with b^12=R=2^384. Residues are kept in Montgomery representation xR mod p. All limbs are in [0,b). p is odd. Compute n0=-p[0]^-1 mod b by elementary Euclid or Newton iteration during charged setup. Compute R mod p and R^2 mod p by repeated modular doubling, also during setup. Conversion into Montgomery form uses a Montgomery product by R^2 mod p; conversion out uses a product by ordinary one. Equality is exact without conversion because the canonical Montgomery representative is unique.

A modular addition performs a twelve-limb carry pass, subtracts p including the high carry, and selects the canonical result. Subtraction uses a borrow pass and conditional addition of p. Limb values remain below b; carries and borrows occupy bounded words. Fewer than sixteen passes of at most fourteen words, at 16 scalar instructions per word, plus initialization/control, fit within 2^12 instructions. This also bounds field comparison and field copy. Instruction allowances count masking, shifts, loads/stores, address increments and control. The high sum limb is retained until conditional reduction; overflow is never silently dropped.

A Montgomery product can be specified without relying on a special instruction kernel. First form the full 24-limb schoolbook product T of two canonical residues. Initialize a 26-limb temporary array to zero. Each row multiplies a single limb by twelve limbs, accumulates with carry, and propagates its final carry into remaining higher limbs. For a multiply-accumulate, a_i*b_j + T_k + carry is at most (b-1)^2+2*(b-1)=b^2-1, so an unsigned 64-bit intermediate suffices. Extract its low 32 bits and carry by shifts/masks. No unit-cost 384-bit operation occurs.

Then for i=0,...,11 set q=(T_i*n0) mod b. Add q*p*b^i to T with the same twelve-limb multiply-accumulate schedule, propagating the carry into all remaining higher limbs as needed. The new limb i is zero by construction. Discard the first twelve zero limbs after all iterations. Since original T<p^2 and the added multiple is less than R*p, the quotient U after division by R satisfies U<p^2/R+p<2p. Thus one conditional subtraction of p gives the unique canonical Montgomery result. Keep extra limbs through this subtraction rather than truncating a possible high carry. The output is x*y*R^-1 mod p. This is the usual exact Montgomery reduction, here accompanied by finite loop bounds.

For accounting, the product and reduction have 288 inner multiply-accumulates. Charge 64 instructions per cell, including load/store and control; that is 18432 instructions. Carry propagation is bounded by 24 rows times 26 positions: charge 32 instructions per position, adding 19968 instructions. Zeroing, final conditional subtraction, row setup, masks, index arithmetic, call and return fit within another 2^13 instructions. The sum is below 2^16 instructions per base-field product, including all memory transfers of arithmetic temporaries. No carry chain can traverse more than the explicitly allocated 26 limbs. All temporary addresses here are small. Squaring uses this same product. Canonical Montgomery addition and subtraction still use the ordinary modular-add schedule. Inversion uses exponentiation to p-2, fewer than 768 products and loop work, below 2^27 instructions, although the larger setup bounds below remain valid.

## Dense polynomial extension arithmetic

Represent a GT element by twelve canonical Montgomery coefficients in Fp[w]/(w^12-2*w^6+2). This keeps the protected polynomial basis. A dense multiplication uses 144 base products to form coefficients 0 through 22, and at most 144 accumulator additions. Reduce degrees 22 down to 12 using w^k=2*w^(k-6)-2*w^(k-12). Multiplication by two is modular addition. Descending reduction correctly handles higher-degree terms created by earlier reductions. Fewer than 1024 base additions, copies and coefficient operations cover all accumulation, reductions, initialization and output. Charge 144*2^16+1024*2^12 <2^24 instructions for these base operations. Allowing loop and indexing overhead, a complete dense product is below 2^25 instructions. The 2^25-instruction dense-product bound also covers scalar-counter updates and outer loop control: the base-operation subtotal is below 2^24, while fewer than 2^16 loop/address operations at fewer than 16 instructions each add below 2^20, leaving over 2^23 instructions of margin before 2^25. Charge 2^25 instructions per complete BSGS product/loop step. Record copying itself is separately charged below. Neither a reference multiplication nor the internal base products is charged twice.

Extension equality is exact coefficientwise comparison of canonical Montgomery values. An inverse can use exponentiation to p^12-2, fewer than 9216 extension products. The resulting cost is far below the conservative 2^58 allowance used in setup. No Frobenius trick, sparse multiplication price, ideal hash function or number-theoretic heuristic is needed. Input and output basis are unchanged; the only representation change is Montgomery encoding of coefficients, whose conversion is included in setup and whose records are canonical and injective.

## Records, sorting, lookup and address work

Each field coefficient occupies twelve 64-bit words, or 96 bytes. Twelve coefficients occupy 1152 bytes. Add a 256-bit index, tags, alignment and logical metadata; a record fits in 2048 bytes. Full comparisons scan all 144 coefficient words and compare with unsigned word order; they need not decode the numerical coefficient to define a consistent total order. Exact equality is preserved. Index fields do not participate in key comparison. Record comparisons and copies are charged 2^14 instructions apiece, including control and all logical transfers. Even scanning the entire 2048-byte record as 256 words at 32 instructions per word uses only 8192 instructions, leaving a factor two for fixed overhead. Copying the actual record normally needs many fewer instructions.

The theoretical large storage uses logical addresses in three 64-bit words, never a single truncated 64-bit pointer. Array locations, record indices and offsets are calculated with explicitly bounded multiword arithmetic included in the record allowance. The published model charges total logical accesses and transfers, not interconnect latency. A physically realized machine of this size is not claimed. This is the same large-memory abstract interpretation stated in the prior accepted BSGS control. Peak memory remains conservatively below 2^145 bytes; tightening its reported size is irrelevant to the computation objective.

Bottom-up mergesort needs at most 127 passes, each with at most m comparisons and 2m copies allowing transfers between both arrays. Binary search needs at most 128 comparisons per giant. Initialization and residual operations are covered by an additional m operations. This is at most (381+128+1)*m=510*m record operations, below 2^136. We use the looser upper bound 2^138 record operations, covering auxiliary table bookkeeping and fixed additional records as well. At 2^14 instructions per operation they cost less than 2^152 instructions. Arithmetic and record transfers are both charged for baby construction. No hashing, expected-time lookup, ignored sorting cost or uncharged precomputation is used.

## Setup, output and exact pairing

Allocate a separate 2^90-instruction allowance for all fixed setup and finish work: canonical parsing, subgroup checks, sqrt(r), public-constant loading, the two pinned pairings, inverses, exponentiations for the stride and verification, G2 scalar multiplication, encoding and final comparison. This allowance is not amortized across targets. The following elementary bounds explain its adequacy rather than treating a pairing as free.

A subgroup check or scalar multiplication needs fewer than 512 complete point operations. Affine G1 formulas use base arithmetic and inversion. For Fp2, (a+bu)^-1=(a-bu)/(a^2+b^2), u^2=-1; multiplication uses four base products and a bounded number of sums. Complete G2 addition/doubling uses fewer than 1024 base multiplications/additions and inversions, so even charging the inversion bound 2^41 to every one gives below 2^52 instructions per point step. Infinity, equal points, inverse pairs and y=0 are checked before division. Fewer than 2^12 such steps cover membership checks, reconstruction and other point work, well below 2^90.

Compute the exact protected optimal-ate map with the pinned 64-bit loop count and final exponent (p^12-1)/r. A Miller iteration contains bounded line-function arithmetic and point updates, each expressible with fewer than 2^10 extension operations even using affine coordinates. For inverses, exponentiate to p^12-2 as above; thus one extension arithmetic operation, generously including an inverse, costs below 2^58 instructions. The loop, exceptional cases and exponentiation to the less-than-4608-bit final exponent fit below 2^20 such operations per pairing. This gives below 2^78 instructions per pairing. Two pairings, input checks, output and all remaining fixed exponents fit far below 2^90. This uses the supplied P in both pairings. No alternative pairing or unnormalized Miller value is substituted.

## Total and supplementary quantities

At most m baby products and m giant products occur, totaling at most 2^128 complete steps. At 2^25 instructions per step, they cost at most 2^153 instructions. All sorting, lookup and table transfers cost less than 2^152 instructions. The separate 2^90 setup/finish budget covers input, representation conversion, constants, pairings, validation, inverses, output and final verification. The total is strictly below 2^154 scalar instruction equivalents. Dividing once by fixed C=20000>2^14 gives total normalized computation strictly below 2^140 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents. Declare logarithmic upper bound 140 unchanged. This is an analytical bound, not a measured exact runtime.

Preprocessing (baby construction and sorting, plus setup) is a subset of total work and below 2^140 equivalents. Both success and failure executions fit the same total bound; no failed trial is omitted. Two arrays of 2^127 records of at most 2048 bytes use at most 2^139 bytes. Code, arithmetic scratch, constants, supplied input, counters and output fit well within the conservative 2^145-byte peak. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes. No stored random coins, external powers, target amortization or parallel wall-clock discount occur. Memory is supplementary only.

## Provenance, verification scope and limitations

The earlier public submission 487d6285-ba02-4b3a-bb79-e87d17a61fd2 is the source of the deterministic sorted-BSGS design, supplied-point reduction and deliberately loose accounting framework. This revision materially uses those ideas and declares the full dependency UUID in the separate declaration. It improves the accounting with an explicit twelve-limb Montgomery arithmetic schedule and compact full-key records. The first draft in this session used bit arrays and submitted bound 158 (submission 9c949970-6b41-4513-b19c-610e24e56156); this revision also builds on that design. Its predecessor is declared when publication permits it. The Montgomery schedule and compact records were introduced in submission 1594f30f-41ff-4250-9238-eb14665c552e (bound 142), which was still unpublished when this revision was prepared. This revision uses that work and additionally replaces full coverage by a fixed prefix of 2^254 exponents, proving success greater than one half. It also tightens the repeated-step allowance using the explicit loop subtotal above. The declared new bound is 140; dependencies on unpublished predecessors will be added when publication permits. No inversion is performed inside a dense BSGS multiplication. Its broad score of 180 is a comparator, not a score inherited as a qualification. The related enumeration note c57c341 was read as context but supplies no additional feature used here beyond the common BSGS source. Standard group laws and the protected polynomial representation suffice for correctness.

This package intentionally declares no executable experiment. The proof is finite arithmetic accounting and exact group algebra; toy cyclic-group success would not establish the production bound. Candidate files are never run on the host. Intake and empty-plan experiment processing are delegated to the pinned shared verifier; hosted review is the authority for acceptance and score. A missing local review artifact is not acceptance, and successful upload is not promotion. Protected rules, all six tracks, full parameters, success threshold and normalization constants are unchanged. A future improvement could use faster base arithmetic or a less expensive deterministic data structure, but no such unmeasured optimization is included in this claim.
