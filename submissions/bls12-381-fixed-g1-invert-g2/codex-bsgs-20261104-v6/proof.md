# Six-limb deterministic prefix BSGS: bound 135

This is an analytical classical upper bound for the full protected BLS12-381 fixed-G1 pairing inversion target. It is not a practical break or measured runtime. The first claim requests score 135 unchanged: total cold computation below 2^135 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents with fixed C=20000. Success is exactly 2^254/r>1/2>0.39. Preprocessing below 2^135 equivalents is included in that total. Peak memory is below 2^145 bytes; nonuniform advice is zero bytes. All successful and failed executions are charged. There are no auxiliary powers, quantum steps, target amortization or parallel elapsed-time discounts.

## Target, algorithm and success

Use the protected prime order r and standard G2 generator Q0. For the supplied nonidentity P, compute h=e(P,Q0) using the pinned pairing(Q0,P), including final exponentiation. Nondegeneracy makes h order r for every allowed P. Write z=h^a for the unique a in [0,r). The answer must be the G2 point [a]Q0 for this exact P; neither a scalar nor a different P suffices.

Let m=2^127. Construct m full records (h^j,j), 0<=j<m, starting at identity and multiplying by h. Canonically encode all twelve coefficients, using the Montgomery encoding below. Sort by the full coefficient key with bottom-up mergesort and a second array. Compute d=h^(-m) by charged fixed-size exponentiation. Starting with v=z, for i=0,...,m-1 binary-search v in the sorted baby records. A match j gives a=i*m+j. Verify h^a=z, compute Q=[a]Q0, check membership and e(P,Q)=z, and encode Q canonically. If no match occurs, return infinity as the unsuccessful output. For z=1 return infinity immediately after checks. No randomized coins or repeated trials are needed.

The protected order satisfies 2^254<r<2^255. All searched exponents i*m+j lie in [0,2^254), with no modular wraparound. Euclidean division proves that every exponent in this prefix is found. Outside this prefix no comparison can match and returning infinity cannot succeed. Independently uniform z has uniform exponent a relative to h for every supplied P. Success is therefore exactly 2^254/r, approximately 0.552065, and rigorously greater than one half. The algorithm makes no claim about adversarial targets outside the prefix. Failed searches consume their full budget, counted below; no work is divided by the success probability.

## Scalar arithmetic, including both halves of products

Use six radix-b=2^64 limbs for residues modulo p, with R=b^6=2^384. Store canonical Montgomery values xR mod p in [0,p). Prepare n0=-p[0]^-1 mod b, R mod p and R^2 mod p by elementary charged arithmetic. Conversion uses Montgomery multiplication by R^2 or ordinary one. Canonical Montgomery equality is injective and exact; no polynomial basis conversion occurs.

A field add/subtract/compare/copy costs at most 1024 instructions. For addition retain the seventh carry limb, subtract p and select; for subtraction retain the borrow and conditionally add p. At most four passes of at most eight limb positions, at sixteen instructions per position, cost at most 512 instructions; allocate another 512 for fixed initialization, calls, pointer setup and return. Three ordinary passes suffice for these formulas. This budget includes flags, addressing, loads/stores and control. Do not truncate a high carry before reduction.

For a base product form the full twelve-limb schoolbook product in a fourteen-limb temporary. There are six rows of six cells. A cell forms a_i*b_j+T_k+carry, at most b^2-1. Compute the product's low and high halves with scalar AArch64 MUL and UMULH, then use ADDS/ADC for each addend and propagate both low-half carries to the high half. This explicitly charges a two-word product; it does not assume a unit-cost 128-bit multiply. The bound b^2-1 prevents high-half overflow.

Montgomery reduction has six more rows. At row i compute q=T_i*n0 mod b, then add q*p*b^i with the same six cells. Limb i becomes zero. Carry propagation after every row scans at most fourteen positions. After all rows discard the six zero limbs but retain all high temporary limbs through the final subtraction. Since the original product is below p^2 and the added multiple below Rp, the quotient U is below p^2/R+p<2p. One conditional subtraction yields the canonical Montgomery product. No division or variable-width arithmetic oracle is used.

Each multiply-accumulate cell is allowed 32 instructions. A concrete cell needs two loads, MUL, UMULH, four adds with carry, one store, one carry move, two pointer increments and two loop-control instructions: fourteen before minor address setup. The 32 budget covers these plus any remaining control. Seventy-two cells cost 2304 instructions. A carry-propagation position needs a load, add, carry extraction, store, pointer increment and loop control; sixteen instructions suffice. Twelve rows times fourteen positions cost 2688 instructions. Allocate another 2048 for temporary zeroing, row initialization, six q calculations, final conditional subtraction, call/return and fixed addressing. Total 7040<8192=2^13 instructions per field product. This charges full carry scans even when no carry remains. Squaring uses the same schedule.

## Polynomial extension arithmetic

Use the exact protected polynomial Fp[w]/(w^12-2*w^6+2), with twelve canonical Montgomery coefficients. A dense product has 144 base products. Initialize 23 convolution accumulators, add the 144 products into them, and reduce degrees 22 down to 12 using w^k=2*w^(k-6)-2*w^(k-12). Each reduction doubles a coefficient once and adds/subtracts it twice, so at most 33 further field additions are needed. Descending reduction handles any newly created degree >=12. The output copies twelve coefficients. Even allowing more than twice the 23+144+33+12=212 field initialization/add/copy operations, 512 such operations suffice. No extra base product is needed for multiplication by two.

The arithmetic subtotal is 144*8192+512*1024=1703936 instructions. Allow another 262144 for all extension-level loop counters, coefficient offsets, outer BSGS counter updates, key-pointer setup and control. Fewer than 16384 elementary bookkeeping actions at at most sixteen instructions each suffice: the fixed 144-pair convolution and eleven coefficient reductions require far fewer than this. Base-product internal indexing/control is already covered by its 8192 budget. Thus a complete BSGS dense-product/step costs at most 1966080 instructions, including arithmetic temporary transfers. Table record writes are separately charged. Inversion is needed only in setup and is never inside the repeated multiplication loop.

## Records, exact lookup and memory

Twelve coefficients of six 64-bit words occupy 576 bytes. Add a 256-bit index (32 bytes), tags and metadata; a record fits in 1024 bytes. Use a consistent unsigned lexicographic order on all 72 coefficient words; exponent fields are excluded from the key. This is a deterministic exact comparison, not hashing.

Charge 2048 instructions for each full record comparison or copy including all logical address work. Copying even all 128 padded words at ten instructions per word costs 1280: two loads/stores, six three-word-pointer carry updates, and two loop instructions. Allow 512 more for setup and return, yielding 1792<2048. A full key comparison scans 72 words, at twelve instructions each (two loads, six pointer updates, compare, branch, two loop instructions), plus 512 setup: 1376<2048. Index extraction and midpoint arithmetic fit within the setup slack. Every logical address uses three 64-bit words. No 128-bit index is silently truncated to a single hardware pointer.

This is the theoretical large-storage interpretation used by earlier reviewed BSGS controls. The model charges logical transfers and address computation, not physical interconnect latency or fabrication. No physical realization of the exponentially large table is claimed. The review machine only evaluates the analytical argument and need not allocate attack memory.

For m=2^127, mergesort has 127 passes, with at most m comparisons and 2m copies per pass, allowing both-array transfers. Binary search takes at most 128 comparisons for each giant. Allow another m record operations for initialization and residual bookkeeping. Total is at most (381+128+1)*m=510m record operations, each charged 2048 instructions. Baby construction pays both its arithmetic and record-copy cost. No search, sort, transfer or table-initialization work is free.

## Fixed setup and output budget

Allocate 2^90 scalar instructions for all input decoding, subgroup checks, public constant preparation, representation conversions, stride/inverses, pairing computations, final G2 scalar multiplication, output encoding and verification. This is paid once for a cold attack, never amortized. Constant construction uses elementary inversion/doubling and fits easily in this bound. Only public profile constants, not searched advice, are free design inputs.

For explicit adequacy: a base inverse can exponentiate to p-2 using fewer than 768 base products. An Fp12 inverse can exponentiate to p^12-2 using fewer than 9216 dense products (p^12 has fewer than 4608 bits). Allow 2^58 instructions for any fixed extension operation including an inverse, many orders above these arithmetic schedules. A pinned optimal-ate pairing uses the 64-bit Miller loop and final exponent (p^12-1)/r. Fewer than 2^20 extension operations cover affine line arithmetic, point updates, exceptional cases and final exponentiation even with all divisions implemented by exponentiation. Thus one pairing costs below 2^78; two plus all remaining fixed operations fit below 2^90. This computes exactly the pinned map, not raw Miller equality or a replacement pairing.

Fp2 arithmetic uses u^2=-1 and (a+bu)^-1=(a-bu)/(a^2+b^2). Complete G1/G2 affine additions first check infinity, opposite points, equal points and y=0 before division, then use the ordinary slope formulas. Fewer than 1024 base operations including inversions cover a point step. Fewer than 4096 such steps cover the input tests and output scalar multiplication; even the much looser 2^41 base-inverse and 2^52 point-step allowances of earlier controls remain comfortably inside 2^90. Canonical coefficient parsing/encoding and 256-bit scalar arithmetic are finite linear loops also covered. All attack-side verification is included.

## Exact total, preprocessing and storage

At most 2m=2^128 dense steps occur. Their instructions are at most 1966080*2^128. Record work is at most 510*2^127*2048=522240*2^128 instructions. Adding setup gives total at most 2488320*2^128+2^90. The claimed normalization threshold is 20000*2^135=2560000*2^128. Its margin, 71680*2^128, greatly exceeds 2^90. Therefore total/C<2^135, with every success and failure fully charged. Score 135 is a supported conservative upper bound, not measured performance or an automatic tightening of another submission.

Preprocessing is a subset of total work and below 2^135 equivalents. Two arrays of 2^127 records of 1024 bytes use at most 2^138 bytes. Peak below 2^145 bytes leaves large slack for input, code, constants, metadata, counters, arithmetic scratch and output. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes; only the charged constructed baby table depends on P. No stored random coins or auxiliary powers are used. Memory has no independent objective or tie-break.
