# Injective norm-one encoding and its charged computation

Write X=a+bw in Fp12=Fp6[w]/(w²-v), with a,b in Fp6. The protected field has degree12 and its quadratic conjugation fixes Fp6 and sends w to-w; it equals the p^6 Frobenius. Direct modular exponentiation of the protected public p and r checks p^6=-1 mod r. Hence every GT element has X^(p^6+1)=1, so a²-vb²=1. This uses the entire protected GT, including identity, without assuming a subgroup of reduced order.

Given b, the equation a²=1+vb² has at most two roots in the odd-characteristic field Fp6. They are a and-a. Store b's six canonical Montgomery coefficients (48 bytes each) and one byte s. Set s=1 if the six-coefficient unsigned lexicographic tuple for a is greater than the tuple for-a, otherwise s=0. The comparison uses a fixed coefficient/limb/byte order. If a=0 the only root is zero and s=0; otherwise the tuples are different and their comparison reverses under a->-a. Thus (b,s) distinguishes every GT element injectively. Its size is289 bytes. No square root or decompression is needed to hash a stored full element, and full576-byte states remain in the jump table and checkpoint registers. Both elements with the same b and opposite a are distinguished, even if both could occur. Equality testing still compares full states.

Compute each coefficient of-a by canonical modular negation of the stored Montgomery residue, then compare the six coefficients from highest-priority limb downward.
