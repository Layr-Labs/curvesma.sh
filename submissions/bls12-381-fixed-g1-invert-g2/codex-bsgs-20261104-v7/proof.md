# Six-limb deterministic prefix BSGS: bound 134

This is an analytical classical upper bound for the full protected BLS12-381 fixed-G1 pairing inversion target. It is not a practical break or measured runtime. The first claim requests score 134 unchanged: total cold computation below 2^134 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents with fixed C=20000. Success is exactly 2^254/r>1/2>0.39. Preprocessing below 2^134 equivalents is included in that total. Peak memory is below 2^145 bytes; nonuniform advice is zero bytes. All successful and failed executions are charged. There are no auxiliary powers, quantum steps, target amortization or parallel elapsed-time discounts.

## Target, algorithm and success

Use the protected prime order r and standard G2 generator Q0. For the supplied nonidentity P, compute h=e(P,Q0) using the pinned pairing(Q0,P), including final exponentiation. Nondegeneracy makes h order r for every allowed P. Write z=h^a for the unique a in [0,r). The answer must be the G2 point [a]Q0 for this exact P; neither a scalar nor a different P suffices.

Let m=2^127. Construct m full records (h^j,j), 0<=j<m, starting at identity and multiplying by h. Canonically encode all twelve coefficients, using the Montgomery encoding below. Sort by the full coefficient key with bottom-up mergesort and a second array. Compute d=h^(-m) by charged fixed-size exponentiation. Starting with v=z, for i=0,...,m-1 binary-search v in the sorted baby records. A match j gives a=i*m+j. Verify h^a=z, compute Q=[a]Q0, check membership and e(P,Q)=z, and encode Q canonically. If no match occurs, return infinity as the unsuccessful output. For z=1 return infinity immediately after checks. No randomized coins or repeated trials are needed.

The protected order satisfies 2^254<r<2^255. All searched exponents i*m+j lie in [0,2^254), with no modular wraparound. Euclidean division proves that every exponent in this prefix is found. Outside this prefix no comparison can match and returning infinity cannot succeed. Independently uniform z has uniform exponent a relative to h for every supplied P. Success is therefore exactly 2^254/r, approximately 0.552065, and rigorously greater than one half. The algorithm makes no claim about adversarial targets outside the prefix. Failed searches consume their full budget, counted below; no work is divided by the success probability.

## Scalar arithmetic, including both halves of products

Use six radix-b=2^64 limbs for residues modulo p, with R=b^6=2^384. Store canonical Montgomery values xR mod p in [0,p). Prepare n0=-p[0]^-1 mod b, R mod p and R^2 mod p by elementary charged arithmetic. Conversion uses Montgomery multiplication by R^2 or ordinary one. Canonical Montgomery equality is injective and exact; no polynomial basis conversion occurs.

A field add/subtract/compare/copy costs at most 1024 instructions. For addition retain the seventh carry limb, subtract p and select; for subtraction retain the borrow and conditionally add p. At most four passes of at most eight limb positions, at sixteen instructions per position, cost at most 512 instructions; allocate another 512 for fixed initialization, calls, pointer setup and return. Three ordinary passes suffice for these formulas. This budget includes flags, addressing, loads/stores and control. Do not truncate a high carry before reduction.

For a base product form the full twelve-limb schoolbook product in a fourteen-limb temporary. There are six rows of six cells. A cell forms a_i*b_j+T_k+carry, at most b^2-1. Compute the product's low and high halves with scalar AArch64 MUL and UMULH, then use ADDS/ADC for each addend and propagate both low-half carries to the high half. This explicitly charges a two-word product; it does not assume a unit-cost 128-bit multiply. The bound b^2-1 prevents high-half overflow.

Montgomery reduction has six more rows. At row i compute q=T_i*n0 mod b, then add q*p*b^i with the same six cells. Limb i becomes zero. Carry propagation after every row scans at most fourteen positions. After all rows discard the six zero limbs but retain all high temporary limbs through the final subtraction. Since the original product is below p^2 and the added multiple below Rp, the quotient U is below p^2/R+p<2p. One conditional subtraction yields the canonical Montgomery product. No division or variable-width arithmetic oracle is used.

Each multiply-accumulate cell is allowed 32 instructions. A concrete cell needs two loads, MUL, UMULH, four adds with carry, one store, one carry move, two pointer increments and two loop-control instructions: fourteen before minor address setup. The 32 budget covers these plus any remaining control. Seventy-two cells cost 2304 instructions. A carry-propagation position needs a load, add, carry extraction, store, pointer increment and loop control; sixteen instructions suffice. Twelve rows times fourteen positions cost 2688 instructions. Allocate another 2048 for temporary zeroing, row initialization, six q calculations, final conditional subtraction, call/return and fixed addressing. Total 7040<8192=2^13 instructions per field product. This charges full carry scans even when no carry remains. Squaring uses the same schedule.

## Tower extension arithmetic and exact basis conversion

Use Fp2=Fp[u]/(u^2+1), Fp6=Fp2[v]/(v^3-(u+1)), and Fp12=Fp6[w]/(w^2-v). This is the protected polynomial field under v=w^2 and u=w^6-1: w^12=(u+1)^2=2u=2w^6-2. No field or pairing is changed. Given polynomial coefficients c0,...,c11, convert for each k=0,...,5 the pair c_k+c_(k+6)*w^6 to (c_k+c_(k+6))+c_(k+6)*u, and place even k coefficients in the Fp6 constant part and odd k in its w part. This uses six base additions. Inverse conversion uses six subtractions. Both are bijective linear maps and are charged in setup. Store all twelve canonical Montgomery tower coefficients as an exact full key. Sorting may use any consistent order of these keys; equality is invariant under the bijection. The final verification still uses the exact protected pinned pairing and original polynomial target.

Fp2 multiplication uses three base products. For a=a0+a1*u and b=b0+b1*u set t0=a0*b0, t1=a1*b1, output c0=t0-t1 and c1=(a0+a1)*(b0+b1)-t0-t1. This is three base products and five base additions/subtractions.

For Fp6, write a=a0+a1*v+a2*v^2 and b=b0+b1*v+b2*v^2 with Fp2 coefficients. Set t0=a0*b0,t1=a1*b1,t2=a2*b2 and cross values s_ij=(a_i+a_j)*(b_i+b_j)-t_i-t_j for ij=01,02,12. With xi=u+1, output c0=t0+xi*s12, c1=s01+xi*t2, c2=s02+t1. This uses six Fp2 products and fifteen Fp2 additions/subtractions. Multiplication by xi is (x0+x1*u)*(1+u)=(x0-x1)+(x0+x1)*u, two base additions. There are two such xi multiplications. Thus each Fp6 product uses eighteen base products and at most 6*5+15*2+2*2=64 base additions/subtractions.

For Fp12 write A=a+b*w and B=c+d*w. Compute t0=a*c,t1=b*d,t2=(a+b)*(c+d). Output (t0+v*t1)+(t2-t0-t1)*w. This is three Fp6 products. The two input Fp6 sums and two output Fp6 subtractions use 24 base additions. Multiplication by v maps (x0,x1,x2) to (xi*x2,x0,x1), using two base additions; the constant output addition adds six. Total is 54 base products and 3*64+24+2+6=224 base additions/subtractions.

Allow 96 additional field copies/initializations, so 320 lower-order field operations suffice. Evaluate the displayed straight-line formulas with pointers to intermediate slots; an addition or multiplication writes directly into its result slot. No mandatory whole-element copy follows each operation. Alias-safe temporaries and a final twelve-coefficient output copy fit well within 96 extra field copies. In particular, t_i and cross results are retained once and read by pointer, while the v permutation uses coefficient pointers and copies only as needed for output. All base-product internal temporaries/transfers are already charged by their 8192-instruction schedule.

The arithmetic subtotal is 54*8192+320*1024=770048 instructions per dense tower product. Allow another 65536 for all fixed extension-level control, slot offsets, key pointers, scalar-counter and BSGS loop updates. The straight-line expression has 54 base-product calls, 224 sums and at most 96 copies; their fixed-slot pointer and call bookkeeping easily fit below 4096 elementary actions at sixteen instructions each. All loops internal to base kernels were counted there. A complete BSGS step therefore costs at most 835584 instructions. No inversion occurs inside this step. Subgroup tests, conversion and all inverses are confined to the separately charged setup/finish. No sparse calibration, heuristic hash or number-theoretic assumption is used.

## Records, exact lookup and memory

Twelve coefficients of six 64-bit words occupy 576 bytes. Add a 256-bit index (32 bytes), one tag word and three logical-address metadata words: meaningful record contents use at most 640 bytes (80 words). Allocate a 1024-byte slot but never read or copy padding. Key comparisons scan only the 72 coefficient words, with a consistent unsigned lexicographic order. This is deterministic exact lookup, not hashing.

Charge 1536 instructions per full record comparison or copy including all logical addressing, metadata, midpoint/index arithmetic and return. Copying 80 words at ten instructions per word costs 800: one load, one store, six three-word-pointer update instructions, and two loop instructions. Allow another 256 for setup and return, totaling 1056<1536. A full key comparison uses 72 words at twelve instructions each (two loads, six pointer updates, compare, branch and two loop instructions), plus 256 setup, totaling 1120<1536. Computing a slot from its multiword index is a fixed shift by ten; carries, midpoint and metadata fit in the 256 fixed allowance. The full record cost already includes moving its stored scalar index. No index is truncated to a 64-bit host pointer.

The memory interpretation is theoretical large storage as in earlier reviewed BSGS controls: logical addresses use three 64-bit words, and all logical transfers and address work are charged. Physical interconnect fabrication/latency is not claimed. The reviewing machine checks an analytic argument and need not allocate the attack table.

At m=2^127 mergesort has 127 passes with at most m comparisons and 2m copies per pass, including both-array transfers. Binary search takes at most 128 comparisons for each giant. Another m operations cover initial records and residual bookkeeping. At most 510m full record operations are charged, each at 1536 instructions. Baby construction pays its arithmetic and its record copy separately. Padding is never used as a key or initialized unnecessarily; allocating the two arrays uses virtual logical records, and each meaningful record is initialized by its charged write.

## Fixed setup and output budget

Allocate 2^90 scalar instructions for all input decoding, subgroup checks, public constant preparation, representation conversions, stride/inverses, pairing computations, final G2 scalar multiplication, output encoding and verification. This is paid once for a cold attack, never amortized. Constant construction uses elementary inversion/doubling and fits easily in this bound. Only public profile constants, not searched advice, are free design inputs.

For explicit adequacy: a base inverse can exponentiate to p-2 using fewer than 768 base products. An Fp12 inverse can exponentiate to p^12-2 using fewer than 9216 dense products (p^12 has fewer than 4608 bits). Allow 2^58 instructions for any fixed extension operation including an inverse, many orders above these arithmetic schedules. A pinned optimal-ate pairing uses the 64-bit Miller loop and final exponent (p^12-1)/r. Fewer than 2^20 extension operations cover affine line arithmetic, point updates, exceptional cases and final exponentiation even with all divisions implemented by exponentiation. Thus one pairing costs below 2^78; two plus all remaining fixed operations fit below 2^90. This computes exactly the pinned map, not raw Miller equality or a replacement pairing.

Fp2 arithmetic uses u^2=-1 and (a+bu)^-1=(a-bu)/(a^2+b^2). Complete G1/G2 affine additions first check infinity, opposite points, equal points and y=0 before division, then use the ordinary slope formulas. Fewer than 1024 base operations including inversions cover a point step. Fewer than 4096 such steps cover the input tests and output scalar multiplication; even the much looser 2^41 base-inverse and 2^52 point-step allowances of earlier controls remain comfortably inside 2^90. Canonical coefficient parsing/encoding and 256-bit scalar arithmetic are finite linear loops also covered. All attack-side verification is included.

## Exact total, preprocessing and storage

At most 2m=2^128 complete steps cost at most 835584*2^128 scalar instructions. Record operations cost at most 510*2^127*1536=391680*2^128 instructions. Include the separate 2^90 setup/finish budget. Total is at most 1227264*2^128+2^90. The normalization threshold 20000*2^134 is 1280000*2^128. Its positive margin 52736*2^128 greatly exceeds 2^90. Thus total/C<2^134. This charges every success/failure, all preprocessing, conversion, validation, arithmetic, lookup, transfer, output and final verification once.

Preprocessing is a subset of total work and below 2^134 equivalents. Two arrays of 2^127 slots of 1024 bytes use at most 2^138 bytes. Peak below 2^145 bytes covers code, input, all temporaries, constants, metadata, counters and outputs. No nonuniform advice, auxiliary powers or retained random coins exist. Success remains exactly 2^254/r>1/2; neither an expected-time assumption nor amortization is used. Memory is supplementary only. The claim is an analytical conservative bound, not measured runtime.
