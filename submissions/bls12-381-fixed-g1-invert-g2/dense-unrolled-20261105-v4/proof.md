## Algorithm

Use the protected production r,p,P0,Q0 and pinned pairing,not reduced parameters. Validate canonical inputs,nonidentity P and its G1 subgroup,nonzero z and z^r=1. If z=1 return G2 infinity. Otherwise compute h=e(P,Q0) using pairing(Q0,P). Nondegeneracy and prime r make h a generator for every allowed P. Solving z=h^a and returning Q=[a]Q0 solves the exact supplied-P problem by bilinearity.

## Sole heuristic and finite success proof

H: on the uniform protected P,z,the actual fully computed domain-separated SHA-256 functions may be analyzed as independent random functions on their disjoint input domains. This is an explicitly unproved conventional hash idealization at enormous scale. It is not an available free oracle,not a theorem about fixed SHA-256,and not a claim that an adding walk is a uniform random mapping. The following finite argument holds inside H. The work bound holds even if H fails.

Under H rejection gives independent uniform scalars and keys. Since r>2^254,each main rejection attempt succeeds with probability>1/2. All sampler failures together have probability below2^-990. Given z=h^a,the jump group values M_j are independent uniform GT. For each nontrivial character chi,lambda_chi=(1/w)sum chi(M_j) has modulus>rho=1/128 with probability at most4exp(-w/65536),by real/imaginary Hoeffding bounds for independent[-1,1] mean-zero terms. Union over r-1 characters gives bad-table probability<=4r exp(-256)<2^-100. The exact sufficient integer check is4r*3^256*2^100<8^256,using e>8/3. The attack does not identify good tables;bad ones and all initialization work are charged.

On a good table,consider a surrogate with independent uniform jump indices. Let p_d(x) be its d-step increment distribution,epsilon_d=p_d-1/r. Fourier inversion and Parseval on the finite cyclic group give sup|epsilon_d|<=rho^d and its unweighted l2 norm<=rho^d,including d=0. Put T=271,eta=1/65536. For d>=T,rho^d<=eta/r. Count collisions C between pairs i<j in states0..L with j-i>=T. Their number D=(L-T+1)(L-T+2)/2,giving E C>=m=(1-eta)D/r.

Here is a complete second-moment excess bound,so no independent-collision heuristic is added. Identical intervals contribute at most(1+eta)D/r. Give every ordered distinct interval pair baseline(1+3eta)/r^2,at most(1+3eta)D^2/r^2 total. Disjoint or touching increment intervals are independent and covered by that baseline. Nested intervals have lengths a>=0,b>=T,c>=0 with a+c>0;their joint probability p_b(1)p_(a+c)(1) is at most(1+eta)/r^2+(1+eta)rho^(a+c)/r. There are at most(L+1)^2 choices for start and b. Summing a,c with a+c>0 gives1/(1-rho)^2-1=255/16129;both indicator orders yield excess at most(510/16129)(1+eta)(L+1)^2/r. This includes shared first or last endpoints.

Crossing intervals have positive gaps a,b,c with a+b>=T,b+c>=T. Their joint probability is sum_x p_a(x)p_b(x^-1)p_c(x). Expanding uniform+epsilon,linear terms vanish;Cauchy-Schwarz and sup/l2 bounds give at most1/r^2+(rho^(a+b)+rho^(a+c)+rho^(b+c))/r+rho^(a+b+c).
