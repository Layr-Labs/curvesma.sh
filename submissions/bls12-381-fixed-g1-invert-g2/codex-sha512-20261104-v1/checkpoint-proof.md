# Finite equally spaced checkpoint lemma and complete accounting

Let X_0,X_1,... be a deterministic trajectory, with tail mu>=0 and cycle length lambda>=1. Its first repeat is at tau=mu+lambda. Let L>=tau, s=ceil(L/8) and K=L+s. Save complete states at indices0,s,...,8s when reached, and at each step compare with every previously saved checkpoint before making any new save. There are at most9 saved states. Set a=ceil(mu/s)*s. Then mu<=a<=mu+s-1<=L+s-2 and a<=8s, since mu<=L-1 and8s>=L. So index a is among the saved indices. It lies in the cycle, and X_(a+lambda)=X_a. The later index is positive after the checkpoint and at most mu+s-1+lambda=tau+s-1<=L+s-1<K. Thus it is compared and detected within the charged K transitions. An earlier detection only ends sooner. The algorithm never needs mu,lambda, a visited dictionary, or a phase to locate the tail. No assumption on their distribution enters this deterministic lemma.

For any detected equality, the saved index is strictly less than the current index; d is their positive difference with d<=K<r. Conditional on all jump group values, initial group value and partition, the coefficient residues v_j remain independent uniform modulo r, by the inherited one-to-one u_j relation. Checkpoint decisions and equality comparison order use only group states and public counters. They do not inspect v_j. The coefficient denominator is sum_j n_j*v_j, where the nonnegative counts n_j sum to d. Thus at least one n_j is nonzero modulo r, D is uniform, and the failure probability is exactly1/r. The invariant supplies the unique correct target exponent otherwise. Only the first equality is used and a singular relation fails immediately; there is no selective conditioning on a later successful relation.
