# Six-limb deterministic prefix BSGS: bound 137

This is an analytical classical upper bound for the full protected BLS12-381 fixed-G1 pairing inversion target. It is not a practical break or measured runtime. The first claim requests score 137 unchanged: total cold computation below 2^137 dense-Fp12 reference equivalents with fixed C=20000. Success is exactly L/r>0.39, where m=39*2^121 and L=m*(2m-1). Preprocessing below 2^137 equivalents is included in that total. Peak memory is below 2^145 bytes; nonuniform advice is zero bytes. All successful and failed executions are charged. There are no auxiliary powers, quantum steps, target amortization or parallel elapsed-time discounts.

## Target, algorithm and success

Use the protected prime order r and standard G2 generator Q0. For the supplied nonidentity P, compute h=e(P,Q0) using the pinned pairing(Q0,P), including final exponentiation. Nondegeneracy makes h order r for every allowed P. Write z=h^a for the unique a in [0,r). The answer must be the G2 point [a]Q0 for this exact P; neither a scalar nor a different P suffices.

Set m=39*2^121 and s=2m-1. Use the orbit key K(t)=min(t,t^-1) in a consistent full-coefficient order and retain its sign sigma(t) in {+1,-1}, so K(t)=t^sigma(t). Break the identity tie with sign +1. The following paragraph proves the inversion implementation is exact and charges it below. Construct the m baby records (K(h^j),j,sigma(h^j)), 0<=j<m, with successive products by h. Keep these full records stationary and sort four-word indices into them by exact keys, using mergesort and a second index array.

The protected constants satisfy p^6=-1 mod r (direct modular exponentiation verifies this). In the tower below, the p^6 automorphism fixes Fp6 and sends w to -w. Thus for every t=A+B*w in GT, t^-1=t^(p^6)=A-B*w. This applies to h, all babies and every giant because they lie in GT. No inverse is computed by exponentiation inside the loop; conjugation performs six field negations and copies six unchanged coefficients. The choice of canonical orbit key and its sign is exact. For j,k<m, equality of baby keys implies j=+k or j=-k mod r. Since 2m<r, only j=k is possible; identity is handled explicitly. There are no duplicate baby keys from different j.

Set the giant center c_i=i*s+(m-1) for i=0,...,m-1. Compute v_0=z*h^(-(m-1)) and update v by multiplication by h^(-s) between searches. At each i form K(v) and sigma(v), then binary-search the sorted indices. On a match j with stored sigma_j, set a=c_i+sigma(v)*sigma_j*j. Verify h^a=z, construct Q=[a]Q0, verify membership and the exact protected pairing e(P,Q)=z, then encode Q. If all m searches fail, return infinity. Identity targets succeed immediately. All setup exponents, validation and final verification are charged separately.

A match means sigma(v)*(a_true-c_i)=sigma_j*j mod r; multiplying by sigma(v) gives the reconstruction formula. The candidate lies in [i*s,(i+1)*s-1], because j<=m-1. These m intervals are adjacent and disjoint and cover exactly [0,L), L=m*(2m-1). Every a_true in this prefix has the required signed residual and is found; no a_true outside it can match because L<r and the reconstructed candidate is inside the prefix. Infinity on failure cannot accidentally satisfy an outside nonidentity target. Hence success is exactly L/r, with all failures charged in full.

The hexadecimal order has leading byte 0x73, so 115*2^248<r<116*2^248. Here L=3042*2^242-39*2^121. This is less than r, and 100L>39r: use 39r<289536*2^242 while 100L=304200*2^242-3900*2^121, whose positive margin exceeds zero. Thus success is rigorously greater than 0.39 (approximately 0.410005), over uniform z for every allowed P. No random-walk heuristic, random coins, amplification or success-conditioned cost is used. No guarantee is claimed for adversarial targets outside the searched prefix.

## Scalar arithmetic, including both halves of products

Use six radix-b=2^64 limbs for residues modulo p, with R=b^6=2^384. Store canonical Montgomery values xR mod p in [0,p). Prepare n0=-p[0]^-1 mod b, R mod p and R^2 mod p by elementary charged arithmetic. Conversion uses Montgomery multiplication by R^2 or ordinary one. Canonical Montgomery equality is injective and exact; no polynomial basis conversion occurs.

A field add/subtract/compare/copy costs at most 512 instructions. For addition retain the seventh carry limb, subtract p and select; for subtraction retain the borrow and conditionally add p. At most three passes of at most seven limb positions, at twelve instructions per position, cost at most 252 instructions. Allocate another 256 for initialization, calls, pointer setup and return, totaling 508<512. A ripple carry/borrow or conditional selection position requires at most twelve instructions, including its loads/stores and local-pointer loop control. The three passes are sum, subtraction with retained high carry, and conditional selection; subtraction uses the analogous borrow, add-back and selection passes. This budget includes flags, addressing, loads/stores and control. Do not truncate a high carry before reduction.

For a base product form the full twelve-limb schoolbook product in a fourteen-limb temporary. There are six rows of six cells. A cell forms a_i*b_j+T_k+carry, at most b^2-1. Compute the product's low and high halves with scalar AArch64 MUL and UMULH, then use ADDS/ADC for each addend and propagate both low-half carries to the high half. This explicitly charges a two-word product; it does not assume a unit-cost 128-bit multiply. The bound b^2-1 prevents high-half overflow.

Montgomery reduction has six more rows. At row i compute q=T_i*n0 mod b, then add q*p*b^i with the same six cells. Limb i becomes zero. Carry propagation after every row scans at most fourteen positions. After all rows discard the six zero limbs but retain all high temporary limbs through the final subtraction. Since the original product is below p^2 and the added multiple below Rp, the quotient U is below p^2/R+p<2p. One conditional subtraction yields the canonical Montgomery product. No division or variable-width arithmetic oracle is used.

Each multiply-accumulate cell is allowed twenty instructions: the earlier fourteen-instruction sequence plus six for cell addressing. Seventy-two cells cost 1440. Carry propagation needs at most ten instructions per position. Its bound can be much tighter than a fourteen-position scan after every row. In product row i, all earlier product rows have highest possible occupied limb i+5, so the final carry is written directly into zero limb i+6 without further propagation: six positions total. In reduction row i=0,...,5, propagation starts at i+6 and scans no farther than limb 13, giving at most 8-i positions. Their sum is 33. Thus there are at most 39 carry positions, costing 390 instructions. The fourteen-limb temporary remains an upper bound throughout.

Allocate 768 fixed instructions: zeroing at most 42, twelve row controls/initializations 192, six q computations 24, final subtract/select passes 252, call/return and saves 96, plus slack. Total 1440+390+768=2598<3072 instructions per canonical Montgomery base product. Both halves of every product, carry propagation and all temporary transfers are included. Squaring uses the same schedule. This is an explicit analytical scalar bound, not a hardware timing claim.

## Tower extension arithmetic and exact basis conversion

Use Fp2=Fp[u]/(u^2+1), Fp6=Fp2[v]/(v^3-(u+1)), and Fp12=Fp6[w]/(w^2-v). This is the protected polynomial field under v=w^2 and u=w^6-1: w^12=(u+1)^2=2u=2w^6-2. No field or pairing is changed. Given polynomial coefficients c0,...,c11, convert for each k=0,...,5 the pair c_k+c_(k+6)*w^6 to (c_k+c_(k+6))+c_(k+6)*u, and place even k coefficients in the Fp6 constant part and odd k in its w part. This uses six base additions. Inverse conversion uses six subtractions. Both are bijective linear maps and are charged in setup. Store all twelve canonical Montgomery tower coefficients as an exact full key. Sorting may use any consistent order of these keys; equality is invariant under the bijection. The final verification still uses the exact protected pinned pairing and original polynomial target.

Fp2 multiplication uses three base products. For a=a0+a1*u and b=b0+b1*u set t0=a0*b0, t1=a1*b1, output c0=t0-t1 and c1=(a0+a1)*(b0+b1)-t0-t1. This is three base products and five base additions/subtractions.

For Fp6, write a=a0+a1*v+a2*v^2 and b=b0+b1*v+b2*v^2 with Fp2 coefficients. Set t0=a0*b0,t1=a1*b1,t2=a2*b2 and cross values s_ij=(a_i+a_j)*(b_i+b_j)-t_i-t_j for ij=01,02,12. With xi=u+1, output c0=t0+xi*s12, c1=s01+xi*t2, c2=s02+t1. This uses six Fp2 products and fifteen Fp2 additions/subtractions. Multiplication by xi is (x0+x1*u)*(1+u)=(x0-x1)+(x0+x1)*u, two base additions. There are two such xi multiplications. Thus each Fp6 product uses eighteen base products and at most 6*5+15*2+2*2=64 base additions/subtractions.

For Fp12 write A=a+b*w and B=c+d*w. Compute t0=a*c,t1=b*d,t2=(a+b)*(c+d). Output (t0+v*t1)+(t2-t0-t1)*w. This is three Fp6 products. The two input Fp6 sums and two output Fp6 subtractions use 24 base additions. Multiplication by v maps (x0,x1,x2) to (xi*x2,x0,x1), using two base additions; the constant output addition adds six. Total is 54 base products and 3*64+24+2+6=224 base additions/subtractions.

Allow 32 additional field copies, for a total of 256 lower-order field operations beyond the 54 products. Evaluate all displayed formulas into distinct fixed temporary slots with results written directly by the base kernels. The 224 sums/subtractions already include writing their outputs. Intermediate t_i and s_ij are read by pointer; the v permutation changes coefficient references instead of copying an element. No accumulator array is initialized in the tower formula. A final twelve-coefficient output copy and alias handling fit within 32 additional field copies; each new step may write into an alternate buffer and swap pointers rather than copy its input. Base-product temporary zeroing is charged inside its 3072-instruction cost.

The tower arithmetic costs 54*3072+256*512=296960 instructions. Allow another 32768 for fixed extension-level pointer/control and BSGS loop bookkeeping. The straight-line expression has 310 base-kernel operations; allowing 24 instructions for each operation's fixed-slot pointer setup/call preparation gives 7440, and another 8192 for all remaining outer control and counters still leaves more than a factor two inside 32768. Base-kernel internal loops and returns are already charged there. The dense product/loop schedule is thus at most 329728 instructions.

Canonical orbit preparation costs at most another 8192: six unchanged field copies and six field negations cost 12*512=6144, and a full-key comparison costs at most 1280, totaling 7424. Select a pointer and retain a sign bit in the remaining slack. No chosen key is recopied here: its record copy is charged below. A complete baby/giant step including orbit preparation costs at most 337920 instructions. We charge this to every step even where a product or a key conversion is unnecessary. The original value is retained for the next multiplication, with a separate bounded inverse buffer. All are constant-sized scratch. There is no hidden per-step exponentiation or heuristic equality.

## Implemented distributed banks, rather than wide-address loads

The preceding indexed candidate 1181f6e1-3b78-4950-ba02-061253159c3c was rejected because its multiword logical pointers did not implement memory accesses outside a processor's address space. This revision replaces that premise. Every processor now uses only ordinary local 64-bit addresses. Every remote access is an explicit bounded message exchange; no instruction loads from a 192-bit address. The bank network and its initialization are included in work and memory.

A canonical tower orbit key has 576 bytes. With its four-word exponent, sign tag and metadata the meaningful record uses at most 640 bytes, allocated in a 1024-byte slot. There is one stationary baby table and two arrays of 32-byte indices. A bank holds at most 2^40 bytes in its local address space. A baby-table bank holds 2^30 slots and an index-array bank holds 2^35 indices. Split an element index into high bank number and low local offset using ordinary multiword shifts. The local byte offsets have at most forty bits. No local load/store has a wide address.

Use two namespace bits for the three arrays and 97 bank-number bits. A complete binary routing tree of depth 99 therefore provides every needed bank for m<2^127. Using depth at most 102 in the cost bound gives slack. Each routing processor has parent and two child links. Adjacent processors share bounded input/output queues mapped to ordinary local 64-bit addresses on those two processors. These are fixed point-to-point links, not a global routing oracle or remote-memory instruction. A packet is relayed by the intervening processors, and all relay instructions are counted across processors. A bank request carries namespace/bank bits, a local offset and an operation tag in at most four header words. Routing nodes retain their level/mask and local queue addresses; these constants are built during charged initialization.

The coordinator issues one remote operation at a time. A request goes down the unique tree path; the response goes back along the reserved parent path. No contention or searching is needed. Each hop chooses its child from one address bit. Queues have fixed locations and enough room for one packet, with ready/acknowledgment flags. Sleeping receivers use event/wait instructions and are woken only for the scheduled data/acknowledgment; no polling loop is assumed free. The fixed control allowance below includes these instructions and acknowledgments. This is a serial algorithm on distributed local-memory processors; waiting time is not the score. No processor's computation is omitted and no speedup is inferred from elapsed time.

A full record packet has at most 84 words (80 meaningful record words and four header/control words). An index packet has at most eight words (four index words and four header words). An acknowledgment/control packet has at most eight words as well. A forwarding node copies the packet between its fixed adjacent queue windows with unrolled local LDR/STR pairs, two instructions per word. All offsets are small immediate offsets within the queue. Packet routing, flag handling, event/wait/acknowledgment and local fixed pointer setup receive 32 more instructions per hop: at most six for level/route selection, ten for queue flag handshakes, two event/wait operations, eight for queue/window setup and six for metadata. The transfer itself is the explicit store into the shared adjacent-link page and the next node's explicit load; an intermediate-node relay reads its incoming page and writes its outgoing page. There is no uncharged second software copy per hop.

Thus an index-sized forwarding hop costs at most 8*2+32=48 instructions; a full-record hop at most 84*2+32=200. For a full read or write, request/response together need no more than 102*(48+200)=25296 forwarding instructions. Allow another 2048 for coordinator/leaf setup, local offset arithmetic, the leaf's local data access, copying the response and all remaining call/control work: total 27344<32768 instructions. For an index read or write, the round trip costs at most 102*(48+48)+2048=11840<12288 instructions. These bounds include acknowledgment direction, whether it is the small request or small response. Shared-link hardware is only local point-to-point transport as with ordinary memory stores; no large address is decoded without passing through the counted routers.

The 32-instruction fixed per-hop control budget is achieved with a fixed single-request protocol, not arbitrary asynchronous scheduling. Each node's handler knows whether its reserved response is pending; data-ready and acknowledgment events are deterministic. An idle node remains asleep until an addressed event. Environmental interrupts, operating-system scheduling noise and physical wire propagation are outside instruction-equivalent work just as ordinary instruction latency is; all instructions of the defined protocol itself are charged. Hardware fabrication is not counted in the protected computation metric. Constructing routing state, copying code/data and starting all nodes is charged separately below.

## Complete indexed sorting and remote access ledger

All comparisons fetch their inputs through that network. A full-key comparison can fetch two four-word sorted indices (two index reads) and two baby records (two full reads). Charge 2*12288+2*32768+2048=92160 instructions. The extra 2048 covers full local 72-word comparison, sign/index extraction, midpoint/merge counter calculations and result handling. A comparison scans the fetched local buffers; it does not perform remote loads for individual words. Binary search only needs one remote index/key but receives the same generous two-input allowance. Exact canonical equality and full coefficient ordering are unchanged.

An index copy reads its source through one index request and writes its destination through another: 24576 instructions. The request allowances already include local coordinate/control arithmetic. Initial baby construction writes one full record at 32768 and one index at 12288, totaling 45056 per baby. Padding is never read or copied. Each key's sign occupies the existing tag word. The scalar arithmetic and orbit preparation remain separately charged per step.

As m<2^127, mergesort has at most 127 passes with at most m comparisons and 2m index copies each. Binary search uses at most 128 comparisons per giant. Total remote/local data-structure work is bounded by 255m*92160+254m*24576+m*45056=29788160m instructions. This includes record construction, index initialization, sorting, lookup, all bank requests, packets and coordination. No expected hashing, free lookup or free wide memory access is used. The network intentionally makes accesses more expensive than the earlier unsupported ledger.

## Network storage and initialization

There are fewer than 2^100 nodes in the complete depth-99 binary tree. Allocate each routing node up to 8192 bytes for local code, metadata and its at-most-three adjacent-link queues. Three pairs of 84-word queue windows use at most 4032 bytes, leaving room for unrolled forwarding code and local state. Every leaf additionally has a bank of at most 2^40 bytes. With 2^99 leaves, bank capacity is at most 2^139 bytes; routing/queue storage is below 2^113 bytes. The coordinator's field code, input, scratch and outputs add only a fixed amount. Peak below 2^145 bytes includes all processors' code, queues, banks, metadata and advice; memory is not reported per processor.

Charge a separate 2^140-instruction initialization budget to construct/clear all required state, distribute node code, assign masks and queue locations, and initialize link flags. It is deliberately much larger than 8192 instructions per node (<2^113 instructions), than routing the bounded code images down the depth-99 tree, and even than a wordwise pass over every reserved 2^139-byte bank. Actual meaningful table/index writes are additionally charged in the per-record ledger, so no hidden preprocessing is amortized. A deterministic tree construction by node number produces parent/child links and masks with bounded 128-bit arithmetic per node; the startup allowance covers this explicitly. Public circuit topology is algorithm design, but all its populated routing state and code copies are counted here. No precomputed challenge-specific advice is used.

## Fixed setup and output budget

Allocate 2^90 scalar instructions for all input decoding, subgroup checks, public constant preparation, representation conversions, stride/inverses, pairing computations, final G2 scalar multiplication, output encoding and verification. This is paid once for a cold attack, never amortized. Constant construction uses elementary inversion/doubling and fits easily in this bound. Only public profile constants, not searched advice, are free design inputs.

For explicit adequacy: a base inverse can exponentiate to p-2 using fewer than 768 base products. An Fp12 inverse can exponentiate to p^12-2 using fewer than 9216 dense products (p^12 has fewer than 4608 bits). Allow 2^58 instructions for any fixed extension operation including an inverse, many orders above these arithmetic schedules. A pinned optimal-ate pairing uses the 64-bit Miller loop and final exponent (p^12-1)/r. Fewer than 2^20 extension operations cover affine line arithmetic, point updates, exceptional cases and final exponentiation even with all divisions implemented by exponentiation. Thus one pairing costs below 2^78; two plus all remaining fixed operations fit below 2^90. This computes exactly the pinned map, not raw Miller equality or a replacement pairing.

Fp2 arithmetic uses u^2=-1 and (a+bu)^-1=(a-bu)/(a^2+b^2). Complete G1/G2 affine additions first check infinity, opposite points, equal points and y=0 before division, then use the ordinary slope formulas. Fewer than 1024 base operations including inversions cover a point step. Fewer than 4096 such steps cover the input tests and output scalar multiplication; even the much looser 2^41 base-inverse and 2^52 point-step allowances of earlier controls remain comfortably inside 2^90. Canonical coefficient parsing/encoding and 256-bit scalar arithmetic are finite linear loops also covered. All attack-side verification is included.

## Exact total, preprocessing and storage

There are at most 2m orbit-product steps, each at 337920 instructions, totaling 675840m. The implemented banked data structure costs 29788160m. Add initialization 2^140 and fixed setup/finish 2^90. Total is at most 30464000m+2^140+2^90=1188096000*2^121+2^140+2^90. The normalization threshold 20000*2^137 equals 1310720000*2^121. The margin 122624000*2^121 exceeds 2^140+2^90. Therefore total/C<2^137, including all processors' work, bank routing, startup, successful and failed searches, output and verification.

Preprocessing is included as a subset of total work and below 2^137 equivalents. Peak total storage is below 2^145 bytes as established by the bank/tree capacity calculation. Nonuniform advice is zero bytes. Success remains exactly L/r>0.39. There is no target amortization, quantum step, auxiliary power, wall-clock discount or additional metric. This remains an analytical algorithm on enormous storage, not a physically executed production attack; unlike the rejected predecessor, it supplies an instruction-level implementation of every remote memory access.
