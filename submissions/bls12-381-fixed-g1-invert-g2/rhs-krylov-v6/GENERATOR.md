## Recursion and correctness

For a k-by-c power-series matrix F and integer shift s, let M(F,l)={v in F_r[x]^(1 by k): vF=0 mod x^l}. A shifted order basis B spans this module and has full-rank shifted leading matrix. Here k=m+n and c<=k. The shifted degree of a row is max_j(deg(v_j)+s_j).

Base l=1: sort rows by (shift,index). In that order Gaussian-eliminate each constant row against previously selected pivot rows, carrying the same operations on an identity matrix. Mark rows that introduce pivots. Multiply each marked transformation row by x; leave the zero-discrepancy rows constant. Constant transformed rows are the kernel of F(0), marked rows form a complementary space; thus these rows span exactly M(F,1). Sorted pivot selection is rank-profile elimination: transformations use only preceding rows of no greater shift. Consequently their shifted leading matrix has full rank and their shifted degrees are minimal. No arbitrary-degree arithmetic is hidden in this base case.

For l>1 put h=floor(l/2). Compute B1=PM(F,h,s), with shifted row degrees t. Form G=(x^-h B1 F) mod x^(l-h). Compute B2=PM(G,l-h,t); return B2 B1 and its row degrees. Since every v in M(F,l) belongs to M(F,h), write v=w B1 uniquely. Its remaining condition is w G=0 mod x^(l-h), so w lies in the module spanned by B2. This proves spanning and independence inductively. Shifted row reduction is preserved: if the shifted leading matrix of B1 is nonsingular, leading coefficients of wB1 have degree exactly the t-shifted degree of w; applying this to the independent leading rows of B2 gives the result. Each entry of a basis at order l has degree<=l, by induction from base degree1; this does not assume balanced shifts.

For S_i=X^T B^iY use F=[S^T;I_m], with shift(0 repeated n,1 repeated m). RHS.md proves from the two truncated Krylov rank conditions that exactly n least-shifted-degree rows give a basis of the true relation module. Reverse each denominator row into Q_i(t), and write Q_i=C_i+t R_i. The Horner vector u_i=sum_j R_ij(B)Y_j obeys B u_i=-Y C_i^T. RHS.md proves how a basis of ker_left(C_V) recovers the original A kernel, including when its Schirokauer part is zero. This is a concrete changed recovery, not a claim that an arbitrary finite approximant basis automatically supplies a production generator. The finite pm_toy and rhs_toy tests cover their stated synthetic cases.

## Scalar polynomial multiplication

Canonical coefficients lie in [0,r), r<2^255. For lengths<=T+1, encode coefficient a_i at bit576i. For T=2^49, every convolution coefficient is <(T+1)r^2<2^560, so slots cannot carry into one another. A single integer multiplication of the encoded operands returns the exact polynomial convolution; extract slots and reduce modulo r. The product uses at most18T+18 64-bit limbs. The inherited H-FFT function F(z)=8.5 z log2(z)^2+1200+504z beyond2^26 limbs is charged for the complete integer product, including its distributed transfers. At recursion order l, a scalar product has up to2l+1 output coefficients. Charge raw outer coefficient work by32768l, including every coefficient reduction, input packing, extraction, matrix additions and outer transfers. The4096T term in the priced formula below is part of a global128*k^3 envelope, not a claim that one product has onlyT reductions. Use a generic nine-limb Barrett reduction, not the narrower specialized row reducer. Let mu=floor(2^832/r). For z<2^576 take q=floor(z*mu/2^832), then z-q*r lies in[0,2r); one subtraction canonicalizes it. The9-by10 and6-by4 limb products need114 word partial products, at most20 instructions each, plus fewer than1000 for extraction, carries, copies and corrections: below4096. mu is built once and charged in setup. The finite packing test also checks this Barrett formula at full9-limb inputs. The optional packing toy compares this representation with independent schoolbook multiplication at the production r, lengths1..128. It proves neither the large-size integer-multiplication price nor that price's extrapolation.

## Explicit upper accounting

Here N<=2^57.2346 and the conservative L<=559020000000000 gives T=2^49 and h=log2(T)=49. The algorithm processes actual L, not a series falsely padded to T; T only bounds the recursion tree. At each internal node of order l, two polynomial matrix products use at most2k^3 scalar polynomial products of lengths<=l+1. Each costs F(18l+18)+32768l. At a depth the sum of orders is<=2T and integer-product costs sum to<=4F(18T+18), using monotonicity of F(z)/z for z>=36 and the explicit1200 term. Thus a whole internal depth costs at most8k^3 F(18T+18)+131072k^3 T.

Base elimination uses at most16k^3 bounded field operations at<=1024 scalar instructions each and at mostk inversions at<=2^20 each. For k=8517 this is below2^16 k^3 per leaf, hence at most65536k^3 T for all leaves. Combining h depths and leaves gives

 k^3 [8h F(18T+18)+131072hT+65536T]
 <128 k^3 h [F(18T+18)+4096T].

The inequality holds already for h>=1: the priced RHS has128hF+524288hT and leaves large positive slack. It therefore covers the raw32768l coefficient allowance (including up to2l+1 reductions); no factor-of-two coefficient count is discarded. This is an explicit upper instruction envelope for a schoolbook matrix-product PM-Basis.

## Memory and support

Even storing several full k-by-k degree-L matrices uses <2^90 bytes (k<2^14, L<2^49, 32-byte field coefficients and bounded recursion multiplicity). Distributed arrays use labeled chunks of at most2^60 bytes; charge owner/offset manipulation and copying within the4096-per-coefficient allowance. H-FFT's504-per-limb distributed transfer charge is retained. Krylov/Horner checkpoints grow from4*17 to4*325 vectors, still below2^80 bytes. These bounds fit the inherited total2^105-byte core peak, dominated by descent, and2^106-byte submitted memory bound. Work is summed across all owners.
