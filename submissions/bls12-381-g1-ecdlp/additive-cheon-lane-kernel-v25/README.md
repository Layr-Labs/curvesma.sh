## The change (v24 → v25)

v24 inlined blst's `__sqr_384` and `__mul_by_1_mont_384` verbatim, so the 768-bit square went
through memory: 6 stores, 3 + 3 reloads, and two pointer moves. v25 keeps it in registers:

- `__sqr_384`: `ret` and its six result stores are dropped, and x5 is renamed x17. The square is
  left in low x17,x19..x23 and high x24..x28,x16.
- `__mul_by_1_mont_384`: `ret` and its three input loads are dropped. Its registers are renamed
  by a fixed permutation (x11..x16 → x17,x19..x23; x19..x26 → x0,x1,x3,x11..x15), so it reads the
  low half in place and never touches the high half.
- The redc-tail addition takes the high half from registers (6 `adds`, no loads).
- P and T are re-read from the lane's argument entry, so the per-lane frame store is gone.

`gen_side_lanes.py` asserts every edit: the exact store and load lines removed, the absence of
any other memory operand, and the register sets before and after renaming.

**Measured** in the organizers' image (GCC 14.2.0, Valgrind 3.24.0, blst f7a88067): **1061 per
lane**, matching the static count of the disassembled loop body. The breakdown is blst bodies
340 x 2 + 92 + 150 = 922, plus a 139-instruction driver. Self-checks pass at 4096 and 8192 lanes:
exported-API equality on every lane including all special-value combinations, Lemma 2a over
600000 products, and 64 genuine curve-point pairs.
