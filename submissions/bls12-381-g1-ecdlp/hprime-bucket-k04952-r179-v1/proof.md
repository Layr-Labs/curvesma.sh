## 1. Problem and reduction

Given T = [s]P0 and A_i = [s^i]P0 for i = 1..n, n >= 2^20, recover s. Let d = 2^20. Since v_2(r-1) = 32, d | r-1 and 6d | r-1. H = {x^d : x in F_r^*} has order m = (r-1)/d, log2 m = 234.857089. z = s^d is uniform in H and A_d = [z]P0 is an input. Only T, A_1 and A_d are read, so the same W applies to every n >= 2^20.

Lemma 1 (recovery of s from z). Twenty successive square roots in F_r (Tonelli-Shanks, each at most 2369 F_r multiplications, Section 4) give one t with t^d = z. Then s = t * zeta for one of the 2^20 d-th roots of unity zeta = g^k, g a fixed primitive 2^20-th root. Write k = i + 2^10 j: compute the 2^10 points [t g^i]P0 and the 2^10 points [g^(-2^10 j)]A_1 (2^11 scalar multiplications, 2^23 units), sort the first list by x and look each point of the second up in it. Charged at 2^24 units in the ledger.

Lemma 2 (mu6 in H). The group mu6 = {+-1, +-omega, +-omega^2} of sixth roots of unity lies in H because 6d | r-1 (asserted by the script). On this j = 0 curve [omega](x, y) = (beta x, y) and [-1](x, y) = (x, -y), so the six points [u]X, u in mu6, share the unordered pair {y, p-y}.

## 2. Algorithm A

H-primes. A prime q is an H-prime if q^m = 1 mod r. The walk list W is the set of H-primes w_0 < w_1 < ... above 2^39, taken in order until L of them are listed (L < 2^51.6292) (last element about 2^77.4, and at most 2^78 by H2). Base H-primes a are the first 2^65 H-primes above 2^80, the first 2^64 for the baby side and the next 2^64 for the giant side; the search that finds them H-tests at most the first 2^86 primes above 2^80, all of which lie in (2^80, 2^80 + 2^92] (H2, Section 6). Every scalar x = a w is below 2^93 x 2^78 = 2^171 < r, so x is a well-defined element of H and (a, w) is recovered from x by unique factorisation.

Chains. Chain a holds X_j = [a w_j]Q, with Q = P0 on the baby side and Q = A_d on the giant side. X_{j+1} = X_j + [a g_j]Q with g_j = w_{j+1} - w_j is even and, by H2', at most 2^33 except for at most 16 gaps of at most 2^34, each split into two table steps (Section 3); the table G_a[g/2] = [a g]Q has 2^32 affine entries (96 bytes each, 2^38.6 bytes) and is built by repeated addition of [2a]Q in 2^16 strands. Each strand starts with one scalar multiplication; strand 0 starts at k = 2 (entry k = 1, the point [2a]Q itself, is the one extra scalar multiplication in the ledger), so the first addition of every strand is [2ka]Q + [2a]Q with k >= 2. The chain itself runs as 2^16 lockstep segments of at most ceil(L / 2^16) < 2^35.630 steps (segment starts by scalar multiplication), so each round is 2^16 independent incomplete affine additions sharing one Montgomery inversion. Each addition is the reference operation (batched 6M); the round's one F_p inversion is not part of the 6M convention and is charged separately (Section 4).

Lemma 3 (no degenerate addition). X_j = +-[a g_j]Q would need w_j = +-g_j in H, impossible since w_j > 2^39 > g_j > 0 and w_j is prime. So no doubling or point at infinity occurs in the chains or the table strands (table strands add [2a]Q to [2ka]Q with 2 <= k < 2^32: equality would need k = +-1 mod r, false). The toy builds its small tables by scalar multiplication, so it exercises the chain half of this lemma only; it asserts a non-zero denominator at every batched step.

Key. The record for X = (x, y) is (key, id): key = bits 1..192 of the even one of y and p - y (192 bits), id = 64-bit chain index. 32 bytes. By Lemma 2 the six points [u]X have the same key.

Lemma 4 (distinct ratios). Baby point [a w]P0 equals [u][a' w']A_d = [u a' w' z]P0 for some u in mu6 iff z = a w / (u a' w') in F_r. Such a pair has equal keys by Lemma 2, but the converse does not hold: the key keeps only 192 bits of y, so a truncated-key match does not imply the same orbit. It is a candidate, which the charged false-match handling (Section 3) and the scalar verification [z]P0 = A_d (Section 2) resolve. As fractions of integers, the N^2 ratios a w / (a' w') are pairwise distinct except on the diagonal w = w' (fraction 1/L of pairs, below 2^-51.62), by unique factorisation with a, a' in disjoint sets. Their images in H, and the six mu6 multiples of each, can coincide; Lemma 4 only rules out the trivial repeats. That the 6 N^2 (1 - 1/L) candidates cover z like independent uniform elements of H, to within the 0.0005 allowance stated in Section 6, is heuristic H1, and the success line rests on it (support: an exact census of the candidate set on toy primes, Section 6).

Order of the sides. The whole baby side runs first: every baby record is generated, routed and inserted before any giant chain starts, and a giant record is only ever compared against a complete table. The 2^20 generating nodes each run one chain at a time (2^44 baby chains each, then 2^44 giant chains each), holding one gap table at a time, which is the generating-node figure of at most 2^39.1715 B.

Routing. 2^84 owner nodes, node index = key bits 0..83, read as three 28-bit fields (t, u, v). Each generating node scatters records into 2^28 send blocks of 16 records (512 B) by t; a full block is posted to the hop-1 relay for t, which is owner node (t, 0, 0). That node re-scatters the records it receives by u into its own 2^28 send blocks and posts full blocks to the hop-2 relay for (t, u), owner node (t, u, 1), which re-scatters by v and posts to the owner (t, u, v). Every node has one send array of 2^28 blocks and three receive FIFOs, one per hop, and the relay work is executed at the relay and charged per record (listing in Section 4). Three hops, each a data-independent copy of 32 bytes plus addressing, posting and polling. Within an owner node, baby records are inserted into a direct-mapped head array of 2^33 8-byte slots indexed by key bits 84..116, chained through a pool of 2^33 32-byte records (capacity above 2.58 x the mean, which is below 2^31.63). The node index and the head slot are implied by where a record sits, so a pool record stores key bits 117..191 (75 bits), the 64-bit chain id and a 33-bit link to the next pool record, the rest spare; a head slot is a 33-bit pointer to the first pool record and a 7-bit count. A head slot holds at most 64 baby records; a 65th makes the node report fail. Giant records are not stored: a giant record reads its head slot and compares its 75 remaining key bits with each pooled record on the chain (8 instructions per comparison: load 24 bytes, compare, branch, pointer advance, and a decrement of the node's comparison counter). Equal key gives a candidate record (key, id, id'): the full 192-bit key, reassembled from the node index, the slot and the stored bits, and both chain ids, 40 bytes, appended to the node's candidate list of 2^16 slots. A 2^16 + 1st candidate makes the node report fail.

Per-pair verification. After the giant side, every owner node reports its candidate count through the status tree below; if the total exceeds 2^44 the run outputs fail. Otherwise each 40-byte candidate is copied into one 512-byte block for each of the two generating nodes g(id) and g(id'), where g(id) is the top 20 bits of the chain id, posted and polled (charged 128 instructions per candidate). Each generating node has a candidate receive area of 2^24 slots x 40 B; a 2^24 + 1st candidate makes it report fail. Then each of the two chains is re-walked once, linearly, from its start to its end by the generating node that owns it (its gap table rebuilt, its strands and segments restarted, all charged as in the table), and at every step the key of the point is extracted and compared with the retained key: the 47 instructions of walk overhead from the baby listing plus 32 (parity select and key extraction 23, compare 8, position bookkeeping 1), 2079 per step with the addition. The positions whose key equals the retained key are kept, at most 4 per chain; a fifth makes the run output fail. g(id') sends its kept values a' w' to g(id) as one 168-byte block (40 B header plus 4 x 32 B; copy, post and poll charged at 256 instructions). g(id) now holds at most 4 values a w and at most 4 values a' w', so at most 16 pairs, and for each pair the six z = a w / (u a' w') are tested by [z]P0 = A_d: at most 96 scalar multiplications per candidate, all at g(id). On a hit g(id) runs Lemma 1 and writes s into its status record. Nothing is ever compared against the whole other chain, so the re-walk is O(L) per chain, never L^2. A wrong candidate never produces a wrong answer. Charged in full for every candidate up to the cap (Section 3).

Node overflow and caps, all enforced locally. An owner node fails if it receives more than 2^33 baby records, if any head slot exceeds 64 records, if its candidate list exceeds 2^16, or if its comparison counter, preset to cap_node = ceil(1.01 x the node mean), below 2^30.273, reaches zero; a generating node fails if its candidate receive area exceeds 2^24; a node that fails stops and the run outputs fail. The global candidate cap is 2^44 (checked from the per-node counts before any re-walk), and a re-walk fails if one chain carries the retained key at more than 4 steps. The work charged is the maximum: every record, 2^84 x cap_node comparisons, every candidate up to the cap with the full 96-multiplication recovery.

Status collection. Every node keeps a 48-byte status record: s if found (32 B), then a fail flag (1 bit), its candidate count (17 bits; a node fails above 2^16) and its own baby and giant counts (34 bits each, at most 2^33 before a node fails), 86 bits in the remaining 16 B. Each node checks its own baby and giant caps, so the counts are not summed up the tree: a combined record holds the OR of the fail flags, the sum of the candidate counts saturated at 2^45 (46 bits; only the comparison with the 2^44 global cap uses it) and one found s, if any. It is collected three times, after the baby side, after the giant side and after recovery, through a fan-in-256 tree over all 2^84 + 2^20 + 2^30 nodes (depth 11): a node posts its record, or the combined record of its 256 children, to its parent. Charged at 2^12 instructions per node per pass in the ledger.

## 3. Sizes, caps and success

N = L 2^64 with L the least integer such that 6 N^2 >= 0.4952 m: L < 2^51.6292, N < 2^115.6292, K = 6 N^2 / m, at least 0.4952 and below 0.4952 + 2^-57.6. Under H1 the probability that z is among the 6 N^2 (1 - 1/L) off-diagonal candidates is at least 1 - exp(-K) - 0.0005; 1 - exp(-K) rounded down is 0.390550 (Section 6).

Comparisons. 2^117 head slots in total, load lambda = N / 2^117, below 0.387 per slot. The giant side makes sum over slots of (baby count x giant count) comparisons, mean N^2 / 2^117, below 2^114.26, per node below 2^30.26 against the node cap, 1.01 x the node mean rounded up (below 2^30.273). Three per-node tails under H3, each already multiplied by the number of nodes or slots: (i) a head slot with more than 64 baby records, Poisson(lambda) tail at 65, 2^-274.6 over all 2^117 slots; (ii) a node's baby or giant count more than 2^-10 (0.098 %) off its mean, Chernoff, 2^-1438 over all nodes (both counts within 2^-10 puts the conditional mean at most (1 + 2^-10)^2 - 1 < 0.196 % above the nominal, and 0.196 % + 0.8 % < 1 %); (iii) given (i) and (ii), the node's comparisons are a sum of its giant records' independent slot draws, each a baby count in [0, 64] with second moment lambda + lambda^2 (the ledger takes 4 x this), so Bernstein with M = 64 and deviation 0.8 % of the mean gives 2^-10285 over all nodes. Every tail in this document is rounded towards the larger probability. The 1 % cap is therefore essentially never reached and the charge 2^84 x cap_node is a bound on the total.

False matches. Pairs with equal 192-bit key but different mu6 orbit: mean N^2 / 2^192, below 2^39.26, cap 2^44, Chebyshev tail below 2^-47.6 under H3. True matches: I budget 2^20 of them inside the same cap. Off the diagonal (w != w') their number is Poisson under H1 with mean at most K (below 0.4952 + 2^-57.6), and it exceeds the budget with probability below 2^-20521935. On the diagonal it is not Poisson. If z = a / (u a') for one baby base a, one giant base a' and one u in mu6, then at every walk position w the baby point [a w]P0 and the giant point [a' w]A_d = [u^-1 a w]P0 lie in one mu6 orbit, so this one event gives L > 2^51.62 true matches, more than the budget and more than the 2^44 cap (L > 2^44 is asserted by the script). In that event the run outputs fail: the candidate counts that the status tree collects after the giant side total at least L > 2^44, unless a node has already failed on one of its own caps, and that check comes before any candidate is forwarded or re-walked. These z form a set of at most 6 n_b^2 = 6 x 2^128 elements of H, with n_b = 2^64 base primes per side, and z is uniform in H, so the event has probability at most 6 x 2^128 / m, below 2^-104.27; it is a separate failure term in the success line below. Outside that event, per node the candidate count is Poisson with mean (N^2 / 2^192 + 2^20) / 2^84, below 2^-44.74, so the chance that any of the 2^84 nodes exceeds its 2^16 candidate slots is below 2^-2932113. Per generating node the forwarded candidates have mean 2 (N^2 / 2^192 + 2^20) / 2^20, below 2^20.26, against a 2^24-slot receive area; the chance that any of the 2^20 generating nodes exceeds it is below 2^-40381628.

Re-walk key repeats. Under H3 a chain of L steps carries some key twice with probability about L^2 / 2^193, below 2^-89.7, and carries one key at more than 4 steps with probability below L^5 / (5! 2^768); over all 2^65 chains that is below 2^-451. Both are far inside the 2^-40 budget below. The step with the retained key is found by the linear re-walk because the key is a function of the point and the chain is deterministic from its start.

Node overflow. Mean below 2^31.63 baby records per node, capacity 2^33 (above 2.58 x). Under the binomial form of H3 the Chernoff bound per node is below 2^-(3.3 x 10^9); times 2^84 nodes it is still astronomically small. I budget 2^-20 for modelling error.

Gaps. The walk list lies in (2^39, 2^78] (H2), where the mean gap between H-primes is at most 2^20 ln 2^78 = 2^25.76, so the expected number of gaps above 2^33 among its L - 1 gaps is below 2^-166.9 (exponential gaps, H2'); a larger gap is split into two table steps and the ledger reserves 2^69 units for 16 such splits per chain; a separate row charges what the reserve's reference price leaves out, the 47-instruction walk overhead of each reserved split addition and the 2 x 16 split steps of each re-walked candidate pair at cap, at their maximum although this path has expected count below 2^-166.9. The list construction checks the gaps and stops the run with no scalar if more than 16 exceed 2^33 or one exceeds 2^34. A split delays one segment by one round; Section 4 counts those rounds.

Success claimed, under H1 to H3: 0.390550 - 0.0005 - 2^-20 - 2^-40 - 2^-104.27 > 0.3900 (the script prints 0.390550 after the three failure terms and 0.390050 after the H1 allowance as well, both rounded down, and asserts the claim 0.3900 at most the second and at least 0.39). The lowest census prime at K = 0.4955 and m >= 2^30 (-0.00023204) lies inside the 0.0005 allowance, which is 2.1 times its deviation; the same prime recomputed at the production K = 0.4952 reaches K = 0.495603 with diff -0.00023204 digit for digit, and the lowest from 2^31 up recomputed at K = 0.4952 reaches K = 0.495784 with diff -0.00021477 digit for digit (Section 6). After the allowance the margin over the required 0.39 is 0.000050, 0.2 times the lowest K = 0.4955 prime, 0.6 times the lowest at K = 0.4965 (-0.00007302) and 0.4 times the lowest at K = 0.4975 (-0.00010833) (all printed by the script, Section 6); against the required 0.39 before the allowance the line tolerates 0.000550, 2.3 times the lowest K = 0.4955 prime. ## 4. Instruction listing per record (AArch64 scalar, on top of the 2000-instruction reference addition)

Baby record (listed 147): gap stream read and pointer advance 5; gap stream transport from the storage nodes (4 bytes per step, blocked) 3; table address 2; operand loads and stores for the addition beyond the reference's own 18; batch-inversion prefix and suffix product loads and stores 6; lane control and loop 8; flow control and counters 5; the F_p inversion shared by the round, amortised over the chain's records (below) 4 (exact bound 3.56); parity select and key extraction 23; scatter at 3 hops (block index, slot, bounds, 12 per hop) 36; post and poll of full blocks amortised over 16 records at 3 hops 9; transport copy of 32 bytes at 3 hops (4 ldp/stp pairs plus addressing, 6 per hop) 18; insert (head load, slot count check, link write, record store, head update) 10.

Giant record (listed 143): the same without insert, plus lookup fixed cost 6 (head load, chain pointer test, end). Comparisons are charged separately at 8 each up to the node cap. The hop items are executed at the generating node, the two relays and the owner; all are charged per record wherever they run. The first seven items (gap stream through flow control, 47 instructions) are the walk overhead, and the same 47 are charged on every gap-table addition and every re-walk step; the inversions of the gap tables and the re-walks have their own ledger rows.

Charged: 179 per record on both sides. The listed items sum to at most 146.56: the table lists the shared inversion as the rounded 4, while its exact bound is 3.56 (Section 4 (i)), so the listed total is 147 - 0.44 = 146.56. The charge is 32.44 above that (1.221 x), a margin for items I did not list.

Field inversions. Every F_p inversion is the Fermat power y^(p-2) by binary exponentiation: p - 2 has 381 bits, 229 of them set, so 380 squarings and 228 multiplications, 608 in all, each charged at the 383 instructions the cost model measures for one blst_fp_mul (S = M): 232864 instructions per inversion. The 6M reference convention covers the batch products of Montgomery's trick but not this inversion. F_p inversions occur in four places. (i) Record generation: each round of a chain's 2^16 lockstep segments shares one inversion. A chain runs at most ceil(L / 2^16) rounds, plus at most one more for each of the at most 16 split gaps (Section 3), so its inversions cost 232864 x (ceil(L / 2^16) + 16) / L < 3.56 instructions per record, listed above as 4. (ii) Gap tables: each round of a table's 2^16 strands shares one inversion; a strand makes at most 2^16 additions, so a table needs at most 2^16 rounds, 2^65 x 2^16 inversions over all tables (ledger row). (iii) Re-walks: a re-walked chain runs the rounds of (i) and its rebuilt table the rounds of (ii), so a candidate pair costs at most 2 (ceil(L / 2^16) + 16 + 2^16) inversions, charged for every candidate up to the 2^44 cap (ledger row). (iv) Scalar multiplications: each ends with one inversion, inside its 2^12-unit price. The six z of a kept pair are formed in F_r: one Fermat inversion y^(r-2) per kept giant value (254 squarings and 163 multiplications, 417), at most 4 per candidate, the 8 integer products a w and a' w', and per pair one multiplication for a w / (a' w') and six for the z (one by each element of mu6, which is closed under inversion, so no u is inverted), at most 16 pairs: at most 1788 multiplications per candidate, each charged at 383 instructions (ledger row). No other step inverts: Lemma 1 computes g^(-2^10 j) as g^(2^20 - 2^10 j), and the square roots and H-tests use only multiplications.

Fixed prices, counted from the cost model (383 instructions per multiplication or squaring, S = M, for (i) to (iii); an F_r element has 4 limbs against 6 for F_p and is charged the same 383; the script prints each count under `prices`). (i) Scalar multiplication, charged 2^12 units = 8192000 instructions: an affine point times a scalar below r (255 bits), left to right, takes at most 254 Jacobian doublings (EFD dbl-2009-l for a = 0, 2M + 5S = 7) and 254 mixed additions (EFD madd-2007-bl, 7M + 4S = 11), then one Fermat inversion of Z (608) and 4 multiplications for X / Z^2 and Y / Z^3: 5184 multiplications, 1985472 instructions, so the charge is 4.12 times the count and leaves over 12000 instructions per formula for field additions, loads and the scalar loop. (ii) H-test q^m mod r, charged 2^18 = 262144 instructions: m has 235 bits, 133 of them set, so binary powering takes 234 squarings and 132 multiplications, 366 at 383 = 140178 instructions (charge 1.87 times the count); q comes from the sieve, so no primality test is added. (iii) Lemma 1, charged 2^24 units per run: each of the 20 Tonelli-Shanks square roots (r - 1 = 2^32 Q) takes one power a^((Q - 1) / 2) (221 squarings, 131 multiplications), 2 products and at most 31 rounds of at most 31 + 31 squarings and 3 products, 2369 multiplications; with the fixed non-residue power that is 47734 multiplications for all 20; then the 2^11 scalar multiplications (2^23 units), 3 x 2^10 F_r products for the two lists and 2 x 2^10 x 11 comparisons of 48-byte x-coordinates at 64 instructions for the sort and the lookups: at most 8399059 units, so the charge is 1.99 times the count. (iv) Sieve, charged 128 instructions per integer of each sieved range, itemised here; the script counts every item under `prices.sieve` and asserts the total of each range below the charge. Three ranges are sieved: the walk window (2^39, 2^78], the base window (2^80, 2^80 + 2^92] and (0, 2^47], which makes the sieving primes. Where it runs: on the 2^20 generating nodes, before any chain starts, one segment at a time. A segment is S = 30 x 2^38 integers from a multiple of 30, held as a mod-30 wheel bitmap (8 bits per 30 integers, 2^38 bytes) in the node's gap-table area (96 x 2^32 B, unused until the chains start; asserted). Rounding a range out to whole segments adds at most 30 + S < 2^43 integers, so the ranges are charged as 2^78 + 2^43, 2^92 + 2^43 and 2^47 + 2^43 integers. Where the sieving primes live: the primes from 7 to isqrt(2^80 + 2^92) < 2^46.0002, fewer than 2^41.34 by Rosser and Schoenfeld, 8 bytes each, are spread over the 2^30 storage nodes by rank: the i-th sieving prime in increasing order goes to storage node i mod 2^30, so each node holds at most ceil(count / 2^30) of them, which is at most 2580 (printed as `primes_per_holder_at_most`), beside each node's share of the lists and inside its 2^40 B (asserted per node; the memory total does not move, since storage nodes are counted at their full allocation). How they reach the sieving node: for each segment every holder copies its primes into 512-byte blocks of 64 and posts them to the generating node that sieves the segment, on a fixed schedule that does not depend on the data (for a walk segment only its primes up to 2^39); at most 2^30 x 41 blocks per segment, each listed at 116 instructions (copy at the holder, 32 ldp/stp pairs, 64; post and poll 48, as on the record route; the receiver's block loop and prime count 4) and charged 256. Crossing-off does not depend on order, so the node processes blocks as they arrive and ends the segment when its count reaches the known number of primes. Start offsets are not stored: for each prime and segment the node computes them from the segment start lo (two limbs, below 2^93), 130 instructions: load p 1; 30 p 2; lo mod 30 p from the high limb and six chunks of the low limb with udiv and msub 32; p mod 30 and its class-table row 4; for each of the 8 wheel classes c, the first multiple of p in class c at or after lo (from a table of the k with k p = c mod 30), wrapped into [0, 30 p), its byte index by multiply-high and shift, the bit mask and the loop entry test, 11 each, 88; the prime loop 3. Crossings-off: in class c the multiples of p are 30 p integers apart, which is p bytes, so one crossing is ldrb, orr, strb, add, cmp and branch, 6 instructions. A prime crosses at most S / (30 p) + 1 integers of each class in a segment, so per integer the crossings are below 8/30 times the sum of 1/p (below 0.674 for the walk window's primes up to 2^39 and 0.718 for the base window's, by Rosser and Schoenfeld's bound on the sum of 1/p), plus 8 per prime per segment, which is counted separately. Survivors: the scan reads the bitmap 8 bytes at a time (8 instructions per 240 integers) and only over the range itself, so the padding of a range's first and last segment is never read; each survivor costs 12 (bit extraction 4, its value in two limbs 3, a store into an H-test queue of 2^20 16-byte entries in the stream buffers with its full test 4, loop 1; on a full queue the node H-tests the entries and resumes) plus 8 for emitting a 16-byte entry to a storage node (the record route's 9 per 32 bytes), charged on every survivor although only H-primes and the sieving primes are emitted; the H-test reads q from the queue inside its own price. Clearing the bitmap costs 7 per 64 bytes, and each segment 2^10 for its start, counters and end. The range (0, 2^47] needs no stream: each of the nodes that sieve its 18 segments first sieves (0, 2^24] plainly (charged 64 per integer) to hold the primes up to 2^24 itself, and after its segment restores those of them that the sieve crossed off as their own first multiple (16 each); 2, 3 and 5 are the wheel and are not sieving primes. Totals per charged integer (printed, each item rounded up): at most 65.9 for the base window (43.672 per prime per segment, 16.125 crossings at 8 per prime per segment, 4.306 the sum part, 1.367 blocks, 0.394 survivors), at most 6.47 for the walk window and at most 4.01 for (0, 2^47], so the charge of 128 is 1.94 times the largest count. The score keeps 2^95.14 units of rounding headroom (2^score - W, printed), so up to 17629 further instructions per sieved integer, in all three ranges, would leave the claimed score unchanged.

## 6. Heuristics

H1 (coverage). The 6 N^2 (1 - 1/L) distinct rationals a w / (u a' w') hit z nearly like independent uniform elements of H. Precisely: let R be the set of the mu6 multiples in H of the off-diagonal ratios a w / (a' w'), w != w'. The lists do not depend on the challenge and z = s^d is uniform in H, so the probability that z is an off-diagonal candidate is exactly |R| / m; a z among the diagonal values a / (u a') is the separate failure term of Section 3. Independent uniform elements in the number of these ratios would cover at least 1 - exp(-K (1 - 2^-51.62)) of H in expectation, which differs from 1 - exp(-K) by less than 2^-52. The census below has primes under that value (136 of 504 at K = 0.4955 and m in [2^30, 2^31), lowest -0.000232; 10 of 32 at K = 0.4965 and m in [2^30, 2^31), lowest -0.000073; 11 of 32 at K = 0.4975 there, lowest -0.000108; 13 of 48 at K = 0.50 there, lowest -0.00021), so H1 is not stated as that bound. H1 is the statement that at production size |R| / m >= 1 - exp(-K) - 0.0005, a coverage within the census deviation: at K = 0.4955 no census prime from m = 2^30 up falls more than 0.00024 below 1 - exp(-K) (1907 primes, lowest -0.000232); at K = 0.4965 no census prime from m = 2^30 up falls more than 0.00008 below 1 - exp(-K) (74 primes, lowest -0.000073); at K = 0.4975 none from 2^30 up falls more than 0.00011 below (74 primes, lowest -0.000108); at K = 0.50 none from 2^30 up falls more than 0.00022 below (128 primes, lowest -0.00021), none from 2^28 up more than 0.0005, one of the 20 primes at 2^26 does (-0.00052), primes at 2^24 and below fall further, and the spread across primes falls about as m^-1/2 (Trend, Margin). The two maintained anchors recomputed at the production K = 0.4952 below confirm the K = 0.4955 rows apply at K = 0.4952 as well: the lowest K = 0.4955 prime reaches K = 0.495603 with diff -0.00023204 at both K targets, and the lowest from 2^31 up reaches K = 0.495784 with diff -0.00021477 at both K targets. The success line carries the 0.0005 as an explicit term (Section 3); a shortfall up to 0.000550 would still leave the success above the required 0.39 (printed by the script as `coverage_deficit_tolerated`). Lemma 4 removes only the trivial repeats (equal fractions), so H1 stays a heuristic. Its support is an exact count of R on toy primes, below. That census covers m up to about 2^35 (2^35.24 at K = 0.4955, 2^34.9 at the other K), while production has m = 2^234.857; H1 remains a heuristic, supported by the census and not proved by it.

Census (mode `census`). For a toy prime r = 6 c 2^k + 1 with m = (r - 1) / 2^k, H the subgroup of index 2^k, the script builds the lists by the production rules: an H-prime is a prime q with q^m = 1 mod r; the walk list is the first L H-primes above w_min = w_max^(39/77.4) (production: 2^39 and about 2^77.4); the base primes are the first 2 n_b H-primes above 2^2.6 w_max (production: 2^80 over 2^77.4), the first n_b baby and the next n_b giant; n_b is within 10 % of N^(64/115.6503) (split 0.5534; this package's split, 64/115.6292 = 0.5535, would move n_b by under 1 % at the census sizes), and L is the least integer with K = 6 n_b^2 L (L - 1) / m >= the target K (0.4952, 0.4955, 0.4965, 0.4975 or 0.50 for the rows below), so K counts only the off-diagonal pairs, as production's K (1 - 1/L) does. It takes the discrete log in H of every listed prime (Pohlig-Hellman with baby-step giant-step, each result checked by exponentiation), so the mu6 coset of a w / (a' w') is the integer e_a - e_a' + e_w - e_w' mod m/6; it marks every such coset in a byte array of m/6 entries and counts |R| exactly, with no sampling, then tests 40 random z and candidates against the array by residue arithmetic. The count was also checked offline by other means: enumeration of all of H on 9 primes with m below 2^17, a recount from residues on 180 rows (60 primes at three K) with m below 2^21, and the ec toy below, whose exact success is |R u D| / m = 0.39404 truncated to five places (D the diagonal set; the toy counts diagonal hits as successes); a replay of that toy's seeded draws of z finds z in R u D in 161 of 400 and 779 of 2000 trials, the toy's own success counts (Section 7). Each row prints diff = |R| / m - (1 - exp(-K)) at the K its prime reached.

Walk list: the search sieves the integers in (2^39, 2^78] and H-tests its primes in increasing order, stopping at the L-th H-prime. H2 states that at least L (below 2^51.6292) of them are H-primes; the expected count is above 2^52.24, at least 1.53 times L. Base primes: the search sieves the 2^92 integers in (2^80, 2^80 + 2^92] and H-tests their primes in increasing order, at most 2^86 of them as charged, stopping at the 2^65-th H-prime. The window holds more than 2^86.005 primes, so the search examines at most the first 2^86 primes above 2^80, never a prime beyond the sieved window. H2 states that at least 2^65 of those first 2^86 primes are H-primes; the expected count is 2^66, twice the need. Gaps (H2'): among the L listed walk H-primes at most 16 consecutive gaps exceed 2^33 and none exceeds 2^34; with exponential gaps of mean 2^20 ln q <= 2^25.76 in the window, the expected number above 2^33 is below 2^-166.9. Sanity on this r: 402 of the 6542 primes below 2^16 are 16th powers (expected 408.875) and 27 are 256th powers (expected 25.55).

H3 (key bits). The 192-bit keys of the 2N records behave like independent uniform strings across mu6 orbits: node and slot occupancies are binomial and cross-orbit key equality has probability 2^-192. Support: the toy bins truncated keys into 32 pseudo-nodes x 16 heads and the chi-square statistic matches its expectation (31.32 against 31, standard error 0.42). That agreement is consistent with the H3 occupancy model at toy size; it does not certify the production independence and concentration that every cap and tail bound in Section 3 uses, and those rest on H3 as a stated premise.
