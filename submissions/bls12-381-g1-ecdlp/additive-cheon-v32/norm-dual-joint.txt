Primitive rectangle lemma
==========================
A determinant-D lattice has at most floor(2AB/D)+1 integer-primitive
points in a positive rectangle (0,A]x(0,B].
Order the primitive rays by slope; adjacent determinants are nonzero
multiples of D. The disjoint triangles from the origin have area>=D/2
and lie in the rectangle of area AB.
For first coordinate positive and second coordinate signed, the
corresponding bound is <=4AB/D+2, splitting by the sign of the second
coordinate. We apply it only to nonzero coordinates.

PART I. Pruned INNER exactly-one-shared-small theorem
======================================================
Pruning rule: for each used baby/giant pair (a,c), among coverage
indices with x=a p and y=c p for p in their shared prefix, retain
at most one p (and apply the selected rule consistently for all U).
This removes known integer duplicates without changing the support.

INNER here means the shared small prime lies WITHIN one original
coverage triple: either x=a p,y=c p or x'=a' p,y'=c' p.
It does NOT mean p is shared between x,x' or between y,y'.
Assume gcd(P,Q)=p is exactly one small prime, with no common large
prime. Thus the remaining four large coordinates are all distinct,
and the remaining two small coordinates b,b' are coprime.

Claim:
 E_inner,exactly1S,noL,pruned <=48 Pup^2 D(X1^2).
At production this is <=4.10202264460009e-8 B0.

Proof for the first inner placement:
Write x=a p,y=c p,x'=a' b,y'=c' b'.
After canceling p, the collision is
 b' A ==rho b A' (mod r), A=a c', A'=a' c.
The large-prime disjointness and exactly-one-small gcd ensure
gcd(A,A')=1, and A,A'<=X1^2.
For each fixed ordered pair b,b' there are at most D(X1^2)
primitive positive pairs A,A'. Each product A has one baby and one
giant prime, so tier separation and disjoint blocks recover its
factor assignment uniquely; likewise for A'.
The retained p is then uniquely determined by (a,c), if any.
Hence there is no extra sum over possible p.
There are four nonreal root ratios, six ordered U-pairs per ratio,
and two inner placements: coefficient 4*6*2=48.
Shared-root rho=1 wrapped cases and rho=-1 cases are excluded by

PART III. Prefix-preserving ordinary divisor-minimum F theorem
==============================================================
This is PER ONE nonreal rho and ONE same-side large-prime block.
Let a,a' be distinct primes in (X1/2,X1], both from that block.
Let t_a>=0 be actual row prefix lengths, sum_a t_a=n.
Put w(a,a')=min(t_a,t_a') when their chi classes agree, otherwise0.
The two same-side shared-small placements use this exact weight.

Let d,e be coprime positive integers <X1/2. Define
 Lambda_de(a,a')={ (s,t) in Z^2:
                  a s==rho a' t (mod r), d|s, e|t }.
Its determinant is r d e.
For a canonical shortest nonzero vector z of this lattice in sup norm,
choose first coordinate positive. Define F_de(T) as the total weight
of pairs (a,a') whose shortest vector has norm<=T.
Assume T<r and T<X1^2/8; production also has 3X1^2<r.

CONTENT STEP.
The integer content g=gcd(z1,z2) divides de.
Otherwise some prime factor of g could be divided out while
preserving both divisibility conditions and the modular congruence,
contradicting shortestness. The prime r cannot occur because T<r.
Write z=g(s,t), with gcd(s,t)=1. Set
 d0=d/gcd(d,g), e0=e/gcd(e,g).
Since (d,e)=1 and g|de, d0 e0=de/g. The primitive core has height<=T/g
and satisfies d0|s,e0|t and a s==rho a' t.

NONCROSS CASE a' does not divide s.
Map (s,t) to (s,a't). It is primitive and belongs to the determinant
r d0 e0 lattice a u==rho v, d0|u,e0|v.
It lies in (0,T/g]x[-X1 T/g,X1 T/g].
Its second coordinate has at most two eligible a' prime divisors:
three such primes would exceed (X1/2)^3, whereas X1 T<(X1/2)^3.
For fixed a, the primitive signed-rectangle bound therefore allows
at most 8X1 (T/g)^2/(r d0 e0)+4 antecedent a'.
Use w(a,a')<=t_a and sum_a t_a=n, then sum over g|de:
 F_de,nc(T)<= [8n X1/r] [sigma_-1(de)/(de)] T^2
              +4n tau(de).

CROSS CASE a' divides s.
Write s=a'v. If also a|t, canceling a a' gives v==rho(t/a),
with both absolute coordinates <2(T/g)/X1. Its nonzero norm is
<12T^2/X1^2<3X1^2/16<r, impossible.
Thus a does not divide t. Map to the primitive fixed-root point
 (U,V)=(a v,t), so U==rho V, d0|U,e0|V.
It has 0<U<=2T/g, |V|<=T/g.
For fixed (U,V), there are at most two eligible a prime divisors
(in fact at most one under the stated T bound).
For each resulting a, drop reconstruction/height restrictions and use
 sum_{a'} w(a,a')<=sum_{a'}t_a'=n.
The signed-rectangle lemma and summation over g then give
 F_de,cross(T)<= [16n/r] [sigma_-1(de)/(de)] T^2
                 +4n tau(de).
Furthermore F_de,cross(T)=0 whenever 12T^2<r, because
U^2 +/- UV+V^2<=12(T/g)^2<r.

These are counts for ORDINARY shortest vectors, not merely the first
primitive vector. The content step is essential to that distinction.
The bounds preserve the actual prefix weights; no division by d is
inserted after dropping masks.
Also F_de(T)<=F_1,1(T), since Lambda_de is a sublattice of Lambda_1,1.
Combining the two bounds by taking their minimum can improve the
constant-term integration in a lattice discrepancy estimate.

This theorem does NOT itself prove a Selberg remainder or RSS bound.

Independent rank-two audit agreed with these content/noncross/cross
