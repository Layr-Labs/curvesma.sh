2. Short-lattice removal and a fully paid list-prime exception

Let
 t0=floor((r-1)/(2Z))=4592394459902008069420521096196,
 z0=floor(2t0/X1)=1899369.
The code checks 3X1^2X2<r. For a coefficient pair a,a', let Lambda be

and lambda_infty its first sup-norm minimum. If lambda_infty<=t0, take
a shortest ordinary primitive vector v. It is primitive because its
content can be divided out modulo the prime r to shorten it.
For any actual primitive positive variable point w in [1,Z]^2,
 |det(v,w)|<=2t0*Z<r.
That determinant is a multiple of r, so it is zero. Since both points
are ordinary primitive, w=+/-v and ||w||infty<=t0. Each coordinate is a
large prime >X1/2 times its list prime. Thus both remaining list primes
are <=z0. Consequently all such actual collisions are included in the
following explicit exception. Remove the ENTIRE bad coefficient lattice
from subsequent majorants; retaining its long nonprimitive rays would
unnecessarily enlarge the artificial sieve sequence.

Here is a self-contained exception argument, with NO primality
distribution or coprimality conjecture. Let H_s(z) count entries of side s
whose list prime is <=z, and s_2 count entries with list prime2.
H_B+H_G<=2M*pi(z), B_2+G_2<=2M.

For a fixed shared list prime p, the integer convolution
 c_p(v)=#{(a,y):p in P_a on baby side, y in G, a*y=v}
is BOOLEAN by unique factorization: v has one baby large prime, one giant
large prime, and one list prime. Its marked/even masses are b_p*H_G(z)
and b_p*G_2, with sum_p b_p=n.
Every such v is at most Y=X1^2X2<r/3. A U-orbit has at most three positive
representatives <=Y, one per opposite pair. At most two are odd: three odd
representatives would satisfy a signed cubic-root sum0 modulo r, hence
an integer zero since their absolute sum is <r, impossible by parity.
Thus the nonreal-root graph on odd representatives is a matching, while
the full graph has degree<=2.
For a boolean support with all even points marked, ordered nonreal edges
touching a mark are <=2*(marked mass)+2*(even mass). Odd-odd matching
edges cost at most twice the marked odd mass; edges touching an even point

summing p bounds same-baby exception by12n(H_G+G_2); same-giant similarly.
Therefore, for the TWO same-side placements together,

3. Selected-vector events preserve the divisor density

Select an L1-SHORTEST vector v=(a,b) for each ORIGINAL coefficient lattice,
with first coordinate positive; put ell=||v||infty. Section4 proves
||v||1<=sqrt(2r)<r. It is ordinary primitive: otherwise divide its content
out of the congruence modulo the prime r, making a shorter vector.
The original sup-minimum was trimmed, so ell>t0. No monotonicity of a
selected norm under passing to sublattices is asserted or required.

Elementary primitive fan lemma. In a determinant-D integer lattice, the
number of ordinary primitive points in (0,U]x(0,V] is at most
floor(2UV/D)+1. Order them by angle: distinct primitive points cannot
lie on the same ray; adjacent nonzero determinants are multiples of D.
The resulting disjoint triangles lie in the rectangle. Thus
(number-1)D/2<=UV. Splitting signs gives at most4UV/D+2 points in
(0,U]x[-V,V]. This argument uses ordinary primitive points throughout.

Fix ONE placement, one nonreal rho, and large-coefficient endpoint A
(AB or AG). For a squarefree divisor h of the finite wheel, consider
the ACTUAL selected original directions with h|v1*v2 and ell<=T.
Ordinary primitivity means each p|h divides exactly one coordinate,
giving one of2^omega(h) coprime allocations d*e=h with d|v1,e|v2.
There is NO content sum here: the selected original direction is already
ordinary primitive. This is stronger than using an arbitrary ordinary
minimum of a divisor sublattice, which could have nontrivial content.

For one allocation write the selected direction as (s,t), s>0.
Noncross case: a' does not divide s. The point
 (s,w)=(s,a'*t)
is ordinary primitive, belongs to the determinant-(r*h) lattice
 a*s == rho*w mod r, d|s,e|w,
and lies in (0,T]x[-A*T,A*T]. The small divisors d,e are coprime to
all large primes because h<X1/2. If X1*T<(X1/2)^3, |w| has at most
two eligible a' divisors. The signed fan lemma bounds possible a'
for fixed a by8A*T^2/(r*h)+4.
Since the ACTUAL prefix overlap is <=t_a and sum_a t_a=n, the weighted
event count for this allocation is at most
 8nA*T^2/(r*h)+4n.                                   (3.1)

Cross case a'|s: put U=a*s/a', V=t.
Simultaneous a|t is impossible under12T^2<r*X1^2: cancel both large
primes to get a nonzero cubic-root norm divisible by r but smaller
than r. Thus(U,V) is ordinary primitive, satisfies U==rho*V mod r
with d|U,e|V, and lies in(0,2T]x[-T,T]. Under T<X1^2/8, U has at
most two eligible a divisors. For each root point and each such a,
sum the ACTUAL overlap weights over all a':
 sum_a' min(t_a,t_a')1_sameclass <=sum_a' t_a'=n.
The signed fan lemma therefore bounds this weighted event by
 16n*T^2/(r*h)+4n.                                   (3.2)
It is ZERO for T<sqrt(r/12), because the nonzero norm of U,V is at
most12T^2 and divisible by r. The range T<X1^2/8 and3X1^2<r also
implies the simultaneous-cross exclusion. Both maps use ordinary
primitive selected points and retain actual weights. They do not
replace a class list by an assumed ambient density.

Let I_h=sum w(a,a')/ell over the selected directions with h|v1*v2.
Put C_A=8nA/r,C_x=16n/r,tc=floor(sqrt((r-1)/12)).
Sum(3.1),(3.2) over the2^omega(h) allocations. The layer-integral bound
sum w/ell<=Qbar/T+integral_(t0)^T F(t)/t^2 dt, optimized in its quadratic
part, proves
 I_h <=2sqrt(Qbar*(C_A+C_x))*sqrt(2^omega(h)/h)
       +4n*2^omega(h)*(1/t0+1/tc).                   (3.3)
The cross constant is integrated only from tc, where cross events
can first occur. The certificate checks EVERY optimizing endpoint
T=sqrt(Qbar*h/((C_A+C_x)*2^omega(h))) for EVERY divisor of EVERY
tested wheel lies above t0 and below X1^2/8 and satisfies all norm
and factor-count conditions. Equation(3.3) retains the prime-divisor
density 1/h without a class-density postulate.

4. Row geometry and the L1-shortest direction

Extend the chosen direction v=(a,b) to a lattice basis(v,w),det(v,w)=r.
Rows have det(v,z)=r*j, j integer. Let f(j) be the continuous positive-box
chord length measured in units of v. For a square of side Y,
integral f=Y^2/r,max f<=Y/ell, and the row-index interval length is
 L=(|a|+|b|)*Y/r.
For EVERY integer h>=1, excluding row0,
 S_h=sum_(j!=0,h|j) f(j)
satisfies the SHARP two-sided bound
 Y^2/(r*h)-max f <= S_h <= Y^2/(r*h).                 (4.1)

For same-sign coordinates, the full sampled chord sum is a scaled
interval CROSS-CORRELATION. Exactly, for positive u,v,
 sum_(j integer) (1_(0,u) star 1_(0,v))(j)
 =u*v+min({u},{v})-{u}{v} >=u*v.
One proof decomposes each interval into full unit pieces plus its
fractional piece; any full-unit term periodizes exactly, and the two
fractional pieces overlap at sample0 by min({u},{v}). After scaling by h
the full sum is at least the integral/h. Row0 has chord f(0)=max f;
deleting it gives the lower bound in(4.1). The upper follows from
monotone right/left Riemann sums away from row0.

For opposite signs the chord is a scaled interval CONVOLUTION, with
exact sampling identity
 sum_j (1_(0,u)*1_(0,v))(j)=u*v-E({u},{v}),
 E(alpha,beta)=alpha*beta if alpha+beta<=1,
              (1-alpha)*(1-beta) otherwise.
Thus the upper in(4.1) holds; the deficit is at most max f, proving
the lower too. Row0 has zero chord here.
These identities hold also when sampled at step h by rescaling.
They were independently algebraically audited and exhaustively checked
in primitive-coefficient-one / unitwheel-row-audit.

On row0 there is at most ONE ordinary primitive point; add it separately.
For nonzero rows, counting all unit-coordinate points is an upper bound
for the actual primitive points.

The chosen L1-shortest vector satisfies
 ||v||1<=sqrt(2r).                                    (4.2)
Self-contained proof: map a half-diamond {||x||1<s/2} of area s^2/2
into a fundamental parallelogram of area r. If s>sqrt(2r), the total
area exceeds r, so two pieces in this projection overlap. Their
preimages differ by a nonzero lattice vector of L1 norm<s. Let
s decrease to sqrt(2r). The vector norm is integer, so
||v||1<=floor(sqrt(2r)), which the certificate uses exactly.
This is an area-pigeonhole proof of the diamond bound, not an
uncomputed lattice constant.

5. Exact row densities and the bad-direction loss

Let Q be squarefree and prime to r, and rho1=phi(Q)/Q.
For a fixed row j, the integer row parameter t maps with j invertibly
to coordinates mod every p|Q because the basis determinant is r.
Put delta_p(j)=1_{p|j}.
If p does NOT divide a*b, the exact proportion of t residues making
both coordinates nonzero mod p is
 g_p(j)=(p-2)/p+(1/p)*delta_p(j).                       (5.1)
The two forbidden roots coincide when j=0 mod p and are distinct otherwise.
If p|a*b, then p divides just one coordinate of v, and
 g_p(j)=(1-1/p)*(1-delta_p(j)).                         (5.2)
Let g_Q(j)=product_p g_p(j)=sum_{h|Q} c_h 1_{h|j}.
Its mean over j is rho1^2, or equivalently sum_h c_h/h=rho1^2.

Let b=omega(gcd(a*b,Q)).
For b=0 all c_h>=0, so(4.1) directly gives
 sum_{j!=0}g_Q(j)f(j)<=rho1^2*Y^2/r.
For b>0, sum c_h=g_Q(0)=0 and
 sum |c_h|=2^b*rho1, so the negative coefficient mass is2^(b-1)*rho1.
Apply the lower side of(4.1) only to those negative coefficients:
 sum_{j!=0}g_Q(j)f(j)
 <=rho1^2*Y^2/r+rho1*2^(b-1)*max f.                       (5.3)
Thus the troublesome inverse-short-vector term occurs only when a wheel
prime divides the selected short direction, and can be bounded by(3.3).

The exact odd-subset identity is
 2^(b-1)*1_(b>0)
 =sum_(h|Q,omega(h) odd)1_(h|a*b).                    (5.4)
There are2^(b-1) odd subsets of a nonempty set of b bad primes, and
none when b=0. Thus no union-bound inflation is needed.
Let a_p=sqrt(2/p). Equation(3.3) sums over these odd divisors with
leading factor
 .5[product_(p|Q)(1+a_p)-product_(p|Q)(1-a_p)]
 =.5 product_(p|Q)(1+a_p),                           (5.5)
since2|Q and a_2=1. The content-constant factor is
 .5[3^k-(-1)^k], k=omega(Q).
Both are exact positive-coefficient divisor sums, so rational outward
bounds for square roots preserve their upper-bound direction.

6. A period-6 average controls point-rounding errors

Pre-sieve primes2 and3 EXACTLY on each row. Let n6(j) be the number of
allowed t residue classes mod6. For each such class, every remaining
wheel prime forbids at most two residues of its free progression variable.
Finite inclusion-exclusion and the elementary interval AP error<=1 give
row-count error at most
 n6(j)*3^(k-2).                                       (6.1)
Indeed the sum of CRT root counts is product_(p>=5)(1+nu_p)<=3^(k-2).

The invertible basis map (j,t) mod6 has exactly phi(6)^2=4 pairs whose
two coordinates are units. Therefore
 sum_{j mod6}n6(j)=4.
For a real interval of row-index length L, splitting integer indices
into periods plus one partial period proves
 sum_j n6(j)<=2L/3+4.                                 (6.2)
This is substantially sharper than charging the worst two classes on
every row. By(4.2), the total row-rounding contribution for one lattice is
at most
 3^(k-2)[(2/3)*floor(sqrt(2r))*Y/r+4]+1.             (6.3)
The final +1 is the possible primitive origin-row point. It must not be
lost when removing that row from chord sums.
