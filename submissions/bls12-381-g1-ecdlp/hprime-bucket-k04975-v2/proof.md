Cost accounting. Work is the sum of instructions executed over all processors (COST_MODEL.md: "Charge total work across processors, including preprocessing, advice construction, random sampling, all trials and failures, sorting, lookup, memory transfers, verification, output and success amplification"; rules.md: "Sum work across processors; do not amortize"). Cluster model, stated here so that every charge below has a data path: nodes with 2^40 bytes of private memory each and no remote-memory instruction; a node reads and writes only its own memory. The only communication is a post, of two kinds: a 512-byte block appended to a receive FIFO of another node, or an 8-byte credit word written into a credit slot of another node. A post is a barrier and one store of a descriptor (destination, FIFO or slot, local source) to the sender's network interface. The receiving interface performs each post whole, writing a block into the FIFO's next slot and then advancing the FIFO's tail word, or writing the credit word; no sender reads or writes a tail. An arriving post, and nothing else, sets the receiving node's event flag, which ends a wfe. Filling the block and the post are charged to the sender, the poll and the read to the receiver; a node that is waiting for a block executes no instructions and is charged nothing, and the wake-ups are charged all the same (Section 4, item 7). This is my model, not a rule of the challenge. Wall time is not claimed.

## 1. Problem and reduction

Given T = [s]P0 and A_i = [s^i]P0 for i = 1..n, n >= 2^20, recover s. Let d = 2^20. Since v_2(r-1) = 32, d | r-1 and 6d | r-1. H = {x^d : x in F_r^*} has order m = (r-1)/d, log2 m = 234.857089. z = s^d is uniform in H and A_d = [z]P0 is an input. Only T, A_1 and A_d are read, so the same W applies to every n >= 2^20.

Lemma 1 (recovery of s from z). Twenty successive square roots in F_r (Tonelli-Shanks, each at most 2369 F_r multiplications, Section 4) give one t with t^d = z. Then s = t * zeta for one of the 2^20 d-th roots of unity zeta = g^k, g a fixed primitive 2^20-th root. Write k = i + 2^10 j: compute the 2^10 points [t g^i]P0 and the 2^10 points [g^(-2^10 j)]A_1 (2^11 scalar multiplications, 2^23 units), sort the first list by x and look each point of the second up in it. Charged at 2^24 units in the ledger.

Lemma 2 (mu6 in H). The group mu6 = {+-1, +-omega, +-omega^2} of sixth roots of unity lies in H because 6d | r-1 (asserted by the script). On this j = 0 curve [omega](x, y) = (beta x, y) and [-1](x, y) = (x, -y), so the six points [u]X, u in mu6, share the unordered pair {y, p-y}.

## 2. Algorithm A

H-primes. A prime q is an H-prime if q^m = 1 mod r. The walk list W is the set of H-primes w_0 < w_1 < ... above 2^39, taken in order until L of them are listed (L < 2^51.6325) (last element about 2^77.4, and at most 2^78 by H2). Base H-primes a are the first 2^65 H-primes above 2^80, the first 2^64 for the baby side and the next 2^64 for the giant side; the search that finds them H-tests at most the first 2^86 primes above 2^80, all of which lie in (2^80, 2^80 + 2^92] (H2, Section 6). Every scalar x = a w is below 2^93 x 2^78 = 2^171 < r, so x is a well-defined element of H and (a, w) is recovered from x by unique factorisation.

Chains. Chain a holds X_j = [a w_j]Q, with Q = P0 on the baby side and Q = A_d on the giant side. X_{j+1} = X_j + [a g_j]Q where g_j = w_{j+1} - w_j is even and, by H2', at most 2^33 except for at most 16 gaps of at most 2^34, each split into two table steps (Section 3); the table G_a[g/2] = [a g]Q has 2^32 affine entries (96 bytes each, 2^38.6 bytes) and is built by repeated addition of [2a]Q in 2^16 strands. Each strand starts with one scalar multiplication; strand 0 starts at k = 2 (entry k = 1, the point [2a]Q itself, is the one extra scalar multiplication in the ledger), so the first addition of every strand is [2ka]Q + [2a]Q with k >= 2. The chain itself runs as 2^16 lockstep segments of at most ceil(L / 2^16) < 2^35.6325 steps (segment starts by scalar multiplication), so each round is 2^16 independent incomplete affine additions sharing one Montgomery inversion. Each addition is the reference operation (batched 6M); the round's one F_p inversion is not part of the 6M convention and is charged separately (Section 4).

Lemma 3 (no degenerate addition). X_j = +-[a g_j]Q would need w_j = +-g_j in H, impossible since w_j > 2^39 > g_j > 0 and w_j is prime. So no doubling or point at infinity occurs in the chains or the table strands (table strands add [2a]Q to [2ka]Q with 2 <= k < 2^32: equality would need k = +-1 mod r, false). The toy builds its small tables by scalar multiplication, so it exercises the chain half of this lemma only; it asserts a non-zero denominator at every batched step.

Key. Coordinates are stored as Montgomery representatives, Y = y 2^384 mod p for y. The record for X = (x, y) is (key, id): key = bits 1..192 of the even one of Y and p - Y (192 bits), id = 64-bit chain index. 32 bytes. The representative of -y is p - Y, so by Lemma 2 the six points [u]X share the pair {Y, p - Y} and have the same key; no conversion out of Montgomery form is made. The toy keys the canonical y, which is the same rule with the multiplier 2^384 replaced by 1.

Lemma 4 (distinct ratios). Baby point [a w]P0 equals [u][a' w']A_d = [u a' w' z]P0 for some u in mu6 iff z = a w / (u a' w') in F_r. Such a pair has equal keys by Lemma 2, but the converse does not hold: the key keeps only 192 bits of y, so a truncated-key match does not imply the same orbit. It is a candidate, which the charged false-match handling (Section 3) and the scalar verification [z]P0 = A_d (Section 2) resolve. As fractions of integers, the N^2 ratios a w / (a' w') are pairwise distinct except on the diagonal w = w' (fraction 1/L of pairs, below 2^-51.63), by unique factorisation with a, a' in disjoint sets. Their images in H, and the six mu6 multiples of each, can coincide; Lemma 4 only rules out the trivial repeats. That the 6 N^2 (1 - 1/L) candidates cover z like independent uniform elements of H, to within the 0.0005 allowance stated in Section 6, is heuristic H1, and the success line rests on it (support: an exact census of the candidate set on toy primes, Section 6).

Order of the sides. The whole baby side runs first: every baby record is generated, routed and inserted before any giant chain starts, and a giant record is only ever compared against a complete table. The 2^20 generating nodes each run one chain at a time (2^44 baby chains each, then 2^44 giant chains each), holding one gap table at a time, which is the generating-node figure of at most 2^39.1715 B.

Routing. 2^84 owner nodes, node index = key bits 0..83, read as three 28-bit fields (t, u, v). Each generating node scatters records into 2^28 send blocks of 16 records (512 B) by t; a full block is posted to the hop-1 relay for t, which is owner node (t, 0, 0). That node re-scatters the records it receives by u into its own 2^28 send blocks and posts full blocks to the hop-2 relay for (t, u), owner node (t, u, 1), which re-scatters by v and posts to the owner (t, u, v). Every node has one send array of 2^28 blocks and three receive FIFOs of 2^27 slots of 512 B, one per hop, and the relay work is executed at the relay and charged per record (listing in Section 4). In use: (t, 0, 0) receives hop 1 from the generating nodes and hop 3 from (t, 0, 1); (t, u, 1) receives hop 2 from (t, 0, 0) only; every other owner receives hop 3 from (t, u, 1) only. No node is both a hop-1 and a hop-2 relay (v = 0 against v = 1). No node posts to itself: a generating node is not an owner, (t, 0, 0) and (t, u, 1) differ in v, and each of the 2^56 hop-2 relays (t, u, 1) inserts (baby) or looks up (giant) its own records, those with v = 1, at once instead of posting them, which skips the copy, post and receive charged for them. Three hops, each a data-independent copy of 32 bytes plus addressing, posting and polling. Within an owner node, baby records are inserted into a direct-mapped head array of 2^33 8-byte slots indexed by key bits 84..116, chained through a pool of 2^33 32-byte records (capacity above 2.58 x the mean, which is below 2^31.633). The node index and the head slot are implied by where a record sits, so a pool record stores key bits 117..191 (75 bits), the 64-bit chain id and the 40-bit address of the next pool record on the slot's chain (0 at the end), as word 0 = key bits 128..191, word 1 = key bits 117..127 in bits 53..63 and the link in bits 0..39, word 2 = the chain id, word 3 spare; a head slot is the 40-bit address of the first pool record (0 for an empty slot) and a 7-bit count in bits 40..46. A head slot holds at most 64 baby records; a 65th makes the node report fail. Giant records are not stored: a giant record reads its head slot and subtracts the slot's count from the node's comparison counter, and compares its 75 remaining key bits with each pooled record on the chain (charged 8 instructions per comparison, listed 7 in Section 4: one 16-byte load of words 0 and 1, the two compares, a branch, the link extraction and the end test). Equal key gives a candidate record (key, id, id'): the full 192-bit key, reassembled from the node index, the slot and the stored bits, and both chain ids, 40 bytes, appended to the node's candidate list of 2^16 slots. A 2^16 + 1st candidate makes the node report fail.

Flow control. A sender keeps per destination its count of blocks posted there and a credit slot, into which the receiver writes by credit word its count of that sender's blocks consumed; it posts only while the difference is below its quota. Counts are 8-byte words that wrap, and the post's subtraction takes the difference modulo 2^64, which is exact because it never exceeds the quota (at most 2^27). The quotas partition each FIFO's 2^27 slots among its senders: 128 blocks for each of the 2^20 generating nodes at a hop-1 relay (2^20 x 128 = 2^27), all 2^27 for the single sender of a hop-2 or hop-3 FIFO; so no FIFO overflows and no slot is overwritten unread. A hop-1 block's sender is the top 20 bits of any of its records' chain ids. After reading a block the receiver adds one to its count for that sender (2^20 counts at a hop-1 relay, one elsewhere) and posts it as a credit word, which overwrites its slot and so never waits. Drain rule: a node's poll loop serves its FIFOs in hop order 3, 2, 1; a node without credit sleeps in wfe and at each wake-up checks the credit and serves only its hop-3 FIFO, if it has one (generating nodes and hop-2 relays have none). Serving a hop-3 block posts only credit words, so it never waits. Hence a wait for hop-3 credit always ends, a wait for hop-2 credit is delayed only by hop-3 waits at its receiver, and one for hop-1 credit only by hop-2 waits: the waits-for relation strictly raises the hop number, has no cycle, and every wait ends, including at (t, 0, 0) and (t, 0, 1), which post to each other at hops 2 and 3. Every wake-up follows one arriving post, and a node receives one credit word per block it posted and one arrival per block posted to it, so the record route has at most two wake-ups per block, each charged as a pass of the wait loop (Section 4, item 7); the gap stream's are charged with its blocks (item 4). At the end of each side every forwarding node (2^20 generating, 2^28 hop-1 and 2^56 hop-2 relays) checks its 2^28 send blocks and posts each non-empty one, its empty slots filled with copies of its last record (so the sender rule above holds); a receiver skips a record equal in all 32 bytes to the one before it in its block (item 8). Such a record adds nothing: its route and use depend only on its bytes, and its twin was already forwarded, inserted or looked up there, giving the same candidate (key, id, id'); a skip only lowers slot, comparison and candidate counts, which are capped from above. Per block and side 2 + 77 (padding) + 116 + 150 (pad records read and skipped) instructions, below 2^93.44 over all of them, inside the per-node fixed row's flush reserve of 3 x 2^33 per node summed over every node, above 2^118.58 (asserted; work sums over processors).

Per-pair verification. After the giant side, every owner node reports its candidate count through the status tree below; if the total exceeds 2^44 the run outputs fail. Otherwise each 40-byte candidate is copied into one 512-byte block for each of the two generating nodes g(id) and g(id'), where g(id) is the top 20 bits of the chain id, posted and polled (charged 128 instructions per candidate). Each generating node has a candidate receive area of 2^24 slots x 40 B; a 2^24 + 1st candidate makes it report fail. Then each of the two chains is re-walked once, linearly, from its start to its end by the generating node that owns it (its gap table rebuilt, its strands and segments restarted, all charged as in the table), and at every step the key of the point is extracted and compared with the retained key: 47 instructions charged for the walk overhead plus 32 charged for the key extraction, the compare with the retained key and the position bookkeeping (listed 34 and 21, Section 4), 2079 per step with the addition. The positions whose key equals the retained key are kept, at most 4 per chain; a fifth makes the run output fail. g(id') sends its kept values a' w' to g(id) as one 168-byte block (40 B header plus 4 x 32 B; copy, post and poll charged at 256 instructions). g(id) now holds at most 4 values a w and at most 4 values a' w', so at most 16 pairs, and for each pair the six z = a w / (u a' w') are tested by [z]P0 = A_d: at most 96 scalar multiplications per candidate, all at g(id). On a hit g(id) runs Lemma 1 and writes s into its status record. Nothing is ever compared against the whole other chain, so the re-walk is O(L) per chain, never L^2. A wrong candidate never produces a wrong answer. Charged in full for every candidate up to the cap (Section 3).

Node overflow and caps, all enforced locally. An owner node fails if it receives more than 2^33 baby records, if any head slot exceeds 64 records, if its candidate list exceeds 2^16, or if its comparison counter, preset to cap_node = ceil(1.01 x the node mean), below 2^30.28, would go below zero; a generating node fails if its candidate receive area exceeds 2^24; a node that fails stops and the run outputs fail. The global candidate cap is 2^44 (checked from the per-node counts before any re-walk), and a re-walk fails if one chain carries the retained key at more than 4 steps. The work charged is the maximum: every record, 2^84 x cap_node comparisons, every candidate up to the cap with the full 96-multiplication recovery.

Status collection. Every node keeps a 48-byte status record: s if found (32 B), then a fail flag (1 bit), its candidate count (17 bits; a node fails above 2^16) and its own baby and giant counts (34 bits each, at most 2^33 before a node fails), 86 bits in the remaining 16 B. Each node checks its own baby and giant caps, so the counts are not summed up the tree: a combined record holds the OR of the fail flags, the sum of the candidate counts saturated at 2^45 (46 bits; only the comparison with the 2^44 global cap uses it) and one found s, if any. It is collected three times, after the baby side, after the giant side and after recovery, through a fan-in-256 tree over all 2^84 + 2^20 + 2^30 nodes (depth 11): a node posts its record, or the combined record of its 256 children, to its parent. Charged at 2^12 instructions per node per pass in the ledger.

## 3. Sizes, caps and success

N = L 2^64 with L the least integer such that 6 N^2 >= 0.4975 m: L < 2^51.6325, N < 2^115.6325, K = 6 N^2 / m, at least 0.4975 and below 0.4975 + 2^-51.6. Under H1 the probability that z is among the 6 N^2 (1 - 1/L) off-diagonal candidates is at least 1 - exp(-K) - 0.0005; 1 - exp(-K) rounded down is 0.391951 (Section 6).

Comparisons. 2^117 head slots in total, load lambda = N / 2^117, below 0.388 per slot. The giant side makes sum over slots of (baby count x giant count) comparisons, mean N^2 / 2^117, below 2^114.265, per node below 2^30.265 against the node cap, 1.01 x the node mean rounded up (below 2^30.28). Three per-node tails under H3, each already multiplied by the number of nodes or slots: (i) a head slot with more than 64 baby records, Poisson(lambda) tail at 65, 2^-274 over all 2^117 slots; (ii) a node's baby or giant count more than 2^-10 (0.098 %) off its mean, Chernoff, 2^-1441 over all nodes (both counts within 2^-10 puts the conditional mean at most (1 + 2^-10)^2 - 1 < 0.196 % above the nominal, and 0.196 % + 0.8 % < 1 %); (iii) given (i) and (ii), the node's comparisons are a sum of its giant records' independent slot draws, each a baby count in [0, 64] with second moment lambda + lambda^2 (the ledger takes 4 x this), so Bernstein with M = 64 and deviation 0.8 % of the mean gives 2^-10327 over all nodes. Every tail in this document is rounded towards the larger probability. The 1 % cap is therefore essentially never reached and the charge 2^84 x cap_node is a bound on the total.

False matches. Pairs with equal 192-bit key but different mu6 orbit: mean N^2 / 2^192, below 2^39.265, cap 2^44, Chebyshev tail below 2^-47.6 under H3. True matches: I budget 2^20 of them inside the same cap. Off the diagonal (w != w') their number is Poisson under H1 with mean at most K (below 0.4975 + 2^-51.6), and it exceeds the budget with probability below 2^-20514925. On the diagonal it is not Poisson. If z = a / (u a') for one baby base a, one giant base a' and one u in mu6, then at every walk position w the baby point [a w]P0 and the giant point [a' w]A_d = [u^-1 a w]P0 lie in one mu6 orbit, so this one event gives L > 2^51.63 true matches, more than the budget and more than the 2^44 cap (L > 2^44 is asserted by the script). In that event the run outputs fail: the candidate counts that the status tree collects after the giant side total at least L > 2^44, unless a node has already failed on one of its own caps, and that check comes before any candidate is forwarded or re-walked. These z form a set of at most 6 n_b^2 = 6 x 2^128 elements of H, with n_b = 2^64 base primes per side, and z is uniform in H, so the event has probability at most 6 x 2^128 / m, below 2^-104.27; it is a separate failure term in the success line below. Outside that event, per node the candidate count is Poisson with mean (N^2 / 2^192 + 2^20) / 2^84, below 2^-44.735, so the chance that any of the 2^84 nodes exceeds its 2^16 candidate slots is below 2^-2931675. Per generating node the forwarded candidates have mean 2 (N^2 / 2^192 + 2^20) / 2^20, below 2^20.265, against a 2^24-slot receive area; the chance that any of the 2^20 generating nodes exceeds it is below 2^-40277873.

Re-walk key repeats. Under H3 a chain of L steps carries some key twice with probability about L^2 / 2^193, below 2^-89.7, and carries one key at more than 4 steps with probability below L^5 / (5! 2^768); over all 2^65 chains that is below 2^-451. Both are far inside the 2^-40 budget below. The step with the retained key is found by the linear re-walk because the key is a function of the point and the chain is deterministic from its start.

Node overflow. Mean below 2^31.633 baby records per node, capacity 2^33 (above 2.58 x). Under the binomial form of H3 the Chernoff bound per node is below 2^-(3.3 x 10^9); times 2^84 nodes it is still astronomically small. I budget 2^-20 for modelling error.

Gaps. The walk list lies in (2^39, 2^78] (H2), where the mean gap between H-primes is at most 2^20 ln 2^78 = 2^25.76, so the expected number of gaps above 2^33 among its L - 1 gaps is below 2^-166.9 (exponential gaps, H2'); a larger gap is split into two table steps and the ledger reserves 2^69 units for 16 such splits per chain; a separate row charges what the reserve's reference price leaves out, the 47-instruction walk overhead of each reserved split addition and the 2 x 16 split steps of each re-walked candidate pair at cap, at their maximum although this path has expected count below 2^-166.9. The list construction checks the gaps and stops the run with no scalar if more than 16 exceed 2^33 or one exceeds 2^34. A split delays one segment by one round; Section 4 counts those rounds.

Success claimed, under H1 to H3: 0.391951 - 0.0005 - 2^-20 - 2^-40 - 2^-104.27 > 0.3914 (the script prints 0.39195 after the three failure terms and 0.39145 after the H1 allowance as well, both rounded down, and asserts the claim 0.3914 at most the second and at least 0.39). The margin over the required 0.39 left after the H1 allowance is 0.00145, 13.3 times the lowest census prime at K = 0.4975 and m >= 2^30 (-0.00010833) and 6.8 times the lowest at K = 0.50 and m >= 2^30 (-0.00021029; all printed by the script, Section 6). The 2^-40 covers every cap and re-walk tail above (each is below 2^-47); the 2^-20 is the modelling budget for node overflow; the 2^-104.27 is the diagonal true-match event; the 0.0005 is the coverage allowance of H1 (Section 6).

## 4. Instruction listing per record (AArch64 scalar, on top of the 2000-instruction reference addition)

The listing is data in `evidence.py` (`WALK`, `KEY`, `SCATTER`, `HOP_BLOCK`, `RECEIVE`, `INSERT`, `LOOKUP`, `COMPARISON_LISTED`, `REWALK_LISTED`), which the script sums per side, amortised items as exact fractions, rounding up once (`overhead_listed`). What the 2000 covers: the cost model's 6M convention prices the batched incomplete affine addition at six multiplications (three for the formula, three for Montgomery's batch trick), S = M, with the formula's field additions and subtractions at A = 0 and no inversion. Everything else the loop executes is listed below. The six multiplications are calls to the measured routine, whose 383 includes its call and return; I list 3 instructions per call for its three operand pointer registers (18) even though the measured loop also sets them. The six field subtractions of the formula (x2 - x1 once, stored for the backward pass, y2 - y1, the two for x3 and the two for y3) are the formula's own A = 0 work and address their operands with base-plus-offset loads, so they need no setup.

Data at a generating node during a chain walk: a lane state of 256 B for each of the 2^16 segments (X in Montgomery form 96 B, the stored denominator x2 - x1 48 B, the prefix product 48 B, the gap pointer and the table-entry pointer), a 2 x 512 B gap buffer per lane, and scratch for the running inverse t, below 2^27 B in all, inside the 2^36 B of stream buffers and state. The loop body, statement by statement (Section 2, Chains, Key and Routing), with the listed instructions per record:

1. Forward pass over the lanes (lane pointer add, end compare, branch: 3). Read the gap: load the lane's gap pointer, load the 4-byte g/2 with post-increment, store the pointer back (3); test for the end of the 512 B gap block and branch (2); a block end switches to the lane's other buffer and requests the next one. Table entry address base + 96 (g/2 - 1) (add with lsl 1, add with lsl 5: 2), stored in the lane state for the backward pass (1). dx = x2 - x1 into the lane state (A = 0) and c_i = c_(i-1) dx (multiplication 1).
2. Once per round: one Fermat inversion of the last prefix product, shared by the 2^16 records of the round (232864 instructions over the chain's records, at most 3.56 per record (below), listed 4), plus at most 2^10 instructions of round setup (pass pointers, t, the event check for segment ends and split gaps, the round counter), 2^10 / 2^16 per record.
3. Backward pass over the lanes (3). Load the entry pointer (1). inv = c_(i-1) t, t = t dx (multiplications 2 and 3); dy = y2 - y1, lambda = dy inv, lambda^2, x3 = lambda^2 - x1 - x2, y3 = lambda (x1 - x3) - y1 (multiplications 4 to 6), written in place into the lane state. Operand pointers of the six multiplications: 18.
4. Gap stream transport: a storage node copies the lane's gaps into 512 B blocks of 128 (32 ldp/stp pairs, 64) and posts each block when the lane asks for it with a credit word (post, the two wake-ups and poll, 50 as on the record route, item 7), and the lane's block end costs 8: 122 / 128 per record.
5. Key: load limbs 0..3 of Y (2 ldp) and of p (2 ldp), p - Y on four limbs (subs, 3 sbcs: 4), the parity test and four selects (tst, 4 csel: 5), key words k0, k1, k2 = bits 1..192 (3 extr): 16. The low 256 bits of p - Y are exact whatever the upper limbs hold.
6. Field inversions. Every F_p inversion is the Fermat power y^(p-2) by binary exponentiation: p - 2 has 381 bits, 229 of them set, so 380 squarings and 228 multiplications, 608 in all, each charged at the 383 instructions the cost model measures for one blst_fp_mul (S = M): 232864 instructions per inversion. The 6M reference convention covers the batch products of Montgomery's trick but not this inversion. F_p inversions occur in four places. (i) Record generation: each round of a chain's 2^16 lockstep segments shares one inversion. A chain runs at most ceil(L / 2^16) rounds, plus at most one more for each of the at most 16 split gaps (Section 3), so its inversions cost 232864 x (ceil(L / 2^16) + 16) / L < 3.56 instructions per record, listed as 4 (Section 4, item 2). (ii) Gap tables: each round of a table's 2^16 strands shares one inversion; a strand makes at most 2^16 additions, so a table needs at most 2^16 rounds, 2^65 x 2^16 inversions over all tables (ledger row). (iii) Re-walks: a re-walked chain runs the rounds of (i) and its rebuilt table the rounds of (ii), so a candidate pair costs at most 2 (ceil(L / 2^16) + 16 + 2^16) inversions, charged for every candidate up to the 2^44 cap (ledger row). (iv) Scalar multiplications: each ends with one inversion, inside its 2^12-unit price. The six z of a kept pair are formed in F_r: one Fermat inversion y^(r-2) per kept giant value (254 squarings and 163 multiplications, 417), at most 4 per candidate, the 8 integer products a w and a' w', and per pair one multiplication for a w / (a' w') and six for the z (one by each element of mu6, which is closed under inversion, so no u is inverted), at most 16 pairs: at most 1788 multiplications per candidate, each charged at 383 instructions (ledger row). No other step inverts: Lemma 1 computes g^(-2^10 j) as g^(2^20 - 2^10 j), and the square roots and H-tests use only multiplications.

Fixed prices, counted from the cost model (383 instructions per multiplication or squaring, S = M, for (i) to (iii); an F_r element has 4 limbs against 6 for F_p and is charged the same 383; the script prints each count under `prices`). (i) Scalar multiplication, charged 2^12 units = 8192000 instructions: an affine point times a scalar below r (255 bits), left to right, takes at most 254 Jacobian doublings (EFD dbl-2009-l for a = 0, 2M + 5S = 7) and 254 mixed additions (EFD madd-2007-bl, 7M + 4S = 11), then one Fermat inversion of Z (608) and 4 multiplications for X / Z^2 and Y / Z^3: 5184 multiplications, 1985472 instructions, so the charge is 4.12 times the count and leaves over 12000 instructions per formula for field additions, loads and the scalar loop. (ii) H-test q^m mod r, charged 2^18 = 262144 instructions: m has 235 bits, 133 of them set, so binary powering takes 234 squarings and 132 multiplications, 366 at 383 = 140178 instructions (charge 1.87 times the count); q comes from the sieve, so no primality test is added. (iii) Lemma 1, charged 2^24 units per run: each of the 20 Tonelli-Shanks square roots (r - 1 = 2^32 Q) takes one power a^((Q - 1) / 2) (221 squarings, 131 multiplications), 2 products and at most 31 rounds of at most 31 + 31 squarings and 3 products, 2369 multiplications; with the fixed non-residue power that is 47734 multiplications for all 20; then the 2^11 scalar multiplications (2^23 units), 3 x 2^10 F_r products for the two lists and 2 x 2^10 x 11 comparisons of 48-byte x-coordinates at 64 instructions for the sort and the lookups: at most 8399059 units, so the charge is 1.99 times the count. (iv) Sieve, charged 128 instructions per integer of each sieved range, itemised here; the script counts every item under `prices.sieve` and asserts the total of each range below the charge. Three ranges are sieved: the walk window (2^39, 2^78], the base window (2^80, 2^80 + 2^92] and (0, 2^47], which makes the sieving primes. Where it runs: on the 2^20 generating nodes, before any chain starts, one segment at a time. A segment is S = 30 x 2^38 integers from a multiple of 30, held as a mod-30 wheel bitmap (8 bits per 30 integers, 2^38 bytes) in the node's gap-table area (96 x 2^32 B, unused until the chains start; asserted). Rounding a range out to whole segments adds at most 30 + S < 2^43 integers, so the ranges are charged as 2^78 + 2^43, 2^92 + 2^43 and 2^47 + 2^43 integers. Where the sieving primes live: the primes from 7 to isqrt(2^80 + 2^92) < 2^46.0002, fewer than 2^41.34 by Rosser and Schoenfeld, 8 bytes each, are spread over the 2^30 storage nodes by rank: the i-th sieving prime in increasing order goes to storage node i mod 2^30, so each node holds at most ceil(count / 2^30) of them, which is at most 2580 (printed as `primes_per_holder_at_most`), beside each node's share of the lists and inside its 2^40 B (asserted per node; the memory total does not move, since storage nodes are counted at their full allocation). How they reach the sieving node: for each segment every holder copies its primes into 512-byte blocks of 64 and posts them to the generating node that sieves the segment, in a fixed order that does not depend on the data, each block drawn by a credit word from the sieving node, which keeps at most 2^10 requests outstanding (2^19 B of FIFO, in its stream buffers) (for a walk segment only its primes up to 2^39); at most 2^30 x 41 blocks per segment, each listed at 118 instructions (copy at the holder, 32 ldp/stp pairs, 64; post, the two wake-ups and poll 50, as on the record route, the request being the poll's credit word; the receiver's block loop and prime count 4) and charged 256. Crossing-off does not depend on order, so the node processes blocks as they arrive and ends the segment when its count reaches the known number of primes. Start offsets are not stored: for each prime and segment the node computes them from the segment start lo (two limbs, below 2^93), 130 instructions: load p 1; 30 p 2; lo mod 30 p from the high limb and six chunks of the low limb with udiv and msub 32; p mod 30 and its class-table row 4; for each of the 8 wheel classes c, the first multiple of p in class c at or after lo (from a table of the k with k p = c mod 30), wrapped into [0, 30 p), its byte index by multiply-high and shift, the bit mask and the loop entry test, 11 each, 88; the prime loop 3. Crossings-off: in class c the multiples of p are 30 p integers apart, which is p bytes, so one crossing is ldrb, orr, strb, add, cmp and branch, 6 instructions. A prime crosses at most S / (30 p) + 1 integers of each class in a segment, so per integer the crossings are below 8/30 times the sum of 1/p (below 0.674 for the walk window's primes up to 2^39 and 0.718 for the base window's, by Rosser and Schoenfeld's bound on the sum of 1/p), plus 8 per prime per segment, which is counted separately. Survivors: the scan reads the bitmap 8 bytes at a time (8 instructions per 240 integers) and only over the range itself, so the padding of a range's first and last segment is never read; each survivor costs 12 (bit extraction 4, its value in two limbs 3, a store into an H-test queue of 2^20 16-byte entries in the stream buffers with its full test 4, loop 1; on a full queue the node H-tests the entries and resumes) plus 8 for emitting a 16-byte entry to a storage node (a 16-byte share of a 116-instruction 512-byte block of the record route is 3.625), charged on every survivor although only H-primes and the sieving primes are emitted; the H-test reads q from the queue inside its own price. Clearing the bitmap costs 7 per 64 bytes, and each segment 2^10 for its start, counters and end. The range (0, 2^47] needs no stream: each of the nodes that sieve its 18 segments first sieves (0, 2^24] plainly (charged 64 per integer) to hold the primes up to 2^24 itself, and after its segment restores those of them that the sieve crossed off as their own first multiple (16 each); 2, 3 and 5 are the wheel and are not sieving primes. Totals per charged integer (printed, each item rounded up): at most 65.9 for the base window (43.672 per prime per segment, 16.125 crossings at 8 per prime per segment, 4.306 the sum part, 1.367 blocks, 0.394 survivors), at most 6.47 for the walk window and at most 4.01 for (0, 2^47], so the charge of 128 is 1.94 times the largest count. The score keeps 2^92.2 units of rounding headroom (2^score - W, printed), so up to 2297 further instructions per sieved integer, in all three ranges, would leave the claimed score unchanged.

## 6. Heuristics

H1 (coverage). The 6 N^2 (1 - 1/L) distinct rationals a w / (u a' w') hit z nearly like independent uniform elements of H. Precisely: let R be the set of the mu6 multiples in H of the off-diagonal ratios a w / (a' w'), w != w'. The lists do not depend on the challenge and z = s^d is uniform in H, so the probability that z is an off-diagonal candidate is exactly |R| / m; a z among the diagonal values a / (u a') is the separate failure term of Section 3. Independent uniform elements in the number of these ratios would cover at least 1 - exp(-K (1 - 2^-51.63)) of H in expectation, which differs from 1 - exp(-K) by less than 2^-52. The census below has primes under that value (11 of 32 at K = 0.4975 and m in [2^30, 2^31), lowest -0.000108; 13 of 48 at K = 0.50 there, lowest -0.00021), so H1 is not stated as that bound. H1 is the statement that at production size |R| / m >= 1 - exp(-K) - 0.0005, a coverage within the census deviation: at K = 0.4975 no census prime from m = 2^30 up falls more than 0.00011 below 1 - exp(-K) (74 primes, lowest -0.000108); at K = 0.50 none from 2^30 up falls more than 0.00022 below (128 primes, lowest -0.00021), none from 2^28 up more than 0.0005, one of the 20 primes at 2^26 does (-0.00052), primes at 2^24 and below fall further, and the spread across primes falls about as m^-1/2 (Trend, Margin). The success line carries the 0.0005 as an explicit term (Section 3); a shortfall up to 0.00195 would still leave the success above the required 0.39 (printed by the script as `coverage_deficit_tolerated`). Lemma 4 removes only the trivial repeats (equal fractions), so H1 stays a heuristic. Its support is an exact count of R on toy primes, below. That census covers m up to 2^34.9, while production has m = 2^234.857; H1 remains a heuristic, supported by the census and not proved by it.

Census (mode `census`). For a toy prime r = 6 c 2^k + 1 with m = (r - 1) / 2^k, H the subgroup of index 2^k, the script builds the lists by the production rules: an H-prime is a prime q with q^m = 1 mod r; the walk list is the first L H-primes above w_min = w_max^(39/77.4) (production: 2^39 and about 2^77.4); the base primes are the first 2 n_b H-primes above 2^2.6 w_max (production: 2^80 over 2^77.4), the first n_b baby and the next n_b giant; n_b is within 10 % of N^(64/115.6503) (split 0.5534; this package's split, 64/115.6325 = 0.5535, would move n_b by under 1 % at the census sizes), and L is the least integer with K = 6 n_b^2 L (L - 1) / m >= the target K (0.4975 or 0.50 for the rows below), so K counts only the off-diagonal pairs, as production's K (1 - 1/L) does. It takes the discrete log in H of every listed prime (Pohlig-Hellman with baby-step giant-step, each result checked by exponentiation), so the mu6 coset of a w / (a' w') is the integer e_a - e_a' + e_w - e_w' mod m/6; it marks every such coset in a byte array of m/6 entries and counts |R| exactly, with no sampling, then tests 40 random z and candidates against the array by residue arithmetic. The count was also checked offline by other means: enumeration of all of H on 9 primes with m below 2^17, a recount from residues on 180 rows (60 primes at three K) with m below 2^21, and the ec toy below, whose exact success is |R u D| / m = 0.394045 (D the diagonal set; the toy counts diagonal hits as successes); a replay of that toy's seeded draws of z finds z in R u D in 161 of 400 and 779 of 2000 trials, the toy's own success counts (Section 7). Each row prints diff = |R| / m - (1 - exp(-K)) at the K its prime reached.

H3 (key bits). The 192-bit keys of the 2N records behave like independent uniform strings across mu6 orbits: node and slot occupancies are binomial and cross-orbit key equality has probability 2^-192. Support: the toy bins truncated keys into 32 pseudo-nodes x 16 heads and the chi-square statistic matches its expectation (31.32 against 31, standard error 0.42). ## 8. Limitations and what was not done

Analytic bound, no full-size run. The instruction counts are itemised, not measured on hardware; the cluster model is the one stated in the cost accounting above, mine and not a rule of the challenge. H1 is supported by an exact census of the candidate set on toy primes up to m = 2^34.9 at K = 0.4975, 0.50 and 0.51, against m = 2^234.857 in production, and H3 by the toy run; neither is proved at production size. Memory is exponential as in any BSGS-form Cheon attack. This package is 17ed8646's package (hprime-bucket-k04975-v1), itself 0a45ddb2's bucket-matching package (hprime-bucket-v1) at K = 0.4975 instead of 0.52, with one change: the per-record charge is lowered from 180 to 160 on a complete per-record listing (Section 4), the three layout details that listing fixes (the key on the Montgomery representative, 40-bit pool links, one comparison-counter update per giant record) and the record route's flow control stated in full (Section 2), its counters itemised in memory. N, L, the caps, the toy procedure (mode ec, whose output also prints its diagonal-corrected K'), the sizes and the success line are those of 17ed8646; W, the score, the preprocessing bound and the memory sum are recomputed by evidence.py. Relative to 0a45ddb2, the listing also names the shared inversion. Two statements inherited from 0a45ddb2 are corrected, without changing the algorithm or any ledger row. Its true-match tail ignored the diagonal event (z = a / (u a') gives L true matches from one base pair, so that budget is exceeded with probability at least 1/m, not below a Poisson tail); Section 3 now accounts the event as a separate failure term below 2^-104.27. Its Lemma 4 said that an equal key means an orbit match; a truncated-key match is only a candidate. Two gaps in the inherited accounting are also closed, again without changing the algorithm or the value of any inherited ledger row. H2 named the base range (2^80, 2^99), while the capped base-prime search examines at most the first 2^86 primes above 2^80; H2 is now stated over exactly the windows the two searches examine, with the expected counts and what the run does on a shortage (Section 6). The F_p inversion shared by each round of 2^16 additions was performed but neither listed nor charged by name; it is now listed per record (4 instructions, inside the per-record charge) and charged by new rows for the gap tables and the re-walks, with a row for the F_r inversions that form the six z (Sections 4 and 5). The three rows add about 2^88.7 units, and a fourth row (2^63.59 units) charges the walk overhead of the split additions that the 2^69 reserve prices at the reference addition, and the re-walks' split steps; the score is unchanged. A third gap changes inherited row values: the inherited sieve price of 4 instructions per integer was asserted, not counted; Section 4 (iv) now itemises the segmented sieve, the streaming of its sieving primes and their per-segment start offsets, and charges 128 per integer, which raises the walk-list row from 2^80.035 to 2^80.057 units and the base-prime row from 2^93.036 to 2^93.079 and adds a row (2^43.122 units) for sieving (0, 2^47], which makes the sieving primes; W's integer moves and the score does not. The H1 margin is thinner than in 0a45ddb2 (1 - e^-K = 0.391951 against the required 0.39, 0.00195 after the failure terms and before the 0.0005 allowance); a new census mode counts the coverage exactly at K = 0.4975 itself, and at 0.50, on toy primes (Section 6), and production success still rests on H1.
