Cheon's attack on bls12-381-g1-ecdlp with planned pair walks, a measured side-output kernel and
synchronous wave transport. This revision replaces the per-lane C call of the P - T kernel by a
measured lane-loop kernel (`side_lanes`) with blst's Montgomery bodies inlined and a lazier x'.

## The change

v23 charged the pair kernel as KERNEL = 1116 (the call-only body of `side_fused`) plus an
11-instruction C lane loop, i.e. 1127 per lane as measured. Of those 1127, 36 were per-call
overhead: prologue/epilogue 17, constant loads 6, frame stores 2, and the C loop 11, plus 4 `bl`
and 4 `ret`.

`side_lanes(args, n)` is called once per round over all lanes, after the round's batched
inversion; lane i reads its pointers (out, P, T, inv) from args[i]. Then:

1. **Lane loop inside the kernel.** The blst bodies are inlined (verbatim, final `ret` dropped),
   which frees x18 and x30 to hold the argument cursor and end across lanes. Per lane, the loop
   costs 7: two `ldp` for the pointers, the cursor `add`, `str`/`stp` of out/P/T for the
   reloads inside the multiplication, `cmp` and `b.ne`. Prologue and constant set-up run once
   per call and cancel in (Ir(8192) - Ir(4096)) / 4096.
2. **Lazy x'.** v23 applied the redc tail's conditional subtraction and then two full modular
   subtractions (23 + 45). v24 keeps V = mu^2 unreduced in [0, 2p), forms S = x1 + x2 < 2p and
   D = V - S in (-2p, 2p), adds 2p under the borrow mask and subtracts p once conditionally,
   which gives x' in [0, p). This costs 9 + 47.

Measured (organizers' image, GCC 14.2.0, Valgrind 3.24.0, blst f7a88067): **1076 instructions per
lane**, lane control included. That is 931 for the inlined blst bodies (340 x 2 + 98 + 153)
plus a 145-instruction driver; the static count of the disassembled loop body is also 1076. The
self-checks pass at 4096 and 8192 lanes: every lane matches blst's exported fully reduced API,
including all 625 special-value combinations; Lemma 2a holds over 600000 products; and 64
genuine curve-point pairs equal blst's affine P + (-T).
