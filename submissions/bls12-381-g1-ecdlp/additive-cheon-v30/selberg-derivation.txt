The two explicit input hypotheses are:
(P1) on EACH side s=B,G, writing t_a=|P_a|,
 W_s(c)=sum_{a:class(a)=c} t_a, one has sum_c W_s(c)=n and
 d*sum_c W_s(c)^2/n^2-1<=500.
(END) every used baby block prime is <=(57/100)X1 and every used

The package's separate endpoint component proves END with its explicitly
quoted prime-count theorem. END is an input to this calculation; no
prime-count result is silently used here. Put ZB=(57/100)Z, ZG=(64/100)Z.

We bound the UNION of wrapped collisions having at least one common
SMALL/list prime across the two sides of xy'=rho*x'y and satisfying
x!=x', y!=y'. Additional common primes, including common large primes,
are ALLOWED. It is important not to add a separate multi-common-small
stratum afterward. Four placements below may overcount a collision;
this is an upper bound, so that is harmless.

For rho=+1 or -1, cancel any common small prime p. Both remaining
positive three-prime products are <=Y=X1^2*X2. The script checks 3Y<r,
hence in particular 2Y<r. The congruence is then an exact positive
integer equality for rho=1 (same-root k=0), or impossible for rho=-1.
Thus wrapped common-small collisions can only have the FOUR nonreal
ratios rho in U. Each rho has exactly SIX ordered pairs (u,u') in U^2.

2. The maximum class mass, retaining actual prefixes

Let W=max_c W_s(c). Cauchy on the other d-1 classes gives
 W^2+(n-W)^2/(d-1) <= 501*n^2/d.
Rearranging yields (dW/n-1)^2<=500(d-1), hence
 W_s(c)<=n*w, w=(1+sqrt(500(d-1)))/d.                         (2.1)
The verifier upper-bounds this square root by an explicit rational
using one integer square root at scale 2^128; no floating comparison
is used to establish a bound.

Let b_p=#baby rows a with p in P_a, and g_p analogously. Then
 sum_p b_p=sum_p g_p=n.
Let R_s(c) be the number of used rows in class c. Prefixes in different
classes are disjoint; in the same class their overlap is min(t_a,t_a').
Consequently
 sum_p b_p^2
 =sum_{a,a' baby} |P_a intersect P_a'|
 <=sum_c W_B(c) R_B(c)
 <=n*w*M,                                                  (2.2)
and identically sum_p g_p^2<=n*w*M. This uses min(t_a,t_a')<=t_a
and sum_c R_s(c)<=M; partial final rows are included exactly.
Define
 QB=sum_p b_p(b_p-1), QG=sum_p g_p(g_p-1),
 QBG=sum_p b_p*g_p.
Then QB,QG<=n*w*M and Cauchy gives QBG<=n*w*M. The slightly sharper
QB<=n*w*M-n is not needed.

3. The four placements and continuous main area

For a common small prime in x and x' (same baby), fix their DISTINCT
large primes a,a'. The exact coefficient weight is
 w(a,a')=|P_a intersect P_a'|.
After canceling the common small prime the remaining pair is in G^2,
so enlarge to the positive integer box [1,ZG]^2.
For a common small prime in y' and y (same giant), fix distinct giant
large primes and use [1,ZB]^2. Distinctness follows from x!=x',y!=y';
in a same-side row, equality of the small prime would otherwise make
the corresponding coordinates equal.

The other two placements share the small prime between a baby and a
giant element on opposite sides. Fix that baby and giant large prime
with weight |P_a intersect P_a'|. The remaining box has side lengths
ZB,ZG, oriented according to the congruence. Repeated-coordinate
cross cases may be included as extra nonnegative terms.

In every case the canceled congruence has form
 a*y' == rho*a'*y (mod r),                                 (3.1)
possibly after relabeling coordinates and replacing rho by rho^-1.
It defines a determinant-r sublattice of Z^2. Let q label the placement,
the ordered coefficient pair, and one of the four nonreal rho. Its
exact prefix weight is w_q and its oriented continuous box is box_q.
Define the EXACT continuous area quantity
 V=6*sum_q w_q*area(box_q)/r.                               (3.2)
The sum in (3.2) already includes four nonreal roots. From Section 2,
 V <= (24/r)*(QB*ZG^2+QG*ZB^2+2*QBG*ZB*ZG)
   <=24*n*M*w*(ZB+ZG)^2/r.                                (3.3)
Thus A=V/B0<=12.580221880370326... . The companion certificate records
the exact rational upper A, with w rounded up and all other operations
exact. No claim that a finite lattice count equals its area is made.

4. The graph lemma for small remaining list primes

This section independently proves the prime-exception constant and
does NOT assume primitive canceled products.

For any U-orbit in F_r^*, consider its positive integer representatives
in [1,Y], Y=X1^2*X2<r/3. At most one representative can come from each
opposite pair {v,-v}, since 2Y<r. Therefore there are at most three.
Join two representatives when their ratio is a nonreal element of U.
This graph has degree at most two.

There are at most TWO ODD representatives. Indeed, if three were odd,
one would come from each of {v,-v},{L*v,-L*v},{L^2*v,-L^2*v}, where
L^2+L+1=0 mod r. Suitable signs therefore give a sum of the three
representatives equal to 0 mod r. Its absolute value is at most 3Y<r,
so it is an integer zero. But a signed sum of three odd integers is
odd and cannot be zero. Thus the induced graph on odd representatives
is a matching (degree at most one).

Here are two elementary counting consequences. All supports below
are BOOLEAN functions, and all even vertices are marked.
(a) For one support C and marked subset C*, the number of ordered
nonreal edges in C^2 with at least one marked endpoint is at most
 2|C*|+2|C_even|.
Proof: odd-odd edges contribute <=2|C*_odd| because the odd graph is
a matching; edges with an even endpoint contribute <=4|C_even| because
the full graph has degree <=2. Add and use C_even subset C*.
(b) For two supports C,D with marked subsets C*,D*, the number of
oriented nonreal edges C->D with a marked endpoint is at most
 |C*|+|D*|+|C_even|+|D_even|.
The odd matching contributes <=|C*_odd|+|D*_odd|; edges with an even
endpoint contribute <=2|C_even|+2|D_even|. The displayed bound follows.
These inequalities can also be checked on K3; the verifier exhausts
all 1,468 support/mark/parity cases.

Let H_B(R),H_G(R) count B/G elements whose list prime is <=R (R>=2).
Let B2,G2 count those with list prime 2. Since each used row has at
most pi(R) such entries,
 H_B+H_G<=2M*pi(R), B2+G2<=2M.                              (4.1)

For a fixed shared small prime p define A_B(p) to be the baby large
primes whose rows contain p. Its cardinality is b_p. The integer
convolution
 c_p(s)=#{(a,y) in A_B(p) x G : a*y=s}
is BOOLEAN: s has one baby large prime, one giant large prime, and
one small prime. Unique factorization and the disjoint tiers/blocks
recover these three factors, even if OTHER factors are shared by
the two sides of a collision. Thus c_p has mass b_p*n; its marked
mass (remaining G list prime <=R) is b_p*H_G, and its even mass is
b_p*G2. Apply (a), multiply by the six (u,u') pairs per rho, and sum p.
Since sum_p b_p=n, the same-baby exception is at most
 12*n*(H_G+G2).
The same-giant exception is at most 12*n*(H_B+B2).

For one cross placement also define the BOOLEAN convolution
 d_p(s)=#{(x,a') in B x A_G(p) : x*a'=s}.
Its marked/even masses are g_p*H_B and g_p*B2; c_p's are as above.
Apply (b), multiply by six, and sum p. Each cross placement contributes
at most 6*n*(H_B+H_G+B2+G2). There are two cross placements. Adding all
four gives the rigorous union bound
 E_exc(R)<=24*n*(H_B+H_G+B2+G2)
          <=48*n*M*(pi(R)+1).                             (4.2)
This excludes only the two REMAINING list primes in the canceled
coordinate pair. The fixed common list prime is allowed to be small.

5. An elementary finite pi bound, with integer endpoints

For j>=2 let k_j count primes in (2^(j-1),2^j]. Every such prime
divides binomial(2^j,2^(j-1)): it occurs once in the numerator
factorial and not in either denominator factorial. Their product is
therefore at most this binomial, which is STRICTLY less than
2^(2^j), the sum of all binomial coefficients in that row.
Each prime is STRICTLY greater than 2^(j-1). Hence
 (j-1)*k_j < 2^j
and, using integrality,
 k_j <= floor((2^j-1)/(j-1)).
The prime 2 is handled separately. At R=2^35 this proves
 pi(R)<=1+sum_{j=2}^35 floor((2^j-1)/(j-1))
       =2086667226.                                       (5.1)
Combining (4.2),(5.1) and dividing by B0, exact rational arithmetic gives
 E_exc/B0 <=0.002641841664166639...                         (5.2)
A certified outward decimal is 0.00264184166416664. The script also
checks the binomial divisibility/endpoints exactly through j=12;
the argument above proves every j, including j=35.

6. The actual Selberg sequence and its unproved remainder

For real squarefree-supported lambda_d, d<=R, lambda_1=1, put
 F_R(v)=(sum_{d|v,d<=R} lambda_d)^2.
F_R(v)=1 for integers with no prime factor <=R, and F_R(v)>=0 always.
After removing Section 4's exception, both remaining coordinates in
(3.1) have no prime factor <=R, since their large factors exceed R.
Thus F_R(y*y')=1 on the desired rough pairs. Replacing the actual
remaining B/G products by ALL positive integer lattice points gives
a valid nonnegative majorant. In particular no gcd restriction remains.

For squarefree k set
 g(k)=product_{p|k}(2/p-1/p^2).
The determinant-r lattice maps onto all pairs modulo k when (k,r)=1:
given any two residues mod k, solve (3.1) simultaneously mod r by CRT.
Of its k^2 residue pairs, exactly 2p-1 out of p^2 have p|y*y', so
g(k) is the exact local density. All k here are <=R^2<r.

Define using the ACTUAL coefficient primes and prefix masks
 C(k)=6*sum_q w_q*#{(y,y') in lattice_q intersect box_q : k|y*y'},
 Rcal(k)=C(k)-g(k)*V.                                     (6.1)
No equidistribution is asserted by this definition. Expanding the square
gives, exactly,
 E_small,nonrepeat <= E_exc(R)+V*H_R(lambda)+Ecal_R,          (6.2)

7. Exact finite optimal weights, proved here

Let
 h(k)=mu(k)^2 product_{p|k}(2p-1)/(p-1)^2,
 G(R)=sum_{k<=R} h(k).
For squarefree d<=R take
 lambda_d = mu(d)/G(R) *
            product_{p|d}(1-g(p))^-1 *
            sum_{k<=R/d,(k,d)=1} h(k),                    (7.1)
and lambda_d=0 for nonsquarefree d. Then lambda_1=1 and
 H_R(lambda)=1/G(R) EXACTLY.

Proof. Restrict indices to squarefree values. The identity
 g(lcm(d,e))=g(d)g(e)/g(gcd(d,e))
and divisor expansion
 1/g(v)=sum_{ell|v} kappa(ell),
 kappa(ell)=product_{p|ell}(1-g(p))/g(p)=1/h(ell)
give
 H_R=sum_ell kappa(ell)*y_ell^2,
 y_ell=sum_{d<=R,ell|d} lambda_d*g(d).
Finite Mobius inversion yields sum_ell mu(ell)y_ell=lambda_1=1.
Cauchy gives H_R>=1/G(R), with equality for
 y_ell=mu(ell)h(ell)/G(R).
Inverting this equality over multiples yields (7.1), since
h(d)/g(d)=product_{p|d}(1-g(p))^-1 and h is squarefree multiplicative.
This proves the identity and the weights without citing a sieve theorem.
The certificate checks it by exact rational arithmetic at R=2,4,8,16,32.

8. A finite lower bound for G, without asymptotic constants

For every prime p,
 h(p)=(2p-1)/(p-1)^2=sum_{nu>=1}(nu+1)/p^nu.
Multiplying this absolutely convergent positive series over the finitely
many primes of each squarefree k gives
 h(k)=sum_{rad(v)=k} tau(v)/v.
Therefore
 G(R)=sum_{rad(v)<=R} tau(v)/v
     >=sum_{v<=R} tau(v)/v
      =D(R):=sum_{u*v<=R} 1/(u*v).                        (8.1)
Thus no prime-number theorem, standard sieve constant, or limiting
formula is needed for the finite lower bound.

At R=34359738368 and J=floor(sqrt(R))=185363, splitting the hyperbola
along u<=J or v<=J gives the EXACT identity
 D(R)=2*sum_{u=1}^J H_floor(R/u)/u-H_J^2.                   (8.2)
For n>=J, convexity of 1/t bounds each integral over [j,j+1] by the
endpoint trapezoid. Summing and rearranging proves
 H_n>=H_J+log(n/J)-1/(2J)+1/(2n).                          (8.3)

The companion implements a rigorous lower certificate for (8.2):
- Scale B=2^128. Hlo=sum_{j<=J}floor(B/j) <= B*H_J
  and Hhi=Hlo+J >= B*H_J.
- Lower-bound each logarithm by range-reducing n/J=2^k*v,1<=v<2,
  and using 24 POSITIVE terms of
  log(v)=2*sum_{j>=0} z^(2j+1)/(2j+1), z=(v-1)/(v+1).
  Log(2) uses the same positive series with z=1/3.
  Every positive fixedpoint multiplication/division rounds DOWN.
  Omitting the positive tail also rounds down.
- In (8.3), use Hlo and the log lower bound, subtract
  ceil(B/(2J)), add floor(B/(2n)), then divide by u rounding down.
- Subtract ceil(Hhi^2/B), rounding the negative H_J^2 term UP in size.
Consequently every assertion establishing the final bound is integer
or Fraction arithmetic, independent of machine floating point.

9. What is and is not obtained

Under the declared P1 and END inputs, this component proves
 E_small,nonrepeat/B0
 <=0.03897665922609721+0.00264184166416664+Ecal_R/B0.
The actual combined upper from the two rational certificates is
about 0.04161850089026384, before the SIGNED weighted remainder.
It covers every common-small-prime wrapped nonrepeat collision,
including cases having additional shared primes.

This does NOT prove P2. Neither the coprime excess nor Ecal_R is bounded
at the required scale here. A package using a joint target must carry
the SUM of the coprime excess and Ecal_R; it may not silently put the
coprime excess at zero. If the coprime baseline allocation is M0, retain
Delta0=E_coprime-36*M0/m, delta0=1-M0/n^4, and distinguish
Delta0_star=E_coprime-B0=Delta0-delta0*B0.

For a prospective remainder estimate the actual mask can be factorized
exactly by layers:
 |P_a intersect P_a'|
 =sum_c sum_{ell>=1}1_{class(a)=c,t_a>=ell}
                       *1_{class(a')=c,t_a'>=ell}.
For squarefree k one may also unfold, prime by prime,
 1_{k|y*y'}=
 sum_{d,e|k,lcm(d,e)=k}mu(gcd(d,e))*1_{d|y,e|y'}.
Writing y=d*u,y'=e*v leaves the congruence
 a*e*v == rho*a'*d*u (mod r).
Thus the remainder kernel still involves inverses of coefficient primes
modulo the fixed LARGE r and bounded integer representatives. Their
residues modulo the SMALL lcm(d,e) do not determine the kernel.
The fact R^2=2^70 is below a product-coefficient square-root scale is
therefore not itself a factorable large-sieve/BV proof. No such missing
estimate is inferred from the exact identities.
