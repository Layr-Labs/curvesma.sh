PRIME-COUNT DEPENDENCY AND FINITE ARITHMETIC

Standard analytic input (quoted displayed equations):
  x/(ln x - 1) <= pi(x) <= x/(ln x - 1.1).
Validity domains as printed: lower x>5393; upper x>60184.
These strict domains are the ones invoked by this package.

Source: Pierre Dusart, Estimates of Some Functions Over Primes without R.H.,
arXiv:1002.0442v1, Theorem6.9, equation(6.6), printed p.9.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.0442
The bounds are unconditional published theorems. Their proof is an analytic
input; the package does not claim to re-prove the prime number theorem or
the zeta-zero estimates underlying that paper.

Every numerical use is recomputable by prime_count_inputs.py using Python
integers and Fraction. It never calls a floating-point logarithm. It proves
the logarithm enclosures by
 ln u = 2 sum_{j>=0} [(u-1)/(u+1)]^(2j+1)/(2j+1)
after dyadic range reduction, and bounds the omitted positive tail by a
geometric series. Substituting outward log bounds in Dusart's denominators
then gives exact rational pi lower and upper bounds.

Production consequences checked:
 pi(2^70) <=24896332478097058409.
 At least5940219918861239907136 primes lie in (X1/2,.57X1], more thanM.
 At least11946945700771265222865 primes lie in (X1/2,.64X1], more than2M.
 Here X1=2^82, M=5893133993817406318491, and2M=NA+1 becauseNA is odd.
 Thus every declared baby prime is<=.57X1 and every declared giant prime
 is<=.64X1, independently of chi and regardless of which prefixes are used.

An independent sieve through2^20 verifies the formulas at2^13 (lower only),
2^16,2^18 and2^20. These are small-scale transcription/constant checks.
They do not independently certify the production counts at2^70 or2^82;
those conclusions follow from the cited theorem plus exact arithmetic.

The exact arithmetic is suitable for copying into the package evidence.py
or importing from this compact helper. No PDF, external numerical table,
large prime list or network access is needed when the verifier runs.
