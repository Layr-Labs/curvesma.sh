including all lattice and weighted-density lemmas. It uses the one

standalone standard-library program shared_large_certificate.py.

Thus the earlier printed 0.002332911916573674 was a rounded display,
not an outward upper endpoint. The safe upper endpoint is

E_one-large here counts wrapped ordered collisions whose two integer

needed in this theorem. A final section gives the explicit two-large-only

D. Reduction of the exactly-one-large family
--------------------------------------------
Write an ordered collision as
 u*x/y == u'*x'/y' mod r,
or xy'==rho*x'y, rho=u'/u.
The large prime sets on the two sides are disjoint. Therefore a large
prime common to xy' and x'y is either the common baby factor of x,x',
or the common giant factor of y,y'. In the exactly-one-large family
these placements cannot both occur.

For a common baby large prime a, write x=ab,x'=ac, b,c in P_a.
Here b!=c, and the two variable giant products y',y are coprime:
otherwise the original cross-products would have another common prime.
After cancelling a,
 b*y'==rho*c*y mod r, with(y',y) in G times G.
The common giant placement is identical with the sides exchanged.

The roots rho=1,-1 give no such wrapped collisions. After cancelling a
common large prime, both remaining products are<=X1X2^2<r/2.
For rho=1, equality modulo r is integer equality; their common gcd
would then contain the whole remaining product, which is>1, contrary
to exactly one common prime. For rho=-1 their positive sum is<r,
impossible. Only the four nonreal roots remain.

For fixed distinct primes b,c and nonreal rho define
 Lambda_(b,c,rho)={(s,t) in Z^2:b*s==rho*c*t mod r}.
Its index/determinant is r: for each t modulo r exactly one s works.
Let lambda be its first sup-norm minimum. The variables(y',y) are
positive primitive points of this lattice. Later, after the coordinate
wheel is imposed, it is safe to count all integer points in the allowed
cosets, which is an upper bound for these primitive variables.

E. Two elementary lattice-counting lemmas
------------------------------------------
E1. For any affine translate v+Lambda of a determinant-r lattice whose
first sup-norm minimum is lambda,
 |#[(v+Lambda) intersect(0,X]^2]-X^2/r|
 <=X/lambda+2X*lambda/r+1.                             (E1)

Proof. Choose a shortest lattice vector(a,b), sign/exchange coordinates
so a=lambda>0, |b|<=lambda. It is primitive as a lattice vector:
otherwise dividing it inside the lattice gives a shorter vector.
Using Bezout on its coordinates in any lattice basis extends it to a
basis with determinant r. The parallel lattice lines in this direction
have determinant coordinates spaced by r. At most2Xlambda/r+1 meet
the square, since the projection width is at most(|a|+|b|)X.

Measure each line's square chord in units of(a,b). The resulting
function f of the real line index is nonnegative, unimodal, and has
maximum<=X/lambda. To see unimodality without another geometric theorem,
the allowed line parameter is the intersection of two intervals whose
endpoints are affine in the line index. Its length is concave where
positive and has interval support. Its integral is X^2/r by the
Jacobian determinant r. Its integer sum differs from this integral by
at most max f: each superlevel set is an interval, whose integer count
differs from its length by at most one; integrate over the superlevel.
On each active line, counting lattice points differs from its chord
length by at most one. Adding the line-rounding error proves(E1).
The same argument applies to translated lattices and shifted line
indices.

E2. The number of primitive positive points of a determinant-r congruence
lattice in(0,X]^2 is at most
 D(X)=floor(2X^2/r)+1.                                (E2)

Proof. Order their distinct rays by angle. Each ray has at most one
primitive positive integer point. Adjacent vectors have a nonzero
positive determinant, which is a multiple of r. Their triangles with
the origin are disjoint inside the square and have area>=r/2.
Thus(K-1)r/2<=X^2 for K points, proving(E2).

M. Covering boundary: two common large primes and no common small prime
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
The principal theorem is exactly-one-large. To combine it with a
shared-small sieve safely, partition the remaining nonrepeated wrapped
collisions as follows:
 * any common small prime: covered by the four-placement shared-small
   count, which counts all integer variable pairs rather than only
   primitive ones;
 * no common small prime, one common large prime: this theorem;
 * no common small prime, two common large primes: the correction below.
Repeated coordinates are handled separately when the shared-small
reduction excludes them. Multiple memberships in a sieve superset are
harmless for an upper union bound; no cancellation between covers is used.

For the last family fix the common baby large prime a and common giant
large prime a'. There are at most M^2 choices. Cancelling both leaves
 s=b1*b2', t=b2*b1', with s,t<=X2^2 and gcd(s,t)=1.
For each nonreal rho, the primitive-ray bound(E2) gives at most
 D2=floor(2X2^4/r)+1
pairs(s,t). Each semiprime s has at most two assignments to its baby
and giant list-prime roles, and likewise t, giving a factor<=4.
There are four nonreal rho and six ordered root pairs. Therefore
 E_two-large,no-small<=96M^2 D2,
 E_two-large,no-small/baseline
 <=1.6330900757069875e-17<1.64e-17.                     (M1)
The formula and final rational comparison are checked by the certificate.

Why the lower-coefficient issue does not invalidate this partition:
in(J2) all integer variable pairs are counted, so nonprimitive ones are
already harmless. In(K1) a nonprimitive variable pair would create a
second common large prime or a common small prime. Those cases belong
to the other explicitly covered strata; they must not be silently
declared part of the exactly-one-large primitive estimate.

For any wrapped collision sharing at least one prime, rho=1,-1 can
also be excluded directly under2X1^2X2<r: cancel one shared prime.
Both remaining products are<=X1^2X2. For rho=1 a congruence implies an
integer identity and hence k=0; for rho=-1 the positive sum is<r.

 (i) b1=b2 and d2=d1: the diagonal;
 (ii) b1=d1 and d2=b2: the two ratio pairs are
      (a*b1,c*b1) and(a*b2,c*b2).
In (ii), both b1,b2 lie in P_a intersect P_c; distinct indexed pairs
require b1!=b2. This is precisely the swap family. For intersection
size s there are s(s-1) ordered distinct choices. Therefore the
ordered distinct-index k=0 energy is exactly6sum s(s-1), and the
pruning in SectionN removes every such integer-identity collision.

P. Repeated coordinates: a complete tiny bound
-----------------------------------------------
Suppose x=x'. For a distinct-index collision, rho=1 would force y=y'
and u=u', the same index, so it is excluded. Rho=-1 cannot occur since
y+y'<=2Z<r. Thus y'==rho*y for one of the four nonreal roots.
If y,y' share a large prime, cancel it to obtain a nonzero nonreal-root
pair of list primes, whose norm is<=3X2^2<r, impossible. If they share
a small prime, cancellation gives a nonreal-root pair of large primes
with norm<=3X1^2<r, also impossible. Consequently gcd(y,y')=1.
The primitive-ray lemma(E2) bounds their pairs by D(Z).
There are n choices of x, four nonreal roots and six ordered root pairs:
this case contributes<=24nD(Z). The symmetric repeated-y case contributes
the same, so their union is at most
 E_repeat<=48nD(Z),
 E_repeat/baseline<=2.670714293117111e-19<2.671e-19.
The certificate verifies3X1^2<r and the exact rational comparison.
This is a union bound, so possible double membership could only make
it conservative.
