## Maximal orders and fields

The data copies the six rows BAS2 of common12.py, divided by 2. The checker calculates determinant 1/4 in absolute value, verifies all 36 products and all six power-basis vectors have integral coordinates (so the order contains Z[y]), computes the trace matrix and its discriminant 184832, and computes disc(H)=2957312=16*184832. H is irreducible modulo 3, checked by Rabin's test, so this is an order in a degree-six field. Any remaining index prime divides 2*19.

At 2 the checker uses pi4=(-1-5y+2y²-y³+y⁴)/2, as in runs16.py, and w=y+1. It checks their norms 4 and 2, nonmembership in each other's principal ideals, and 2=u*w²*pi4² with integral u of norm 1. It checks 2 lies in both ideals and exhibits a root of t²+t+1 in the quotient by pi4. Its norm is 4, so that quotient is F4; the norm-2 quotient by w is F2. These distinct maximal ideals exhaust the factors of (2), including multiplicity. Their localizations are DVRs: each maximal ideal is generated by a non-zero-divisor, and a one-dimensional Noetherian local domain with principal maximal ideal is regular and hence a DVR. The order is therefore 2-maximal. At 19 the checker independently factors H modulo 19, forms the integer product of monic lifts with their multiplicities, and checks the standard Dedekind index criterion: the repeated factors are coprime to (H-product)/19 modulo 19. Hence it is 19-maximal too, and D_K=184832. This is not a coprime-discriminant argument.

The checker verifies disc(F)=-34371=-3³*19*67. At 19 and 67 the discriminant exponent is one, so the power order is locally maximal. Modulo 3, F=(z-1)²(z²+1)². Hensel separates the two coprime clusters. Translation by 1 has constant 3 and derivative divisible by 3. Over the unramified quadratic extension with i²=-1, translation by i has constant -3i and derivative divisible by 3. Each separated quadratic is Eisenstein; its discriminant exponent is respectively 1 and 2 in Q, accounting for all three powers of 3. Thus Z[z] is maximal at 3 as well. The checker verifies these factorizations, evaluations and derivatives.

At 41 the checker verifies the complete factorization of H, including y+11, and irreducibility of F of degree 6. As 41 does not divide disc(H), reduction at this degree-one prime proves F irreducible over K. Thus [E0:Q]=36. F is irreducible modulo 2. The primes (w) and (pi4) have (e,f)=(2,1),(2,2). Base change by F yields one and two E0 primes of degree 6. At the prime above w, wz has valuation 1, since z is a unit, and its square root is Eisenstein. Hence [Ef:Q]=72. The other two primes have at most two extensions each, so five degree-six factors bound the Euler factor at 2. Also v_w(-Uw)=2v2(U)+1 is odd, so [Eg:Q]=12.

## Class number one for K

Minkowski's bound for K is at most (40/31)^3*(6!/6^6)*431 <15; using three complex places is conservative for any degree-six signature. The factors of H at 3,5,7,11,13 have degrees respectively (6), (2,2,2), (3,3), (2,2,2), (6). Their prime-ideal norms are all at least15. These are the only odd rational primes that could give an ideal norm below15, and all factor lists are verified. The two primes over2 have norms2 and4 and are principal, as proved above. Every ideal class has an integral representative of norm below15; all prime factors of every such representative are therefore principal. Hence hK=1. This is stronger than r-coprimality and justifies the class-number-one assertion in the inherited descent discussion.

## Bounds

The order O_K[z,sqrt(wz)] has discriminant (184832*34371)^12*2^78. Minkowski's formula and pi>31/10 give an Ef ideal-representative bound B<2^149, verified using integer square roots and rational arithmetic. For Eg the integral element beta=sqrt(-Uw)/256 yields order discriminant 184832²*4^6*(U/2^16)^6*2. Minkowski gives B_g<2^168. For K, B_K<512. The elementary degree-n ideal-count bound B(1+ln B)^(n-1) gives hK<512*513^5<r and hEg<2^168*169^11<r.

For the Ef bound use s=13/10. For each odd p<100 except 19, the certificate supplies complete monic factorizations of H and F. The checker independently enumerates the primes, reconstructs each polynomial, checks all factors irreducible, and accounts for local degrees. For factors of degrees a,b put L=lcm(a,b). There are gcd(a,b) primes before the quadratic extension. The norm of w in the first residue field is (-1)^a h(-1); that of z in the second is (-1)^b f(0). Their product after raising to L/a and L/b gives the quadratic character of wz in the common residue field. A square contributes two degree-L factors per prime; a nonsquare contributes one of degree 2L. At these odd primes at most one constituent ramifies, the other is unramified, and wz is a unit. At 19, shared ramification is handled conservatively by 72 degree-one factors. The checker includes five degree-six factors at 2 and 72 degree-one factors at 19. It bounds each local log by 1/(p^d-1), a rational upper bound for -log(1-p^(-ds)), and obtains a sum <6.

For p>=100, partial summation with the cited prime-counting bound gives a tail at most 72*(1.25506*(13/10)*E1((3/10)ln 100)+1/99). Since ln 100>23/5 and exp(-69/50)<63/250, E1((3/10)ln 100)<21/115.
