# Six-class checkpoint rho

Recover \(x\) from \(T=[x]P_0\) in BLS12-381 G1. The search runs on the quotient by the order-6 group generated by negation and the GLV endomorphism. The iteration is an adding walk with a table of \(2^{20}\) jumps. Stored states are distinguished checkpoints only. A repeated checkpoint with dependent coefficients abandons the trail (cycle escape). A repeated checkpoint with independent coefficients solves for \(x\).

The submitted score is an upper bound on the work of one truncated run, not an average that a later run is allowed to exceed. The truncation is inside the budget below. Success probability, under the heuristics stated at the end, is at least \(39/100\).

Parent constants are the profile subgroup order \(r\), the profile generator \(P_0\), \(C=2000\), and the pinned base-field multiplication price of 383 scalar instructions. \(\lambda=\) `0xac45a4010001a40200000000ffffffff` is the small root of \(X^2+X+1=0\) in \(\mathbb{F}_r\), and \(\lambda^2+\lambda+1=r\) exactly. \(\beta\) is the profile cube root of unity in \(\mathbb{F}_p\) with \(\varphi(x,y)=(\beta x,y)\) and \(\varphi(P_0)=[\lambda]P_0\). The experiment checks that one identity on \(P_0\). Both \(\varphi\) and \([\lambda]\) are group homomorphisms, and \(G_1=\langle P_0\rangle\), so the identity extends to every point of \(G_1\).

## Quotient

\(r\equiv 1\pmod 6\), so \(N=(r-1)/6\) is an integer. On \(G_1\setminus\{O\}\) the maps \(P\mapsto -P\) and \(P\mapsto\varphi(P)\) generate a group of six distinct automorphisms. The identity is its own orbit and is handled before the walk: if \(T\) is the point at infinity, return \(x=0\). That comparison is inside the bookkeeping budget below.

A canonical label of an affine point is the minimum, as an integer in \(0,\ldots,p-1\), of the three \(x\)-coordinates \(\{x,\beta x,\beta^2 x\}\), together with the sign bit that selects the smaller of \(y\) and \(-y\). Applying \(\varphi\) or negation multiplies the unknown scalar by \(\lambda\) or by \(-1\), and the coefficient pair is multiplied by the same unit. The walk state is that canonical point plus a pair \((a,b)\) with
\[
W=aP_0+bT.
\]

## Walk

Precompute \(R=2^{20}\) jumps. Jump \(j\) is the affine point \(u_j P_0+v_j T\), with \((u_j,v_j)\) produced by a fixed 4-limb counter (not a searched table). Build each jump by Shamir joint double-and-add in Jacobian coordinates, then one Fermat inversion to affine. The 64 starting points of the lockstep batch are built the same way.

The batch has 64 walks so that each round is a batched incomplete affine addition in the sense of the track reference operation. Each such addition is charged as \(1\), and its internal field products are not charged again.

One step on a walk:

1. Replace the current affine point by its canonical image. Multiply \((a,b)\) by the same unit \(\pm\lambda^k\).
2. If the canonical \(x\) has bits \([20,117)\) equal to zero, the point is a checkpoint. Look it up.
   - Absent: store \((a,b)\).
   - Present with \(b\not\equiv b'\): output \(x=(a'-a)(b-b')^{-1}\bmod r\) and verify \([x]P_0=T\).
   - Present with \(b\equiv b'\): cycle escape. Drop the trail and start another one inside the same budget. Checkpoints already stored stay.
3. Add the precomputed jump indexed by the low 20 bits of the canonical \(x\). Add the matching \((u_j,v_j)\) into \((a,b)\).

The step budget is the same on every challenge. It does not stop early in the accounting: the charged cost is the full budget plus preprocessing, lookup, and one verification scalar multiplication.

## Step budget

Let \(\sigma=\sqrt{\pi N/2}\). A Machin truncation in `reference.pi_upper` produces a rational \(\pi^\star>\pi\). The code takes the least integer \(s\) with \(s^2\ge \pi^\star N/2\), so \(s\ge\sigma\). The value is
\[
s=117165182625047167472644087141503521155.
\]

For i.i.d. uniform draws from a set of size \(N\), the first-repeat time \(T\) satisfies
\[
\Pr(T>k)\le\exp\bigl(-k(k-1)/(2N)\bigr)
\]
whenever \(k-1<N\), because \(1-i/N\le e^{-i/N}\). Write \(f(k)=\exp(-k(k-1)/(2N))\). Then \(f(0)=f(1)=1\), \(f\) is decreasing on the integers \(k\ge 1\), and
\[
\sum_{k\ge 2}f(k)\le\int_1^\infty f\le\int_0^\infty f.
\]
Completing the square,
\[
f(t)=\exp\bigl(1/(8N)\bigr)\exp\bigl(-(t-1/2)^2/(2N)\bigr).
\]
The gaussian integral from \(0\) to \(\infty\) is at most \(\sigma+1/2\), since the half-line integral of \(e^{-u^2/(2N)}\) equals \(\sigma\) and the extra interval of length \(1/2\) is at most \(1/2\). Hence
\[
\mathbb{E}[T]\le 2+\exp(1/(8N))(\sigma+1/2).
\]
For \(x=1/(8N)\in(0,1)\), \(\exp(x)<1+x+x^2\). The integer comparison in `reference.bound_holds` is exactly
\[
\bigl(1+1/(8N)+1/(64N^2)\bigr)(s+1/2)\le s+2,
\]
which holds for this \(N\) and this \(s\). Therefore the i.i.d. expectation is at most \(s+4\).

The walk is not an i.i.d. sampler. Heuristic H1 below identifies its repeat time with that envelope and uses the same integer budget. On top of \(s+4\) the run always pays a detection slack of \(8\cdot 2^{97}\) steps, eight mean gaps of the checkpoint predicate \(\theta=2^{-97}\). A geometric wait longer than that has probability \(<e^{-8}\).

\[
\begin{align*}
S&=s+4+8\cdot 2^{97}\\
&=117165183892697767700873488638206726535.
\end{align*}
\]

Allocated checkpoint slots are \(2^{30}\). The expected number occupied under H2 is \(\theta S<2^{30}\).

\(C=2000\). One reference addition = 1. One base-field multiplication = 383 instructions. Squarings are charged as multiplications.

| Two Montgomery multiplications by \(\lambda\) on \((a,b)\) | \(2\cdot 256\) |

The 256-instruction cap on a multiply by \(\lambda\) sits above this limb count. \(\lambda\) has two 64-bit limbs and the coefficient has four. Two CIOS outer iterations, each with a 4-limb multiply (8 mul/umulh and 12 adc) and a 4-limb modulus reduction (1 mul for the Montgomery factor, 8 mul/umulh, 12 adc, 4 limb moves), give \(2\cdot 45=90\). A conditional subtract is 16. Loads of the inputs and the modulus, stores, and address arithmetic are 47. The sum is 153. Charging 256 leaves a second conditional subtract and extra carries.

Bookkeeping, charged at 128, covers two 6-limb comparisons among \(\{x,\beta x,\beta^2 x\}\) (24), the sign comparison of \(y\) with \(-y\) (12), two 4-limb adds of the jump scalars (20), the low-20-bit index (4), the 97-bit checkpoint test (12), and the pointer update (16). That list is 88.

Per step the charge is \(1+1406/2000=1703/1000\) target-group operations. Across \(S\) steps and 64 walks the additions still total \(S\), because the score sums processors.

## Heuristics

H1. The adding walk on canonical classes has its first repeat inside \(s+4\) steps with probability at least \(1/2\), in the same way the i.i.d. envelope does. The classical leading term for a random mapping is the same \(\sqrt{\pi N/2}\) (Flajolet–Odlyzko). Teske's adding walks with at least twenty jumps already track that constant; this table has \(2^{20}\) jumps. The numerical gap between the Rayleigh mass \(1-e^{-\pi/4}\approx 0.544\) at the mean and the required \(0.39\) is the room for the \(O(1)\) term and for a small adding-walk defect. The detection slack is not part of that room: it is paid in full, and a miss of eight mean checkpoint gaps has probability \(<e^{-8}\).

H2. The low 20 bits of the canonical \(x\), and bits \([20,117)\), behave as independent fair bits for a uniform input point. The canonical \(x\) is a smooth function of a uniform point on a 381-bit field, and \(p\) is odd, so a fixed window of 117 bits is equidistributed up to an error far below \(2^{-200}\). This is what makes the jump index a function of the class only, and what makes the checkpoint rate \(\theta=2^{-97}\).

H3. A checkpoint collision with \(b\equiv b'\) but a fruitless relation does not eat the gap between \(0.54\) and \(0.39\). On a single functional graph the coefficient sum around a cycle vanishes with probability about \(1/r\). Escapes keep previously stored checkpoints and keep spending the same budget.

The toy in `experiment.py` is not evidence for these probabilities at \(N=(r-1)/6\). It checks three finite statements: the generator identity \(\varphi(P_0)=[\lambda]P_0\), orbits of size 6 in a prime-order toy group, and exact recovery of a planted scalar from a checkpoint collision. At toy orders the checkpoint gap is a visible fraction of \(\sigma\), so the observed step counts are not a measurement of the production ratio \(W/s\).

## What is not claimed

The budget is not a high-probability bound with a proved random-mapping tail. It is the birthday integer \(s+4\), plus a deterministic detection slack, under H1–H3. No claim is made that the constant is optimal, that memory is practical, or that a production run was executed. The score is \(\log_2 W\) for the ledger above, rounded upward at six decimal places.
