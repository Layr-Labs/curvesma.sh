# Machine model and instruction ledger

All work is charged in scalar AArch64 instruction equivalents R and divided by C = 20,000
(`tracks/docs/COST_MODEL.md`, BN254 section). No BN254 operation is charged as one unit;
the dense Fp12 multiplication and the cyclotomic squaring are priced through their
implementation, down to the kernels listed below. Sections 1, 4 and 5 and the three
base-field leaves are those of the accepted base package 2ece9f04; the fused Fp2 leaves of
section 2a, the stored table sums and the recounted routines of section 3 are new.

## 1. Representation

Fp elements are four 64-bit limbs in Montgomery form x~ = x * 2^256 mod p, fully reduced
(0 <= x~ < p). The same kernels serve Z/r (exponent arithmetic) with the constants of r.
p and r are both below 2^254, so a + b < 2^255 never carries out of 256 bits, and the
Montgomery product of reduced inputs needs one final conditional subtraction.

Tower (the one the profile maps to, checked in `experiment.py` part A):
Fp2 = Fp[u]/(u^2+1), Fp12 = Fp2[w]/(w^6 - xi), xi = 9 + u. Grouped for the product as
Fp6 = Fp2[v]/(v^3 - xi), v = w^2, Fp12 = Fp6[w]/(w^2 - v). The profile basis
(w^12 = 18 w^6 - 82, u = w^6 - 9) maps to tower coefficients by
c_m(tower) = (c_m + 9 c_{m+6}) + c_{m+6} u; this conversion is done once for z at setup.

## 2. Leaf kernels

The three leaves are straight-line listings that read operands through x1, x2, write the
result through x0, use only caller-saved registers x0..x17 (no spills, no frame) and end
in `ret`. Their constants (m0..m3 and n0 = -m^-1 mod 2^64) sit at label K of a block per
modulus. `experiment.py` part K executes every listing on an instruction-level simulator of
the AArch64 subset used (ldr/ldp/stp/adr, mul/umulh, adds/adcs/adc, subs/sbcs, csel,
csetm, and), on 12 x 12 edge pairs plus random and all-limbs-large inputs for both p and
r, and checks the result against Python integers. Declared run: 519 cases per modulus,
0 failures. The executed instruction count is the listing length (straight line, `ret`
included).

| Leaf | Listing length | Call site (BL + up to 3 pointer set-ups) | Charged per call |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fp_mul (Montgomery product, product scanning then 4 word REDC rounds, 1 conditional subtraction) | 158 | 4 | **162** |
| fp_add (add, trial subtract, 4 csel) | 22 | 4 | **26** |
| fp_sub (subtract, borrow mask, masked add of m) | 23 | 4 | **27** |

## 2a. Fused Fp2 leaves (new)

Three further straight-line listings work on Fp2 elements (two consecutive Fp residues, 64
bytes). They read through x1 (and x2), write through x0, and besides x0..x17 use the
callee-saved registers x19..x28, which they save in a stack frame on entry and restore
before `ret`; the save, restore and `sp` adjustment instructions are part of the listing
and are counted. Part K executes each listing on the simulator (now also `sub`/`add` with
an immediate for `sp`) for the modulus p, with a separate output and with the output
aliased to operand a, on edge, random and all-limbs-large inputs, compares with Python
integers and checks after every run that `sp` and x19..x28 hold their entry values and
that only the operands' output area and the frame were written. Declared run: 342 runs per
listing set, 0 failures. Every output is fully reduced, so all leaves compose freely.

Two code blocks are shared (generated by `mulu_core` and `red_core` in `experiment.py`):

* `mulu` (63 instructions, registers only): the unreduced 8-limb product of two 4-limb
  integers by operand scanning; it is the first 69 instructions of `fp_mul` without its 6
  loads. Exact for all 4-limb inputs.
* `red` (82 instructions, registers only): Montgomery reduction of an 8-limb integer
  T < p*2^256: four word rounds (74 instructions, the same rounds as in `fp_mul`) give
  (T + q p)/2^256 < 2p, and T + q p < 2p*2^256 < 2^511 never carries out of 8 limbs; one
  conditional subtraction (8) gives the canonical residue T*2^-256 mod p.

The range facts used: p < 2^254, hence 2p < 2^255, 4p < 2^256 and 4p^2 < p*2^256.

| Leaf | What it computes (stored Montgomery residues) | Range argument | Listing length | Charged per call (+4) |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| f2mul | (a0 + a1 u)(b0 + b1 u): T0 = a0 b0, T1 = a1 b1, T2 = (a0+a1)(b0+b1) by three `mulu`; c1 = red(T2 - T0 - T1); c0 = red(T0 - T1, plus p*2^256 if the subtraction borrowed) | sums a0+a1, b0+b1 < 2p fit 4 limbs; T2 < 4p^2 < 2^512; T2 - T0 - T1 = a0 b1 + a1 b0 exactly, in [0, 2p^2); T0 - T1 in (-p^2, p^2), so after the masked addition of p to the high half it lies in [0, p*2^256) and is unchanged mod p | 443 | **447** |
| f2sqr | (a0 + a1 u)^2: c1 = red(2 a0 a1); c0 = red((a0+a1)(a0-a1+p)) | 2 a0 a1 < 2p^2; a0+a1 < 2p; a0-a1+p lies in (0, 2p), so its 256-bit wrap-around computation is exact; product < 4p^2 | 336 | **340** |
| f2mulxi | (9 + u)(a0 + a1 u) = (9 a0 - a1) + (9 a1 + a0) u | 9t by three doublings and one addition, each followed by a conditional subtraction of p in registers; one masked-borrow subtraction; no multiplication | 151 | **155** |

Instruction make-up of f2mul: frame and register save/restore 13; 12 operand loads (ldp)
and 8 stores of T0, T1 (stp), 8 reloads; 3 x 63 `mulu`; 8 for the two sums; 16 + 8 limb
subtractions, 1 mask, 4 `and`, 4 additions; 4 constant loads; 2 x 82 `red`; 4 result
stores. In the base package the same Fp2 product was a routine of 3 fp_mul, 2 fp_add and
3 fp_sub leaf calls charged 3*162 + 2*26 + 3*27 + 32 = 651; the Fp2 squaring was
2*162 + 2*26 + 27 + 32 = 435; the multiplication by xi was 9*26 + 27 = 261.

Non-leaf routine calls (Fp4 squaring, Fp6 multiplication, the two Fp12 routines) are charged
**32** instructions each: call site, a frame with four callee-saved registers, pointer
bookkeeping for temporaries on the stack, the epilogue and slack. Fp2 additions and
subtractions are inline pairs of leaf calls and are charged as those leaf calls.

## 3. Counted Fp12 routines

`experiment.py` implements the two routines in a counting tower (every leaf call and every
routine call increments a counter) and checks their results against an independent
schoolbook product on random GT elements of the real BN254 group (part A, `mul_ok`,
`sq_ok`; `mul_ok` also checks that the product with stored table sums equals the product
that recomputes them).

**Stored table sums.** In the Karatsuba product x * y with y = (Y0, Y1) over Fp6, the
operand y enters through Y0, Y1, Ys = Y0 + Y1 and, for each of these three Fp6 elements
b = (b0, b1, b2), the sums b1 + b2, b0 + b1, b0 + b2. In a multiply step y is a table entry,
so Ys and the nine Fp2 sums (12 Fp2 elements, 768 bytes) are computed once when the table
is built and stored next to the entry (`Tower.presums`); the step only loads them. This
removes 24 fp_add calls per product. The sums of the walk operand x are still computed in
the step. Building them costs 24 additions per table entry (12,288 entries), which is far
inside the setup bucket of section 5.

| Routine | routine calls | f2mul | f2sqr | f2mulxi | fp_add | fp_sub | Instructions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| dense Fp12 product by a table entry (3 Fp6 Karatsuba products of 6 Fp2 products) | 4 | 18 | 0 | 7 | 48 | 48 | 4*32 + 18*447 + 7*155 + 48*26 + 48*27 = **11,803** |
| Granger-Scott cyclotomic squaring (3 Fp4 squarings of 3 Fp2 squarings) | 4 | 0 | 9 | 4 | 42 | 18 | 4*32 + 9*340 + 4*155 + 42*26 + 18*27 = **5,386** |
| 12 Frobenius keys (section 2 of proof.md; base-field leaves: 4 fp_mul, 1 fp_add, 8 fp_sub) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 8 | 4*162 + 26 + 8*27 = **890** |

The base package counted 16,841 and 6,665 for the same two products (54 and 18 fp_mul leaf
calls with every intermediate reduced). The 48 fp_add of the dense product are the 24 sums
of the walk operand and 24 output additions; the 48 fp_sub are the Karatsuba corrections.

## 4. Per-step bookkeeping

The walk step itself is the Fp12 routine of its branch plus the following scalar work. The
figures are itemised instruction estimates for straightforward code, then charged with a
margin.

Every step:

| --- | ---: |

| table address ((12h + t) * 384 + base: madd, lsl, add) | 4 |
| exponent update mod r: two fp_add leaf calls (alpha += A[h][t], beta += B[h][t], or alpha += alpha, beta += beta) | 52 |
| 2-cycle test: four limbs of T against T two steps back (2 ldp each, cmp, 3 ccmp, b.eq) | 10 |

| step counter, checkpoint period test (and #63, cbnz), amortised checkpoint store | 6 |

## 5a. Work meter and step counter (this package)

The meter of the rejected GT twin of the gate design (8b901b3d) used a 128-bit accumulator, which
cannot hold a cap of about 2^145.4 instructions. Here both counters are wide enough by
construction and their instructions are charged in the per-step bookkeeping above:

* **Work accumulator: 256 bits** (four 64-bit registers), counting scalar instructions. Each step
  adds its branch's charged price (13,153 or 5,686, an immediate pair selected by the branch bit),
  each report adds 10,000, and every non-walk routine (setup exponentiations, candidate checks,
  restarts, output) adds its charged price at its call. The cap W_meter x 20,000 is below 2^151
  instructions (`gate.py`: `meter_instructions_log2` = 150.90, `meter_fits_256_bits`), so the
  accumulator never wraps: its value is at most the cap plus one step price, below 2^152 < 2^256. Add with
  carry over four words: 4 instructions; unsigned 256-bit compare with the cap held in four
  registers by a subtract-with-borrow chain and a conditional branch: 5 instructions; 9 in all.
* **Step counter: 192 bits** (three registers), incremented once per executed walk step of any walk
  (3 instructions) and compared with S_cap(1+eps)+E (below 2^137.3 steps; 4 instructions); 7 in all.
  192 bits exceed every reachable value by more than 2^54.

The itemised per-step bookkeeping rises from 129 to 139 instructions, still inside the charged
COMMON = 300, so the step prices below are unchanged. The registers are reserved across the walk
loop (AArch64 has 31 general-purpose registers; the Fp12 routines save and restore callee-saved
registers inside their own 32-instruction routine charge).

## 5b. Output (pairing-inversion track)

After a verified x (g^x = z): Q = [x]Q0 by left-to-right double-and-add in Jacobian coordinates
on y^2 = x^3 + 3/(9+u) over Fp2, 254 doublings (2M + 5S + 10 Fp2 add/sub) and at most 254 mixed
additions (7M + 4S + 12 Fp2 add/sub). Priced with the fused Fp2 leaves of section 2a (product
447 with call site, squaring 340) and Fp2 additions/subtractions as two base-field leaves (at
most 54): a doubling is under 3,600 and an addition under 5,700 instructions; 254 of each, one
Fp2 inversion by Fermat in Fp (under 600 fp_mul-equivalent leaf calls, 100,000 instructions)
and the affine conversion stay under 2.5 million instructions = 125 units, **charged 2^10
units**. P0 is a profile constant. For z = 1 the output is (P0, infinity) at no cost.

## 5. Distinguished-point reports and setup

A report (at most 2^65.44 of them, proof.md section 5) computes the keys if the step did
not (1,050), multiplies alpha and beta by the stored constant p^kappa * 2^256 mod r (2 x 162),
extracts the 128-bit fingerprint, hashes it into the chained multimap and writes an 80-byte
record (under 600 instructions including memory traffic): under 2,000 instructions,
**charged 10,000** (0.5 unit). Setup work (pairing-free: g is a profile constant, z is
given): conversion of z, the check z^r = 1, the table of 1,024 multipliers g^a z^b with
their 11 Frobenius images and the 24 stored sums of each entry, 2^20 walk starts g^alpha z^beta, each exponentiation at most 254
squarings and 254 products (under 400 units), all together under 2^31 units.
