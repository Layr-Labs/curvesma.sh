# Finite relation rank and padded block Wiedemann

Three statements are kept apart: (a) relation saturation, (b) finite-length block controllability and observability at the charged lengths on the actual projection support, (c) a Schwartz-Zippel bound that is conditional on (a) and (b). Class numbers prime to r and injective Schirokauer maps imply none of them.

**(a) H-R (finite relation saturation, premise).** For the actually collected, tau-quotiented and filtered relation matrix A=[M|S] over F_r, with D=N+7, M has column rank N and A has rank D-1. Its kernel is exactly the line generated by the virtual logarithms (w,c), with c!=0. This is a structural NFS relation-saturation heuristic for this fixed finite relation set. Counting smooth relations, class numbers prime to r, and injective Schirokauer maps do not prove it. The excess, complete relation-generation rules, occurrence/filtering experiments and standard finite-field NFS framework provide its exploratory support. Synthetic planted matrices and solver replays do not establish saturation of the production relation set. If H-R fails, randomized Wiedemann cannot repair the missing relation information; rank/verification checks can reject but do not supply a failure-probability bound.

Notation. B=[M|0] (D-by-D, D=N+7), V=ImM+span(S); under (a) dim V=D-1 and B(V) lies in ImM, inside V. Y=[S_1,...,S_7,BZ]: the seven Schirokauer columns are fixed start columns, Z has n-7=33 independent uniform columns. X has m=1024 rows; the partition is fixed publicly before sampling: the seven pad coordinates lie in blocks 1..7, the nonpad coordinates in the specified fixed shuffled balanced partition into m blocks; each coordinate has one independent uniform 128-bit entry on its block's row, zeros elsewhere. The solver uses exactly

    d = ceil(D/n) + 1 + ceil(D/(128n)),   e = ceil(D/m) + 1 + ceil(D/(128m)),

the minimal lengths plus a 2^-7 margin, and the terms a_i = X B^i Y for i = 0..d+e-1 (d+e terms).

**(b) H-X (finite controllability and observability, premise).** For this fixed A, partition and support, at exactly these d and e: (i) C_d=[BZ, BY, ..., B^dY] has rank N for some Z over F_r; (ii) O_e=[X; XB; ...; XB^(e-1)] is injective on V for some X with the stated support. Equivalently an N-minor of C_d is a nonzero polynomial in Z, and a (D-1)-minor of O_e U, U a fixed basis of V, is a nonzero polynomial in the supported entries of X. (i) needs the seven fixed columns to keep contributing at length d; (ii) concerns the sparse support, not dense projections. H-X is a property of the actual matrix and support. It does not follow from rank M=N, from invariant factors (which belong to B; finite Krylov increments depend on the start block), or from eventual module generation, and no general block-Wiedemann theorem is invoked for this support. Like H-R it is a declared heuristic premise.

**(c) Conditional bound.** Given (a) and (b), the product of the two minors has degree at most N+(D-1)=2N+6<4N in independently sampled entries, each uniform on at least 2^128 values; so rank C_d<N or O_e not injective on V occurs for the sampled Z, X with probability at most 4N/2^128 (Schwartz-Zippel), charged in success. This bounds unlucky sampling only, not (a) or (b).

Recovery (proved, given (i) and (ii)). A nonzero (w,c) in kerA has c!=0 since M has full column rank, and Sc lies in ImM=ImB. As C_d spans ImB there are e_0, f_1, ..., f_d with Sc+BZe_0+sum_(i=1)^d B^iYf_i=0; with f_0=(c,e_0) this is R_d f=0, R_d=[Y,BY,...,B^dY], and H=O_eR_d (blocks a_(i+j), j<e, i<=d) has a kernel vector with nonzero first seven coordinates. Conversely any f with Hf=0 has R_d f in V and O_e R_d f=0, so R_d f=0 by (ii). The already-priced block minimal-generator algorithm returns a basis of the projected relations of degree at most d; take a basis column whose first seven constant coordinates are not all zero (fail if none: a polynomial combination takes its constant term from constant basis columns). For f_0=(c,e_0), wbar=Ze_0+sum_(i>=1)B^(i-1)Yf_i gives M wbar_firstN+Sc=0, so (wbar_firstN,c) is a nonzero kernel vector, proportional to the virtual logs under (a). No coordinate of c is presumed nonzero. Horner evaluates wbar.

Certificate. The attack computes A(wbar_firstN,c) (one more product) and uses the vector only if this is zero and c!=0, then reconstructs purged logs and verifies the final target equation. A wrong vector is never used; a failure of (b) is detected (the run fails), not bounded.

Charging (la_bn). Block products BZ and B^iY (i<d+e) and their projections: d+e < (1+2^-7)D(1/m+1/n)+6 = bw_steps, charged in place of N(1/m+1/n); generator length L = bw_steps terms. Horner (d-1 products, Ze_0, the certificate product): charged at max(N(1/m+1/n), (1+2^-7)D/n+4) single-vector steps. No wrapper allowance covers these steps. The margin costs about 2^-7 of the Krylov work.

Diagnostics (not proof). Unplanted analogue (our scripts, not shipped). Two genuine two-sided NFS fields, each a totally real quartic f and quintic g, p | Res(f, g), prime r' | p - 1 (f, g = x^4+5x^3+5x^2-5x-5, x^5-8x^4+17x^3+2x^2-24x-1, p = 44351999, r' = 22175999; x^4+4x^3-x^2-16x-11, x^5-10x^4+35x^3-50x^2+24x-1, p = 206417833, r' = 8600743), class numbers 1; line-sieved relations with two large primes, singleton removal, pruning to excess 7, merging over F_r'; s = 3 + 4 = 7 real Schirokauer columns (coordinates injective on the fundamental units). Matrices (N, mean row weight): (2400, 494), (3000, 64), (3500, 196), (4000, 100). In all 26 cases (n = 40 and 127, 149; m = 64, 256, 1024; Y = [S | BZ]; the stated sparse support; the formula lengths ceil(D/n) + 1, ceil(D/m) + 1 without the margin): rank M = N, rank A = D - 1 with nonzero kernel S-part, rank C_d = N, rank O_e A = D - 1, and end-to-end recovery of the kernel line; every block step added n new Krylov dimensions (the 7 fixed columns included) and m observed ones until the last (least lengths d - 2, e - 1). Pads collapsed into one block: rank O_e A = N + 1 < D - 1. Small-scale behaviour of real filtered NFS matrices; it does not establish H-X at production size.

[Author framework](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book44/files/12oliver.pdf). Fried, Gaudry, Heninger and Thome, *A kilobit hidden SNFS discrete logarithm computation*, section 3.1, separate filtering, Schirokauer columns, the homogeneous (M|S) system and virtual logarithms ([arXiv 1610.02874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.02874)); this is the algebraic framework only, not evidence for (a) or (b) on this tower, quotient or support.
